# Design: Dice & Destiny Edition 2 expansion

Date: 2026-11-15.
Status: draft. The human answered the open questions. The human must
approve this design before work starts.

## Intent

Dice & Destiny is the SRD 5.2 rules plus original content. Its purpose is
to let a group play a long campaign in a large world without buying
expansion books. The human says: "the public rules plus everything we add,
as a way to play a vast world without paying for expansions."

Edition 2 makes the game fuller in two ways:

- **Breadth:** about 3.5 times the current original content. More classes,
  subclasses, species, spells, monsters, items, and tools for the GM.
- **Depth:** 8 optional rules systems that change how the game plays.

Ready-made campaign books come later. They are not part of this work.
Edition 2 gives the GM the parts to build any world.

## Goals

- Reach the totals in "Targets" in the two books.
- Add the 8 optional systems.
- Keep each entry original, balanced, and in one consistent style.
- Print as two A4 hardcover books at Lulu.

## Non-goals

- No setting, map, or campaign. (The sample pantheons are generic and a GM
  can replace them.)
- No conversion of distances. The books keep feet. The human decided this.
- No change to the SRD text, except the changes that the build already
  makes (the trademark sentences and the em-dashes).

## Anti-goals

- **Filler.** An entry that only renames or slightly changes another entry
  does not count toward a target.
- **Broken balance.** No option may be clearly stronger than the SRD options
  at the same level, rarity, or CR. The 8 optional systems are the
  exception (see "Power of the optional systems").
- **Copied paid content.** Do not reproduce, paraphrase, or rename content
  from a paid book. An entry can fill the same role (for example, a
  dragon-themed sorcerer) with new rules and new text. Do not use names
  that are not in the SRD and that a publisher owns (for example,
  "beholder" or "mind flayer").
- **Trademarks.** No "Dungeons & Dragons" or "D&D" in the book text.
- **Em-dashes.** None anywhere. `AGENTS.md` has the rule.
- **A book that is too large.** Each book must stay below 700 pages. Lulu
  accepts 800 pages for a hardcover.

only looks up what is necessary), and about 3 times where many choices make

## The 8 optional systems

Each system is a module. A GM turns on only the systems that the table
wants. Each system has its own chapter in an "Optional Rules" part. Each
system works alone, without the others.

Player's Compendium:

1. **Marks.** A character carries up to 3 Marks: curses, blessings, or the
   taint of a Sin. Each Mark has a benefit and a cost, and a track from 0
   to 5 that grows through story events. The Mark changes at thresholds.
   About 20 sample Marks: the 7 Sins, 7 virtues, and curses. Silvio's curse
   and the Envy points in the campaign are the model.
2. **Bonds.** A bond between two characters: friend, rival, debt, or oath.
   A bond die grows from d4 to d10 through shared scenes. A player spends
   it to help the bonded ally. The bond strains when a character betrays
   it.
3. **Techniques.** Each character has technique points equal to the
   proficiency bonus. They return on a short rest. They pay for stances,
   maneuvers, and spell shaping. About 60 techniques.
4. **Legend paths.** A second track that grows with deeds, not XP. Examples:
   Dragonslayer, Saint, Tyrant, Wanderer. 5 tiers for each path. It
   continues after level 20.

Game Master's Compendium:

5. **Injuries and scars.** A critical hit or a drop to 0 HP can cause an
   injury from a table. The injury stays until it heals. A healed injury
   leaves a scar with a small benefit.
6. **Crafting and gathering.** Materials come from monsters and biomes.
   Recipes make potions and magic items. Each recipe has a time, a cost,
   and a DC.
7. **Factions and renown.** Renown from 0 to 50 with a faction, with ranks,
   rewards, and enemies. It uses the faction tables.
8. **Journeys.** Travel roles (guide, scout, quartermaster, lookout),
   supply dice, weather, getting lost, and encounter tables for each biome.

Design Marks and Bonds first. They come from the campaign and test the
style of the systems. The orchestrator session designs them with the human.
The design of Marks decides if the track of a Mark is secret (the GM keeps
it) or open (the player keeps it), and who gives a Mark.

### Power of the optional systems (the human decided)

The optional systems can make characters stronger. They do not cost a feat
or a class feature. Each system chapter in the GM's book tells the GM how to
make encounters harder when the system is on. The style guide gives the
budget for each system.

## Approaches

- **A. One session writes everything in order.** One author gives one
  style. It is very slow, and the context of a long session is compressed
  many times.
- **B. One guide, then parallel sessions.** **The human chose B.** The
  orchestrator session writes a style and balance guide. Then focused
  sessions build the content in batches and push to `main`. The
  orchestrator examines each batch against the guide. Automatic checks
  find problems.
- **C. Fewer, hand-polished entries.** Half the targets. It does not give
  the 3 to 4 times that the human asked for.

## Decisions

### The style and balance guide (first deliverable)

Put it in `docs/dice-and-destiny/style-guide.md`. Every content session
reads it before it writes. It contains:

- Voice: the plain, friendly voice of the current books.
- The format of each entry type, the same as the current content files.
- Power budgets: monster statistics by CR (the monster engine already
  calculates these), spell damage by level, item power by rarity, feat
  power by level, and subclass feature levels for each class.
- Naming rules and a list of names that are not permitted.
- What "original" means, with examples of filling a role without copying.
- Writing rules: the book text is not in STE (see `AGENTS.md`), no em-dash,
  no emoji, no trademarks.

### Content layout

Split `compendium/content/` into one folder for each category:

    compendium/content/
      classes/   subclasses/   species/   backgrounds/   feats/
      spells/    monsters/     npcs/      items/         traps/
      tables/    pantheons/    systems/   downtime/

Each folder is a Python package. Its `__init__.py` collects the entries of
the files in the folder. A new file in a folder needs no other change.
The current `orig_*.py` files move into these folders. This move must not
change the built books (same page count and text as before the move).

Each new category (classes, NPCs, traps, tables, pantheons, systems,
downtime) needs a renderer in `compendium/build/` and a placeholder and a
chapter in a book source.

### Workstreams (one session each)

The workstreams start after the style guide and the layout move. The
systems workstream starts after the human approves Marks and Bonds.

1. Player options: species, backgrounds, feats, spells.
2. Classes and subclasses: 4 new classes, and the new subclasses. The
   session first gives the human 6 to 8 class concepts. The human chooses
   4. (Decision of the agent.)
   The human chose: Captain, Shaper, Spiritbinder, and Apothecary.
3. Optional systems: the 8 systems.
4. Bestiary: monsters and NPCs.
5. Treasure and GM tools: magic items, traps, tables, pantheons, downtime.

The orchestrator session (this session) writes the guide, the layout, and
the checks, starts the sessions, and examines each batch.

### Review of each batch (the human decided)

- A workstream session writes one batch and runs the build and the checks.
- The orchestrator session examines the batch against the style guide.
- If the batch is correct, it goes to `main` with the human as the writer.
- The orchestrator gives the human a short summary of each batch, so that
  the human can examine some entries.

The campaign and tutorial sessions continue with their own work.

### Automatic checks

Add a check script that the build and the release workflow run. It stops
on:

- a duplicate name in a category, or a name on the list that is not
  permitted
- monster statistics that are not in the range for the CR
- spell damage above the curve for the spell level
- an em-dash, or the trademark, in the content files
- a book above 700 pages

### Hardcover covers for Lulu

Add a hardcover mode to `compendium/build/cover.py`. Take the trim, the
wrap, the hinge, the bleed, and the spine width for the page count from the
Lulu cover template for an A4 casewrap hardcover with standard black and
white paper. Keep the paperback mode. `build.sh` makes both cover types.
Each release has both cover types. (Decision of the agent.)

### Releases

- The preview releases continue for each change.
- Tag `edition-2` when the human says that Edition 2 is complete.
- The current books are Edition 1 (the human decided). The tag `edition-1`
  goes on commit `021ce00`, before the first Edition 2 commit. This session
  cannot push a tag, so the human pushes it.

### Order of work

1. The style and balance guide.
2. The content layout move, and the automatic checks.
3. Marks and Bonds (the orchestrator session designs them, with the human).
4. Start the workstream sessions. They work in parallel, in batches.
5. The hardcover cover mode.
6. The new chapters and the parts in the books, when the content for them
   exists.
7. A full review against the targets. Then the human decides on the
   `edition-2` tag.

## Answers of the human (after the content was complete)

1. **The trademark notice.** The copyright page of both books keeps the
   sentence "Dungeons & Dragons is a trademark of Wizards of the Coast LLC."
   It is a legal notice, not book text.
2. **The crafting table.** The Crafting and Gathering chapter keeps the
   cost and the time of each rarity as its own rule. It does not say that
   the SRD has the table.
3. **Lulu templates.** The human downloads the Lulu hardcover templates for
   the final page counts. The covers are checked against them before the
   `edition-2` tag.

## Left to the builder

- The order of the batches inside each workstream.
- The exact budgets in the guide, based on the SRD.
- The names of the new chapters and parts.
