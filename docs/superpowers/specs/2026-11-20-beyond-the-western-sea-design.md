# Design: Beyond the Western Sea

Date: 2026-11-20.
Status: the book is written and builds. The tag `western-sea-1` releases
it.

Related designs: [The Dawnwatch](2026-11-16-the-dawnwatch-design.md),
[the Dawnwatch world bible](2026-11-16-dawnwatch-world.md),
[Edition 2 expansion](2026-11-15-edition-2-expansion-design.md).

## Intent

The Dawnwatch is a long campaign. Book One alone takes about 30 sessions.
Many groups do not like long campaigns (decided by the human). They want a
full story with a true end, in a few months.

**Beyond the Western Sea** is a short campaign for these groups:

- Levels 1 to 5 (decided by the human).
- About 8 to 10 sessions.
- One book of 120 to 140 pages (decided by the human).
- It must feel very different from Waking (decided by the human). Waking is
  epic and dark, it crosses a full land, and its enemy is ancient. This
  campaign is one island, a lost colony, wonder, and a clock that everyone
  can see.

A group can play it with only the dice of a backgammon set: four six-sided
dice and a doubling cube. A group can also play it with normal dice. The
book supports both (decided by the human).

## The story (decided by the human)

### The premise

A ship from Corrantey sails west. A storm wrecks it on an island that is on
no map. A lost colony from old Ostrey lives there. The island is alive: it
is the back of a giant sea creature that sleeps, and its heart is deep
under the island. The party must find a way home before the storm season
comes back.

### The place in the Dawnwatch world (decided by the human)

- The island is off the west coast of the Dawnwatch land, in the Western
  Sea.
- The year is 300, the same year as Waking. The story does not touch the
  Dawnwatch story: no Hush, no Malgrath, no black stones, no Dawnfire.
- The gazetteer of Waking says that ships "do not go far out, because
  nobody knows what is out there and nobody has ever come back to say."
  This campaign explains that sentence. The party can be the first to come
  back.
- The characters of Waking are the five sleepers. A group that plays this
  campaign first starts Waking with new characters. The world carries
  over: the places, the powers, and the gods.

### The colony (approved by the human)

- In about year -60, before the war of the Unlit King, the rival ports
  Corrin and Tey sent one fleet west together. A wall of storms trapped the
  fleet. No ship came back.
- After 360 years, the colonists keep the customs of old Ostrey. They say
  "Light to you", they pray to the Morning Flame, and they count in suns,
  stars, and bits. They do not know of the Unlit King. A group that later
  plays Waking hears this old world again.
- The colonists keep the old rivalry:
  - **The Corrin men** want to go home. They guard the ruins of the
    lighthouse of the fleet, **the Corrin Light**.
  - **The Tey folk** worship the heart of the island as **the Grandam**.
    They believe that to leave, or to light the lighthouse, wakes her and
    sinks everyone.

### The heart and the storm (approved by the human)

- The wall of storms is the breath of the creature.
- Each storm season, she stirs. The Storm Cube (see "The dice") tracks
  this. At 64, she starts to wake.
- Neither side is fully right. The lighthouse can open a path through the
  storm, but only while she stirs. That is the one time when the party can
  leave, and also the time of most danger.

### The end (approved by the human)

The last chapter starts when the cube reaches 64. The party decides three
things:

- who comes home with them,
- whether the heart wakes or sleeps again,
- what they tell Corrantey: the first true news from beyond the sea.

There is no single correct end. The book gives the GM the result of each
choice.

## Names (decided by the human)

The names must sound like real names, not like generated names. The
sources are real legends and real ship and place names:

- **Saint Brendan**, an Irish monk, sailed west and landed on an island.
  His crew lit a fire on it, and the island moved. It was the back of a
  whale.
- **Antillia**, an island of the Atlantic on old maps. Seven bishops fled
  there by sea with their people and built seven towns. It is a lost
  colony.

The Dawnwatch land already uses names of this kind: Kingsholm (*holm* is
Old Norse for a small island), Corrantey, Wenlow.

| Thing | Name |
|---|---|
| The book | **Dice & Destiny: Beyond the Western Sea** (decided by the human) |
| The island | Farholm. The colonists named it. |
| The ship of the party | the *Good Intent*, a real merchant ship name of the 1700s |
| The flagship of the old fleet | the *Morning Star* |
| The heart of the island | the Grandam, an old English word for grandmother |
| The two sides of the colony | the Corrin men and the Tey folk |
| The lighthouse | the Corrin Light |

- The church of the colony has a legend of a saint who lit a fire on the
  back of the Grandam. It is the Brendan story, told in the world.
- Sites have plain names, for example Wreckers' Bay, Gull Rock, the
  Boneyard, Founders' Hill, and the Saltpans.
- Chapters have plain titles, for example "The Wreck of the *Good Intent*"
  and "Storm Season".
- The builder names the other places and the NPCs in the same style. The
  test: a name could be on an old sea chart or in a parish register.

## The dice (decided by the human, rules proposed by the agent)

### Two kinds of group

- **A group with a backgammon set** has four six-sided dice (4d6) and a
  doubling cube (faces 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64).
- **A group with normal dice** has the d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, and d20.

The book supports both. A GM never converts a number at the table.

### The six-sided rules

One page at the front of the book gives these rules. A group with normal
dice does not use them.

- **The d20 roll.** For an attack roll, an ability check, or a saving
  throw, roll **3d6**. The average is 10.5, the same as a d20, so armor
  class, DCs, and modifiers do not change.
- **Advantage:** roll 4d6 and keep the 3 highest. **Disadvantage:** roll
  4d6 and keep the 3 lowest.
- **A critical hit:** a natural 16 or more on the 3d6 (4.6 percent). A
  d20 gives 5 percent.
- **A natural 1** (death saves and other rules that use it): a natural 5
  or less on the 3d6 (4.6 percent).
- **Other dice** become a d6 and a bonus, with about the same average:

  | Die | Six-sided form | Average |
  |---|---|---|
  | d4 | d6 minus 1, minimum 1 | about 2.5 |
  | d8 | d6 + 1 | 4.5 |
  | d10 | d6 + 2 | 5.5 |
  | d12 | d6 + 3 | 6.5 |

- Hit Dice change in the same way.
- **More than four dice:** roll the four dice again and add. For example,
  roll 8d6 as two rolls of 4d6.

### What 3d6 changes

The 3d6 results are near the middle more often. An easy roll becomes
easier, and a hard roll becomes much harder. For example, DC 18 with a +3
bonus needs a 15 or more: 30 percent on a d20, 9 percent on 3d6. So the
book keeps its DCs from 8 to 15 and its armor class from 10 to 16. The
builder checks each encounter with both kinds of dice.

### How the book prints a roll

- Each roll in the book shows the normal dice first and the six-sided form
  in brackets, for example "Hit: 7 (1d8 + 3) [d6 + 4]".
- The build makes the bracket. Nobody writes it by hand. The statblock
  renderer and the item renderer change each dice expression with the
  table above. So each bracket is correct, and the SRD and Compendium
  monsters that this book uses also get the bracket.
- The change applies to this book only. Waking and the Compendiums do not
  change.
- Random tables in this book use d66 (one d6 for tens, one for units, 36
  results) or 2d6. Never a d20 or a d100. Each dice set has d6s, so these
  tables work for both groups.
- The characters of the players come from the Player's Compendium, with
  normal dice. The cheat sheet of this book gives the six-sided form for
  the players who need it.

### The Storm Cube

The doubling cube is the clock and the stakes of the campaign. The group
does not roll it. The cube stays on the table, where each player can see
it.

- It starts on **2**.
- The GM turns it up one face when the storm comes nearer: a long rest in
  the open, a lost day, a bad choice, or an event of a site.
- At **64**, the storm season starts, and the last chapter starts. The
  party may not be ready.
- **To double.** As in backgammon, a player can choose to turn the cube up
  one face early. Then that player gets a large benefit, for example a
  reroll, a sure success on a gathering roll, or a free short rest. Each
  shortcut brings the storm nearer, and the table sees it.
- **With normal dice:** use a d6 and turn it from 1 to 6, or 6 boxes on
  paper. The rules do not change.
- The builder sets the pace. The cube has only five steps from 2 to 64.
  The middle of the campaign takes the cube from 2 to about 32.

## Structure

### Approaches

| Approach | Gives | Costs |
|---|---|---|
| Linear chapters, as in Waking | The easiest to run and to write | An island to explore in a fixed order does not feel like exploration. The cube has few choices to push on. |
| An open island: sites in any order | The most freedom, and the cube is the real pressure | The hardest for a new GM. Levels and difficulty are hard to control. Eight sessions can become thin. |
| **Fixed start, open middle, fixed end** | Exploration and real choices for the cube, with a guided start and a sure end | A little more writing than linear chapters |

The human did not object to the third approach, so the book uses it.

### The parts of the campaign

1. **Chapter 1: the wreck and the shore.** Linear. Levels 1 to 2. It
   teaches the island, the Storm Cube, and the six-sided rules.
2. **The island.** About 10 sites, in any order:
   - **The outer ring:** levels 2 to 3. The coast, the colony, and the
     two sides.
   - **The inner ring:** levels 3 to 4. The interior, the ruins of the
     fleet, and the way to the heart.
   - The parts of the lighthouse, the two sides of the colony, and the
     clues to the heart are in different sites. So each order of sites
     works.
3. **The last chapter: storm season.** Linear. Level 5. It starts when the
   cube reaches 64.

The characters go up a level at fixed points: at the end of chapter 1, and
after a number of sites in each ring. The builder sets the numbers.

## The book

### The parts

Target: 120 to 140 pages (decided by the human).

| Part | Pages (about) |
|---|---|
| Front matter, contents, credits | 4 |
| How to run this book, the six-sided rules, the Storm Cube | 10 |
| A short player's guide (Corrantey, the ship, the voyage) | 6 |
| Chapter 1 | 12 |
| The island: 10 sites | 50 |
| The last chapter | 10 |
| A short gazetteer of Farholm | 14 |
| Appendices: creatures, people, treasures | 18 |
| Maps, handouts, cheat sheet, index | 10 |

The builder can move pages between parts, inside the target.

### What it reuses from Waking

- **The format of the text:** read-aloud text, a box for each new rule,
  and a Scaling box for 3 to 6 characters in each fight.
- **The authoring rules** in
  [the Dawnwatch authoring rules](2026-11-16-dawnwatch-authoring.md),
  where they apply.
- **Handouts:** each handout is on its own page, with nothing on the back.
- **The reference:** appendices from A to Z, and an index of each name.

### New content

- About 8 new creatures, 12 people, and 8 treasures. The builder sets the
  numbers. The other creatures come from the Compendiums by name.
- No new player options: no classes, subclasses, species, feats, or
  spells.
- No new rules system, except the six-sided rules and the Storm Cube. The
  story uses four of the optional systems of Edition 2:
  - **Journeys:** travel on the island, and the supply die.
  - **Crafting and gathering:** materials of the island, and the repair of
    the lighthouse.
  - **Injuries and scars:** the dangers of the wreck and of the storm.
  - **Factions and renown:** the Corrin men and the Tey folk.

### Maps

- One map: Farholm. The design had a second map, of the colony town. inkland
  1.3.3 cannot draw a town plan (no streets and no buildings), so the agent
  removed the town map while the human did not give input. The gazetteer
  describes Hythe in words. A later version can add a town map.
- The maps come from inkland, as the maps of Waking do. They must look
  hand drawn, not like a machine made them.
- The builder decides how inkland makes the town map. If inkland cannot
  make a good town map, the builder tells the human before another tool
  makes it.

## Goals

- A full campaign from level 1 to level 5 in about 8 to 10 sessions.
- A true end with real choices.
- A book of 120 to 140 pages, printed as an A4 hardcover at Lulu.
- A group with a backgammon set can play it, and so can a group with
  normal dice.
- Names that sound real.

## Non-goals

- No change to the two Compendiums or to Waking.
- No link to the plot of the Dawnwatch.
- No new player options.

## Anti-goals

- **A small copy of Waking.** If a part of the book feels like Waking,
  change it.
- **Names that sound generated.** For example, names made of two English
  words with a capital letter on each ("Stormheart", "Saltwake").
- **Six-sided rules that change the balance.** The averages must stay the
  same as with normal dice.
- **A book above 140 pages.**
- **Spoilers in the player's part.** The player's guide and the handouts
  must not tell the secret of the island.
- **Em-dashes.** None anywhere. `AGENTS.md` has the rule.

## Content and build

- The new content goes into its own folder,
  `compendium/content/western_sea/`, with the same formats, the same
  balance guide, and the same checks as the Dawnwatch content.
- The book source goes into `compendium/books/`, in the same layout as
  Waking.
- The build makes the same five PDFs as for Waking: screen, home print,
  Lulu interior, Lulu hardcover cover, and paperback cover.
- The six-sided bracket is a change to the renderers. It is on for this
  book only. It has its own tests.
- New facts about the Western Sea and the fleet go into the world bible
  first, in the same commit as the text that uses them.
