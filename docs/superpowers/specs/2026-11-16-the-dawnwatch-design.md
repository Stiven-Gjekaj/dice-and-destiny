# Design: The Dawnwatch

Date: 2026-11-16.
Status: draft. The human reviews it before work starts.

Related designs: [Dice & Destiny](2026-11-15-dice-and-destiny-design.md),
[Edition 2 expansion](2026-11-15-edition-2-expansion-design.md).

## Intent

Edition 2 gives the GM the parts to build any world. It has no setting and
no campaign. The Edition 2 design says: "Ready-made campaign books come
later."

The Dawnwatch is the first campaign. It continues the tutorial adventure,
[The Last Watch of the Sleepers](../../../adventures/tutorial-one-shot/README.md),
into a full story from level 1 to level 20. The campaign is two books
(decided by the human):

| Book | Acts | Levels | Chapters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Dawnwatch: Waking** | I and II | 1 to 10 | 10 |
| **The Dawnwatch: The Last Dawn** | III and IV | 11 to 20 | 9 |

The short form: Edition 2 gives you the parts. The Dawnwatch gives you a
world to play in.

### Why two books

A campaign from level 1 to level 20 takes many groups two or three years.
Many groups stop at about level 10. So Book One ends at level 10 with a
true end: a victory and the big twist. A group can stop there and have a
full story. A group that continues takes Book Two.

## The story base

The tutorial already starts the story:

- Three hundred years ago, **Malgrath, the Unlit King**, tried to put out
  the sun. The old kingdom pushed him back into the dark.
- The kingdom put five guards, **the Dawnwatch**, to sleep in crystal under
  **Dawnwatch Keep**. They wait for his return.
- Today the crystal cracks. The characters are the Dawnwatch. Their guide
  is **Wick**, a talking brass lantern.
- The tutorial ends when the characters reach the surface and see how much
  the world changed.

The campaign starts at that moment. The finale is the fight against
Malgrath for the sun.

## The world (decided by the agent)

- **Aubade** is the old kingdom. The word is an old name for a song at
  dawn. The Dawnwatch knew Aubade. It fell 200 years ago.
- **The Vesperlands** is the world of today. "Vesper" is an old word for
  the evening, and the sun is dying. Three powers hold the land:
  - **The Ember Marches.** A state of soldiers in the south. Its lords say
    that they are the true heirs of Aubade.
  - **Vesperhold.** The seat of the **Church of the Five Sleepers**. The
    Church prays for the return of the Dawnwatch.
  - **The Lantern Coast.** Free cities on the sea. The guild of lantern
    makers lives here. It kept the craft of Wick alive.
- **The Unlit Crown** is the fortress of Malgrath, in the north, at the edge
  of the world.

The names are new. Check each name against the anti-goals before use.

## The story (approved by the human)

The theme: heroes who made a promise to a world that forgot them.

The tutorial already gives three hooks. The campaign uses all three:

- The **black stone** in the pocket of each character. It hums when
  Malgrath speaks.
- The words of Malgrath at the end of the tutorial: "Sleepers. I also
  slept."
- The **Before** and **After** questions. Each character lost a person or a
  place 300 years ago.

### Book One: Waking

#### Act I: Waking (levels 1 to 4, chapters 1 to 4)

- Chapter 1 is the tutorial. It ends with the cliffhanger, as it does now.
- In the first town, the Dawnwatch learn that Aubade fell 200 years ago.
  Their names are now a religion: the **Church of the Five Sleepers**
  prays for their return. The Church does not know that they are real.
  Some people want to worship them. Some want to use them. Some say that
  they are impostors.
- The land has new borders, new roads, and old ruins. The **Journeys**
  system starts here.
- At the end of the act, a cult of the Unlit King tries to take the black
  stones. The party learns that the stones are linked to Malgrath, and
  that he can feel where the stones are.

#### Act II: The Ashes of the Kingdom (levels 5 to 10, chapters 5 to 10)

- The three powers and the cult compete. Each wants the Dawnwatch on its
  side.
- The party takes back **Dawnwatch Keep** as its base. The **Strongholds**
  system starts here.
- The party looks for the pieces of the **Dawnfire**, the flame that kept
  Malgrath asleep.
- **The end of Book One** has two parts:
  - **A victory.** The party breaks the cult and holds the keep.
  - **The twist.** The history in the player's guide is a lie. **Malgrath
    was the first Dawnwatch.** He was a knight of the sun. He tried to save
    a sun that was dying, and he lost himself. Aubade did not defeat him.
    It put him to sleep with the same crystal spell. The black stones are
    pieces of his crystal. "I also slept" is the truth.

### Book Two: The Last Dawn

Book Two starts with a summary of Book One for the GM. It also has a way to
start at level 11 with new characters: a second group of sleepers that
Aubade hid in a different place.

#### Act III: The Long Night (levels 11 to 16, chapters 1 to 6)

- The cloud of Malgrath spreads. The days become shorter. Full regions
  fall into night.
- The characters become the legends that the people prayed to, or they
  become something different. The **Legend Paths** system starts here.
- The party learns why Malgrath wants to put out the sun. The sun is
  really dying. He thinks that only a sun that goes out and burns again can
  last. He can be correct. The cost is each living thing under the sun.
- The Before and After stories return. The party finds the people and the
  places that each character lost, after 300 years of change.

#### Act IV: The Last Dawn (levels 17 to 20, chapters 7 to 9)

- The party attacks the Unlit Crown.
- **The finale is a choice, not only a fight.** The players choose one end:
  - **Defeat Malgrath.** The sun continues to die, slowly. That is a
    problem for the next age.
  - **Redeem Malgrath.** The first Dawnwatch and the last Dawnwatch light
    the sun again, together.
  - **Take his place.** One character sleeps in the crystal for the next
    300 years.
- **The end of Wick:** his flame is the last true piece of the Dawnfire.
  Wick can give it to light the sun again. The GM can make this a choice
  of the players or a sacrifice that Wick makes.

### Wick and the tone (approved by the human)

- Wick stays with the party for the full campaign.
- The campaign starts light and spooky, as the tutorial does. It becomes
  darker as the light goes. It ends with hope.

## Names and versions (decided by the human)

- The campaign is **The Dawnwatch**. It is not "Edition 3". An edition is a
  new version of the two Compendiums. The Dawnwatch books are new
  products, and they do not change the Compendiums.
- Each book is a product with its own versions:

  | Product | Version tags | Rolling release |
  |---|---|---|
  | The two Compendiums | `edition-1`, `edition-2`, ... | `compendium-latest` |
  | The Dawnwatch: Waking | `dawnwatch-waking-1`, ... | `dawnwatch-waking-latest` |
  | The Dawnwatch: The Last Dawn | `dawnwatch-last-dawn-1`, ... | `dawnwatch-last-dawn-latest` |

- Each Dawnwatch release says which Compendium edition it needs, for
  example "Requires Edition 2".

## Goals

- A campaign from level 1 to level 20 in two books (decided by the human).
- Book One has a true end at level 10.
- The tutorial is chapter 1 of Book One, with no change to its rules
  lessons.
- A gazetteer of the world of today (decided by the human).
- New content for the story: monsters, NPCs, and unique magic items
  (decided by the human).
- A short player's guide (decided by the human).
- A reference part in each book, so that the GM can find each name fast.
- Maps, made by the build.
- Print each book as an A4 hardcover at Lulu, as the two Compendiums are.

## Non-goals

- No change to the two Compendiums. Their page counts and spines stay the
  same.
- No new player options (classes, subclasses, species, feats, spells). The
  players use the Player's Compendium.
- No new rules system. The story uses the 8 optional systems of Edition 2.

## Anti-goals

- **Filler.** The Edition 2 rule applies: an entry that only renames
  another entry does not count.
- **Copied paid content and trademarks.** The Edition 2 rules apply.
- **Spoilers in the player's part.** The player's guide and the handouts
  must not tell anything about the world of today.
- **Em-dashes.** None anywhere. `AGENTS.md` has the rule.
- **A book that is too large.** Each book stays below 700 pages. The target
  is about 350 pages for each book. Published campaign books have about
  250 to 320 pages.

## Structure of each book

### For the GM

1. **How to run this book.** The arc, the level of each chapter, and how to
   start with new players or with a group that knows the game. Book Two
   adds the summary of Book One and the start with new characters.
2. **The chapters.** Each chapter is one part of the story and one region
   of the world. Each chapter adds one of the 8 optional systems when the
   story needs it (for example Journeys for a long road, Strongholds for a
   base). So the campaign teaches the full game, step by step, as the
   tutorial teaches the core rules. In Book One, chapter 1 is the tutorial.
3. **The gazetteer.** Book One has the full gazetteer of the Vesperlands.
   Book Two has the changes of the Long Night: the regions in darkness and
   the new powers.

### The world: then and now (decided by the human)

The Dawnwatch slept for 300 years. They know the old world only. The
campaign uses this gap:

- **The player's guide shows Aubade as it was 300 years ago.** The old
  kingdom, the Dawnwatch oath, the first war against Malgrath, and
  backgrounds from that time. It cannot spoil the campaign, because the
  campaign is about how much that picture is now wrong. It is in Book One.
- **Wick tells the player's guide**, in his own voice. The GM can read it
  aloud, or the players can read it.
- **The gazetteer shows the Vesperlands of today.** It is in the GM's part
  only. The players find it at the table. It has the regions, the powers,
  the history of the 300 lost years, and the maps. A GM can also run new
  stories in it.

### Handouts

An appendix of handouts in each book: maps, letters, and notes from Wick.
Each handout is on its own page with nothing on the back, so that the GM
can print or copy it.

### Reference (the "wiki" part)

- **In the chapters:** a creature that appears in only one scene has its
  statblock on that page. The GM does not turn pages in a fight.
- **Appendices, from A to Z:**
  - **Creatures:** the new monsters, with full statblocks.
  - **People:** each named NPC, with a one-line role, the chapters where
    the NPC appears, and a statblock if the NPC fights.
  - **Places:** the gazetteer entries, with the chapters that use them.
  - **Treasures:** the unique magic items.
- **Index:** each name, with its page numbers.
- **Screen edition:** each name is a link to its entry.

## Maps (decided by the agent)

- The build draws the maps as vector art, in the same way as it draws the
  covers and the icons. No outside artist is necessary, and a map changes
  with the text.
- The style is a line-drawn atlas: ink lines, hatching for mountains and
  sea, and labels in the book fonts. It prints well in black and white.
- Book One has two world maps:
  - **Aubade, 300 years ago.** A player handout, with the player's guide.
  - **The Vesperlands of today.** For the GM, with the gazetteer.
- Each act has a regional map for its chapters.
- Book Two has a map of the Long Night, with the regions in darkness.

## The tutorial after the change (decided by the human)

The tutorial lives in two places:

- It is chapter 1 of Book One.
- It stays as a free adventure in `adventures/tutorial-one-shot/`. It still
  works alone. At the end, it gets a short hook into Book One.

One source makes both. The adventure must not exist as two copies that
change apart.

## Content and build

- The new content goes into `compendium/content/dawnwatch/`. The collector
  reads each file in a content folder, so the Compendium folders must not
  hold Dawnwatch content. A separate folder keeps the Compendiums the same.
- The new content uses the same formats, the same balance guide, and the
  same checks as the Compendium content.
- The build makes each book from its own source file in
  `compendium/books/`, in the same forms as the Compendiums: screen, home
  print, Lulu interior, and Lulu hardcover cover.
- The build makes the index with the page numbers. It finds them in the
  same way as it finds the page numbers of the table of contents.

## Releases and the README (decided by the human)

- A version tag makes a release of its product. The table in "Names and
  versions" gives the tags.
- GitHub has only one "latest" release for each repository. So the
  workflow also keeps one rolling release for each product. Each release of
  a product replaces the files of its rolling release.
- The README links to the files of the rolling releases, for example
  `releases/download/dawnwatch-waking-latest/<file>.pdf`. These links always
  give the newest version, and no commit is necessary after a release.
- The README has one row for each product: its newest PDFs, its Lulu
  hardcover, and the Compendium edition that it needs. A product that comes
  later gets its own row and its own rolling release.
- The release workflow does not write commits. A commit has a human writer.

## Order of work (decided by the agent)

1. Book One is first. Book Two starts after Book One is complete.
2. The world, the gazetteer, and the maps of Book One come first. The
   chapters need them.
3. Then the chapters, in order, with their content.
4. Then the reference, the index, and the release changes.

## Left to the builder

- The page budget of each chapter, inside the target of each book.
- The names of the chapters.
- The names of the NPCs and the places inside each region.
