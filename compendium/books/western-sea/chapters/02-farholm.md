# Farholm

| | |
|---|---|
| **Levels** | 2 to 4 |
| **Time at the table** | About 4 to 6 sessions (16 to 24 hours) |
| **Places** | Hythe, then the whole island: the outer ring of the coast and the inner ring of the interior |
| **Systems that start** | Journeys, Crafting and Gathering. Factions and Renown get their full rules here. |
| **The Storm Cube** | From 2 to about 32 |
| **People** | Amos Tulloch, Absalom Pascoe, Elspeth Flett, Father Gilbert Rundle, Kit Hocking, Erlend Linklater, Nathaniel Gale, Margery Nance, Inga Halcrow, Thomas and Hester Spence |

Amos Tulloch walks the party over the Saltpans to Hythe, and four hundred
people who have never seen a stranger come down to the Bridge to look at
them. By the next night, the Moot of Hythe has met, both sides of the
colony want the party on their own bank, and the whole island is open.

This chapter is the open middle of the book. After the Moot, the party
goes where it likes, in any order. The coast is near and friendly, the
interior is far and strange, and the Storm Cube sits on the table the
whole time.

This chapter has three jobs:

1. **Show the colony.** Hythe is the heart of the book. The two sides of
   the colony are both right about one thing and both wrong about another.
   Let the players love them and argue with them.
2. **Give the party a way home.** The Corrin Light can be mended. Its
   parts are spread over the island. Collecting them is the spine of the
   chapter, and the Corrin men are glad of it, and the Tey folk are not.
3. **Let the party find the truth.** Each site holds a clue to what the
   island is. No single clue proves it. Together, they do.

## Running This Chapter

### What happens

1. **The road to Hythe.** Amos brings the party into town. The colony
   comes to look. Everybody asks which town sent them.
2. **The first night.** The Corrin men and the Tey folk each try to take
   the party home for supper.
3. **The Moot.** The Moot of Hythe meets to decide what to do with the
   strangers. Teach Factions and Renown here. The Moot ends with the
   island open.
4. **The open island.** The party visits the sites in any order. Each site
   has a main scene. A site is finished when its main scene ends.
   - **The outer ring** (levels 2 to 3): Hythe, the Corrin Light, Founders'
     Hill, Gull Rock, the Boneyard, and the Saltpans.
   - **The inner ring** (levels 3 to 4): the Kettles, the Ribs, the wreck
     of the *Morning Star*, and the Saint's Hearth. The party can also find
     and scout the Grandam's Well, but chapter 3 owns what happens down it.
5. **The end of the island.** When the cube reaches 64, the storm season
   starts and so does chapter 3, wherever the party is.

### The open island

The party does not have to see every site. A group that plays four or five
sessions sees about eight. Let the players choose, and help them choose:

- **The colonists point the way.** Each person in Hythe knows a few sites
  and has a reason to send the party there. "Where the Island Points" in
  the Hythe section lists them.
- **The parts and the clues are spread out.** Each part of the Corrin Light
  has at least two places where the party can get it. Each clue to the
  Grandam is in a different site. So any order of sites works, and no
  single missed site breaks the book.
- **The interior is harder.** The sites of the inner ring are written for
  level 3. A party that goes inland at level 2 finds them hard. The
  colonists warn it. If it goes anyway, let it.

### The level points

| Level | When |
|---|---|
| 3 | After the party finishes 2 sites of the outer ring. Hythe counts as one, once. |
| 4 | After the party finishes the first 2 sites of the inner ring. |
| 5 | When chapter 3 starts. |

The level up happens at the next rest, Short or Long. Each site names its
main scene.

### The cube on the island

Each site has a box called **The cube** that lists what gives a storm
sign there and what turns the cube. Most outer sites give storm signs, and
turn the cube only for a big bad choice. Three sites have a turn that
happens to most parties: the Corrin Light, the Kettles, and the wreck of
the *Morning Star*. Long Rests in the open add storm signs, and the inner
ring is a long way from Hythe.

| When | The cube is about | Where it usually comes from |
|---|---|---|
| The Moot | 2 | Chapter 1 does not turn it. |
| After 2 sites of the outer ring | 4 | The squall at the Corrin Light, or three storm signs |
| The end of the outer ring | 8 | Storm signs from the coast, a bad choice, or a double |
| After 2 sites of the inner ring | 16 | The boil at the Kettles, nights in the open |
| The end of the inner ring | 32 | The wreck of the *Morning Star*, nights in the open, doubles |
| Storm season | 64 | Chapter 3 |

If the cube runs fast or slow, use "The Pace" in How to Run This Book. Do
not hold the cube back to keep a site for later. A storm that comes early
is part of the story.

### Inga's dreams

Inga Halcrow, a girl of fourteen on the Tey bank, dreams the Grandam's
dreams. She does not know that. She only knows that the dreams change.
Each time the party talks to her, tell them her last dream, from the face
of the cube. This is the best way to show the players that the cube is
more than weather.

| The cube | Inga's dream |
|---|---|
| 2 | "Cold. Dark. So heavy. Like being at the bottom of the sea, but I'm not afraid. I'm too sleepy to be afraid." |
| 4 | "It's warmer. Something itches, far off, on my back. I can't reach it." |
| 8 | "There's a little light, very high up, like a star through deep water. I want to look at it." |
| 16 | "I'm rising. Slowly. The weight is coming off. Everything is moving, and I'm so big, and I'm so slow." |
| 32 | "The light is close now. I can nearly see it. When I see it, I'll wake up. I want to wake up. I don't want to wake up." |

> **Secret (chapter 3):** Inga's dreams follow the cube because the cube
> is the Grandam stirring. At 64, Inga does not dream at all. She wakes
> in the night, cold and calm, and says: "She's awake."

### The two sides

Both sides want the party, and both sides watch it. Hythe is small. What
the party does on one bank is known on the other by supper. The full
rules of the factions are in "The Moot", where you teach them.

The party also needs the colony. The Corrin men have the lamp, the
shipwright, and the notes of Captain Gale. The Tey folk have the guides,
the seal hunters, the egg climbers, and the Grandam's Well. A party that
makes enemies of one side finds half the island shut.

### No fire on the ground

Every colonist keeps one custom above all others: no fire burns on the
bare ground of Farholm. A cook fire goes on a flat stone carried for the
purpose. Tell the players this the first time they make camp, through Amos
or a guide. A character who lights a fire on the bare turf, where a
colonist can see it, loses renown with the Tey folk (see "The Moot").

The custom is wiser than the colonists know. A fire on the bare ground of
the interior makes the ground shiver under it. On the coast, nothing
happens. In the inner ring, a fire on the bare ground gives a storm sign.
At the Saint's Hearth, it does much more.

## Journeys on Farholm

> **Teach: Journeys.** "The island is about twenty miles across, so you
> can walk anywhere in a day. But there are no roads, the weather turns
> fast, and the interior is bog, stone, and fog. Before each trip, each of
> you takes a role: guide, scout, quartermaster, or lookout." Explain the
> four roles from the Journeys rules in the Game Master's Compendium, and
> the supply die. On Farholm, a journey counts hours, not days.

### How a journey works here

The Journeys rules count days. Farholm is small, so this chapter uses
**legs**. A leg is one trip from one place to another, from one hour to a
full day of walking.

- **Roles.** Each character takes a role for the leg and makes its check
  once. The DCs are in the table below. A role that nobody takes fails.
- **Weather.** Roll the weather once each day, in the morning, on the
  table of Farholm below.
- **Encounter.** Roll a d6 for each leg. On a 1, roll 2d6 on the
  encounters of the island. On a leg of 4 hours or more, or at night, an
  encounter happens on a 1 or a 2.
- **Supplies.** Roll the supply die at the end of each day that the party
  spends away from Hythe. A day in Hythe needs no roll: the colony feeds
  its guests.
- **Getting lost.** If the guide fails, the leg takes 2 hours more. If the
  guide fails by 5 or more, the party loses the day and arrives the next
  morning. A lost day gives a storm sign.
- **The pace.** At a Fast pace, a leg takes half the hours (round up), and
  the guide and the scout have Disadvantage. At a Slow pace, it takes half
  as long again, and they have Advantage.

A track needs no guide check. Most colonists know the tracks from
childhood.

## Mending the Corrin Light

The party wants to go home. The Corrin men say that the Corrin Light can
open a way through the Wall, because it did once. The Tey folk say that
it will kill everyone. Both sides are partly right. Chapter 3 decides what
happens when the Light burns. This chapter is about getting it ready.

The Light needs four parts, a roof, and the help of the people who know
how. The Corrin men can tell the party what is missing. Nathaniel Gale
can tell it how to make each part.

> **Teach: Crafting and Gathering.** "The Light needs a lamp, a mirror, a
> lens, and oil. You can find some of them. You can make the rest, if you
> gather the right materials and do the work." Explain gathering (one hour
> of searching, and a check against the DC of the material) and crafting
> (a tool, a quiet place, workdays, and a check) from the Crafting and
> Gathering rules in the Game Master's Compendium. On Farholm, the
> recipes are the parts of the Light, below.

### The parts of the Light

| Part | What it is | Where to get it |
|---|---|---|
| **The lamp** | The brass frame and the burner of the great lamp of the *Morning Star*, black with age | The wall of the Lamp Room in Hythe (the Corrin men give it at renown 10, or for a great deed) |
| **The mirror** | A round dish of polished metal, 3 feet across, that throws the light forward | The spare mirror in the hold of the *Morning Star*, or beaten from copper of the Boneyard |
| **The lens** | A great lens of glass, 2 feet across, that makes the light into a beam | Cast new at the Kettles, from 20 pounds of glass and the pattern in Captain Gale's notes |
| **The oil** | Four barrels, enough for a long night of storm | One barrel from the Corrin men's store, and seal blubber from Gull Rock, rendered in the great pan of the Saltpans |
| **The roof** | The lamp room at the top of the tower has no roof, and the rain puts out a lamp | Two good spars from the Boneyard, and two workdays at the Light |

The full rules of each part are in the site where the party finds it. In
short:

- **The lamp.** One workday to clean it, with Tinker's Tools, DC 10.
- **The mirror.** The spare in the *Morning Star* needs no work. A new one
  needs 4 sheets of copper sheathing from the Boneyard, salt and sand to
  polish it, and 3 workdays with Smith's Tools or Tinker's Tools, DC 13.
- **The lens.** It needs 20 pounds of glass, the pattern, and the heat of
  the Oven at the Kettles, the only fire on Farholm hot enough to melt
  glass. Casting takes 1 workday with Glassblower's Tools or Smith's Tools,
  DC 13, then 2 workdays of grinding with sand, DC 10.
- **The oil.** One barrel of oil for each unit of seal blubber, and 1
  workday in the pan of the Saltpans for each barrel. No check.
- **The roof.** Two spars and 2 workdays at the Light with Carpenter's
  Tools, DC 10. The Corrin men help.

A character with no proficiency in the tool can still help. Margery Nance
the shipwright counts as a crafter with each of these tools, once the
party has 10 renown with the Corrin men. Nathaniel Gale knows the work but
cannot do it with his hands.

> **GM tip:** Do not make the repair a list of chores. Each part is a
> reason to go somewhere strange and meet someone who cares. Skip the
> workdays in a sentence, and spend the time at the table on the people.

### Where the glass comes from

The lens needs 20 pounds of glass. Glass is rare on Farholm. The party can
find it here:

- **The Boneyard:** old bottles and sea glass in the shingle. Each hour of
  searching gives 5 pounds on a DC 10 Wisdom (Perception) check.
- **The *Good Intent*:** bottles, a lantern, and window glass of the
  master's cabin, if chapter 1 left the party any of the stores.
- **The broken lens:** the Tey folk keep the pieces of the old lens over
  their hearths. They give them only to a party with 10 renown that swears
  on the middle arch never to light the Corrin Light. A party that swears
  and then lights it breaks a Moot oath.
- **An encounter:** the crabs and the wreck wood of the coast give 5 pounds
  each.
- **The Moot House:** its carved stern windows have the last big panes of
  glass in Hythe, enough for the whole lens. To take them is a big bad
  choice. The party loses 5 renown with the Tey folk and 2 with the Corrin
  men. Turn the cube.

### The materials of the island

These are the materials of Farholm for the Crafting and Gathering rules.
Each one is Common, so each check is DC 10.

| Material | Where | Check | What it is for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seal blubber | Harvest a **Tusked Seal**, or buy it from the Tey sealers | Wisdom (Survival) | Lamp oil: 1 unit makes one barrel. A Tusked Seal is big, so it gives a full unit. |
| Copper sheathing | The Boneyard, from the hulls of the old wrecks | Strength (Athletics) | The mirror: 4 sheets |
| Sea glass and old bottles | The Boneyard, the shore | Wisdom (Perception) | The lens: 5 pounds each hour |
| Ship spars | The Boneyard | Strength (Athletics) | The roof of the Light: 2 spars |
| Louse shell | Harvest a **Rib Louse** | Wisdom (Survival) | A Shield, with Leatherworker's Tools, 2 workdays |
| Bonxie feathers | Harvest a **Bonxie** | Wisdom (Survival) | Fletching for 20 arrows, 1 workday |

### If the party lights the Light before the storm season

The players may want to test the Light, or light it on a quiet night to
see what happens. Let them, if they have the parts. This is what happens:

- **The Light burns, and nothing else happens.** The beam goes out over
  the sea toward the Wall, and the Wall does not move. On a quiet night,
  the Grandam sleeps too deep to see it.
- **Inga Halcrow screams in her sleep** on the Tey bank. Give a storm sign.
- **The Tey folk come.** Erlend Linklater and the Well Watch march on the
  Light the same night (see "The Corrin Light"). The party loses 5 renown
  with the Tey folk. This is a big bad choice. Turn the cube.
- **The Corrin men lose heart.** Absalom Pascoe stands at the top of the
  tower all night and watches the Wall not open. In the morning, Nathaniel
  Gale shows him the line in the Landing Book (see "Hythe"). The Corrin
  men now believe that the Light works only in the storm season.

Four clues are enough for a sharp group to guess the truth. Six are enough
for any group to be sure. When the players say it aloud, "the island is
alive, and it is breathing", do not confirm it. Say: "What would that
mean?" Then let them argue.

## Arriving in Hythe

### The road from the Saltpans (15 minutes)

**Goal:** The party sees Hythe, and Hythe sees the party.

Chapter 1 ends at the Saltpans, where Amos Tulloch offers to take the
party to town. The track runs along the shore for an hour. Amos talks the
whole way, and most of it is useful. Use him to give the players the names
they need: the Corrin men and the Tey folk, the Captain of the Light, the
Well Keeper, the Moot, and the Bridge.

> The track comes over a low rise, and there it is, all at once: a jumble
> of grey roofs on two slopes, with a quick brown stream between them and
> one stone bridge across it. Smoke from a hundred chimneys leans in the
> wind. The roofs are green with grass and white with daisies. Some of the
> houses are boats, turned upside down on low stone walls, with doors cut
> in their sterns. On the green hill behind the town, a long low church
> rings one cracked bell, and then a second bell that does not agree with
> the first.
>
> Somebody on the shingle sees you and shouts. Then everybody is shouting.

Amos stops at the top of the rise and fills his pipe. "Light to you,
Hythe," he says to nobody. "Here's trouble."

### The Bridge (30 minutes)

**Goal:** Show the two sides. Ask the dangerous question.

By the time the party reaches the Bridge, half the town is there. The
Corrin men crowd the east end, with blue ribbons and boat hooks. The Tey
folk crowd the west end, with green ribbons and children on their
shoulders. Nobody stands on the middle arch.

> They are all looking at you. Old women in white caps, men in long coats
> of patched sailcloth, children with bare feet and huge eyes. Somebody
> touches your sleeve to see if it is real. Somebody else asks if it is
> true that the king has a palace of glass. A boy holds out a fish, as a
> gift, or as a test.
>
> Then a big grey man in a blue coat pushes to the front of the east end,
> and the crowd goes quiet. "Light to you, strangers," he says, slowly,
> like a man reading from an old book. "I am Absalom Pascoe, the Captain
> of the Light. Tell us one thing first. Which town sent you? Corrin or
> Tey?"

The whole Bridge holds its breath. This is the most important question
anybody in Hythe has asked in a hundred years. The party's answer sets its
first renown with each side. See "The Moot" for the rules of renown. Write
the answers down now and explain them at the Moot.

| The party says | What happens | Renown |
|---|---|---|
| "Corrin." | The east end cheers. The west end goes silent and starts to drift away. | Corrin men +1, Tey folk −1 |
| "Tey." | The west end cheers. Absalom's face closes like a door. | Tey folk +1, Corrin men −1 |
| "Both. They are one city now. Corrantey." | Nobody believes it. Then somebody laughs, and then everybody argues. Father Rundle, at the back, crosses himself and smiles. | None. The party gains Father Rundle's respect. |
| A lie, a joke, or no answer | Both sides decide that the party is hiding something, and each side decides that the secret is good for it. | None |

> **GM tip:** The truth is the best answer and the hardest. If a player
> says "Corrantey", let one old Corrin woman say, very quietly, "Corrin
> and Tey together? Never in this world." Then let the Tey Well Keeper's
> people look at each other. They will remember it.

### The tug of war

Before anybody can stop it, the two sides start to argue about where the
strangers sleep tonight. Absalom offers the Lamp Room. A thin dark man with
a scar where his ear was, Erlend Linklater, says from the west end that the
Well Keeper has asked for the strangers at the Net Loft. Kit Hocking, a
loud red-haired youth at Absalom's elbow, says that the Well Keeper can
come and get them. Somebody pushes somebody.

Then a round red-faced priest in faded red and gold steps out onto the
middle arch and raises both hands. This is Father Gilbert Rundle.

> "Peace on the Bridge," says the priest. "Peace on the Bridge, or the
> Moot will hear of it. These people are the guests of the church tonight.
> They will eat with whom they like, and they will sleep under my roof,
> and tomorrow night the Moot will meet, and the Moot will say what is to
> be done with them. Light to you all. Go home."
>
> "And dawn to you," mutters the crowd, and it does not go home.

The party sleeps in the church on Founders' Hill tonight. Before then, it
has a long evening and two invitations.

### The first night (45 minutes)

**Goal:** Let the party meet both sides at home, one at a time.

The party can eat with the Corrin men in the Lamp Room, with the Tey folk
at the Net Loft, or split up and do both. It can also go with Amos to an
ale house on the middle of the Bridge, where people of both banks drink
together and pretend not to see each other. Let the players choose.

**The Lamp Room.** A long low house on the Corrin bank, with the figurehead
of the *Desire* over the door and a lamp of seal oil in the east window.
Absalom Pascoe pours heather ale and asks for news of Corrin: its harbor,
its fish market, its saints, and whether the town still talks of the
fleet. Kit Hocking wants to know if the party will help light the Light.
Nathaniel Gale, the thin short-sighted schoolmaster, sits at the end of
the table and asks careful questions about ships. On the wall hangs the
brass frame of the great lamp of the Corrin Light, black with age. Margery
Nance, the shipwright, asks how ships are built today, and writes down
everything.

**The Net Loft.** A big turf house on the Tey bank, warm and full of nets.
Elspeth Flett, the Well Keeper, sits by the hearth, small and white-haired
and very still. She asks only two things. "Did you feel the ground move,
when you came ashore?" and "Will you go away again?" Erlend Linklater
stands by the door. A thin, freckled girl of fourteen, Inga Halcrow, stares
at the party all evening and says nothing until it leaves. Then she
follows it out and asks one character, very quietly, if people dream of
the sea where they come from.

**The ale house on the Bridge.** Amos introduces the party to Hester
Spence, Absalom's daughter, who lives on the Tey bank with her husband,
the fisherman Thomas Spence. Hester is the best person in Hythe to explain
both sides fairly, and she is tired of both. Thomas says little, but if the
party needs a guide, he says: "I'll take you anywhere. Not the Light."

> **GM tip:** This scene shows the players both sides before they have to
> choose. Make both sides warm. The Corrin men are loud, hopeful, and
> homesick. The Tey folk are quiet, kind, and afraid. Nobody here is a
> villain.

### What people ask the party

Each colonist wants something from the party. Use these questions all
evening, and all through the chapter:

- "What is the king's name now?" (Not Edwy. There is no king.)
- "Do they still remember the fleet over the Wall?" (As a song.)
- "Is the harbor of Corrin still silted? Has Tey mended its wall?" (They
  silted and broke, and the towns became one city.)
- "Did the *Hopewell* come home?" (Nobody in Corrantey has heard of it.)
- "Can you take us home?"

Each true answer hurts somebody. Each kind lie helps somebody tonight and
hurts them later.

## The Moot

**Goal:** The colony decides what to do with the party. Teach Factions and
Renown. Open the island.

**This is the main scene of Hythe.** When it ends, Hythe counts as a
finished site of the outer ring.

The Moot meets the next evening, in the Moot House on the Tey bank: the
great cabin of the *Patience*, carried ashore whole three hundred and
sixty years ago, with carved stern windows that still have their glass.
Six headmen of each bank sit on two benches facing each other. Father
Rundle sits between them, at the end, with a bell. Absalom Pascoe speaks
for the Corrin men. Elspeth Flett speaks for the Tey folk. Everybody else
in Hythe crowds outside the windows.

> The great cabin smells of tar, peat smoke, and three hundred and sixty
> years. Twelve old men and women sit on two benches, and nobody sits on
> the wrong one. The priest rings his bell once.
>
> "The Moot of Hythe is met," he says. "Light to us all. We have strangers
> from over the Wall, the first since the Landing. The Moot will hear them,
> and then the Moot will decide."

### What the Moot argues about

The Moot argues about three things, in this order. Let the party speak to
each one. A good speech can change the vote: a DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion)
check, or a DC 15 check if the party asks for something that one side
hates. The truth helps: a speech that tells something true and hard about
home gives Advantage.

1. **Where the strangers live.** The Corrin men want them in the Lamp Room.
   The Tey folk want them on the Tey bank, "where we can see them". Father
   Rundle offers the church. The Moot usually agrees on the church, which
   pleases nobody and is fair. The party can live wherever it wants, in
   truth. The question is who feeds it, and who it owes.
2. **The old law.** Elspeth Flett asks the Moot to say again its oldest
   law: that nobody lights the Corrin Light. Absalom Pascoe asks the Moot
   to end the law, because the strangers prove that ships can cross the
   Wall. They shout. In the end, the Moot keeps the law, by one vote.
   Unless the party speaks for ending it and wins a DC 15 check, in which
   case the Moot cannot agree, and the law stands only because the Moot
   cannot change anything without a vote.
3. **The question of home.** Absalom asks the party straight out: will it
   help mend the Light, and take the colony home? Elspeth asks the party
   straight out: will it promise to leave the Light dark? The party does
   not have to answer. If it does, write down the answer. Both sides will
   remember it.

### How the Moot ends

The Moot gives the party **guest right** until the Turning. Guest right
means that the colony feeds the party and gives it a roof, that nobody may
harm it on pain of the Moot, and that the party must keep the peace on the
Bridge. Father Rundle rings the bell. Then each side asks the party,
privately, outside, for help.

- **Absalom Pascoe** asks the party to help mend the Light. He tells it
  what is missing: the mirror, the lens, the oil, and the roof. He does not
  yet offer the lamp. "Show me you're with us, and the lamp is yours."
- **Elspeth Flett** asks the party to see the island before it decides
  anything. "Go to the Kettles. Put your hand on the ground. Then tell me
  the Light is safe."

Both sides have now said what they want. The island is open.

> **Teach: Factions and Renown.** "The Corrin men and the Tey folk both
> want your help, and both are watching you. Each side has a renown score
> for your party. It goes up when you help that side and down when you
> work against it. At some scores, the side gives you more: a bed, a guide,
> a part of the Light, its trust." Use the Party Renown option of the Game
> Master's Compendium: one score for the whole party with each side. Tell
> the players their renown after each change, and why. It is never a
> secret.

## Hythe

*Outer ring. The hub of the island. The main scene is the Moot. The party
comes back here between sites.*

Hythe is where the party sleeps, eats, gathers news, and argues with its
friends. Do not run it as a dungeon. Run it as a home: each time the party
comes back, something has changed, and somebody wants to talk.

### What the party can do in Hythe

- **Rest.** A Long Rest in Hythe gives no storm sign. The colony feeds the
  party, so no supply roll is needed. The supply die goes back up to d10
  for free when the party leaves, as the colony fills its packs.
- **The Lamp Room.** Absalom, Kit, and the brass lamp. With 10 renown, the
  lamp. With 3 renown, Nathaniel brings the Landing Book.
- **The Net Loft.** Elspeth, Erlend, and Inga. The talk of the Tey bank.
- **Margery's shed.** The last shed on the slip is locked. With 20 Corrin
  renown, Margery opens it. Inside is a boat of thirty feet, almost done,
  built from the best timbers of three wrecks, with a deep keel and a
  short mast. "She'll go through the Wall," says Margery, "if anything
  will." Chapter 3 tells what the boat can do.
- **The school.** Nathaniel Gale teaches the children of both banks over
  the sail loft. Most Tey children stopped coming two years ago.
- **The Bridge market.** On the first day of each week. The party can
  trade. The colonists pay almost anything for iron, glass, cloth, paper,
  or a coin of Corrantey. See "Money" in the gazetteer.

### The Landing Book

Nathaniel Gale keeps the Landing Book: three thick volumes of records,
from the year of the Landing to today. The first volume has Captain
Humphrey Gale's own notes on the building of the Corrin Light. It holds
two things that matter.

- **The pattern of the lens.** Captain Gale drew the great lens of the
  *Morning Star* before he put it in the tower: its width, its curve, and
  its thickness, with notes on how it was made in Corrin. With this
  pattern, the party can cast a new lens at the Kettles (see "The
  Kettles"). Nathaniel shows it to a party with 3 Corrin renown, or to a
  party that wins a DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion) check and promises to tell
  him what it learns.
- **The line.** In the year of the Landing, Captain Gale wrote: "The Wall
  drew in when the ground breathed hard, and was quiet where the lanterns
  were." Nathaniel has read it a hundred times. He has told nobody what he
  thinks it means.

Nathaniel shows the line only to a party that he trusts: 10 Corrin renown,
or a party that tells him something true about the island that he does not
know, such as one of the clues of the chapter. Then he takes off his
spectacles and says:

> "I think the Light works only when the ground moves. Only in the Turning.
> John Pascoe lit it in the worst night of the Turning, and the Wall
> opened. My great-great-grandfather lit it every night for nineteen years,
> and it never opened once. Do you see? The only way home is in the storm.
> I have not told Absalom. I don't know how."

This is the first half of the truth about the Light. It is a clue, not a
part.

### Inga Halcrow

Inga spends her days near the Net Loft and her evenings walking alone on
the Low Ground, where the Drowning took the old houses. The Tey folk bring
her eggs and ribbons because they think she is holy. She hates it. If a
character talks to her like a normal girl, she talks back, and she tells
them her last dream (see "Inga's dreams" above).

She would very much like someone to tell her that she is not mad. A
character who believes her, and says so, has a friend for the rest of the
book.

### A fight on the Bridge

On the party's first or second visit back to Hythe after a site, Kit
Hocking and his friends pick a fight with the Well Watch on the Bridge. Kit
has heard that the party is helping the Light, or not helping it, and he
wants to show the Tey folk who the strangers belong to. Erlend Linklater
and three Well Watchmen come to the west end. Nobody has drawn a blade yet.

The party can stop it, join it, or watch it.

- **Stop it.** A DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion or Intimidation) check, or a
  good idea, such as standing on the middle arch, or calling the priest.
  Success: +1 renown with both sides.
- **Join it.** It is a brawl, not a battle. Use **Kit Hocking** and four
  **Bandits** (young Corrin men with boat hooks) against **Erlend
  Linklater** and three **Well Watchmen**. Everybody deals nonlethal
  damage. The side that the party joins wins. +1 renown with that side, −2
  with the other. A fight on the Bridge is a crime against the Moot: give
  a storm sign.
- **Watch it.** Somebody goes into the Fleet. Nobody dies. Father Rundle
  arrives and shouts at everybody. Both sides blame the party.

## The Corrin Light

*Outer ring. Levels 2 to 3. Two hours from Hythe by the track to the east
point. The main scene is "The Night Watch".*

The Corrin Light is the hope of half the colony and the fear of the other
half. It is also the first place where the party sees the Wall up close,
and the first place where the cube is likely to turn.

> The track climbs over heather to the east, and the wind gets stronger
> with each step. Then the land ends in a bare finger of grey rock that
> points out to sea, and at its tip stands a round stone tower, forty feet
> tall, with no roof. Gulls wheel round the top of it, screaming.
>
> Beyond the tower there is nothing but grey water, and far out, right on
> the edge of the world, a dark line that does not move like cloud. It
> stands on the sea from one side of the sky to the other.
>
> An old man in a blue coat stands at the tower door with a broom. "Light
> to you," he says. "That way is home."

### The tower

- **The door.** New oak, locked, with the key on a string round the neck
  of the watchman. Digory Rowe opens it for a party with 3 Corrin renown,
  or for anybody whom Absalom sends.
- **The stair.** It winds up inside the wall, worn into hollows by three
  hundred and fifty years of feet. The Corrin men sweep it every morning.
  A narrow window every ten feet looks east.
- **The lamp room.** A round stone room at the top, 15 feet across, open to
  the sky. The iron cradle where the lamp sat is still there, rusty and
  strong. The gulls and the bonxies have taken it over. It is white with
  their mess.
- **The watch hut.** A turf hut by the door, with two bunks, a hearth on a
  ballast stone, and the log of the watch. Two Corrin men keep the watch
  at all times. The hut counts as a shelter: a Long Rest here gives no
  storm sign.
- **The heather above.** A slope of heather runs up behind the point. The
  Well Watch keeps an eye on the tower from there. A DC 13 Wisdom
  (Perception) check spots one or two Tey watchers lying flat in the
  heather. They do not hide well, because they do not try very hard.

### Digory Rowe and the log of the watch

Digory Rowe is about seventy, bent, and spotless. He has kept the stair
clean for forty years. He talks to the party as if it came from Corrin
yesterday, and he asks whether the fishwives on the Corrin quay still sing
the song of the fleet. (They do. A character who sings it to him makes him
cry, and gains 1 renown with the Corrin men.)

The log of the watch is a stack of old books in the hut, one for each
year, written in turn by every Corrin man who ever stood the watch. Most
pages say "Wind west. Nothing." Digory lets the party read them. A
character who spends an hour with the oldest books and makes a DC 12
Intelligence (Investigation) check finds the page of the *Hopewell* night,
in the year 211, in a shaking hand:

> *Lit at the turn of the night with all the oil of Hythe. At the second
> hour the Wall opened to the east, a lane of quiet water, and stars over
> it. The* Hopewell *went into it under all sail. The ground rose under
> the tower as the lane opened, a long slow lifting, and I held to the
> wall. I write this so that it is written. God bring them home.*

The next line is in a different hand: *The Tey came. The lamp is out.*

This is a clue. The ground rose as the lane opened. Nobody in Hythe
remembers this line. Digory did not know it was there.

### Clearing the lamp room

Before anybody can mend the roof, the birds have to go. A colony of
**Bonxies** nests in the lamp room, and a **Swarm of Thieving Gulls** lives
round the top of the tower. They attack anybody who comes up the stair.

{{statblock: Bonxie}}

The bonxies attack in pairs, diving from above. The stair is 5 feet wide,
so only one character at a time can come out into the lamp room. Smoke
drives the birds off: a smoky fire in a pot at the top of the stair makes
them flee after 2 rounds. A fire on the stone of the tower is not on the
bare ground. The Corrin men laugh at the question.

The nest holds a few things that the birds stole over the years: a brass
button of the *Morning Star*, a copper bit, and a curved piece of old glass
as big as a palm. The glass is a piece of the lens that Sander Linklater
broke. It is 1 pound of glass, and it shows that the lens was very good.

### Mending the roof

The lamp room needs a roof before a lamp can burn in a storm. The party
needs two good spars (from the Boneyard), the turf and the lead that the
Corrin men have saved, and 2 workdays with Carpenter's Tools, DC 10. Digory
and the watch help. Each workday the party spends here gives the Tey folk
a reason to talk: −1 Tey renown when the roof is done.

### The squall

The first time the party spends a day at the Corrin Light, the weather
turns. Run this whenever it fits best: as the party clears the birds,
reads the log, or works on the roof.

> The wind stops. All at once, as if a door closed.
>
> Then it comes back from the other side, from the sea, cold and wet and
> hard. Out on the edge of the world, the dark line of the Wall has grown.
> You can see the tops of its clouds now, white and boiling, and the sea
> under them is the color of slate. The gulls leave the tower all at once,
> all of them, and fly inland, screaming.
>
> Digory Rowe takes his pipe out of his mouth. "Early," he says. "That's
> early."

**Turn the cube.** The squall hits the point for an hour, then passes.
Digory has never seen the Wall come in this early. A character who watches
the Wall the whole time and makes a DC 12 Wisdom (Perception) check sees
that it did not come in like weather. It swelled, all along its length at
once, and then it fell back.

This turn happens once, at the Corrin Light, to most parties. If the party
never comes here, it does not happen.

### The Night Watch

**This is the main scene of the Corrin Light.** It happens on a night that
the party spends at the Light: to stand the watch, to guard the roof, or
because the party brought a part of the Light here.

On that night, the Well Watch comes. Erlend Linklater has heard that the
strangers are helping to mend the Light, and he means to show them, and
the Corrin men, that the Tey folk will not allow it. He does not want to
kill anybody. He wants to break the cradle of the lamp with a sledge, so
that nobody can put a lamp in it.

> It is past midnight. The fire in the watch hut is low, on its stone.
> Outside, the wind has dropped, and the sea sounds very close.
>
> Something moves in the heather above the point. Then something else. No
> lanterns. No torches. Only shapes, darker than the dark, coming down the
> slope in a line.
>
> A voice calls out of the dark, calm and low. "Corrin men. Strangers. We
> have no quarrel with you. Stand away from the door."

### Running the Night Watch

The night can end in a fight, a deal, or a trick. Let the players find the
way.

- **The dark.** The Well Watch carries no fire after dark, and it has
  Advantage on Perception and Stealth in Dim Light. The moon gives Dim Light
  on the point. Erlend puts out each torch that he can reach. A party that
  lights the whole point (a fire on each side of the tower, or a spell)
  takes his advantage away.
- **The Corrin watch.** Digory Rowe and one young Corrin man, Sam Hocking,
  are in the hut. Digory is old and stays in the door. Sam is Kit's
  brother, seventeen, and he will charge with a boat hook (use the
  **Bandit** statblock) and get himself killed if nobody stops him.
- **The goal.** Erlend wants the cradle. A watchman who reaches the lamp
  room spends 2 rounds breaking it. If the cradle breaks, the Corrin men
  must forge a new one: 3 workdays at Margery's forge in Hythe, and no part
  of the Light is lost.
- **Talk.** Erlend will talk. He does not hate the Corrin men; he thinks
  they will drown his children. A character who meets him on open ground
  and speaks to him honestly can get him to stop: a DC 13 Charisma
  (Persuasion) check. Advantage if the party has sung the dawn hymn with
  the Tey folk, or tells him that the Light works only in the storm season.
  On a success, he takes his people home. "Then we will meet again in the
  Turning," he says. On a failure, he says: "Then I am sorry."
- **Lethal or not.** Erlend's people fight to drive the party off, and they
  stop when it falls. They do not finish anybody. A character who drops to
  0 Hit Points is left alone. The party can do the same. Ask the players.
- **Retreat.** The Well Watch retreats when Erlend falls, or when two
  watchmen fall. It carries its wounded and leaves no one behind.

## Founders' Hill

*Outer ring. Levels 2 to 3. A short walk above Hythe. The main scene is
"Under the Altar".*

Founders' Hill is the quiet site of the outer ring: the church, the graves
of the first colonists, the bones of the saint, and the best view of the
whole problem. It has no fight unless the party starts one. It has the
words of Saint Mawes himself.

> The path winds up a round green hill, past small brown sheep that do not
> bother to move. At the top, a long stone church stands in its graveyard,
> with a bell tower made from the mast of a ship.
>
> From here, you see everything. Hythe below, on its two banks. The
> Saltpans, white along the shore. The east point and the tower of the
> Corrin Light. The green and grey middle of the island, and steam rising
> far inland. And all the way round, on the edge of the sea, a dark line
> that never quite goes away.

### The graveyard

The graves of the first colonists lie round the church, marked with
timbers of the ships, carved with names. Each name says its ship. Wenna
Kitto, the sexton, a tall woman with a spade and a ring of keys, is happy
to show the party round. She digs the graves of both banks and has opinions
about all of them.

- **The oldest graves.** "Barbara Rendall of the *Dove*, came ashore 0,
  died 2." "William Nance of the *Swallow*, came ashore 0, died 0." "Agnes
  Flett, first Keeper of the Well." Many are from the first winter.
- **Captain Humphrey Gale** lies in the middle of the graveyard under a slab
  cut from the deck of the *Morning Star*. Cut into the slab: "He brought us
  through." Under it, smaller, in a different hand: "He wrecked us." Wenna
  has scrubbed the second line off four times. It comes back.
- **The forty of the Drowning** lie in one long row on the Tey side, with
  one stone: "Taken by the sea, the Turning of 211."
- **The *Hopewell* stone.** At the Corrin edge, a stone with no grave
  under it: "The *Hopewell*, John Pascoe, master, and twenty-two souls.
  Gone home, 211." The Corrin men lay fresh heather on it on Landing Day.

> **GM tip:** If the party has found the name board of the *Hopewell* in
> the Boneyard, this is where it belongs. Laying the board on the empty
> grave lets the ghost of John Pascoe rest. See "The Boneyard".

### The church and Father Rundle

Inside, the church is long and dim, with the Corrin men's benches on the
left of the aisle and the Tey folk's on the right. The two bells came from
the chapels of Corrin and Tey. The dawn fire burns every morning in an iron
dish on a ballast stone. Never on the floor. Over the altar hangs the faded
red banner of the Morning Flame.

Father Gilbert Rundle lives in a small house behind the church. He is fond
of ale, arguments, and the party, if it was honest on the Bridge. He tells
the legend of Saint Mawes as he tells it every midwinter: read it from
"The legend of Saint Mawes" in the gazetteer. Then he tells the three
tellings: what the church hears in it, what the Tey folk hear, and what
the Corrin men hear.

He does not tell the rest. Not at first.

### What Father Rundle knows

Father Rundle has read the saint's own psalter, which lies in the saint's
tomb under the altar. He read it forty years ago, when he became priest.
He read the lines at the back, and he put the book back, and he has never
preached them, because they would tear the colony apart. The Tey folk
would say that the saint proves the Grandam. The Corrin men would say that
the saint was mad.

He shows the psalter to the party only if he trusts it. He trusts a party
that:

- answered "Corrantey", or another honest answer, on the Bridge;
- promises him, sincerely, to try to keep both sides alive (a DC 12
  Wisdom (Insight) check from him; a player who means it does not need to
  roll); or
- tells him one clue to the Grandam that it found at another site.

Otherwise he says that the saint's tomb is holy and closed. The party can
ask Wenna Kitto for the keys (a DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion) check, or a
gift of iron), or break in by night.

### Under the Altar

**This is the main scene of Founders' Hill.**

A trap door behind the altar opens on a short stair down to a small stone
room under the church. It is cold, dry, and very quiet. The saint lies in a
stone box in the middle, with his name cut in the lid in old letters:
MAWES OF THE PATIENCE.

> Father Rundle lifts the lid with both hands, slowly, as if he is afraid
> to wake someone.
>
> Inside, a small brown skeleton lies in the rags of a red and gold robe.
> Its hands are crossed on its chest, and the bones of the hands are
> twisted and black. Under them lies a small book, bound in sealskin.
>
> "He burned his hands," says the priest, "to put out the fire. Here. Look
> at the back."

The book is **Saint Mawes's Psalter**. In the back, after the dawn hymn,
the saint wrote a few lines in a small, careful hand. Father Rundle reads
them aloud:

> *It is not land. It sleeps. I made no light upon it after, and I went
> down to it in the dark, and I was still, and it was still.*

{{treasure: Saint Mawes's Psalter}}

This is the strongest clue in the outer ring. The saint knew. "Went down to
it" means something: there is a way down. A character who knows of the
Grandam's Well makes the link at once.

Father Rundle does not want the psalter to leave the tomb. He can be
persuaded:

- If the party asks to borrow it for the Turning, to keep both sides safe,
  he agrees, with tears in his eyes. "He would want it used."
- If the party takes it against his will, it loses his trust. He tells the
  Moot. Both sides lose 2 renown with the party, and it is a storm sign.

> **Secret (chapter 3):** The psalter's last lines are the way to put the
> Grandam back to sleep. Put out every fire on her back. Go down to her
> heart in the dark. Be quiet with her. The psalter helps in chapter 3,
> but a party without it can still do what the saint did.

### The grave robbers

If the party comes to Founders' Hill at night, or spends a night in the
church, it finds Kit Hocking and three young Corrin men digging up the
grave of Captain Gale. Kit believes that the Captain was buried with his
own notebook, the one with the true secret of the Light. He is wrong.

- **Talk.** Kit wants the notebook, not a fight. A character who tells him
  that the notebook is more likely in the wreck of the *Morning Star* (it
  is) stops him at once. He wants to go there tomorrow, with or without the
  party. A DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion or Intimidation) check also sends him
  home.
- **Fight.** Use **Kit Hocking** and three **Bandits**. They fight with
  fists and spades, and they run when Kit falls. It is a Low fight for
  level 2.
- **Wenna and the priest.** If the party lets the digging go on, Wenna
  Kitto wakes and screams. Father Rundle comes out with a lantern. In the
  morning, the Moot hears of it. The Corrin men are ashamed, and the party
  loses 1 renown with them for being there.

The grave holds the Captain's bones in a sailcloth shroud, a brass
compass that no longer works, and nothing else.

## Gull Rock

*Outer ring. Levels 2 to 3. Five hours from Hythe over the moor, or four
from the Kettles. The main scene is "The Top of the Rock".*

Gull Rock is a white stack off the north coast, loud with ten thousand
birds. It is the pride of the Tey egg climbers, the home of the seals whose
blubber makes lamp oil, and the one place on Farholm where a person can
watch the Wall breathe.

> You hear it before you see it: a roar like a crowd, high and endless.
> Then you come over the last rise of the moor, and the north sea opens in
> front of you, grey and white, and there it stands, a quarter of a mile
> out. A tower of white rock, four hundred feet high, every ledge crowded
> with birds. The air round it is full of wings.
>
> At its foot, black shapes lie on flat rocks just above the water. Seals.
> Between the shore and the rock, the sea runs fast over a low ridge of
> slippery stone, and the ridge is coming out of the water as you watch.

### The egg climbers

If the party comes with Tey renown of 3 or more, or with Thomas Spence,
the Tey egg climbers are here for the last climb of the year: five men and
women from three families, with ropes, baskets, and hats with a stick on
top. Otherwise, the party meets them on the shore by chance on a roll of 4
or more on a d6.

Their leader is **Thorfinn Harcus**, about seventy, small, tough as a
root, with one finger missing on each hand. He has climbed Gull Rock every
year for fifty years. He is suspicious of strangers and very proud of his
rock. He lets the party climb with his people if it asks politely and does
not boast. If a character boasts, he says, "Then you'll go first."

Thorfinn knows one thing that matters, and he tells it only at the top, to
anybody who gets there: "Watch the Wall. Watch it long enough, and it
breathes."

### The ridge and the tide

The ridge of stone is out of the water for about 6 hours at each low tide.
The tide comes back fast. A party that is still on the rock when it comes
back is stuck until the next low tide, 12 hours later, in the wind and the
spray, on a ledge with ten thousand angry birds.

- Crossing the ridge takes 10 minutes. It is slippery: each character makes
  a DC 10 Dexterity (Acrobatics) check. On a failure, the character falls
  into the fast water and must make a DC 12 Strength (Athletics) check to
  climb out, or be carried 50 feet and take 3 (1d6) Bludgeoning damage on
  the rocks.
- The climb up and down takes about 3 hours, if nothing goes wrong. The
  seal ledges take an hour. A party that does both has time, but not much.
- **Stuck on the rock.** A night on the rock is a Long Rest in the open. It
  gives a storm sign. If the weather that day was a squall or worse, each
  character also gains 1 Exhaustion level.

### The seal ledges

At the foot of the rock, a dozen seals lie on the flat rocks: grey seals,
and three big bulls, **Tusked Seals** as big as cattle, with tusks as long
as a hand. The Tey folk hunt the seals here in autumn for oil, meat, and
skins. They take only a few each year, and only the old ones.

The party needs blubber for the oil of the Light: one unit for each
barrel, and each Tusked Seal gives a full unit (see "Mending the Corrin
Light"). The bulls defend the herd. To take one, the party must fight it.

The rocks are slippery and wet: Difficult Terrain. A creature that is

**The Tey custom.** The Tey sealers take only the old bulls, and they
thank each seal aloud before they cut it. A party that hunts here with
the egg climbers and follows the custom gains 1 renown with the Tey folk.
A party that kills a young seal, or a cow, loses 1. A party that says the
blubber is for the Corrin Light loses 2, and the sealers go home.

Harvesting a seal takes 30 minutes. Carrying the blubber home is heavy
work: each unit weighs 60 pounds.

### The climb

The climb goes up the east face, on the ropes of the egg climbers, from
ledge to ledge. It has three stages. At each stage, each character makes a
Strength (Athletics) check. A character with the **Egg-Climber's Rope**, or
who climbs on the ropes of the egg climbers, has Advantage.

| The lower ledges | DC 10 | The character slips and hangs on the rope. Lose 10 minutes. |
| The Gully, a wet crack with the wind in it | DC 12 | The character falls 20 feet to the lower ledges: 7 (2d6) Bludgeoning damage. With a rope tied above, no damage. |
| The Crown, an overhang under the top | DC 13 | The character falls 30 feet: 10 (3d6) Bludgeoning damage, and climbs the Gully again. With a rope tied above, the character falls 10 feet and takes no damage. |

A character who falls 30 feet or more and drops to 0 Hit Points risks an
injury, if the table uses Injuries and Scars.

### The bonxies

ledges and they hate climbers. Each one dives at a climber's head.

### The Top of the Rock

**This is the main scene of Gull Rock.** The party reaches the top.

> The top of Gull Rock is a slope of short grass and white stone, no bigger
> than the deck of a ship, and the wind is so strong that you lie down
> without deciding to. The birds wheel below you now.
>
> And there is the Wall.
>
> From down on the island, it is a line on the edge of the sky. From here,
> it is a cliff of cloud, black at the bottom, white and boiling at the
> top, standing on the sea from the far west to the far east. You can see
> the lightning in it, silent at this distance. You can see the waves
> under it, white for miles.
>
> Thorfinn Harcus lies down beside you and puts his chin on his fists.
> "Now," he says. "Watch."

Ask the players how long they watch. If a character watches the Wall for a
few minutes and makes a DC 12 Wisdom (Perception) check, or watches for ten
minutes with no check, the character sees it: the whole Wall swells
outward, slowly, all along its length at once, and then draws back. A few
minutes later, it does it again. A character who looks through **Gale's
Glass** sees it at once.

This is a clue. The Wall is not weather. It breathes.

Thorfinn has seen it every year for fifty years. "My grandfather showed me.
His grandfather showed him. The Wall breathes, the Well breathes, the
Kettles breathe. All the same breath. I don't say what it means. I climb
rocks." If the party asks him whether the breath is faster this year, he is
quiet for a long time. Then he says: "Yes."

**The view.** From the top, a character can also see the whole north coast,
the Boneyard far to the west with its black beach, the steam of the Kettles
inland, and, on a clear day, a dark speck high on the grey ridges of the
middle of the island. A DC 10 Wisdom (Perception) check, or a spyglass,
shows that it is a ship.

### Coming down

The way down is the same three stages, with the same checks, and the
bonxies are still angry, but they do not attack again unless a character
goes near a nest. The egg climbers take their baskets of late eggs and
young birds and go home happy. A party that climbed with them gains 2
renown with the Tey folk, and Thorfinn says, grudgingly, "You'll do."

If the party has 10 Tey renown, Thorfinn gives it the **Egg-Climber's
Rope** at the bottom, for the next time.

> - **Turn the cube:** none.

## The Boneyard

*Outer ring. Levels 2 to 3. Six hours from Hythe by the track past the
Kettles, or three from the Kettles. The main scene is "The Master of the
Hopewell".*

The Boneyard is the sad, loud beach of the north-west coast where the sea
leaves everything that it breaks. It holds the copper, the glass, and the
spars that the Light needs. It also holds the answer to the oldest
argument in Hythe, and the ghost of the man who started it.

> The track drops off the moor into a steep black beach, and the noise hits
> you first: the sea, grinding the shingle, and under it a deep groaning,
> as of wood moving against wood.
>
> Then you see them. Ribs of ships stand up out of the black stones as far
> as you can see along the shore, like the bones of whales. Keels, hulls,
> masts, a stern with carved windows, a figurehead with no face. Some are
> silver with age. Some are black and new. The sea pushes them, slowly, and
> they groan.

### Jory Treloar

A Corrin man with a barrow is here when the party arrives: **Jory
Treloar**, about fifty, weathered, missing his front teeth, cheerful about
everything except the dark. He comes here twice a month for wood and iron,
and he knows the beach better than anybody alive. He goes home before
dusk, always.

Jory can show the party where to find what it needs, and he can tell it the
stories of the beach. He has a turf hut at the top of the beach, the
**barrow hut**, with a hearth stone and a door that locks. It counts as a
shelter: a Long Rest inside gives no storm sign. He lets the party use it,
but he will not stay the night. "Not on this beach. Not me."

### What the beach holds

The party can spend hours searching the Boneyard. Each search is one hour
and one check (see "The materials of the island").

| What | Check | What the party finds |
|---|---|---|
| Copper sheathing | DC 10 Strength (Athletics) | One sheet of copper from an old hull, green and whole. The mirror needs 4. |
| Sea glass and old bottles | DC 10 Wisdom (Perception) | 5 pounds of glass. The lens needs 20. |
| Ship spars | DC 10 Strength (Athletics) | One good spar, straight and sound. The roof of the Light needs 2. Each spar takes 2 people to carry. |
| Something old | DC 12 Intelligence (History) | Roll a d6: 1, a ship's bell with no clapper; 2, a green copper bit of King Edwy; 3, a carved name board, "*Content*"; 4, a cutlass, rusted solid; 5, a tin box of buttons; 6, a timber older than the fleet (see below). |

### The old ships

Most of the wrecks are from the fleet, or from ships that the Wall took
since: the *Swallow*, the *Content*, the *Unity*, and the hulls of fishing
boats that went too far. But some timbers are older. Much older.

A character who examines the oldest timbers and makes a DC 13
Intelligence (History) check finds hulls built in a way that nobody builds
today, or built three hundred and sixty years ago: planks sewn together
with cord, keels of a single tree, a figurehead of a beast with a long neck.
Jory says that his grandfather called them "the first ships". Nobody knows
where they came from.

> **Secret (chapter 3):** The oldest ships came in with earlier Long
> Counts, when the Grandam rose and her breath pulled whatever sailed
> nearby onto her back. The fleet was not the first. It will not be the
> last, unless the Wall breaks.

This is a clue, but a soft one. Ships came here before the fleet, over a
very long time, and none ever left.

### The *Hopewell*

Under the timbers of three older wrecks, at the western end of the beach,
lies a hull that is newer than the fleet: a small ship, about sixty feet,
built of old timbers, broken in half. The Corrin men who come for wood
never dig this far, because the older wrecks lie on top of it.

A character who climbs into the heap and searches for an hour, with a DC
13 Intelligence (Investigation) check, finds the stern, and on it, a
carved name board, half buried:

> *HOPEWELL*
> *of Hythe*

Under the board, in the sand of the stern, lies a cracked compass in a box
of black wood: **Pascoe's Compass**.

{{treasure: Pascoe's Compass}}

The *Hopewell* did not get through. She came back in the storm and broke
here, a hundred and fifty years ago, and her crew died. The Corrin men
have believed for a hundred and fifty years that she reached home. The Tey
folk have said for a hundred and fifty years that she came back, and that
the Corrin men knew it and lied. Both were wrong about something. The Tey
folk never found the board. They guessed.

### The Master of the *Hopewell*

**This is the main scene of the Boneyard.** It happens at the first high
tide after dark that the party spends on the beach, or as soon as the
party takes the name board out of the heap.

> The tide comes up the black beach, and the wrecks groan louder. Then, out
> past the breakers, a light: a ship's lantern, swinging, as if on a mast
> in a heavy sea. There is no ship under it.
>
> The light comes in through the surf. Under it, a young man in a blue coat
> walks up out of the water, wet to the skin, with weed in his hair. Behind
> him come others, men and women in the clothes of old Hythe, slow and
> grey, with the sea pouring off them.
>
> The young man raises his lantern and looks past you, east, along the
> coast. "Is it lit?" he says. "I can't see the Light. Is it lit?"

The ghost is **John Pascoe**, the master of the *Hopewell*, and his crew are
**Drowned Sailors**. Each night at high tide, they walk up out of the sea
and try to sail out again, through a gap in the Wall that closed a hundred
and fifty years ago. They do not know that they are dead. John Pascoe
believes that the Tey folk have put out the Light, and that if somebody
lights it again, the lane will open and he can go home.

### Running the Master of the *Hopewell*

John Pascoe does not want to fight. He wants to go home. He attacks only if
the party stands between him and the sea, tries to take the name board from
him, or attacks first. His crew follow his lead.

- **Talk.** The ghost answers questions as if it were the night of the
  *Hopewell*, year 211. He asks the party which bank they are from, whether
  they are from the Tey and put the Light out, and whether the Light is
  lit. He thinks that the party are new colonists he has never met. He
  talks about his wife, Mary, and his son, who is six, and who will be the
  grandfather of Absalom Pascoe's grandfather.
- **The truth.** A character can tell him the truth: that he is dead, that
  the *Hopewell* broke on this beach, that a hundred and fifty years have
  passed. He does not believe it, at first. A DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion)
  check, with Advantage if the party shows him the name board, makes him
  look down at himself, and at his crew, and understand. Then he weeps,
  and the drowned sailors stand still in the surf.
- **Rest.** He asks the party to take the name board to Founders' Hill, to
  the empty grave that the Corrin men made for him, and to tell his family
  that he tried. If the party promises and keeps the promise, he and his
  crew rest, and they never walk again. "Tell them the lane was real," he
  says. "Tell them it was real, and it closed."
- **Fight.** If the fight starts, the ghost fights until he drops to 0 Hit
  Points, then fades into the surf. He comes back at the next high tide,
  unless the board lies on Founders' Hill. The drowned sailors fall into
  wet heaps of weed and bone.

> the players see Kit in his face.

## The Saltpans

*Outer ring. Levels 2 to 3. One hour from Hythe along the shore. The main
scene is "The Dry Tide".*

The party knows the Saltpans already: chapter 1 ends here, at the hut of
Amos Tulloch. Come back, and the place has work for the party, a strange
thing in the water, and the only pan on the island big enough to render oil
for the Light.

> The pans lie flat and bright along the shore, square after square of
> shallow water inside low walls of clay, with the sky reflected in each
> one. At the east end, smoke rises from the stone hut where the great
> iron pan sits over its peat fire, on its ballast stones.
>
> Amos Tulloch waves from the hut door with a long wooden rake. "Light to
> you!" he shouts. "You came back! Jenefer said you would."
>
> There is nobody beside him.

### The salt boilers

Three people work the Saltpans. They boil all the salt of Farholm, and
salt is the money of the island.

- **Amos Tulloch,** about seventy-five, bent, cheerful, born on the Tey
  bank, married on the Corrin bank fifty years ago, and outcast by both. He
  still talks to his wife Jenefer, who died ten years ago. He is not mad.
  He just likes her company.
- **Loveday Penrose,** about fifty, a widow of the Corrin bank. She came
  out here when her husband drowned and the Lamp Room told her to remarry.
  She talks less than Amos and sees more. She is the one who notices that
  the party is hungry.
- **Ola Drever,** twelve, a boy of the Tey bank. His family thinks he is
  learning the salt trade. He is learning about ships, from Amos's
  stories, and he wants to be a sailor more than anything. He asks the
  party about the sea, the *Good Intent*, and the world. Every day.

### The great pan

The iron pan in the hut is eight feet across, from the galley of the
*Increase*. It is the only pan on Farholm big enough to render blubber into
lamp oil. The Corrin men render their oil here each autumn, with Amos's
help, for a share of salt.

Amos renders the oil for the Light, for a fair share: one iron tool, or a
coin of Corrantey, or a day of the party's work at the pans for each barrel.
He does not care what the oil is for. "Oil is oil." Each barrel takes 1
workday and one unit of blubber (see "Mending the Corrin Light"). Loveday
does the work if the party is away.

Amos also gives the party, free, as much salt and fine sand as it needs to
polish a mirror.

### Jenefer's marks

On the post of the hut door, somebody has cut many small notches, in rows,
each row with a year beside it. Amos says that Jenefer cut them. "The dry
tides," he says. "She counted them. I keep it up, for her."

A **dry tide** is what the salt boilers call it when the water in the pans
goes down, and then comes up again, a few inches, with no tide due. On a
still day it happens every few minutes. On most days, nobody notices. The
salt boilers notice, because the water of the pans tells them everything.

A character who looks at the notches with a DC 10 Intelligence
(Investigation) check sees that each year has more notches than the year
before, and that this year has more than any two years before it put
together. A character who sits by a pan on a still day and watches it for
ten minutes sees the water draw down and rise, slowly, twice.

This is a clue. The sea round Farholm rises and falls on its own, in a

### The Dry Tide

**This is the main scene of the Saltpans.** It happens on the party's first
day here in this chapter, or when Ola Drever runs to find the party (see
"Encounters of the island", result 12).

> The water in the pans starts to go down. Not a few inches: all of it,
> fast, draining out through the walls. Out past the shore, the sea pulls
> back, and back, and the wet sand shines for a hundred yards. Fish flap on
> it. Old ribs of wrecks stand up out of it.
>
> "Jenefer," says Amos quietly. "Jenefer, it's doing the big one."
>
> Then the sea comes back, and it brings things with it.

The sea comes back in one long, low wave that floods every pan on the shore.
It does not hurt anybody. But it brings crabs: **Razorback Crabs** and
**Shellcastle Crabs**, flung in from the deep water, angry and hungry, in
the middle of the pans. One Shellcastle Crab wears a big curved piece of
old glass as its shell.

### Running the Dry Tide

- **The pans.** The water in the pans is 1 foot deep, with walls of clay 2
  feet high between them. The walls are easy to walk on. The pans are
  Difficult Terrain. A creature that walks on a wall of a pan must make a
  DC 10 Dexterity saving throw when it takes damage, or fall into the pan.
- **The salt boilers.** Amos stands at the door of the hut with his rake.
  Loveday drags Ola inside. Ola does not want to go inside. Ola wants to
  fight a crab. On the second round, he runs out with a stick, and a
  Razorback Crab goes for him. If nobody helps him, he takes a bad wound
  (he drops to 0 Hit Points, and stabilizes).
- **The salt.** If the crabs break more than three walls of the pans, a
  season of salt is lost. The colony will feel it. The crabs break a wall
  when they take a Dash action across it.
- **The glass.** The Shellcastle Crab with the glass shell has 5 pounds of
  glass on its back, a big piece of the old lens of the Corrin Light. Once
  the crab is dead, the glass is the party's.

### After the Dry Tide

The salt boilers are grateful, whatever happened.

- If Ola is safe, Amos gives the party his last gold sun of King Edwy,
  which he kept for fifty years, "for the boy". Ola follows the party
  around for the rest of the chapter, asking about ships.
- If the pans are safe, the salt of the colony is safe. Both sides hear of
  it: +1 renown with each.
- Amos says, while he mends the walls: "Jenefer always said the island's
  alive. I said it was the moon. She said the moon doesn't do it every
  seven minutes." He laughs. Then he does not laugh.
