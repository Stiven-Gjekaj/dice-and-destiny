# Storm Season

| | |
|---|---|
| **Levels** | 5 |
| **Time at the table** | 1 to 2 sessions (about 6 to 8 hours) |
| **Places** | Hythe, the Corrin Light, the Grandam's Well, the lane through the Wall, Corrantey |
| **Rule that starts** | The Rising: what the cube counts in the storm season |
| **Handouts** | The Long Count. The Letter of the Moot. |

The cube reaches 64, and the Turning comes in over Farholm. The Wall
stands on the island itself, and a girl on the Tey bank wakes in the night
and says, very calmly, "She's awake." For a few days, the way home is open
to anybody who dares to light the Corrin Light. Every hour that it burns,
the Grandam rises a little more.

This chapter has three jobs:

1. **Pay off the island.** The parts of the Light, the clues, the renown,
   the people the party saved and the people it angered: all of it matters
   now. Use your notes from the end of chapter 2.
2. **Give the party three choices.** Who comes home with it. Whether the
   Grandam wakes or sleeps again. What it tells Corrantey. No choice is the
   right one. The book gives the result of each.
3. **End the story.** At the end, the party is home or it is not, and
   Farholm is above the sea or it is not.

## Running This Chapter

### What really happens

> **GM tip:** Farholm is the back of the Grandam, a creature of the deep
> sea, asleep at the surface. The Wall is her breath. Once in about 360
> years she rises toward waking, and this is the year. A great light draws
> her up, as a lantern draws a fish. While she rises, she holds her breath,
> and the Wall on the side of the light goes quiet. That quiet is the lane,
> the only way through the Wall.
>
> So every hour that the Corrin Light burns, she rises further. If she
> wakes, she dives within days, Farholm goes under the sea, and the Wall
> breaks up forever. To make her settle, do what Saint Mawes did: go down
> to her heart in the dark, and be still with her.
>
> Read "The Truth of the Grandam" in the gazetteer before you run this
> chapter.

### What happens

1. **The Turning (Scene 1).** The storm comes in. The characters go to
   level 5. Both sides come to the party.
2. **The Last Moot (Scene 2).** The party makes its plan, and decides who
   comes home.
3. **The Night of the Light (Scene 3).** The Light burns. Three things
   happen at once: at the Light, on the boats, and at the Grandam's heart.
4. **The Lane (Scene 4)** and **the Heart (Scene 5).** The party is in one
   place or the other. The book tells you what happens in the place where
   it is not.
5. **Dawn (Scene 6).** The Grandam sleeps or wakes.
6. **Ending the Book.** Corrantey, now or a year late.

**Things to prepare:** your notes from "What chapter 3 needs to know" in
chapter 2; the number of the crew who lived (8, 11, or 14, from chapter 1);
the two handouts; and backgammon checkers, or a second d6, for the Rising.
The statblocks of Erlend Linklater, the Well Watchman, the Ghost of John
Pascoe, and the Drowned Sailor are in chapter 2.

## The Rising

When the cube reaches 64, it stops. It never turns again. From now on, the
checkers next to it count the **Rising**: how far the Grandam has risen
toward waking.

> **Teach: The Rising.** "The cube is full. The storm season is here. From
> now on, I put a checker down each time the storm gets worse. At six,
> something happens that nobody on Farholm has ever seen." Do not tell the
> players what. Most of them will guess.

| The Rising | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0 | She sleeps. The Turning ends at the next dawn, and the Wall draws back out to sea. |
| 1 to 2 | She stirs. The ground sighs every few minutes. |
| 3 to 4 | The whole island lifts and settles, like the chest of a sleeper. The sea draws back from the coasts and comes back. |
| 5 | The ground tilts. The Kettles throw boiling water fifty feet into the air. |
| 6 | **She wakes.** See "If she wakes" in Scene 6. |

- **It starts at 0** when the cube reaches 64.
- **+1 at the dawn of the fourth day** of the Turning, and **+1 at the dawn
  of the seventh**. The longer the party waits, the less room it has.
- **+1 at the end of each hour that the Corrin Light burns.**
- **+1 for a great fire on the bare ground,** such as Kit's fire at the
  Saint's Hearth.
- **When the Light goes out,** the Rising stops going up. It goes down by 1
  at each dawn. People at the heart bring it down faster (Scene 5).

With normal dice, put a second d6 next to the cube when the Rising first
goes up, with the 1 on top, and turn it one face at a time.

## Scene 1: The Turning

**Goal:** The storm comes in. Both sides come to the party.

The cube reaches 64 at the end of a scene of chapter 2. Finish the scene,
then read this, wherever the party is.

> The wind stops. Everywhere at once, the way a sleeper stops breathing for
> a moment between one breath and the next. The gulls are silent.
>
> Then, from every side of the sky, the Wall comes in. You see it walk over
> the sea toward the island: a cliff of black cloud, white at the top, lit
> inside by lightning. The first wind hits you from the west and the second
> from the east, and the rain comes sideways, warm as blood.
>
> Under your feet, the ground breathes out, long and slow. And does not
> breathe in again for a long, long time.

**The characters go to level 5** at their next rest. A character at level 3
goes up two levels. A party away from Hythe gets back in one leg of a
journey in the storm.

### That night

**Inga wakes.** In the middle of the night, Inga Halcrow walks out into the
rain in her nightdress and stands on the middle arch of the Bridge, looking
east at the dark Light.

> "I'm not dreaming," she says. "That's how I know. I always dreamed, and
> now there's nothing." She thinks about it. "She's waking up. She's looking
> for something. She wants to see."

**Elspeth sends for the party** before dawn. If the party has 20 renown
with the Tey folk, or 10 renown and three or more clues, she shows it **The
Long Count**, and asks: "You have seen the island. Tell me what she is."

{{handout: 03-the-long-count}}

If the party tells her what the saint did, or shows her the psalter, she is
quiet for a long time. "Down. In the dark. We have sung at the top of the
Well for three hundred years. Nobody ever went down."

**Absalom sends for the party** at dawn. He has the brass lamp off the wall
and every Corrin man who can stand. "John Pascoe lit the Light on the
worst night of the Turning, and the Wall opened. I mean to light it, with
or without the Moot. I would rather it was with you." If he knows that the
Light works only in the storm season, he adds: "Nathaniel was right. It was
always going to be now."

### The Turning

The Turning lasts nine days. The weather is always "The Wall draws in", so
each leg of a journey takes twice as long. There are no storm signs and no
doubles. Each morning, roll a d6.

| d6 | The Turning, each morning |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The sea comes up the Fleet,** as in the Drowning. If nobody warns the Low Ground, 1d6 people of the Tey bank drown. A party that warns them gains 2 renown with the Tey folk. |
| 2 | **Kit Hocking goes to the Hearth** with fire, if he still doubts the Grandam. Run "Fire on the Hearth" from chapter 2, with twice the Rib Lice. If the fire burns, add 1 to the Rising. |
| 3 | **The Saltpans go under.** Amos Tulloch and Ola Drever come to Hythe with a cart of salt. |
| 4 | **Margery's shed loses its roof.** A DC 12 Strength (Athletics) check saves the boat, or it needs 1 more day of work. |
| 5 | **Both banks pray.** The Tey folk sing at the Well all night, and the Corrin men sing in the church. |
| 6 | **The ground lifts.** Add 1 to the Rising. Every bell in the church rings by itself. |

**A Light that is not ready.** A party that lacks a part can still make it,
with the rules of "Mending the Corrin Light" in chapter 2. In the Turning,
each workday takes two days, and casting the lens at the Kettles means "The
Boil" again, with twice the Scalding Eels. A party can still finish a part
and light the Light on the seventh night, with less room.

## Scene 2: The Last Moot

**Goal:** The party makes a plan, with the colony or without it, and decides
who comes home.

On the first evening of the Turning, the Moot meets in the storm. Water
runs down the inside of the stern windows, and the whole town stands
behind the two benches. "The Turning is here," says Father Rundle. "Somebody
had better tell us what to do."

### What each side wants

| Who | Wants | Agrees if |
|---|---|---|
| **Absalom Pascoe** | To light the Light and send a boat home. | The party has 10 Corrin renown, or a better plan that still sends a boat. |
| **Elspeth Flett** | To keep the Light dark. | The party has 10 Tey renown and tells her what the saint did. Then she offers to go down the Well herself. |
| **Erlend Linklater** | To stop the Light, by force. | Elspeth agrees, or the party talked him down in chapter 2. |
| **Kit Hocking** | To go home, today. | He follows the party if it saved his brother or talked him down at the Hearth. Otherwise he follows Absalom. |
| **Father Rundle** | Nobody dead. | He backs any plan that both benches agree to. |

### The plan

The best plan needs both banks. Let the players find it. Do not give it to
them.

1. **The Light burns** until the boats are through the Wall, and not one
   hour longer. Somebody whom both sides trust keeps it.
2. **The boats sail** down the lane as soon as it opens.
3. **Somebody goes down the Well** to the heart, in the dark, and is still
   with her while the Light burns and after it goes out.

A party that has the psalter, Gale's log, or three or more clues can make
this plan. A party with fewer clues probably plans for the Light only.
Scene 6 tells you what happens.

**Winning the Moot.** The party makes a DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion) check
for each bench that does not already agree. Advantage with proof: the
psalter, the log of the *Morning Star*, the name board of the *Hopewell*,
or Inga, if she will speak. If both benches agree, Father Rundle rings the
bell three times. A side that does not agree acts against the party on the
night.

### Who keeps the Light, and who goes down

| Task | Who can do it |
|---|---|
| **Keep the Light** | **Absalom** offers first: "That is what the Captain of the Light is for." He puts it out at the hour agreed if he trusts the party (20 Corrin renown, or the scene of Hester and her father). Otherwise he keeps it burning until dawn, "so that every soul can get through". **Erlend**, if won over, keeps it for the hours agreed and not one minute more. A **character** can stay. |
| **Go down the Well** | **Elspeth** goes, if she agreed, on a rope in a sling. **Inga** goes with her, whatever anybody says. **Characters** can go, but then they do not sail. Erlend and the Well Watch lower the climbers and guard the top. |

### Who comes home

This is the first choice of the book. Ask the players to make it together,
out loud. Two boats can cross the open sea:

- **Margery's boat,** if the party has seen it and Margery offers it (20
  Corrin renown, or the Corrin bench agreed). She carries **24 people** and
  food for a month. Margery sails her. She has no name yet, and Margery asks
  the party to give her one.
- **The ship's boat of the *Good Intent*,** which Jory Kitto saved. On the
  open sea, she carries **10 people**, "if God is kind and the wind holds".

The fishing boats of Hythe can go into the lane, but not across the open
sea. So there are 34 seats, or 10 without Margery's boat. Each character
needs a seat. So does each of the crew who lived. The rest are for the
colony, and about two hundred Corrin men want one.

| Who wants a seat | If they come |
|---|---|
| **Kit Hocking** | He weeps at the sight of home, and signs on the first ship west again. |
| **Nathaniel Gale** | He brings the Landing Book. In Corrantey, it is the proof. |
| **Inga Halcrow** | Beyond the Wall, she sleeps for two days without a dream. |
| **Hester, Thomas, and their two children** | Four seats. Absalom watches his daughter go, and keeps the Light anyway. |
| **Amos Tulloch** | He wants to see Corrin once before he dies. He does. |
| **Ola Drever** | He is a sailor for the rest of his life. |

A party that does not choose finds that Kit chooses, and he chooses young
men. **Absalom, Elspeth, Father Rundle, and Erlend stay,** whatever they are
offered. A character who stays chooses a life on Farholm, or a year of
waiting.

Before the boats go, the Moot gives the party **The Letter of the Moot**.

{{handout: 03-the-letter-of-the-moot}}

## Scene 3: The Night of the Light

**Goal:** The Light burns, the boats sail, and somebody keeps the Grandam
from waking, or does not.

The party chooses the night. Each night that it waits gives time to finish
parts, and the Rising goes up at the fourth and seventh dawn.

### Three places at once

Ask the players where each character is. Run the place where most of them
are, and roll for the others.

| Place | What happens | If no character is there |
|---|---|---|
| **The Corrin Light** | The Light is lit at dusk and must burn until the boats are through. | "The Light without the party", below |
| **The boats** | They wait under the east point and sail when the lane opens. | "The lane without the party", in Scene 4 |
| **The Grandam's Well** | The climbers go down while the Light burns. | "The heart without the party", in Scene 5 |

A character can stand at the Light until the lane opens, then run down to
the boats under the point. The boats wait one hour for it, if asked. A
character at the Well cannot sail.

### Lighting the Light

> Rain blows through the lamp room in sheets. Somebody holds a sailcloth
> over the brass lamp while Digory Rowe pours the oil, with hands that do
> not shake at all. Absalom Pascoe strikes the light himself.
>
> The mirror takes the flame and throws it forward, and the lens takes it
> from the mirror, and a beam of white light as thick as a man goes out
> over the sea, straight east, toward home.
>
> It touches the Wall.

### The strength of the Light

| The Light has | The lane opens after | The crossing takes | Helm check each hour |
|---|---|---|---|
| The lamp, the mirror, and the lens | 1 hour | 3 hours | None |
| The lamp and the lens | 2 hours | 3 hours | DC 10 |
| The lamp and the mirror | 2 hours | 4 hours | DC 12 |
| The lamp only | 3 hours | 4 hours | DC 13 |
| No lamp: a beacon fire of peat and oil | 3 hours | 5 hours | DC 15 |

- **The hours.** The Light burns until the lane opens, then for the
  crossing. A full Light burns 4 hours and adds 4 to the Rising: on one of
  the first three nights, that leaves her asleep with no help. On the
  seventh night or later, it wakes her unless somebody is at her heart. A
  beacon burns 8 hours, and wakes her unless somebody holds her down for 3
  of them.
- **The helm check.** Each hour, the person at each helm makes a Wisdom
  (Survival) check, or a check with Water Vehicles. On a failure, the lane
  bends, and the crossing takes 1 hour more.
- **The oil.** Each barrel burns 2 hours. With no oil left, the Light goes
  out.
- **The roof.** With no roof, roll a d6 each hour. On a 1 or 2, the rain
  puts the lamp out, and lighting it again takes the rest of the hour.
- **If the Light goes out while a boat is in the lane** and does not burn
  again before the end of the next hour, the Wall closes on that boat. See
  "Thrown back" in Scene 4.

### Each hour at the Light

At the end of each hour, add 1 to the Rising, and roll a d6.

| d6 | At the Light |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Lightning** hits the tower. Each creature in the lamp room makes a DC 13 Dexterity saving throw, taking 22 (4d10) Lightning damage on a failed save, or half as much on a success. |
| 2 | **The wind.** Each creature outside makes a DC 12 Strength saving throw, or is pushed 10 feet toward the cliff and has the Prone condition. |
| 3 | **Birds** fly into the beam in thousands, as birds do to every light in a storm: two **Swarms of Thieving Gulls**. |
| 4 | **The storm.** Nothing new. The beam holds. |
| 5 | **The sea draws back** from the point. For a mile out, the rocks are bare and steaming. Then the sea comes back, all at once. |
| 6 | **The glow.** Under the sea, all round the island, a faint green light shows, in a shape much longer than the island. Then it fades. |

### The fight at the Light

**The Well Watch comes** in the second hour if Elspeth did not agree, or if
nobody won Erlend over. He comes as Sander Linklater came in the *Hopewell*
year, in the dark, with the Watch and the seal hunters of the Tey bank, to
put the Light out. Erlend's statblock and the Well Watchman's are in "The
Corrin Light" in chapter 2. The seal hunters use the **Warrior Veteran**
statblock, with harpoons.

{{statblock: Warrior Veteran}}

| Party size | Monsters | XP |
|---|---|---|
| 4 characters | Erlend Linklater, 4 Well Watchmen, 2 Warrior Veterans | 2,250 |

> **Scaling:** This is a Low fight for level 5 (500 XP for each
> character), made harder by the storm, the dark, and the goal of the Watch.
> Count only the characters at the Light. With fewer than 3, use the line
> for 3, and Digory and the Corrin watch fight beside them.
>
> - **3 characters (1,500 XP):** Erlend, 3 Well Watchmen, 1 Warrior Veteran
>   (1,450 XP).
> - **4 characters (2,000 XP):** as in the table.
> - **5 characters (2,500 XP):** Erlend, 5 Well Watchmen, 2 Warrior
>   Veterans (2,350 XP).
> - **6 characters (3,000 XP):** Erlend, 6 Well Watchmen, 3 Warrior
>   Veterans (3,150 XP).

A watchman who reaches the lamp room puts the lamp out with his cloak, as
an action. Erlend still talks, even now: a DC 15 Charisma (Persuasion)
check, with Advantage if the party tells him the saint's way or Elspeth is
at the Well. On a success, he asks, "How many hours?", and guards the Light
for exactly that long.

**The drowned come** in the third hour if John Pascoe did not rest in
chapter 2. The crew of the *Hopewell* walk up out of the sea toward the
beam, and the drowned of a hundred older wrecks walk with them. They want
the lane. Each one that reaches the lamp room puts the lamp out with its
wet hands.

| Party size | Monsters | XP |
|---|---|---|
| 4 characters | The Ghost of John Pascoe, 8 Drowned Sailors | 2,050 |

> **Scaling:** This is a Low fight for level 5 (500 XP for each
> character). The drowned do not want to fight. They want the light.
>
> - **3 characters (1,500 XP):** the ghost, 5 Drowned Sailors (1,450 XP).
> - **4 characters (2,000 XP):** as in the table.
> - **5 characters (2,500 XP):** the ghost, 10 Drowned Sailors (2,450 XP).
> - **6 characters (3,000 XP):** the ghost, 12 Drowned Sailors (2,850 XP).

A character who tells John Pascoe that the lane is open, and points to it,
can make a DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion) check. On a success, he walks down
to the water, and a ship of pale light goes down the lane ahead of the
boats, with all the drowned aboard. They are never seen again. Give each
character Heroic Inspiration.

### The Light without the party

If no character is at the Light, roll a d6 each hour and add the numbers
below. On a total of 1 or less, the Light goes out that hour.

| The Light | Add |
|---|---|
| Absalom keeps it | +1 |
| Erlend keeps it, or guards it | +2 |
| The Well Watch comes, and nobody won Erlend over | −3 |
| The drowned come | −2 |
| No roof on the lamp room | −1 |

At the hour agreed, the keeper puts the Light out, if the keeper trusts the
party. Otherwise it burns until dawn.

## Scene 4: The Lane

**Goal:** The boats sail out through the Wall. The party sees what Farholm
is.

> The beam touches the Wall, and the Wall goes quiet. A long way off, then
> nearer, as if a held breath were spreading along the sea toward you. The
> rain stops on your face. The waves fall down flat. In front of the boats,
> under the beam, a lane of black, still water opens, a quarter of a mile
> wide, with a wall of storm on each side, going up and up.
>
> Above the lane, for the first time since you came to Farholm, you see the
> stars.

In the lane, a light wind blows from the west, the ordinary wind of the
open sea. It blows toward home.

### Looking back

In the second hour, a character who looks back sees this.

> Behind you, the island stands up out of the sea. You can see bare rock
> under the cliffs of the east point, rock that was under water this
> morning, and the sea pours off it in white sheets. The whole of Farholm is
> lifting, very slowly, the way a sleeper lifts a shoulder.
>
> Then, far beyond the south coast, a long dark curve comes up out of the
> water, as long as a town, shining wet. It hangs there for a long moment.
> Then it goes back under, without a sound.

Whatever the players thought the island was, they know now.

### The lampreys

Also in the second hour, something bumps the hull. As the Grandam rises,
the **Great Lampreys** let go of her side and come up to look at the
lights. Two of them find the boats.

{{statblock: Great Lamprey}}

| Party size | Monsters | XP |
|---|---|---|
| 4 characters | 2 Great Lampreys | 2,200 |

> **Scaling:** This is a Low fight for level 5 (500 XP for each
> character). A character who falls in is in real danger.
>
> - **3 characters (1,500 XP):** 1 Great Lamprey, 2 **Reef Sharks** (1,300
>   XP).
> - **4 characters (2,000 XP):** as in the table.
> - **5 characters (2,500 XP):** 2 Great Lampreys, 3 Reef Sharks (2,500
>   XP).
> - **6 characters (3,000 XP):** 3 Great Lampreys (3,300 XP).

- **The boats.** Margery's boat is 30 feet long and 10 feet wide, the ship's
  boat 20 by 6. They sail 30 feet apart. A rocking boat is Difficult Terrain.
- **Lights.** The lampreys go for the lantern at each stern. A boat that
  puts out all its lights gives each lamprey Disadvantage on its first
  attack against it. If Inga is aboard, she says so at once.
- **In the water.** A rope and a DC 10 Strength (Athletics) check pull a
  character back aboard. The lampreys go for that character first.

### Through the Wall

> The wall of storm ends ahead of you, as a street ends. Past it there is
> nothing but grey sea, a long slow swell, and a wind from the west.
> Behind you, the Wall stands on the sea from one end of the world to the
> other, and in the middle of it, a tiny star burns.
>
> As you watch, the star goes out. The lane closes behind you, quietly,
> like a door.

If the Light burns on, the star does not go out. Tell the players that,
too.

### Thrown back

If the Wall closes on a boat, each character aboard makes a DC 13
Constitution saving throw, or gains one injury. Then roll a d6 for the
boat. On a 1 to 3, it is thrown back, as the *Hopewell* was, and wrecks on
the north coast at dawn. The characters live. Half of the colonists aboard
do not. On a 4 to 6, it comes out of the Wall half swamped, with a broken
mast, and everybody lives.

### The lane without the party

If no character is on the boats, roll a d6 for each boat at the end of the
crossing. Margery's boat adds 2. On a 1 or 2, the lampreys or the storm
sink it. On a 3 or more, it comes out, and its people tell the story in
Corrantey a month later.

### The voyage home

The *Good Intent* beat into the west wind for forty days. A boat that runs
before it comes home in about twenty-five. Play one scene of the voyage at
most: a calm day, a dead whale, a ship of Corrantey that does not believe a
word. Then the coast of home comes up out of the morning.

## Scene 5: The Heart

**Goal:** The climbers go down to the heart in the dark, and are still with
her until she settles.

In chapter 2, the warm breath of the Well tore climbers off the wall. On the
night of the Light, it stops. While the Light burns, the Grandam holds her
breath, and the shaft is still.

> For three hundred years, the Well has breathed in and out, every few
> minutes. Tonight it is as still as a held breath. Elspeth Flett puts her
> hand on the white wall. "She's listening," she says. "Go down."

- **The way down.** The gift ledge is 60 feet down. Below it, the shaft goes
  down 300 feet more, and the wet black stone turns warm, then soft, then
  it is not stone. Each 100 feet takes a DC 12 Strength (Athletics) check,
  or none with the **Egg-Climber's Rope**. On a failure, a character slides
  20 feet and takes 7 (2d6) Bludgeoning damage.
- **No light.** Below the ledge, each light adds 1 to the Rising at the end
  of each hour, and draws the flukes. A character with no Darkvision is
  Blinded. The Tey folk count the knots in the rope.

> **GM tip:** Turn down the lights at the table, and speak quietly. Ask the
> players to speak quietly too.

### The flukes

Halfway down, the walls are alive with flukes, as worms live in any animal.
They are blind, and feel movement, sound, and light. A climber who stops
moving in the dark is safe.

{{statblock: Swarm of Flukes}}

| Party size | Monsters | XP |
|---|---|---|
| 4 characters | 4 Swarms of Flukes | 1,800 |

> **Scaling:** This is a Low fight for level 5 (500 XP for each
> character). It is easy for a still, silent party, and deadly for a loud
> one.
>
> - **3 characters (1,500 XP):** 3 Swarms of Flukes (1,350 XP).
> - **4 characters (2,000 XP):** as in the table.
> - **5 characters (2,500 XP):** 5 Swarms of Flukes (2,250 XP).
> - **6 characters (3,000 XP):** 6 Swarms of Flukes (2,700 XP).

A character who stays still on the wall makes a DC 12 Strength (Athletics)
check each round to hold on. On a failure, it slips 5 feet, and the flukes
feel it. Fire drives them off, and adds 1 to the Rising.

### The heart

At the bottom is a round hollow as big as the church on Founders' Hill. The
floor is warm and soft, and covered in shells. The walls move.

> It is hot, and wet, and completely dark. And then you feel it in your
> chest: one beat, so deep and so slow that it seems to go on for a long
> time. The whole hollow tightens round you, and lets go.
>
> A long time later, another. It is the sound that you hear when you hold a
> shell to your ear. It was always this.

### Being still

The climbers lie in the dark and keep still, as the saint did. At the end
of each hour, each person at the heart makes a DC 12 Wisdom saving throw.
On a failure, they move, or cry out, or reach for a light.

- **While the Light burns,** if at least half succeed, the Rising does not
  go up at the end of that hour.
- **After the Light goes out,** if at least half succeed, the Rising goes
  down by 1. At 0, she sleeps.
- **Help.** A character who sings the dawn hymn very softly has Advantage,
  and so does each person who hears it. The hymn has no magic. It only
  keeps a frightened person calm. **Saint Mawes's Psalter** helps as its
  text says. Elspeth and Inga each succeed on a roll of 3 or more on a d6.
- **The heat.** After the first hour, each character makes a DC 12
  Constitution saving throw at the end of each hour, or gains 1 level of
  Exhaustion. A **Kettle Stone** gives Advantage.

When she sleeps, the beat slows, the hollow loosens, and a warm wind starts
up the shaft. She is breathing again. The breath carries the climbers up:
a DC 10 Strength (Athletics) check for each 100 feet, and they come out at
dawn, into the rain, with the Tey folk singing round them.

In the shells on the floor, a character who searches by feel finds a
**Heart Shell**.

{{treasure: Heart Shell}}

### The heart without the party

If Elspeth and Inga go down without the party, roll for each of them each
hour. If at least one succeeds, the heart holds for that hour. If nobody
goes down, the Rising goes down by 1 only at each dawn after the Light goes
out.

## Scene 6: Dawn

**Goal:** Tell the players what they did.

### If she sleeps again

> The rain stops at dawn. All round the island, the Wall draws back, slowly,
> the way a tide goes out. By noon it is a dark line on the edge of the
> world again. The ground under Hythe breathes in, and out, softly.

Farholm stays above the sea for another long sleep, and the Wall stays. If
both banks made the plan together, the Moot agrees that night, for the first
time in a hundred and fifty years, that the Light may burn again in a
Turning, if ships come for the rest. Each autumn, Absalom keeps oil for one
night.

### If she wakes

> The ground rolls, once, like the deck of a ship, and every person on
> Farholm falls down. Out at sea, the Wall tears apart everywhere at once,
> into hundreds of ordinary storms that spin away across the water, and
> between them the open sea shines in the sun.
>
> And the island begins, very slowly, to tilt.

She dives in three days. For three days, Hythe builds rafts from its houses,
lashes the fishing boats together, and puts to sea. Then roll 2d6 and add:
+2 if the party has 10 renown with both sides; +2 if the party is on the
island and leads; +1 if Margery's boat is still there; +1 for Gale's Glass,
Pascoe's Compass, or Gale's chart.

| Total | The colony |
|---|---|
| 6 or less | About half is lost on the open sea. The rest reach home a month later, starving. |
| 7 to 9 | About one in four is lost. |
| 10 or more | About one in ten is lost. The fleet of Farholm sails into the harbor of Corrantey with the brass lamp lashed to its first mast. |

On the third day, the people in the boats see the island go down, the Light
and all, and a long dark shape go away under the water toward the deep. The
Wall is gone forever. The Western Sea is open, and the party opened it.

### If the boats did not get through

If she sleeps, the party lives on Farholm for a year. Tell it in a
paragraph: a winter of peat smoke, a spring of eggs on Gull Rock, a summer
of work on the boat. In the next Turning, the Light can open the lane again.
There is no Long Count, so the Rising does not go up on the fourth and
seventh days. Run Scene 3 again, quickly. In Corrantey, the party has been
dead for a year, and its families wear black.

If she wakes, the party sails home with the fleet of Farholm.

### When things go wrong

- **No clues.** The party does not know about the heart. It lights the Light
  as John Pascoe did, and keeps it burning. When the Rising reaches 4, Inga
  runs to the Well alone and climbs down, and Elspeth follows her. Roll for
  them. The party learns at dawn what they did.
- **The party wants to wake her,** and open the sea to the world. That is a
  real choice. Let the players make it with their eyes open. The Tey folk
  will fight it.
- **The party wants to take everybody.** It cannot, while the Wall stands.
  It can bring the news that brings ships. See "What they tell Corrantey".
- **A character dies.** In the lane, the dead go into the sea with the
  sailor's words. On Farholm, they lie on Founders' Hill among the first
  colonists, and both sides of the aisle come.

## Ending the Book

### Corrantey

> The coast comes up out of the morning: the brown hills, the smoke of the
> city, the long harbor wall with its light. A fishing boat sees you, and
> somebody in it stands up and shouts, and keeps shouting. By the time you
> round the harbor wall, the quay is black with people. A woman in a grey
> coat at the end of the wall lowers her spyglass.
>
> "What ship?" she calls.

She is **Ruth Polglase**, the harbor master. The colonists in the boat
stand up and look at the city that was two towns, and one of them calls
across the water: "Light to you!" A hundred people on the quay shout back
the only greeting they know: "Wake well!"

Within the hour, **Walter Isbister**, the chief owner of the *Good Intent*,
pushes through the crowd. He lost a ship, and the third article says that
the owners take half of whatever the voyage found. Let him work out, slowly,
what half of a lost colony is. Then let him hear that three families on the
Tey bank of Hythe carry his name, and watch his face. He pays for the first
ship west himself.

### What they tell Corrantey

This is the last choice of the book. The council meets on the bridge, and
the party speaks. Ask the players what they tell.

| They tell | What Corrantey does |
|---|---|
| **The whole truth** | The council sends two ships the next autumn, to wait outside the Wall for the Light to burn in the Turning. The old families of Corrin and Tey pay for it together. But some whalers on the docks hear "a creature as big as an island", and sharpen their harpoons. |
| **The colony, but not the Grandam** | Ships go, and they do not know what they sail into. The first one that comes near the Wall goes in with the wind, unless the Light burns. Only the party knows why. |
| **Nothing, or that there is nothing out there** | The city believes it, because it wants to. Nobody sails west, and the colony waits for ships that never come. The party carries the secret for the rest of its life. |
| **That the Wall is gone** (if she woke) | Nobody believes it until the second ship comes back. Then everybody sails west. |

Whatever the party says, the theaters have a new play within a month. It is
mostly wrong. The colonists go to see it, and laugh, and cry on the way
home.

### Where everyone ends up

| Who | What happens |
|---|---|
| Absalom Pascoe | He keeps the Light, every Turning, for as long as he lives, or he is the last man to leave Farholm. Either way, he sends his cutlass to the party. |
| Elspeth Flett | If she went down the Well, she is never afraid of the Grandam again, and the Well Watch puts away its spears. |
| Inga Halcrow | On Farholm, she dreams quiet dreams of the deep. In Corrantey, she learns to read, and writes the first book of Farholm. |
| Kit Hocking | In Corrantey, he is second mate of the first ship west. On Farholm, he keeps the Light with Absalom, and he is very careful with fire. |
| Margery Nance | She builds ships in the Corrin half of Corrantey for the rest of her life, each with a deep keel and a short mast. |
| The crew who lived | Hannah Rowse becomes a master. Jory Kitto ties a knot in the bell rope of the harbor church for each person who did not come home. Nell Jago goes to sea again at thirteen. |
| The Grandam | She sleeps, or she has gone into the deep. She never knew that anybody was there. |

### The last words

Ask each player one question, and give Heroic Inspiration for the best
answer:

- "Whoever signs the articles comes home with the ship, or the ship does not
  come home. Did you keep the last article?"
- "Ten years from now, a child in Corrantey asks what is out past the
  Western Sea. What do you say?"

Then read this.

> Somewhere far out in the Western Sea, the night is quiet. If you could
> stand on the dark water and listen, you might hear it: very deep and very
> slow, one beat, and a long time later, another.
>
> The ships of Corrantey go a little farther out every year. One day,
> somebody will go and look.

The book ends here. A group that wants more can start Waking with new
characters, in the same year, in the same world. The news that the party
brought home is a rumor on the docks of Corrantey.
