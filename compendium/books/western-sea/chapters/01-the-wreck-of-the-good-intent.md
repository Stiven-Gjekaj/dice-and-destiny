# The Wreck of the *Good Intent*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Levels** | 1, and 2 at the end |
| **Time at the table** | 1 to 2 sessions (about 5 hours) |
| **Places** | The Western Sea, the Wall, the reef, Wreckers' Bay, the wreck of the *Good Intent*, the south shore, the Saltpans |
| **Rules that start** | The six-sided rules, the Storm Cube, Injuries and Scars, and at the end, Factions and renown |
| **People** | The crew of the *Good Intent*: Elias Trewin, Hannah Rowse, Jory Kitto, Simeon Tregear, Nell Jago. Amos Tulloch, the salt boiler. |

Forty days out from Corrantey, the *Good Intent* sails into a storm that
stands on the sea like a cliff. By morning she is on a reef, the master is
gone, and the survivors are on a grey beach that no chart shows. They save
what they can from the wreck, spend a strange night on ground that seems to
breathe, and walk east along the shore. At the Saltpans, an old man in a
coat three hundred years out of fashion looks up from his fire and says,
"Light to you."

This chapter has three jobs:

1. **Teach the six-sided rules in play.** The first check, the first
   attack, the first critical hit, and the first table each come with a
   short box that you can read aloud. A group with normal dice skips the
   six-sided part of each box.
2. **Put the Storm Cube on the table.** The cube starts on 2. This chapter
   shows the players how it works, gives them the first chance to double,
   and teaches storm signs. The GM does not turn the cube in this chapter.
3. **Show the island.** Every scene has one strange thing in it: old coins
   in the sand, lice that should not exist, warm turf, a ship on a hill.
   Do not explain them. Let the players wonder.

## Running This Chapter

### What happens

1. **Forty days out (Scene 1).** The last calm evening. Meet the crew. The
   cube goes on the table.
2. **The Wall (Scene 2).** The storm takes the ship. Three great waves,
   and the first chance to double.
3. **The reef (Scene 3).** The ship strikes, and the master stays at the
   helm. Getting ashore. Injuries and Scars starts.
4. **Wreckers' Bay (Scene 4).** Dawn on the beach. The ship's girl is
   missing. The first fight, with the sea lice of Farholm.
5. **The wreck at low tide (Scene 5).** Two hours to save the stores.
   Something lives in the hold.
6. **The first night (Scene 6).** A fire on the bare ground makes the
   ground shiver and brings the lice. The first storm sign.
7. **The shore path (Scene 7).** The party walks east and finds a path
   that people made.
8. **The Saltpans (Scene 8).** Amos Tulloch offers to take the party to
   Hythe. The characters reach level 2.

The whole chapter happens on the south coast. The gazetteer has the map
of the island and an entry for each place.

### The tone

This is a sea story. Start it with weather, fear, and noise, then let it
go quiet and strange. Give the crew small jokes: sailors make jokes when
things are worst. The characters think that they found an empty island.
Let them. Scene 7 shows them a path, and Scene 8 shows them who made it.

### The crew of the *Good Intent*

The player's guide tells the players who is aboard, but not the names.
Give the names in Scene 1.

| Name | Who | In this chapter |
|---|---|---|
| **Elias Trewin** | The master. About fifty, quiet, grey-bearded, never without his spyglass. | He stays at the helm when she strikes, so the boat can get away. The sea takes him. |
| **Hannah Rowse** | The mate. About thirty-five, loud, fair, and always awake. | She breaks a leg in the wreck. She leads the survivors from a stretcher, loudly. |
| **Jory Kitto** | The bosun. About forty-five, small and very strong, with a knot for every job. | He gets the ship's boat away. He goes back to the wreck with the party. |
| **Simeon Tregear** | The cook and the surgeon. About sixty, round, slow, and calm. | He treats the hurt, and he wants his galley stove back. |
| **Nell Jago** | The ship's girl. Twelve, quick, and sure that she knows best. | The sea puts her ashore alone. She finds the Swallow's Bell. |

The crew also has two ship's boys and about a dozen sailors of the Corrin
half. Name them at the table, with names such as Penrose, Moyle, or Clemo.

> **GM tip:** Children do not die in this book. The ship's boys and Nell
> live through the wreck, whatever happens.

## Scene 1: Forty Days Out (10 minutes)

**Goal:** Start the campaign. Meet the crew. Put the Storm Cube on the
table.

> Forty days out of Corrantey, and still no land.
>
> The sea has been kind to you. Every evening the sun goes down ahead of
> the bowsprit, red and huge, and every evening the master stands at the
> rail with his spyglass and looks at the empty edge of the world until it
> is dark. The chart in his cabin has forty days of careful lines on it,
> and nothing at the end of them.
>
> Tonight the air is very still. The sails hang. The cook says that his
> knees hurt, and that means weather. The mate says that the cook's knees
> always hurt. Then the ship's girl, up in the rigging, calls down that
> the sky to the west has gone black.

Let each player say where the character is on this calm evening. Then
introduce the crew from the table above, one line each. Elias Trewin should say something quiet
and good. The players will remember it in Scene 3.

### The cube goes on the table

Put the doubling cube in the middle of the table, with the 2 on top. Do
not explain it yet. Let the players look at it. When a player asks, read
the box.

> **Teach: the Storm Cube.** "This is the Storm Cube. It shows how near
> the storm is. It starts on 2. When time runs short, or when the weather
> turns, I turn it up one face: 4, 8, 16, 32, 64. It never goes down. At
> 64, the storm season comes, and the last part of the campaign starts,
> ready or not. It stays here where everybody can see it, all the time."
>
> With normal dice, use a d6 with the 1 on top. Each face stands for one
> face of the cube: 1 is 2, and 6 is 64.

The players do not know what the storm season is. They know that the
cube is the clock. That is enough.

## Scene 2: The Wall (40 minutes)

**Goal:** The storm. Teach the six-sided roll. Give the first chance to
double.

> It comes faster than anything that you have ever seen. The black in the
> west is a wall: from the sea to the top of the sky, from one edge of the
> world to the other, standing on the water like a cliff. Lightning runs
> along the face of it, and the sound comes a long time after.
>
> The master gives the order to turn. The ship turns. The wind does not
> care. It blows toward the black from behind you, from the north, from
> the south, from every side at once, and it pushes the *Good Intent* in.
>
> Then the first wave hits, and the world is water.

This is the Wall, the ring of storms around Farholm. No ship that comes
near it can turn away.

### Three waves

In each of three waves, each character takes one job on deck and makes
its check. A character can change jobs between waves.

| Job | What you do | Check |
|---|---|---|
| **The helm** | Help the master hold the wheel, so she meets each wave bow first. | DC 12 Strength (Athletics) |
| **The rigging** | Climb up and cut away the broken topmast before it pulls her over. | DC 12 Dexterity (Acrobatics) |
| **The pumps** | Work the pump below, in the dark, with the water at your knees. | DC 11 Constitution check |
| **The hatch** | Keep the frightened crew at work, and the passengers below. | DC 11 Charisma (Persuasion) or Wisdom (Insight) |
| **The lookout** | Watch the black water ahead for rocks and for the worst waves. | DC 12 Wisdom (Perception) |
| **The boat** | Lash the ship's boat to the deck, so the sea cannot take it. | DC 11 Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) |

A spell that fits a job, such as Thunderwave on the broken rigging, is a
success with no roll, for one wave.

Keep a count of the **successes** of the whole party over the three waves.

**On a failed check,** the character takes 2 (1d4) Bludgeoning damage from
the sea, the deck, or a falling block. This scene cannot kill a character:
a character who drops to 0 Hit Points here is Stable, and the sailors drag
the character to the boat.

> **Teach: the six-sided roll.** Read this at the first check of the
> campaign.
>
> "Each time the rules say to roll the twenty-sided die, for an attack,
> an ability check, or a saving throw, you roll three of the six-sided
> dice instead, and add them up. Add the same bonuses as always. The
> average is the same as the big die, so the DCs and the Armor Classes
> do not change."
>
> With normal dice, roll the twenty-sided die as always.

> **Teach: Advantage with six-sided dice.** Read this the first time that
> a character has Advantage or Disadvantage, for example a character who
> helps another at the helm with the Help action.
>
> "For Advantage, roll all four dice and keep the three highest. For
> Disadvantage, roll all four and keep the three lowest."

### Between the waves

Between the waves, give the players these moments:

- **The second wave.** The ship's girl, Nell Jago, is still in the
  rigging, frozen. She will not come down for anybody but a character whom
  she trusts. A character who climbs to her (DC 11 Strength (Athletics))
  brings her down.
- **The third wave.** Elias Trewin lashes himself to the wheel. "She'll
  go where the sea wants her," he says. "Get the boat ready. All of you."
  He does not argue about it.

### The first double

Wait for a character to fail an important check, most likely in the
second wave. Then stop, pick up the cube, and read the box.

> **Teach: to double.** "Here's the other thing about the cube. Any of
> you can double. You turn the cube up one face yourself, right now, and
> you get a big benefit. The storm comes nearer for everyone, and the
> whole table sees it. Each of you can double once in each session. The
> table can double only once in each scene.
>
> "If you double now, the most useful benefit is a second chance: you roll
> that check again and use the new roll. Or you can choose another benefit
> from the list, or roll 2d6 for one and get Heroic Inspiration with it."
>
> The table of benefits is in "To double" in How to Run This Book.

Do not push. Most players say no, at first.

> **The first turn.** If a player doubles, this is the first turn of the
> cube in the campaign. Let the player turn it by hand, from 2 to 4. Then
> describe it: "The wind screams. Far behind you, the face of the Wall
> lights up from end to end, and for one moment you see the whole of it,
> miles high, going round in a ring. Then it is dark, and you are inside."
> Then give the benefit at once.
>
> If nobody doubles, the cube stays on 2. The first turn by the GM comes in
> the island chapter.

### The count

After the third wave, count the successes. The count decides how many of
the crew live, and what the boat saves. Compare it with the number of
characters:

| Successes | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Twice the number of characters, or more** | The boat gets away full and dry. Fourteen of the crew live. The party keeps all its gear. |
| **The number of characters, or more** | The boat gets away low in the water. Eleven of the crew live. Each character loses one item of gear to the sea: the player chooses, but not a weapon or a spellcasting focus. |
| **Fewer** | The boat gets away half swamped. Eight of the crew live. Each character loses one item, as above, and the GM chooses one more heavy item of the party: armor in its oiled cloth, a chest, or a pack. |

Those who live always include Hannah Rowse, Jory Kitto, Simeon Tregear,
Nell Jago, and the two ship's boys. Elias Trewin is always lost. The party
can find lost gear in the wreck in Scene 5, and a double can bring one
piece back ("The sea gives back").

## Scene 3: The Reef (20 minutes)

**Goal:** The wreck. Get the characters ashore. Teach injuries.

> You hear the reef before you see it: a long roar ahead, under the
> wind, and a line of white in the dark.
>
> The master does not turn. He could not if he tried. "Boat away!" he
> shouts, and his voice comes from far off. "Boat away, and pull for the
> shore, and don't look back!"
>
> The *Good Intent* lifts on the last wave, higher than seems possible,
> and comes down on the rock. The sound goes through your feet and your
> teeth. The mainmast goes over the side like a felled tree. Then she is
> breaking under you, and the sea is on the deck, and you have to choose.

### Getting ashore

Each character chooses one way to the shore. Each way has three checks:
the reef, the surf, and the beach.

| Way | The three checks | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **The boat** | DC 10 Strength (Athletics) to pull, three times | Jory Kitto steers. The boat holds the hurt, the boys, and the mate. Room for two characters. |
| **The surf** | DC 12 Strength (Athletics) to swim, three times | A character in Medium or Heavy armor has Disadvantage, or can let the armor go. |
| **A spar** | DC 11 Constitution check to hold on, three times | Each character on a spar can also hold one sailor or one item of the party. |

**On a failed check,** the character takes 3 (1d6) Bludgeoning damage
from the rocks. As in Scene 2, a character who drops to 0 Hit Points here
is Stable, and the sea puts the character on the beach.

**On two or more failed checks,** the reef cuts deep. The character gets
an injury, even with Hit Points left. Use the box below the first time.

A spell or a good idea, such as a rope from the boat, gives Advantage on
the next check or replaces it.

> **Teach: injuries.** "The reef got you. You can get an injury now. First
> you choose. Take the injury, and roll on the Injury table. Or shake it
> off: you gain one level of Exhaustion instead, and no injury."
>
> If the player takes the injury: "Roll three dice and read the injury from
> the table. Add 5 if you dropped to 0 Hit Points." With normal dice, roll
> the twenty-sided die, as the table says. The Injuries and Scars chapter
> of the Game Master's Compendium has the table.
>
> "Each injury says what it does, and how it heals. Healing magic gives you
> Hit Points back, but it does not fix the injury. The cook can tend it.
> A Minor injury heals after a Long Rest."

> **GM tip:** Do not show Elias Trewin's death. The last that anyone sees
> of him is a lantern on the deck, at the helm, as the ship breaks up. Let
> a character who looks back see the lantern go out.

The scene ends in the dark, in the surf. Then cut to the morning.

## Scene 4: Wreckers' Bay (50 minutes)

**Goal:** The first look at Farholm. Count the survivors. The first fight.

> You wake on sand. It is grey and cold, and it is in everything.
>
> The storm is gone. The sky is low and pale, the color of pewter, and
> the sea is flat and grey and tired. A quarter of a mile out, the *Good
> Intent* lies on the reef, broken behind the mainmast, her stern under
> the water. Gulls stand on her rail.
>
> The beach goes on for miles in a long curve. Behind it are low dunes,
> and behind the dunes, green turf that rises to grey hills. There is no
> smoke, no house, no field, no road. There is nothing at all. Then you see
> the others, here and there along the sand, getting up one at a time.

The survivors gather at the boat, in the middle of the bay.

### Counting heads

- **Hannah Rowse** has a broken leg, splinted with an oar. She takes
  command, as the mate must. "The master's gone. That makes it me. Light a
  fire, find water, count heads, and don't any of you dare cry until we
  have done all three."
- **Simeon Tregear** treats the hurt with what is in his bag. He can tend
  one injury for each character today, as a Healer's Kit does. He can
  also stabilize a character at 0 Hit Points.
- **Jory Kitto** counts heads. The number depends on Scene 2. He counts
  three times. Each time, one is missing: Nell Jago, the ship's girl.
- **The figurehead.** The Lady of the *Good Intent* lies on the sand at
  the edge of the water, on her back, still holding up her carved lamp.
  The sailors will not leave her there. They carry her above the tide
  line and stand her up, facing the sea. Nobody tells them to.

> **GM tip:** If the sea brings a dead sailor in, the crew buries the
> sailor in the dunes, and Hannah says the words. Keep it short and quiet,
> or fade to the next scene.

### Nell is missing

A character who looks along the beach (DC 10 Wisdom (Perception)) sees a
small figure on a rock at the far west end of the bay, about a mile away.
She waves both arms. She does not come down.

When the party gets near, it sees why. The sand around her rock is moving.

> Nell is on top of a rock the size of a cart, with something green
> clutched to her chest. She is shouting at you: "Don't walk on the flat
> stones! The flat stones are alive!"
>
> At first you see nothing. Then one of the flat grey stones on the sand
> lifts up on far too many legs, and turns toward you.

### The fight

{{statblock: Giant Sea Louse}}

Four **Giant Sea Lice** lie buried in the wet sand around Nell's rock.
They came out after the storm to feed on what the sea brought. They do
not climb well, so Nell is safe on top of the rock, but she cannot come
down.

| Party size | Monsters | XP |
|---|---|---|
| 4 characters | 4 Giant Sea Lice | 200 |

> **Scaling:** This is a Low fight for level 1 (50 XP for each character).
> It is the first fight of the campaign, so it is easy on purpose.
>
> - **3 characters (150 XP):** 3 Giant Sea Lice (150 XP).
> - **4 characters (200 XP):** as in the table (200 XP).
> - **5 characters (250 XP):** 5 Giant Sea Lice (250 XP).
> - **6 characters (300 XP):** 6 Giant Sea Lice (300 XP).

### Running the fight

- **The lice are hidden.** Each louse stays buried until a creature comes
  within 10 feet of it (see Under the Sand). Nell's warning gives
  Advantage on the check to see them.
- **The lice want food, not a fight.** A louse that drops below half its
  Hit Points burrows into the sand and swims away into the sea. A louse
  that grapples a character tries to drag it toward the water.
- **The tide.** The sea is 30 feet away. A louse that reaches the water
  with a grappled character pulls it under the waist-deep water until the
  grapple ends.

> **Teach: the bracket.** Read this at the first damage roll of a louse.
>
> "Every roll in this book shows the normal dice first, and the six-sided
> form after it in brackets. A claw that deals 1d8 shows d6 + 1 in the
> bracket. I roll the bracket. You change the dice on your sheet in the
> same way, with the cheat sheet in the player's guide."
>
> With normal dice, read the first form and ignore the bracket.

> **Teach: critical hits with six-sided dice.** Read this at the first
> critical hit, or at the end of the fight if nobody rolls one.
>
> "A natural 16, 17, or 18 on the three dice is a critical hit. That is
> almost the same chance as a natural 20 on the big die. A natural 5 or
> less counts as a natural 1, for the rules that use one. For a critical
> hit, double the dice of the damage first, then change them."

### Nell's bell

Nell holds a small bronze bell, green with age, with no clapper. She dug
it out of the sand. "It rang," she says. "In the dark, after the boat
went over. I heard it ringing. That's how I found the shore." She does
not want to give it up. She gives it to a character whom she trusts, if
the character asks kindly, or after the character saves her.

{{treasure: The Swallow's Bell}}

A character who makes a DC 12 Intelligence (History) check, or who chose
the hook "the lost name", knows that the *Swallow* was one of the eleven
ships of the lost fleet.

### The sand gives

Wreckers' Bay is full of old things. A character who spends an hour
searching the sand finds one thing. Roll on the table.

> **Teach: the d66 table.** "This book has no table for the big die. Its
> tables use two six-sided dice, added up, or d66. For d66, roll one die
> for the tens and one for the units, so a 3 and a 5 is 35."
>
> This table uses 2d6, added up.

| 2d6 | The sand gives |
|---|---|
| 2 to 3 | A green copper coin with the head of a king. The words around it say EDWY REX. No character knows the name. |
| 4 to 5 | A ship's nail of copper, as long as a hand. It is not from the *Good Intent*. Her nails are iron. |
| 6 to 7 | A piece of the party's own gear, lost in the wreck, or something of the ship worth 5 GP. |
| 8 to 9 | Driftwood with letters carved in it: *DOVE*. |
| 10 to 11 | The tracks of bare feet in the sand, a few days old, going east. Somebody else walks on this beach. |
| 12 | A gold coin with a sun on one side and the same king on the other. Worth 10 GP in Corrantey, or a fortune to the right person on this island. |

If nobody finds the tracks today, the party finds them in Scene 7.

## Scene 5: The Wreck at Low Tide (60 minutes)

**Goal:** The salvage. A fight in the dark. Make the gear of the ship
matter.

At midday, the tide goes out, and the reef stands out of the water. "Two
hours," says Jory Kitto. "Then she floods, and after the next blow she's
matchwood." Hannah Rowse sends the party, with Jory and two sailors to
carry.

> **GM tip:** This is the one chance to save the stores of the ship. In
> Hythe, iron, glass, flour, and paper are treasure, and a box of nails
> can buy a house. Do not say so. Let the players choose for their own
> reasons, and let Hythe surprise them.

### Getting out to her

The party rows or wades along the reef. Each character makes a DC 10
Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check. On a failure, the
character slips on the weed and takes 2 (1d4) Bludgeoning damage.

### The tide clock

The party has **six turns** on the wreck. A turn is about 20 minutes.
Each turn, each character can search one part of the ship, or carry one
load to the boat. Tell the players how many turns are left at the start of
each turn. Jory calls it out: "Four more turns, and I mean it."

At the end of the sixth turn, the sea comes back. A character still in the
hold must swim out (DC 12 Strength (Athletics)), or take 3 (1d6)
Bludgeoning damage and lose the load that the character carries.

### The parts of the ship

- **The deck.** Broken, steep, and slick. The mainmast is gone. The wheel
  is still lashed, and the lashing is cut, but nobody is there. Near the
  wheel lies the master's lantern, out, with its glass whole. A character
  who searches finds a coil of good rope and a box of tools of the ship's
  carpenter: one load each.
- **The master's cabin.** Aft, half under water. The master's sea chest is
  here, and his writing desk. The desk holds the log of the voyage, the
  chart with forty days of lines on it, and his spyglass in its case.

  {{treasure: Trewin's Glass}}

  The chest holds the money of the voyage, 40 GP and 120 SP, for the
  owners. Hannah Rowse wants it kept safe for them, as the articles say.
- **The galley.** Forward, still dry. Simeon Tregear's iron stove is
  bolted to the deck: a box of iron with a door, small enough for two
  people to carry. It takes one turn to unbolt (DC 11 Strength check, or a
  set of tools) and one load to carry. The cook's medicine chest is here
  too, with two Potions of Healing in it.
- **The hold.** Below the deck, dark, and flooded to the chest. This is
  where the stores are, and where the conger lives. See below.

### What is in the hold

Each load from the hold is one of these. The party can find each one
once.

| Load | What it is |
|---|---|
| Flour | Two sacks of white flour, wet only on the outside. |
| Iron | A cask of iron nails, and a box of iron fittings. |
| Glass | A crate of trade glass: bottles, small mirrors, and lenses, packed in straw. Half is broken. |
| Cloth | A bale of red and blue wool cloth, for trade. |
| Paper | A sealed box of paper, ink, and pens, for the chart and the log. |
| Food | A cask of salt pork and a sack of biscuit. Enough to feed the survivors for a week. |
| Your gear | One item that a character lost in Scene 2. |
| Oil | Two small casks of lamp oil, from the Lampwrights' Guild. |

### The conger

{{statblock: Giant Conger}}

A **Giant Conger** lives in the hold now. The storm brought it in, and it
has found the best home it has ever had: a dark, flooded room full of
food. A **Giant Sea Louse** (its statblock is in Scene 4) came in with it,
and it eats what the conger leaves.

The first character who goes into the hold and touches the water feels
something big move past its legs. The conger attacks on the second turn
in the hold, or at once if a character goes under the water.

| Party size | Monsters | XP |
|---|---|---|
| 4 characters | 1 Giant Conger, 1 Giant Sea Louse | 250 |

> **Scaling:** This fight is between Low and Moderate for level 1 (50 to
> 75 XP for each character). The tide and the dark make it harder.
>
> - **3 characters (150 to 225 XP):** 1 Giant Conger (200 XP).
> - **4 characters (200 to 300 XP):** as in the table (250 XP).
> - **5 characters (250 to 375 XP):** 1 Giant Conger, 2 Giant Sea Lice
>   (300 XP).
> - **6 characters (300 to 450 XP):** 2 Giant Congers (400 XP). The second
>   lives in the galley bilge, and it comes up through the floor.

### Running the fight

- **The hold is dark.** A character with no light or Darkvision fights
  blind. The hatch lets in Dim Light for 10 feet below it. The master's
  lantern works, if a character found it and has oil.
- **The water** is 4 feet deep, and 8 feet deep at the stern. It is
  Difficult Terrain for all who walk. A Small character swims.
- **The conger hides** in a gap in the hull, with Half Cover, and drags
  its prey under (see Suffocation in the Player's Compendium). It does not
  chase. Below half its Hit Points, it leaves through the hull for good.
- **The louse** guards the food. It attacks a character who takes a load
  from the hold.

A clever party can fish for the conger with salt pork on a hook, block
its hole, or simply work fast and leave it alone.

### Back on the beach

Hannah writes each load in the back of the master's log: "Saved from the
*Good Intent*, the first day." Keep the list for the island chapter.

From the deck of the wreck, a character who uses Trewin's Glass, or makes
a DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) check, sees something far inland. On the grey
ridges in the middle of the island, six or seven miles away, there is a
shape like a ship. It looks very small and very high, and it cannot be
there. That is the wreck of the *Morning Star*. Nobody can say what it
is. Do not tell them.

## Scene 6: The First Night (40 minutes)

**Goal:** The ground of Farholm. Teach storm signs. A fight that the
party can avoid.

The survivors make camp in a hollow behind the dunes, out of the wind,
where the sand turns to turf. The sailors cut turf for a windbreak and
drag driftwood up for a fire.

> The light goes slowly. The sea goes from grey to black, and the sky
> after it, and there are more stars than you have ever seen, because
> there is no other light anywhere: no farm, no lamp, no town. Only your
> fire, and the sea, and the island at your back, very quiet.
>
> The sailors sing, a little, to keep warm. Hannah Rowse makes them sing
> the songs of the coast and not the songs of the sea. Nobody says why.

### Storm signs

The party spends the night in the open. Read the box when the party
settles down for a Long Rest.

> **Teach: storm signs.** "Small things bring the storm nearer too, but
> not a whole face at a time. For these, I put a storm sign next to the
> cube." Put one checker from the backgammon board next to the cube. "At
> the third sign, I take the three away and turn the cube up one face. A
> Long Rest in the open is a storm sign. A rest in a safe place, with a
> roof and walls, is not."
>
> With normal dice, use a coin or a tally mark for each sign.

Give the sign at dawn, when the wind goes round to the west.

### The fire

How the camp makes its fire matters, and nobody knows it yet.

**If the fire burns on the bare ground,** on the sand or on the turf:
after an hour, the ground under the camp shivers. Very slightly. A
character who is lying down feels it. A character who makes a DC 12
Wisdom (Perception) check while lying still feels more: the ground rises,
very slowly, and falls again. It feels like the chest of a sleeping
animal.

Then the lice come.

**If the fire burns in iron,** in Simeon Tregear's galley stove from the
wreck, or on a full layer of flat stones, nothing happens. "Proper food,"
says the cook, "on a proper fire." Nobody ever knows what the stove saved
them from.

### The lice come

{{statblock: Swarm of Sea Lice}}

An hour after the ground shivers, the sand around the fire starts to move.
**Swarms of Sea Lice** boil up out of it, drawn by the heat. In some
parties, a **Giant Sea Louse** (Scene 4) comes behind them.

| Party size | Monsters | XP |
|---|---|---|
| 4 characters | 2 Swarms of Sea Lice | 200 |

> **Scaling:** This is a Low fight for level 1 (50 XP for each character).
>
> - **3 characters (150 XP):** 1 Swarm of Sea Lice, 1 Giant Sea Louse
>   (150 XP).
> - **4 characters (200 XP):** as in the table (200 XP).
> - **5 characters (250 XP):** 2 Swarms of Sea Lice, 1 Giant Sea Louse
>   (250 XP).
> - **6 characters (300 XP):** 3 Swarms of Sea Lice (300 XP).

### Running the fight

- **The fire is the target.** The lice go for the creatures nearest the
  fire. The sailors scatter.
- **Put out the fire.** A character can put out the fire with an action:
  sand on it, water on it, or a good kick. When the fire goes out, the
  swarms stop and burrow back into the sand at the start of their next
  turn. The giant louse keeps fighting for one more round, then follows
  them. A character who makes a DC 12 Intelligence (Nature) check sees
  that the lice go for the heat, not the people.

### After the fight

If the party relights a fire on the bare ground, the ground shivers again
an hour later, and the lice come back. If the party keeps a fire burning
on the bare ground all night, give a **second storm sign** at dawn. The
camp wakes up to a sky that looks like a bruise.

Do not explain any of this. The sailors have theories: the lice like the
light, the island is cursed. Jory Kitto says that he has heard of whales
that sleep on the surface so long that birds land on them. The others
laugh at him. Let the players hear that, and say nothing.

> **GM tip:** This chapter gives at most two storm signs. The party
> carries them into the island chapter.

## Scene 7: The Shore Path (30 minutes)

**Goal:** Leave the beach. Find the first sign of people. Get to the
Saltpans.

In the morning, Hannah Rowse points east. "Smoke," she says. "Thin and
low. Fires don't light themselves. Take Jory. Go and see." If the party
wants to take everybody, Hannah agrees, and the sailors carry her and the
stores.

### The path

At the east end of the bay, the party finds a path that people made,
with flat stones across the wet places. At a cairn, somebody carved a
ship on one stone and a fish on another. Somebody else scratched out the
ship.


### On the way

The walk is four miles and takes the morning. Do not use the Journeys
rules yet. Choose one or two of these moments.

- **The rocky point.** The tide is coming in around a point of black rock.
  Wait an hour, or cross now: each character makes a DC 11 Strength
  (Athletics) or Wisdom (Survival) check. On a failure, the character is
  soaked, and one load of the stores is lost.
- **The warm stream.** A stream as warm as a bath crosses the path. It
  smells faintly of eggs. Far inland, a thin steam rises.
- **The gulls.** Thousands of gulls rise from the shore at once and fly
  out to sea, then come back. The Swallow's Bell hums, very softly.
- **The marker.** A post of black ship's timber on a low hill. Words are
  cut deep in it: *HERE THE PATIENCE CAME ASHORE. LIGHT TO HER.*

### The Saltpans

At the end of the morning, the party sees the Saltpans: a mile of square
pools with low walls of clay, shining like a broken mirror. At the far
end, smoke rises from a stone hut.

## Scene 8: The Saltpans (30 minutes)

**Goal:** The first colonist. The first hint of the colony and its two
sides. End the chapter.

> The man at the hut is old, bent, and brown as leather, in a long coat
> of patched brown wool and breeches that end at the knee. He stirs a
> great iron pan over a peat fire, and he is talking to somebody. There is
> nobody there.
>
> "...and I told him, I said, there's no weather in it, not this side of
> the Turning. You'd have laughed. You would." He looks up. He sees you.
> The wooden paddle drops into the brine.
>
> For a long moment, he does not move. Then he takes off his hat and
> holds it in both hands. "Light to you, strangers," he says. His voice
> shakes. "Dark take me. Where did you come from?"

This is **Amos Tulloch**, the salt boiler (see the gazetteer). He talks
to his dead wife out loud, from habit, and he is not mad.

"Light to you" is the greeting of old Ostrey. The answer is "And dawn to
you". A character who makes a DC 12 Intelligence (History) check knows it
from old plays. If a character says "Wake well", Amos looks puzzled.
"Wake who?" To him, the party talks fast and rough. To them, he talks
like a play about kings.

### What Amos asks and tells

Amos asks a hundred questions at once. "You came through the Wall? Which
town sent you, Corrin or Tey? Does King Edwy still reign? Have you iron?"
He does not mind which town, but everybody in Hythe will.

Over laver bread and heather ale, Amos explains, without taking a side:

- **The island is Farholm.** His people came here on the fleet of Corrin
  and Tey, "three hundred and sixty years ago, this summer". The Wall took
  them, as it took the party. Nobody has ever left.
- **Hythe is the town,** four miles east, about four hundred people.
- **The town has two sides.** "The Corrin men, on the east bank, want to
  go home. They keep the old lighthouse of the fleet, and they dream of
  lighting it. The Tey folk, on the west bank, say that if you light it,
  or leave, the Grandam wakes and we all go down." "I was born a Tey man. I married
  a Corrin girl. So now I boil salt."
- **The Turning.** "The storm season, when the Wall comes in over the
  island. It is early this year." If the players show him the cube, he
  nods. "Aye. That's about right."

Amos does not know what the Grandam is. "I believe what I see," he says.
"I've never seen her. But I've felt the ground move, same as everybody."

If the party tells him about the fire and the lice, he goes pale and
then laughs. "On the bare ground? The Well Keeper would have a fit. Put
it on a stone, friends. Always on a stone." He does not know why. It is
the custom.

> **Teach: Factions and renown.** "On this island, there are two sides:
> the Corrin men and the Tey folk. Each of them is a faction. Each of you
> has a renown score with each faction. Both start at 0. Deeds for a side raise your renown with
> it. Deeds against it lower it. At 3, the side starts to trust you and to
> help you. The two sides are rivals. Sooner or later, they will each ask
> you to choose."
>
> Give each player a small card, or a corner of the character sheet, for
> the two scores. The island chapter tells you how they change.

### The offer

> Amos puts on his hat. "Well," he says, to nobody, or to his wife. "Well,
> my love. Here's a thing." Then he turns to you. "You'll want Hythe. The
> Moot will want you, and the Captain of the Light will want you, and the
> Well Keeper will want you more than the both of them. I'll take you.
> It's four miles, and the light's good, and I want to see their faces."
>
> He picks up his stick. "Only, when they ask you which town sent you,"
> he says, "think about your answer. Think hard."

Amos sends to Hythe for a cart for the survivors. The chapter ends here. The island chapter starts on the track to Hythe,
with Amos Tulloch, in the afternoon.
