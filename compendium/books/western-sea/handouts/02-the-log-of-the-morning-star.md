# The Log of the *Morning Star*

*The last entries of the log of the flagship of the fleet, open on the
table of the great cabin, high on the Ribs. The hand is Captain Humphrey
Gale's. The pages are dry and stiff, and the ink is still black.*

---

> **The thirty-ninth day.** Wind light and foul, from the west. The birds still fly
> west ahead of us at dawn. Magnus Isbister signals from the *Patience*
> that he will follow no Corrin man past the fortieth day. I signal back
> that he may go home alone, then.

> **The fortieth day.** The Wall came at us at noon, standing on the sea
> like a cliff, and the wind blew toward it from all sides, and I could
> not turn the fleet. We went in at dusk.
>
> I write what I saw, and the men who saw it with me will swear it. In the
> middle of the night the sea stood up under us. But it was not the sea.
> It was the ground, coming up out of the sea, under our keel, and it
> lifted us. I felt her take the ground, and the ground rose under her,
> higher and higher, and the sea ran off it on every side like water off
> a whale's back. When the morning came, we were here, and the sea was
> three miles off.
>
> I do not know what land rises out of the sea in the night. I pray God it
> does not go back down.

> **The forty-first day.** Sounded at noon, a cable's length from the
> shore. No bottom at a hundred fathoms. The *Patience* is ashore on the
> south coast, and Isbister lives. Of the others I have no word.

*After this page, the log is empty.*
