# The Landing Book

*Two pages from the first volume of the Landing Book of Hythe, kept by
Nathaniel Gale. The hand is Captain Humphrey Gale's, small and square. The
ink is brown with age, and the corners of the pages are soft from three
hundred years of fingers.*

---

> **Of the great Lens of the *Morning Star*, as it was made in Corrin**
>
> Two feet across, and four inches thick at the middle, and one inch at
> the rim. Cast in one piece, of clear glass of Corrin, in a mould of fine
> sand and clay. Ground after on a wheel with sand, and with finer sand,
> and with rouge, until a man may read a letter through it at ten paces
> and see the letter large.
>
> The curve is the same on both faces. Take a cord of five feet and three
> inches. Fix one end. Sweep the other end round on the sand of the mould,
> and that is the curve.
>
> It wants twenty pounds of glass, and a fire hot enough to make the glass
> run like honey. We have no such fire on this island, save at the hot
> springs, where the Oven is.

---

> **The first year of the Landing. The month of the long nights.**
>
> The men of the *Speedwell* came back from the hot springs today and say
> that the ground there rises and falls. I have not seen it. I set it down
> because they believe it.
>
> I set down this also, which I have seen. The Wall drew in when the
> ground breathed hard, and was quiet where the lanterns were.
>
> I do not know what it means. I pray that whoever reads this knows more
> than I.
>
> *H. Gale*
