# The Letter of the Moot

*A letter on the last good paper in Hythe, folded small and sealed with
the wax of the church, in a packet of oiled sealskin. On the outside, in
three different hands:* To the Reeve and Burgesses of Corrin. To the Reeve
and Burgesses of Tey. To whoever reads this.

---

> **From the Moot of Hythe, on the island of Farholm, beyond the Western
> Sea, in the Turning of the year three hundred and sixty of the Landing.**
>
> Light to you.
>
> We are the people of the fleet that sailed west in the reign of King
> Edwy, the six ships of Corrin and the five of Tey. We are alive. We are
> about four hundred souls. We keep the Morning Flame, and the old
> speech, and the old count of suns, stars, and bits. We have not
> forgotten you.
>
> The bearers of this letter came to us out of the Wall, and they will
> tell you more than we can write. Believe them.
>
> *Absalom Pascoe, Captain of the Light, for the Corrin men*
>
> We of Corrin ask you this. Send ships in the autumn, in the season of
> storms. Wait outside the Wall, to the east of the island. Watch for a
> light on the east point. When it burns, the way is open. Come quickly,
> and take us home.
>
> *Elspeth Flett, Well Keeper, for the Tey folk*
>
> We of Tey ask you this. Light no fire on our land, whatever you are
> told. Come quietly, if you come. We are not all of us sure that we want
> to leave, and we will not be hurried.
>
> *Gilbert Rundle, priest of the Morning Flame, for the church*
>
> I ask you this. Be kind to the bearers. They have had a hard year. And
> say a prayer for the people who keep the Light, and the people who keep
> the dark. We need both.
>
> And dawn to you.
