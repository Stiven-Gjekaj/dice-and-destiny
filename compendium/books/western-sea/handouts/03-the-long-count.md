# The Long Count

*A long strip of sealskin, rolled on a stick of driftwood, from the Net
Loft on the Tey bank. Along it run knots in a thin cord, one knot for each
Turning, three hundred and sixty of them. At the start of the strip, words
are burned into the skin. The Well Keeper keeps it, and the Well Keeper
shows it to nobody.*

---

> **The Count of the Well, as Agnes Flett set it down.**
>
> In the year that we came ashore, the Grandam rose under the ships, and
> set the great ship upon the hills, and lay down again.
>
> Tie a knot for every Turning. When she stirs, tie it tight. When she
> sleeps through, tie it loose.
>
> The old ones of the *Patience* say that she rose once in a long life of
> the world before us, and the ships of that time lie at the Boneyard.
> They say that she will rise again when the count is full.
>
> The count is full at the three hundred and sixtieth knot.
>
> Keep the dark. Keep the hymn. Keep the count. When it is full, may God
> and the Grandam forgive us all.

---

*The last knot on the cord is the three hundred and fifty-ninth. It is tied
very tight. There is room on the cord for one more.*
