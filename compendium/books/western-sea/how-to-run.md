# How to Run This Book

This book is a short campaign for Dice & Destiny. It takes a group of
characters from level 1 to level 5 in about eight to ten sessions. It has a
true end, and the group can reach it in a few months of play.

The characters sail west from Corrantey on the *Good Intent*, a merchant
ship. A storm wrecks her on Farholm, an island that is on no chart. A lost
colony lives there. The party must find a way home before the storm season
comes back, and the clock of the campaign sits on the table, where every
player can see it.

You can run the whole book with the dice of a backgammon set: four
six-sided dice and a doubling cube. You can also run it with normal dice.
Each rule in this book works for both.

This section is for the GM. Read it before you start. It tells you the full
story, with all of its secrets.

## The Story in Short

About three hundred and sixty years ago, before the war of the Unlit King,
the rival ports of Corrin and Tey did a thing that they never did before or
after: they worked together. They sent one fleet west, eleven ships and
about seven hundred people, to find land beyond the Western Sea. The
flagship was the *Morning Star*, of Corrin. No ship came back. Each town
said that the other town lost it.

The fleet did not sink. A wall of storms caught it and threw it onto
Farholm, and the people lived. Their children's children still live there
today, about four hundred people in the town of Hythe. They keep the
customs of old Ostrey. They say "Light to you", they pray to the Morning
Flame, and they count in suns, stars, and bits. They never heard of the
Unlit King, because they left before his war.

They also kept the old rivalry. **The Corrin men** want to go home. They
guard the ruins of the lighthouse of the fleet, **the Corrin Light**, and
they dream of lighting it. **The Tey folk** worship the heart of the island
as **the Grandam**. They believe that to leave, or to light the lighthouse,
wakes her and sinks everyone.

In year 300, the *Good Intent* comes through the storm. She is the first
ship from the land in three hundred and sixty years.

### The truth (for the GM only)

- **The island is alive.** Farholm is the back of a giant sea creature that
  sleeps. Her heart is deep under the island. The Tey folk call her the
  Grandam, and they are right that she is real. The island moves very
  slowly, which is why no chart shows it.
- **The wall of storms is her breath.** It is a ring of storms about twenty
  miles out. A ship that comes near the wall goes in with the wind and
  wrecks on the island. No ship has ever gone out through it.
- **Each storm season, she stirs.** The storms come in over the island. The
  Storm Cube tracks this. At 64, she starts to wake.
- **Neither side is fully right.** The Corrin Light can open a path through
  the storm, but only while she stirs. That is the one time when the party
  can leave, and also the time of most danger. The Corrin men are right
  that the Light can take people home. The Tey folk are right that it is
  dangerous to try.
- **The saint.** The church of the colony tells of a saint who lit a fire on
  the back of the Grandam, and the island moved. It is a true story, told
  wrong. Use it as a hint.

### The theme and the tone

The theme is **wonder, and the price of going home.** The characters find a
whole lost world in one small place: people who talk like an old book, a
ship on a hill miles from the sea, warm ground that seems to breathe. Then
they must decide what to take from it, what to leave, and what to tell.

This is not a dark campaign. It is a sea story. It has storms, wrecks, and
real danger, but it also has strange beauty, kind people, and jokes. A
good session of this book has one wonder, one argument between the two
sides of the colony, and one look at the cube.

## The Shape of the Campaign

The book has three parts. The start and the end are fixed. The middle is
open: the party explores the island in any order it likes.

| Part | Name | Levels | Sessions | How it runs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chapter 1 | The Wreck of the *Good Intent* | 1 to 2 | 1 to 2 | Linear. It teaches the island, the Storm Cube, and the six-sided rules. |
| Chapter 2, the outer ring | Farholm | 2 to 3 | 2 to 3 | Open. The coast, the colony, and the two sides. |
| Chapter 2, the inner ring | Farholm | 3 to 4 | 2 to 3 | Open. The interior, the ruins of the fleet, and the way to the heart. |
| Chapter 3 | Storm Season | 5 | 1 to 2 | Linear. It starts when the cube reaches 64. |

A session in this table is about four hours. The full book is about eight
to ten sessions.

### Chapter 1: the wreck and the shore

The *Good Intent* breaks on the reef off Wreckers' Bay. The characters come
ashore with what they can carry, find the other survivors, and learn the
first rules of the island. The chapter ends at the first meeting with the
colony. Run it in order. It is the tutorial of this book.

### Chapter 2: the island

The island has about ten sites in two rings.

- **The outer ring** is the coast: Hythe, the Corrin Light, Founders' Hill,
  the Saltpans, Gull Rock, and the Boneyard. Here the party meets the
  colony and the two sides. The sites are written for levels 2 to 3.
- **The inner ring** is the interior: the Kettles, the Ribs, the wreck of
  the *Morning Star*, the Saint's Hearth, and the Grandam's Well. Here the
  party finds the ruins of the fleet and the way to the heart. The sites are
  written for levels 3 to 4.

The party can go to the sites in any order. The parts of the Corrin Light,
the trust of the two sides, and the clues to the heart are in different
sites, so each order works. A party that goes inland early finds harder
fights. Let it. Tell the players that the interior is dangerous, through
the colonists, and let them choose.

Each site says which events turn the Storm Cube.

### Chapter 3: storm season

When the cube reaches 64, the storm season starts, and so does the last
chapter, wherever the party is. The party may not be ready. That is the
point. The party decides three things:

- who comes home with them,
- whether the heart wakes or sleeps again,
- what they tell Corrantey: the first true news from beyond the sea.

There is no single correct end. Chapter 3 gives you the result of each
choice. What the party found on the island shapes the choices it has.

## The Six-Sided Rules

Use this page if your group plays with a backgammon set. A group with
normal dice does not need it. The players' cheat sheet at the end of the
player's guide has the same rules.

### The roll of the twenty-sided die

For an **attack roll**, an **ability check**, or a **saving throw**, roll
**3d6** and add the same modifiers as always. The average of 3d6 is 10.5,
the same as the average of the twenty-sided die. Armor Class, DCs, and
modifiers do not change.

- **Advantage:** roll 4d6 and keep the 3 highest.
- **Disadvantage:** roll 4d6 and keep the 3 lowest.
- **A critical hit:** a natural 16 or more on the 3d6 (4.6 percent). The
  twenty-sided die gives 5 percent.
- **A natural 1:** for death saves and each other rule that uses a
  natural 1, a natural 5 or less on the 3d6 (4.6 percent).
- **A natural 20:** for death saves and each other rule that uses a
  natural 20, a natural 16 or more on the 3d6.

### The other dice

Each other die becomes a d6 and a bonus, with about the same average.

| Die | Six-sided form | Average |
|---|---|---|
| d4 | d6 minus 1, minimum 1 | about 2.5 |
| d6 | d6 | 3.5 |
| d8 | d6 + 1 | 4.5 |
| d10 | d6 + 2 | 5.5 |
| d12 | d6 + 3 | 6.5 |

- **More than one die:** change each die. Two d8s become 2d6 + 2. A d8 and
  a d4 become 2d6 with no bonus. For a d4, a d6 that shows 1 still counts
  as 1.
- **Hit Dice** change in the same way. A d10 Hit Die is d6 + 2.
- **Critical hits:** double the dice first, then change them. A d8 on a
  critical hit is two d8s, so 2d6 + 2.
- **More than four dice:** roll the four dice again and add. For example,
  roll 8d6 as two rolls of 4d6.

### What 3d6 changes

The results of 3d6 are near the middle more often. An easy roll becomes
easier, and a hard roll becomes much harder. For example, DC 18 with a +3
bonus needs a 15 or more: 30 percent with the twenty-sided die, and 9
percent with 3d6. This is why the DCs of this book stay between 8 and 15,
and the Armor Class of each creature stays between 10 and 16. Each
encounter of this book stays inside these limits, so it works with both
kinds of dice.

When you make up a DC at the table, keep it at 15 or less. A DC of 16 or
more is almost impossible with 3d6 at these levels.

### How the book prints a roll

Each roll in this book shows the normal dice first and the six-sided form
in brackets. A sword that hits for 7 (1d8 + 3) shows the six-sided form
after it. A group with normal dice reads the first form. A group with a
backgammon set reads the bracket. You never change a number at the table.

The statblocks of the Compendium monsters in this book also have the
bracket. If you take a monster from the Compendiums that this book does not
print, change its dice with the table above.

### Random tables

The random tables of this book use **d66** or **2d6**. For d66, roll two
dice of different colors, or roll one die and then the other. The first is
the tens and the second is the units: a 3 and then a 5 is 35. A d66 table
has 36 results, from 11 to 66. Each dice set has d6s, so these tables work
for both kinds of group.

## The Storm Cube

The doubling cube of the backgammon set is the clock of the campaign, and
the stakes. The group does not roll it. It stays in the middle of the
table, where every player can see it, from the first scene to the last.

The cube shows how near the storm season is. In the story, it is the
Grandam stirring in her sleep. The players do not know that. They see a
cube that goes up when the weather turns, when they waste time, and when
they take a shortcut. That is enough.

### How the cube works

- **It starts on 2.** Put it on the table at the start of chapter 1, with
  the 2 on top.
- **It only goes up.** It goes up one face at a time: 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64.
  It never goes down.
- **At 64, the storm season starts,** and the last chapter starts with it.
  Finish the scene that the party is in, then go to chapter 3.

The cube has only five steps from 2 to 64. So each turn of the cube is a
big moment. Turn it slowly, with a little ceremony, and say what the
players see: the wind changes, the sea goes dark, the warm ground of the
Kettles shivers, the gulls leave Gull Rock all at once.

### Storm signs

Small things bring the storm nearer too, but not a full face at a time.
For these, give a **storm sign**. Put one checker from the backgammon
board next to the cube. At the third checker, take the three checkers away
and turn the cube up one face.

Give a storm sign for:

- **A Long Rest in the open.** A rest in Hythe, or in a shelter that a site
  names, gives no sign.
- **A lost day.** The party gets lost on a journey, or it spends a day and
  gains nothing from it.
- **A small bad choice.** Each site says which choices give a sign: for
  example, a fight in the streets of Hythe, or an insult to a holy place of
  the Tey folk.

### What turns the cube

Turn the cube up one face at once for:

- **An event of a site.** The text of each site says "Turn the cube" where
  it happens.
- **A big bad choice.** Each site says which choices turn the cube.
- **The third storm sign.**
- **A double** (see below).

Chapter 1 shows the cube and teaches the storm signs. It does not turn the
cube. The cube is still on 2 when the party meets the colony.

### To double

As in backgammon, a player can choose to **double**: to turn the cube up one
face early. That player then gets a large benefit. Each shortcut brings the
storm nearer, and the whole table sees it.

- **Who can double.** Each player can double once in each session. The
  table can double only once in each scene. The GM never doubles.
- **When.** At any moment, also in a fight and also after a roll. A double
  that rerolls a roll happens after the player sees the result.
- **What it gives.** The player chooses one benefit from the table below.
  Or the player can roll 2d6 for it. A player who rolls gets the benefit of
  that row and also gains Heroic Inspiration.
- **At 32.** A double at 32 turns the cube to 64 and starts the storm
  season. Tell the player before the player chooses. The benefit still
  happens, then the scene ends, and the last chapter starts.
- **At 64.** Nobody doubles in the last chapter. Chapter 3 tells you what
  the cube does in the storm season.

| 2d6 | What the double gives |
|---|---|
| 2 | **The eye of the storm.** The party can take a Long Rest now, wherever it is. Nothing interrupts it, and it gives no storm sign. |
| 3 | **Old luck.** The next attack roll that you make in this scene and that hits is a critical hit. |
| 4 | **Hard to catch.** Until the end of your next turn, attack rolls against you have Disadvantage. |
| 5 | **A fair wind.** The next journey of the party on the island takes no time: it has no encounter and no supply roll, and it gives no storm sign. |
| 6 | **A breath.** The party takes a Short Rest of only 10 minutes. |
| 7 | **A second chance.** One attack roll, ability check, or saving throw that a character just made is rolled again. Use the new roll. |
| 8 | **A sure thing.** One ability check that you make in this scene succeeds with no roll, if its DC is 15 or less. |
| 9 | **A good find.** A gathering roll succeeds with no roll, and it gives twice the material. |
| 10 | **Stand together.** Each ally who can see or hear you gains Temporary Hit Points equal to 1d6 + your level. |
| 11 | **The sea gives back.** You find a thing that you lost in the wreck: a piece of your own gear, or an item that costs 25 GP or less. |
| 12 | **The eye of the storm.** As 2. |

> **GM tip:** A double is the best moment of the session for a player. Make
> it big. Let the player turn the cube by hand, and let the table groan.
> Then give the benefit with no argument.

### Normal dice

A group with no doubling cube uses a d6 as the cube. Put it on the table
with the 1 on top, and turn it up one face at a time. Each face stands for
a face of the cube:

| d6 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The cube | 2 | 4 | 8 | 16 | 32 | 64 |

Or draw six boxes on a card, write 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, and 64 in them, and
fill the boxes in. Use coins or tally marks for the storm signs. The rules
do not change.

## The Pace

The cube is the pace of the campaign. Chapter 1 leaves it on 2. The middle
of the campaign, the island, takes it from 2 to about 32. Then one more
turn starts the storm season.

| When | The cube is about |
|---|---|
| The start of chapter 1 | 2 |
| The first meeting with the colony, at the end of chapter 1 | 2 |
| After 2 sites of the outer ring | 4 |
| The end of the outer ring | 8 |
| After the first 2 sites of the inner ring | 16 |
| The end of the inner ring | 32 |
| The storm season | 64 |

Each site has about one storm sign and one turn in it. A party that visits
eight sites and doubles once or twice reaches 32 at about the right time.

### If the cube runs fast

The party loses time, or the players double a lot. That is their choice,
and the last chapter can start early. If the cube is two faces ahead of
the table above, change the next "Turn the cube" of a site into a storm
sign. Do this only once. A storm that comes early is a good story, not a
mistake.

### If the cube runs slow

The party is careful and lucky. If the cube is one face behind the table
above at a level point, give a storm sign at each Long Rest, also in Hythe,
until it catches up. The weather is turning, and everybody in the colony
can feel it.

### The end of the island

When the party has done what it wants on the island, or has seen each site
of the inner ring, the storm comes on its own. Turn the cube once at each
Long Rest until it reaches 64. Tell the players why: the colonists start
to bring in their boats, the Tey folk sing at the Well all night, and the
Corrin men count their oil.

## Milestone Levels

This book uses milestone levels. Do not count experience points. The
characters go up a level at fixed points:

| Level | When |
|---|---|
| 2 | At the end of chapter 1, at the first meeting with the colony. |
| 3 | After the party finishes 2 sites of the outer ring. |
| 4 | After the party finishes the first 2 sites of the inner ring. |
| 5 | When the last chapter starts. |

- **A site is finished** when the party ends its main scene. Each site says
  which scene that is. A short visit does not count.
- **The level up happens** at the next rest, Short or Long, after the
  level point.
- **Hythe** counts as a site of the outer ring once. The party goes back to
  the town many times, but the level point counts it only one time.
- **If the storm season comes early,** the characters still go to level 5
  when the last chapter starts. A character at level 3 goes up two levels.
  The party is not ready in what it knows and what it found, but its
  levels fit the last chapter.
- **The encounters** use the XP budgets of Building Encounters in the Game
  Master's Compendium, for the level of the ring: level 2 in the outer ring
  and level 3 in the inner ring. A party that is one level higher finds the
  fights easier. Do not change them.

Milestone levels are in Levels, XP & Progression in the Game Master's
Compendium.

## The Optional Systems

The story uses four optional systems of Dice & Destiny. Each system starts
with a **Teach** box in the text, where the story needs it. The full rules
are in the Player's Compendium and the Game Master's Compendium.

| System | Where it starts | What it does here |
|---|---|---|
| Injuries and Scars | Chapter 1 | The dangers of the wreck, and later of the storm. |
| Factions and Renown | The end of chapter 1 | The Corrin men and the Tey folk. |
| Journeys | Chapter 2 | Travel on the island, and the supply die. |
| Crafting and Gathering | Chapter 2 | The materials of the island, and the repair of the Corrin Light. |

### The systems with six-sided dice

Most rolls of the systems are attack rolls, checks, and saves, so they use
3d6 as always. Three rolls need a word:

- **Encounters on a journey.** Use the 2d6 or d66 encounter table of the
  island in chapter 2, not the tables of the biomes.
- **The supply die.** It is a size from d4 to d12, and it becomes one size
  smaller on a 1 or a 2. With six-sided dice, roll as below. The chances
  are about the same.

  | Supply die | Six-sided roll | It becomes smaller on |
  |---|---|---|
  | d12 | d6 | 1 |
  | d10 | d6 | 1 |
  | d8 | 2d6 | 5 or less |
  | d6 | d6 | 1 or 2 |
  | d4 | d6 | 1, 2, or 3 |

- **The Injury table.** Roll 3d6 in place of the twenty-sided die. Add 5 if
  the character dropped to 0 Hit Points, as always. The worst results of
  the table come a little less often with 3d6. This is a good thing in a
  short campaign.

### If you do not want a system

Each system is optional. If your group does not want it, skip the Teach
box and use the core rules:

- **Injuries and Scars:** use the normal rules for 0 Hit Points.
- **Factions and Renown:** keep a short note of which side likes the party.
  Where a site asks for renown, ask instead if that side trusts the party.
- **Journeys:** say how long the trip takes, and roll once on the encounter
  table of the island.
- **Crafting and Gathering:** the party finds each part of the Corrin Light
  in its site. The colonists make the repairs.

The story does not change. Only the extra rules go away.

## Starting the Campaign

### The characters

The players make characters of level 1 with the Player's Compendium, with
normal dice. A player who uses the backgammon dice keeps the normal dice on
the character sheet, and uses the cheat sheet at the table.

The book has no new classes, species, feats, or spells. Each class and
species of the Player's Compendium works. A character from Corrantey, from
anywhere on the west coast, or from a family of sailors gets the most out of
the story, but any character can be aboard.

### Session zero

Before the first session, give the players the player's guide. It tells
them about Corrantey, the *Good Intent*, and the voyage. It tells them
nothing about the island. Then spend fifteen minutes on these questions:

- **Why are you aboard?** Each player chooses a hook from the player's
  guide, or rolls one. Write the answers down. The colony can answer some
  of them.
- **Who do you know aboard?** Each character knows one other character from
  before the voyage. Go round the table.
- **The dice.** Decide if the group plays with the backgammon dice, with
  normal dice, or with both. A table can mix them: each player rolls the
  dice that the player has.
- **The tone and the lines.** See "Safety Tools" below.

### Before or after Waking

This book happens in year 300, the same year as Waking, the first book of
The Dawnwatch. It does not touch the story of Waking. The island has no link
to the troubles of the land.

- **This book first.** The characters of Waking are the five sleepers, so a
  group that plays this book first starts Waking with new characters. The
  world carries over: the places, the powers, and the gods. The players
  hear old Ostrey for the first time in the mouths of the colonists, and
  they hear it again in Waking.
- **Waking first.** The players know old Ostrey well. They hear "Light to
  you" and "And dawn to you" in Hythe, and they know what it means: these
  people left before the long sleep. Let them enjoy it. The colonists know
  nothing of the Unlit King, the sleepers, or the Church of the Five
  Sleepers. To them, the news of the land is all new.
- **A side trip.** You can also run this book between two chapters of
  Waking with new characters, as a story told at a fire. Keep the two
  parties apart.

The gazetteer of Waking says that ships on the Western Sea "do not go far
out". It says that nobody knows what is out there, and nobody has ever
come back to say. This book explains that sentence. The party can be the
first to come back.

## With 3 to 6 Characters

The book is written for four characters. It works with 3 to 6.

- **The fights.** Each fight has a **Scaling** box for 3, 4, 5, and 6
  characters. Use it. Do not change the levels.
- **The cube.** The cube and the storm signs do not change. The rule of one
  double for each scene keeps a big table from running the cube too fast.
- **Three characters.** A party of three has no spare hands. Give it a
  friend: a survivor of the *Good Intent*, if chapter 1 leaves one alive, or
  a young colonist who wants to see the world. The friend takes a role on
  a journey, carries a light, and holds a rope. The friend does not fight,
  and does not solve problems. On a journey, the fourth role can stay empty,
  or the friend can take it with a +3 bonus, as a hireling does.
- **Five or six characters.** A big party moves slowly and argues more. The
  two sides of the colony are a gift here: let different characters take
  different sides. A big party also gets more doubles. That is fine: it
  only means that the last chapter can come sooner.
- **Gathering and repairs.** The amounts of material in the book are for
  four characters. With 5 or 6, the party can find one more of each
  material. With 3, it needs one less for each repair.

## Keeping the Secret

The island has three secrets: the colony, the Grandam, and the wall of
storms. The players learn the first in chapter 1. They learn the others
slowly, on the island.

- **The player's guide is safe.** It tells the voyage and nothing of the
  island. The handouts are safe too. Give each one only when its site says
  so.
- **Each colonist knows a piece.** The Corrin men know the Light and the
  storm, and they think the Grandam is a tale. The Tey folk know the
  Grandam, and they think the Light is death. Nobody knows the whole truth.
  Let the party put it together.
- **Hint, but do not tell.** These hints are safe at any time: the warm
  ground that seems to rise and fall, the *Morning Star* high on the Ribs,
  the legend of the saint and his fire, a storm that comes in when the cube
  turns. A sharp player can guess. That is fine. A guess is not proof.
- **The cube is not a secret.** The players can see it, and they know that
  it is the storm. They do not have to know what the storm is.

## Safety Tools

This book has shipwreck, drowning, the death of the crew, a people who are
cut off from the world, a faith that fears the end, and a choice about who
stays behind. Most of it is sea adventure, not horror. Still, talk about
it before you start.

| Theme | Where | How strong |
|---|---|---|
| Shipwreck and drowning | Chapter 1, chapter 3 | Medium. The wreck, deep water, people lost at sea. |
| Being lost and cut off | All chapters | Light. The party may never get home. |
| Faith and fear | Chapter 2, chapter 3 | Medium. A people who fear that the end will come, and two sides who will not listen to each other. |
| A huge living thing | Chapter 2, chapter 3 | Light. Wonder more than horror. |
| Leaving people behind | Chapter 3 | Medium. Not everybody can come home. |
| Injuries | All chapters | Medium, if you use Injuries and Scars. |

The book has no torture, no sexual violence, and no harm to children on the
scene. Do not add them.

### At the table

- **Pause.** Any player can tap the table or say "pause" to stop a scene at
  any time. Nobody asks why. You go back, change the scene, or go on when
  everybody is ready.
- **Check in.** After a hard scene, such as a drowning or a death, ask: "Is
  everybody good to continue?" Take a short break if anybody is not.
- **Fade to black.** Each scene can end before the hard part. Say "and the
  wave takes the boat", and go to the next morning.

### Losing a character

A character can die in this book, most of all in chapter 1 and in the last
chapter. If a character dies, the player can bring in a new character: a
survivor of the *Good Intent* who washed up on another beach, or a
colonist who wants to see the land of the old stories. A colonist character
uses the Player's Compendium as normal, and talks like an old book.

## After the Book

This book has a true end. At the end of chapter 3, the party is home, or it
is not, and the Grandam sleeps, or she does not. Either way, Corrantey gets
the first true news from beyond the sea, and the party decides what that
news is.

A group can stop there. That is a full story. A group that wants more can
start Waking with new characters, in the same year, in the same world. The
news that the party brings home can be a rumor on the docks of Corrantey in
chapter 6 of Waking.
