# Gazetteer of the Land

This gazetteer shows the land as it is today, three hundred years after
the Dawnwatch went to sleep. It is for the GM. The players find it out at
the table, one village at a time.

Use it in three ways:

- **When you prepare a chapter.** Read the entry of the place first. It
  tells you what the land around the chapter is like, who lives there,
  and what the people think of the Dawnwatch.
- **When the players go off the path.** Each place has enough to run a
  short visit. The rumor tables and the adventure seeds give you more.
- **After Book One.** A GM can run new stories in this land. The last
  part of the gazetteer has seeds for that.

### Secrets

Some facts in this gazetteer are secrets for a later chapter. A box with
the label **Secret** marks each one, with the chapter where the players
can learn it:

> **Secret (chapter 10):** An example. Do not tell this before chapter 10.

The players must not learn a secret before its chapter. NPCs do not know
most secrets. If an NPC knows one, the entry says so. A secret with the
label "Book Two" is not for Book One at all. It is here so that you can
hint at it.

### Names old and new

The names of today are worn-down forms of the old names. The characters
half know them. When an NPC says "Corrantey", a character from old Ostrey
hears "Corrin and Tey" said badly. Let the players work it out. It is one
of the best ways to show the three hundred years.

| Old name (the player's guide) | Name today |
|---|---|
| Ostrey | The land. People still say "old Ostrey" for the time before. |
| The March of Gauntry | Gaunt |
| The shrine of Saint Ivo | Saint Ivo's |
| Corrin and Tey | Corrantey |
| Caer Dunholm | Coldharrow |
| The Field of the Five | Ashby Field |
| The Morning Flame | The Morning Flame. The faith lives on, changed. |

## The Three Hundred Lost Years

The years in this book count from the long sleep. "Today" is year 300.
Nobody in the land counts years this way. Each power counts in its own
way, and the dates do not agree.

### The timeline

| Year | What happened | What people say today |
|---|---|---|
| About -40 | Sir Garrath, the first knight of the sun, sees that the sun is weaker each winter. | Nothing. Nobody remembers. |
| About -20 | Garrath tries to take the fire of the sun into himself. He becomes the Unlit King. | "Malgrath came out of the dark beyond the mountains." |

| 100 to 140 | No king, no law, no roads. | "The Broken Years." |

### Year 0: the last night

The war of the Unlit King ended with no battle. Steward Haskel Orme of
Dawnwatch Keep found a way to put Garrath to sleep, in crystal, in a vault
under Kingsholm. Then he used the same spell on the last five knights of
the Dawnwatch, under the keep, so that they would wake if Garrath ever
did. On the last night, he made Wick.

Orme wrote the history of the war himself. The king and a handful of
lords knew the truth, and they all agreed to the lie. They told the
kingdom that the army of Ostrey pushed the demon king back into the dark,
beyond the Grey Teeth. Within one lifetime, the lie was the only story
left.

> **Secret (chapter 10):** The vault under Kingsholm. The name "Mal
> Garrath", the bad Garrath, that became "Malgrath". Orme's last letter
> tells it all.

### Years 0 to 100: the Bright Years

After the war, Ostrey was tired and poor, but it was at peace. The winters
became normal again. The fields came back. The people remember this time
as a golden age, because it was the last time that the land had a king.

In these years, Ostrey split the Dawnfire into pieces. The priests said
that a single flame could go out with a single blow, and that many flames
are safer. They carried the pieces to shrines and churches all over the
kingdom. One piece stayed in the chapel of Dawnwatch Keep, over the
sleepers.

- A small coal went to the shrine of Saint Ivo. Today it is the **Ember
  of Saint Ivo**.
- A flame in a lamp went with the apprentices of Haskel Orme. Today it is
  the **First Lamp** of the Lampwrights, in Corrantey.
- A piece went to the church of Fennick. Today it is under the water.
- Other pieces went to other places. Nobody today knows how many there
  were, or where the rest are.

> **Secret (chapter 7):** The pieces were not for safety. They kept
> Garrath asleep. Orme says so in his first letters, in his closed study
> at the keep.

> **Secret (Book One, never told):** Wick is the last true piece of the
> Dawnfire. Orme lit him from it on the last night. Nobody in Book One
> knows this, Wick included.

Haskel Orme died some years after the sleep. His letters (chapters 7 and
10) tell where and how. His apprentices went west to Corrin and Tey and
started a guild of lantern makers. Later stewards held Dawnwatch Keep for
the crown until the fall.

### About year 100: the Grey Winter

For three years, summer did not come. The snow stayed on the Wolds until
the middle of the year. The harvests failed. People ate their seed grain,
then their animals, then their boots.

In the second winter, the last king of Ostrey died with no heir. The lords
fought for the crown. Kingsholm burned in the fighting. The people who
lived left it, and nobody came back.

In the third spring, the snow melted all at once. The River Brann rose
over its dikes, and the low land had no way to let the water out. In one
night, the water covered the farmland of the south-east and the town of
Fennick. It never went back down. That water is the Low Fen.

The steward of Dawnwatch Keep left for the war of the lords and did not
return. The keep stood empty. The last human to visit the Hall of
Sleepers came in this year. Wick still remembers the footsteps.

> **Secret (Book Two):** The Grey Winter was not the work of the Unlit
> King, who was asleep. It was the sun, tired, as Garrath saw it. It is
> the first sign in the history of the land that Garrath was right about
> the sun.

### Years 100 to 140: the Broken Years

With no king, the land broke into valleys, towns, and farms that each
looked after themselves. Bandits held the roads. A plague came twice.
The old stone road of Ostrey cracked and nobody mended it. The forest
grew back over the fields around Kingsholm. People forgot how to read,
because there was nothing to read and nobody to teach.

The pieces of the Dawnfire in the small shrines went out or were lost in
these years. Some were stolen for the gold of their bowls. Some were
simply forgotten, when the last priest died and nobody kept the flame.

### About year 140: the First Vigil

The pilgrims did not stop coming to the shrine of Saint Ivo. They came
for the well, and they told stories by its side: about the Long War, and
about five knights who lay down under a hill to sleep until the dark came
back. Every village had its own form of the story.

A circle of priests at the shrine wrote the stories down and made them
into a faith. The Five Sleepers sleep in the light of the Morning Flame,
they taught, and on the day of the last dark, the Five will wake and
save the land. The faith gave people hope in a hard time, and it grew.
The town of Saint Ivo's grew around the shrine. The Church of the Five
Sleepers counts its years from this time.

The Church never knew where the Five slept. Its books say "under a hill
somewhere in the north". Most priests think that it is a holy story, not a place.

### About year 150: the Founding of Gaunt

The Gauntry family never stopped being soldiers. In the Broken Years,
their fortress in the south was the only place with walls, food, and law.
People came to it. The family took the land around it, then the roads,
then the towns, and held them with an army. The March of Gauntry became
the state of Gaunt, and the town under the fortress grew into Stannard.

The Gauntrys said, and still say, that they are the true heirs of Ostrey.
Their story is that a Gauntry married a daughter of the last royal house.
The proof is a ring, a letter, and an army.

### About year 210: the Charter of Corrantey

Corrin and Tey hated each other for four hundred years. Then the sea
silted up the harbor of Corrin and a storm broke the harbor wall of Tey,
in the same autumn, and the two towns had one choice: work together or
starve apart. The guilds of both towns signed a charter. They built one
harbor between the river mouths, and the two towns grew into one city.
The city still argues with itself, but now it argues in a council.

The oldest guild of the council is the Lampwrights, the lantern makers.
They started with the apprentices of Haskel Orme, and they still keep the
First Lamp in their hall.

### About year 270: the short days

Thirty years ago, the days started to become shorter. Not by much: a few
minutes each year, more in winter. Farmers say that the harvest is a
little later. Old people say that winters were shorter when they were
young, as old people always say. Most people do not notice.

The Lampwrights noticed. Their guild keeps a book of the times of sunrise
and sunset, because their trade depends on the dark. Their numbers are
clear: the light is going.

In the same years, a woman who would become Mother Sallow started to
gather people in grey who spoke little and put out lights. That was the
start of the Hush. It grew slowly and in secret.

> **Secret (Book Two):** The sun is dying. Garrath was right. The Hush is
> partly right too. See the Hush in The Powers of the Land.

### Year 300: the Waking

This spring, the Hush found Dawnwatch Keep. Its people came in through
the crack in the roof of the chapel, put out the Dawnfire, and left
hounds of cinder behind to guard the dark (the Brimstone Pups of the
tutorial). Under the keep, the crystal cracked.

On the same morning, under Kingsholm, the other crystal cracked too.

> **Secret (chapter 10):** Garrath woke on the same morning as the
> Dawnwatch, and left the vault. When the party reaches it in chapter 10,
> the crystal is empty. The black cloud that the characters see from the
> Dawn Door at the end of the tutorial is the first sign of him in the
> north.

The people of the land saw something too. On the day that the characters
woke, the sky over the Grey Teeth went black at noon, for the time it
takes to say a prayer. Every village has a story about it now. Most say it
was a sign. Nobody agrees what of.

## The Land Today

There is no kingdom now. People call it "the land", and they mean
everything between the Grey Teeth and the southern hills, from the
Western Sea to the edge of the Low Fen. Nobody rules all of it. Three
powers hold the parts that matter, and a fourth works in the dark.

| Region | Who holds it | What it is like |
|---|---|---|
| The south | Gaunt, from Stannard | Good roads, toll gates, soldiers, order. |
| The middle | The Church, from Saint Ivo's | Pilgrims, shrines, farms that pay tithes. |
| The west coast | Corrantey, by its council of guilds | Ships, trade, money, and arguments. |
| The Wolds and the east | Nobody | Sheep, small villages, old ruins. |
| The north and the Dunn | Nobody | Forest, ruins, and the old capital. |
| The Low Fen | Nobody | Marsh, black water, and the drowned town. |
| Past the Grey Teeth | Nobody alive | The dark country. Coldharrow. |

Most people live in villages of fifty to three hundred souls and never go
more than a day from home. A village has a headman or a headwoman, a
shrine of the Five Sleepers, and an opinion about the next village.

### The shorter days

The days are a little shorter each year. Since the Waking, they are
shorter each month. In Book One, the change is small. Show it with
small things:

- Shepherds bring the flocks in earlier. The light goes before the work
  is done.
- A sundial in a town square is wrong, and the town argues about who
  moved it.
- An old woman says that she has never needed a lamp at supper in
  summer, and this summer she does.
- Candles and lamp oil cost a little more each season. Corrantey grows
  rich on it.
- In the Wolds, the shadows at noon are a hand longer than an old
  shepherd remembers.

Toward the end of Book One, after chapter 7, let the characters notice
that the dawn comes late. Wick notices first. He counts.

### The faith of today

The Morning Flame is half forgotten. The Church keeps a saint's day for
it, and old people still face east at dawn, but people pray to the Five
Sleepers now. The priests of the Church wear grey and blue, not the red
and gold of Ostrey. The Church teaches that the Five sleep in the light
of the Morning Flame, and that they will wake on the day of the last dark
to save the land. Every village has a shrine of the Five: five candles in
a niche, and five small figures in wood or clay.

- **The faces are wrong.** The Church art shows the Five as tall, golden,
  calm, and young. A character who sees a figure of the Five sees a
  stranger with the badge of the Dawnwatch on the chest. The Shield is
  always a tall man with a beard. The Light is always a human woman. The
  Shadow is always tall. Nobody knows that the Spark is an elf. The Storm
  is shown as a human with a big axe.
- **The relics are fake.** Pilgrims buy bones, hair, teeth, and scraps of
  cloth "from the Five". There are enough teeth of the Captain in the land
  to fill three mouths. Dace, the pedlar of chapter 2, sells them.
- **The holy days are new.** The Longest Night is now the Vigil of the
  Five. The great feast of the Church is the Feast of the Five Sleepers in
  Saint Ivo's, in chapter 3.

### Speech

The language is the same, but three hundred years wore it down. The people
of today speak faster, drop the ends of words, and use new words that the
characters do not know. The characters sound old-fashioned and very
formal, like a priest reading from an old book.

| Old Ostrey | Today |
|---|---|
| "Light to you." "And dawn to you." | "Wake well." "And see the morning." The greeting comes from the Sleepers. |
| "Dark take it." | "Darkit." |
| A sun, a star, a bit | A gold piece, a silver, a copper |
| The March of Gauntry | Gaunt |
| Corrin and Tey | Corrantey |
| The shrine of Saint Ivo | Saint Ivo's |
| Caer Dunholm | Coldharrow |
| The Dawnwatch | The Five, or the Five Sleepers |

When a character speaks to a person of today for the first time, describe
the reaction: a long look, a smile, a question. "You talk like a
churchman." In Gaunt, people think that the characters are nobles. In
Corrantey, people think that they are actors.

### Money

Most coins today come from Corrantey, which stamps a lamp on them, or from
Gaunt, which stamps a mailed fist. Both are good gold, silver, and copper,
and everybody takes both. Use the normal prices of the Player's
Compendium.

The old coins of Ostrey are rare now. A gold sun of King Aldwin is still
gold, and any merchant takes it as 1 GP. A collector in Corrantey or a
priest in Saint Ivo's pays 5 GP or more for one in good condition. A coin
with the badge of the Dawnwatch on it would be priceless, if one existed.

### Travel

The old stone road of Ostrey is broken. Gaunt keeps its own roads in good
repair and charges a toll on each one. The pilgrim road west to Corrantey
is safe in summer and muddy the rest of the year. Everywhere else, the
roads are tracks.

Use these days for the Journeys system. A day is about 24 miles on a
road and about 16 miles off the road.

| From | To | Days | Road |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dawnwatch Keep | Brannock's Ford | 1 | A sheep track |
| Brannock's Ford | Saint Ivo's | 3 | The old road of Ostrey, broken in places |
| Saint Ivo's | The shrine at Kell | 2 | Off the road |
| Saint Ivo's | Stannard | 3 | A good road, made by Gaunt |
| Saint Ivo's | Corrantey | 5 | The pilgrim road west |
| Saint Ivo's | Fennick | 4 | Off the road, through the Low Fen |
| Dawnwatch Keep | Kingsholm | 3 | Off the road, along the Dunn |

For a trip that is not on the table, measure it on the map of the land
and use the miles per day above.

#### The dangers of the road

The land is not safe, but it is not a war. Most trouble on a road is
people: bandits in the Broken Years style, toll men who want more than
the toll, and pilgrims who want to touch the characters' cloaks. Wolves
come down from the north in winter. Since the Waking, more of them come,
and some of them are not wolves.

### The Dawnwatch in the eyes of the land

The characters are the Five Sleepers. The land prayed for them for one
hundred and sixty years. Nobody expects them to be real.

When the characters say who they are, people react in one of four ways.
Use the place to choose:

| Reaction | Who | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| Belief | Pilgrims, the poor, children, the old | Kneel, weep, ask for a blessing or a healing. Follow the party. |
| Doubt | Priests, soldiers, merchants | Laugh, or ask for proof. "Everybody says that they are the Five." |
| Use | Abbess Merrow, Marshal Ide Gauntry, the council of Corrantey | Want the party on their side, with a title, a banner, or a contract. |
| Fear | The Hush, and people who heard the Hush | The Five are the enemy of the coming dark. Kill them, or make them sleep again. |

Impostors are common. In each town, somebody claims to be one of the Five,
or to have met them. Garret Pell's troupe of actors plays the Five in
Saint Ivo's every feast day. A character's word is not proof.

#### What counts as proof

- **The Ember of Saint Ivo glows near the characters.** This is the proof
  that convinces the Church, in chapter 3.
- **Knowledge of old Ostrey.** The characters know things that are not in
  any book: the words of the old oath, the plan of Kingsholm, where the
  well was at the shrine of Saint Ivo. A scholar can check some of it.
- **Wick.** A talking lantern is strange enough to make people listen.
  The Lampwrights see the mark of their founder on him, in chapter 6.
- **The old gear.** The badge of the Dawnwatch on a shield is not proof by
  itself. Fakes are everywhere. But the steel of Ostrey is better than the
  steel of today, and a smith can see it.

## Places of the Land

The places go from north to south, as on the map. Each entry tells what
the place was three hundred years ago, what it is now, and what the party
finds there. An entry for a chapter gives the frame. The chapter gives the
scenes.

## The North

### Coldharrow

*The fortress of Malgrath, behind the Grey Teeth. Book Two.*

**Then.** Caer Dunholm, a fortress of Ostrey at the foot of the
mountains, on the high road to the passes. It watched the north. In the
war, the Unlit King took it and made it his seat.

**Now.** Nobody in the land has seen it for two hundred years. The name
wore down to Coldharrow, and it is a word in the stories now, like the end
of the world. Children say "go to Coldharrow" when they mean "go away".
Shepherds in the north of the Wolds say that on a clear winter night they
can see a light on the mountains there that is darker than the dark.

**What the party finds.** Nothing, in Book One. From the high places of the
north, a character can see the black cloud over the mountains where
Coldharrow is. It is bigger after each chapter.

> **Secret (Book Two):** Garrath went to Coldharrow after he left the
> vault under Kingsholm. The cloud is his. Book Two starts there.

### The Grey Teeth

*A wall of mountains across the north.*

**Then.** The northern wall of Ostrey. The goliath guides knew the passes.
The dwarves had holds under the mountains, with gates on the southern
side. Past the mountains was the dark country, where the Unlit King came
from.

**Now.** The mountains have not changed. Mountains do not. The passes are
still there, but nobody uses them, because there is nothing on the other
side that anybody wants. The snow lies longer every year.

- **The goliaths.** A few goliath families still live in the high valleys
  and herd goats. They do not come down to the land, and the land does not
  go up to them. They have their own name for the Unlit King, in their own
  language. It means "the one who ate the fire".
- **The dwarves.** The gates of the dwarf holds closed in the Grey Winter.
  Nobody in the land has seen a dwarf of the holds since. The Light is the
  only dwarf most people of today will ever meet, and the Church has never
  shown one of the Five as a dwarf.
- **The cloud.** Since the Waking, a black cloud sits over the northern
  peaks. It does not move with the wind. Goats will not graze under it.

**What the party finds.** In Book One, the Grey Teeth are a wall on the
horizon. The Storm knows them better than anybody alive. A character who
goes up into them meets goats, snow, wind, and the hard silence of the
goliaths. See the adventure seeds.

> **Secret (Book Two):** The goliath name is closer to the truth than the
> word "demon". Garrath tried to take the fire of the sun into himself.
> The goliaths saw it from the mountains, three hundred years ago, and
> remembered it in a song.

### The River Dunn

*The great river of the north.*

**Then.** The road of the north. Barges carried grain and stone, and the
king's army held the line of the Dunn for most of the war.

**Now.** The barges are gone. The river is wild again, with fallen trees
in it and beavers on its banks. It runs past the ruins of Kingsholm, then
south-west through empty land to the sea north of Corrantey. A few
fishers and trappers live along it. Since the Waking, they say that the
river runs cold even in summer.

**What the party finds.** The way to Kingsholm. From Dawnwatch Keep, the
party follows the Dunn for three days, off the road, through forest that
was farmland. See Kingsholm.

### Kingsholm

*The old capital of Ostrey, now ruins, on the River Dunn. Chapter 10.*

**Then.** The heart of Ostrey: a city of white stone on the River Dunn,
with the palace on a hill and the great bridge below it. Ten thousand
people, bells at every hour, and the best pies in the kingdom.

**Now.** Kingsholm burned in the civil war of the Grey Winter, and nobody
came back. In two hundred years, the forest took the city. Oaks grow in
the great hall of the palace. The great bridge fell into the Dunn and made
a ford of white stones. Ivy covers the walls, and the streets are paths
between mounds of rubble.

The land avoids Kingsholm. The stories say that the dead walk there, that
the old king sits on his throne under the moss, and that anybody who takes
a stone from the city dies within the year. Scavengers go there anyway,
because the old city still has lead roofs, bronze, and the odd coin. Most
come back.

What is in the ruins:

- **The palace hill.** The palace is a shell. Its great hall has no roof,
  and an oak grows in the place of the throne.
- **The temples of the Morning Flame.** Burned. In one of them, the altar
  bowl is still there, cold, with a ring of stones around it that somebody
  set in the last few years.
- **The old square.** In the middle stands a statue of a knight in the
  armor of the Dawnwatch. Its face is cut off, cleanly, with a chisel. The
  name on the base is cut away too.
- **The deep cellars.** The old city has cellars, wine vaults, and drains
  under the whole of the palace hill. Some of them go down a long way.
- **The camp of the Hush.** In Book One, the Hush hides in Kingsholm. Its
  people come and go at night, with no lights. The scavengers who know
  this have stopped coming.

**What the party finds.** In chapter 10, the party attacks the Hush in
Kingsholm and breaks it. Under the city, it finds the vault where Malgrath
slept.

> **Secret (chapter 10):** The vault under the palace hill held the
> crystal of Garrath. The crystal is empty, and the badge of the Dawnwatch
> is on it. The statue in the old square was Sir Garrath. Haskel Orme had
> its face and its name cut away after the war, so that nobody would know
> him. This is the place of the twist. Do not let the party come here
> before chapter 10. If the players try, the forest is thick, the Hush is
> watching, and Wick is very, very afraid of the place, and he does not
> know why.

## The Wolds

### Dawnwatch Keep

*The keep of the tutorial, on a hill in the Wolds. Chapters 1 and 7.*

**Then.** The eye of the kingdom: a great fortress of grey stone with
four towers, a hall for the knights, a chapel with the Dawnfire, an
armory, deep cellars, and, under it all, the Hall of Sleepers. The home
of the Dawnwatch.

**Now.** A ruin on a green hill. Two towers stand. The other two are
stumps. Trees grow through the walls of the knights' hall. Sheep shelter
in the gate. Below the hill, where the keep town stood, there is only
forest. From the walls, the characters can see the smoke of Brannock's
Ford, a day to the south.

The upper keep is open to the sky. The lower keep, under the hill, is
mostly whole, because the steward built it well:

- **The Hall of Sleepers.** The five crystal coffins, cracked and empty
  now. Above the door: "WE SLEEP SO THAT THE DAWN CAN WAKE."
- **The Stair of Teeth, the armory, and the Chapel of Dawn.** As in the
  tutorial. The stone bowl of the Dawnfire is cold. The crack in the roof
  of the chapel is how the Hush came in.
- **The rest of the lower keep.** Store rooms, a well, a forge, cells, the
  rooms of the steward, and doors that the tutorial does not open. One of
  them is closed with a lock that has no keyhole.

The shepherds of the Wolds do not go into the keep. Their grandparents
said that the Sleepers lie under that hill. Most of the shepherds think
it is a story. Some of them leave a cup of milk at the gate on the Vigil
of the Five, to be safe.

**What the party finds.** In chapter 1, the tutorial. In chapter 7, the
party comes back and finds the Hush in the keep, searching the Hall of
Sleepers. The party takes the keep back, and it becomes the stronghold of
the party with the Strongholds system.

> **Secret (chapter 7):** The door with no keyhole is the closed study of
> Haskel Orme. His first letters are in it. They are handouts in chapter
> 7. They tell why the pieces of the Dawnfire matter, and they start to
> show the lie.

> **Secret (chapter 7):** The Hush searches the Hall of Sleepers for more
> black stones, and for any sign of where its king slept. It does not
> find one. The Hall holds no answer, only the empty crystals.

#### Dawnwatch Keep as a stronghold

From chapter 7, the keep is the home of the party. The Strongholds chapter
of the Game Master's Compendium has the rules. Some notes for this keep:

- **What can be mended.** The lower keep is whole. The two standing towers
  can be roofed. The knights' hall needs a year of work. The steward's
  study is the heart of the keep.
- **Who comes to work.** Shepherds from the Wolds, Tamsin's family from
  Brannock's Ford, pilgrims who believe, and soldiers who left Gaunt. Each
  new face is a chance for a spy of the Hush.
- **The chapel.** If the party brings a piece of the Dawnfire to the keep,
  the stone bowl can hold it. Wick wants this very much.

### The Wolds

*Low hills with sheep and old stones.*

**Then.** The sheep hills east of Kingsholm: green, windy, with stone walls
and standing stones that nobody remembered putting up.

**Now.** Very much the same. The Wolds changed less than any part of the
land, because there was not much to change. The sheep are the
great-great-great-grandchildren of the sheep that the characters knew. The
stone walls are the same walls, mended a thousand times. The standing
stones are still there, and still nobody knows who put them up.

The people of the Wolds are shepherds, in small farms and hamlets far
apart. They are quiet, slow to trust, and good with weather. They pay no
tithe to the Church and no toll to Gaunt, because nobody has ever come to
ask. The Church sends a priest through twice a year.

- **The barrows.** Old graves of earth on the hilltops, older than Ostrey.
  The road wardens of Ostrey buried their dead in some of them. Some are
  dug open by treasure hunters. Some should not be.
- **The standing stones.** Rings and lines of grey stones on the high
  ground. Sheep rub on them. On the Vigil of the Five, the shepherds light
  a fire at the biggest ring.
- **The sheep tracks.** No roads, only tracks from farm to farm and down
  to the river.

**What the party finds.** The first walk of the campaign, in chapter 2:
one day on a sheep track from the keep to Brannock's Ford. Show the
characters a land that looks like home, and then show them that it is
not.

### Brannock's Ford

*A village at a ford of the River Brann. Chapter 2.*

**Then.** Not there. The old road of Ostrey crossed the Brann on a stone
bridge.

**Now.** The bridge of Ostrey fell in the Broken Years. A ferryman named
Brannock built a house by the ford about a hundred and fifty years ago,
and a village grew around the ferry. Today it is about two hundred people
in forty houses of grey stone with roofs of reed, a mill, a small church
of the Five with a bell, an inn with a lamp over the door, and a well with
a stone from the old bridge in its wall.

Brannock's Ford is the first village on the old road south of the Wolds.
Pilgrims pass through on the way to Saint Ivo's, more each year. The
village makes its money from them: beds, beer, the ferry, and things to
buy. In the week before the Feast of the Five Sleepers, the village is
full.

- **The ferry.** A flat boat on a rope. Tamsin runs it, and has for fifty
  years. When the river is low, a line of stepping stones crosses it.
- **The inn.** Warm, loud, and full of pilgrims. A painting of the Five
  Sleepers covers the wall over the hearth. In the painting, the coffins
  are gold, and angels guard them.
- **The church.** A painted board over its door shows the Five in white,
  asleep, all with the same face.
- **The barrow on the hill.** A mile north of the village is an old barrow,
  the grave of the road wardens of Ostrey. Treasure hunters dig there.

**People.**

- **Tamsin,** the ferry woman. About seventy, small, sharp-eyed, kind, and
  full of questions. Her family kept the last watch at the keep, two
  hundred years ago, before the steward left. She knows more than she
  says. She will be an ally for the whole book if the party is honest with
  her.
- **Dace,** the pedlar of bones. A thin, quick man who sells "bones of the
  Five Sleepers" to pilgrims. He digs them out of the barrow on the hill.
  He goes on to Saint Ivo's for the feast. See chapter 3.

**What the party finds.** In chapter 2, the first village of the new
world: old coins, a strange accent, a song with all the facts wrong, and a
pedlar who sells the bones of the characters. Then the road south and the
Journeys system.

> **Secret (chapter 2):** Grey folk bought a boat in the village last
> month and paid in old coins. They went up the river at night with no
> lamp. They were the Hush, on their way to the keep.

### The River Brann

*The river from the Wolds to the Low Fen.*

**Then.** A small, friendly river. It ran south through the best farmland
in Ostrey, between dikes, to the town of Fennick.

**Now.** The Brann starts in the Wolds below the keep and runs south past
Brannock's Ford as it always did. Below the ford, it slows and widens.
South of the old road, it loses its banks and spreads into the Low Fen.
Nobody keeps the dikes now. The river goes where it wants.

**What the party finds.** The ford in chapter 2. A party that follows the
Brann south finds the edge of the Low Fen in three days.

## The Middle

### Saint Ivo's

*The town of the Church. Chapters 3 and 9.*

**Then.** The shrine of Saint Ivo: a small chapel, a well, a guesthouse for
pilgrims, and a garden. A quiet place, where nothing ever happened.

**Now.** A town of about four thousand people, the largest town in the
middle of the land, and the seat of the Church of the Five Sleepers. It
grew around the shrine as the Church grew. On a feast day, it has twice as
many people as beds.

The old shrine is still in the middle of the town, very small, inside the
great church that the Church built around it. The town grew in rings
around the church: first the houses of the priests, then the inns for
pilgrims, then the market, then everybody else. A low wall goes around it
all.

- **The Church of the Five.** A great church of yellow stone with five
  towers, one for each Sleeper. Inside, the old chapel of Saint Ivo stands
  in the middle like a toy in a box. The windows of colored glass tell the
  story of the Long War and the Five. They are about a hundred years old.
- **The Ember of Saint Ivo.** In a case of gold and glass on the altar of
  the old chapel is the holiest relic of the Church: a small coal that
  never goes cold. The Church does not know what it is. It knows that it
  is holy, and that it is very old.
- **The well of Saint Ivo.** Still there, in a courtyard of the church.
  Pilgrims drink from it and feel better.
- **The pilgrim market.** Candles, figures of the Five, badges of the
  Dawnwatch in tin, books of prayers, and relics, most of them fake.
- **The play.** Each feast day, a troupe of actors plays the story of the
  Five in the market square. Garret Pell leads it. The crowd loves it.
- **The border.** Saint Ivo's is three days north of Stannard. The land
  between belongs to the Church, but Gaunt says that it does not. Soldiers
  of Gaunt come to the feast, and they do not come to pray.

**People.**

- **Abbess Merrow,** the head of the Church in Saint Ivo's. Warm, clever,
  and certain. She wants the party as living saints: on the altar, in the
  procession, and on her side.
- **Brother Cade,** a priest who thinks that the party are impostors. He
  is honest, stubborn, and brave. He becomes an ally later.
- **Garret Pell,** the leader of the troupe of actors who play the
  Dawnwatch. He has played the Shield for twenty years. He does not like
  competition.
- **Dace,** the pedlar of bones, at the feast with a new stock of relics.

**What the party finds.** In chapter 3, the Feast of the Five Sleepers, a
troupe of actors who say that they are the Dawnwatch, and the Church,
which does not know what to do with the real ones. The party must prove
who it is. The Ember of Saint Ivo glows near the characters. In chapter 9,
Saint Ivo's goes to war with Gaunt.

> **Secret (chapter 3):** The Ember glows near the characters because it
> is a piece of the same fire that burned over them for three hundred
> years. It glows brightest near Wick. Nobody knows why, Wick included.

> **Secret (chapter 3):** One window of the Church shows the sign that the
> Light saw when the crystal closed. The window is about two hundred years
> younger than the sleep. Use the words of the player of the Light. See
> the thread in chapter 3.

> **Secret (chapter 4):** The Hush has a spy in the town. At the end of
> chapter 3, the Hush steals the Ember of Saint Ivo.

### The Shrine at Kell

*An old shrine of Ostrey on a hill. Chapter 4.*

**Then.** A ring of standing stones on a lonely hill, with a small chapel
of the Morning Flame inside the ring. Pilgrims kept a candle burning there
on the Longest Night. An old priest lived there alone.

**Now.** The hill is still lonely. The chapel has no roof and no priest.
The standing stones are still there, and one of them has fallen. The
Church calls Kell "the old shrine" and sends nobody, because it is two
days from Saint Ivo's with no road, and nothing of the Five is there.

Shepherds and hunters avoid the hill in the last few years. They say that
people in grey go up there at night, and that the night on the hill is
darker than the night around it.

**What the party finds.** In chapter 4, the party follows the Hush, and
the stolen Ember, to Kell. It is the end of Act I. The Marks system starts
here, with the black stone.

> **Secret (chapter 4):** Kell is a holy place of the Hush. In the ring,
> Mother Sallow tries to put out the Ember and to take the black stone.
> The Long Shadow, the shadow of the Unlit King, comes through the black
> stone at Kell. At the end, the party learns that Malgrath can feel
> where the stone is. Mother Sallow escapes.

> **Secret (chapter 4):** After the war, the priests of Kell kept a piece
> of the Dawnfire of their own. It went out in the Broken Years, when the
> last priest died. The Hush likes the ring for that reason: it is a
> place where the light already lost.

## The South

### Ashby Field

*Open land on the border of Gaunt and Saint Ivo's. Chapter 9.*

**Then.** The Field of the Five: open land where the hills stop and the
March starts. In the war, the army of the king stopped the hounds of the
Unlit King here, in the snow, at dawn. The Dawnwatch held the center.

**Now.** Grass and wind, as before, and a few farms. The road from Saint
Ivo's to Stannard crosses it. The name wore down to Ashby Field, and few
people remember why the field had a name at all. Farmers plow up arrow
heads and bits of old armor. A small shrine of the Five stands by the
road, where the song says that the Five "held the gate".

Ashby Field is the border, and nobody agrees where. Gaunt puts up border
stones. The Church pulls them down. Each spring, the stones are somewhere
new. Both sides send patrols. So far, nobody has died.

**What the party finds.** In chapter 9, Gaunt and Saint Ivo's go to war
here, and each side says that the Dawnwatch is on its side. The Hush helps
both sides to fight. It is the first true battle, and the Injuries and
Scars system starts here. The characters fight a war on the same field
where they fought a war three hundred years ago.

> **Secret (chapter 9):** The family of the person of the Shield's
> promise lives on Ashby Field today, or fights on it. Use the words of
> the player of the Shield. See the thread in chapter 9.

### Stannard

*The seat of the Gauntry family. Chapter 5.*

**Then.** A fortress of the March of Gauntry on a rock above the south
road. The family held the border for the king.

**Now.** The capital of Gaunt, a fortress-town of about six thousand
people. The old fortress still crowns the rock, with new walls around it.
Below it, the town fills the slope in neat streets, with barracks, drill
yards, forges, stables, and a market that closes at sunset by law.

Stannard is clean, ordered, and loud with drums. Every adult has a
weapon, every family has a soldier, and every street has a name and a
number. Visitors need a pass, which costs a silver and lasts a week.

- **The Marshal's Hall.** The old fortress, where Marshal Ide Gauntry
  rules. Its great hall has the banners of every war of Gaunt, and one
  empty pole, for the banner of Ostrey.
- **The College of War.** The school of the war mages. They keep "the
  spells of Ostrey" in old books, and most of the spells are wrong.
- **The drill fields.** Below the town, where the army trains each
  morning. Thousands of soldiers, all in step.
- **The Gate of the Five.** The main gate of the town, with five statues
  of the Five in Gaunt armor. Gaunt says that the Five were soldiers of
  the March. They were not.

**People.**

- **Marshal Ide Gauntry,** the ruler of Gaunt. A soldier first, a ruler
  second. She wants the Dawnwatch as living banners for her army.
- **Tobin Gauntry,** her son and heir. He does not want a war. He may be
  the best friend that the party has in Gaunt.

**What the party finds.** In chapter 5, Marshal Ide Gauntry offers the
party a place in Gaunt, a title, and an army behind it. The Factions and
Renown system starts here: the party gets ranks with Gaunt, with the
Church, or with both. The war mages teach a "Dawnwatch spell" that is the
spell from the first day of the Spark, cast wrong.

> **Secret (chapter 5):** The war mages cast the Spark's spell wrong
> because they learned it from a copy of a copy of a note by somebody who
> saw it once, three hundred years ago. Use the words of the player of the
> Spark. See the thread in chapter 5.

## The West

### Corrantey

*The city on the west coast, at the mouths of the Corrin and the Tey.
Chapter 6.*

**Then.** Corrin and Tey: two port towns about a mile apart, rivals in
everything, each sure that the merchants of the other were thieves.

**Now.** One city of about fifteen thousand people, the largest in the
land. Corrin and Tey grew toward each other until there was no field left
between them, and the charter made them one. They share one great harbor
between the two river mouths. The old towns are still there, as two
halves of the city, and they still argue. The Corrin half has the docks
and the warehouses. The Tey half has the guild halls and the money.

Corrantey has no lord. A council of guilds rules it, one seat for each
guild, and the council argues about everything. It is a free city of
merchants, and everything in it is for sale, including the truth.

- **The Harbor.** Ships from up and down the coast, with wool, salt, wine,
  oil, and news. The fish market smells as it always did.
- **The Council House.** On the bridge between the two halves. The council
  meets in a round room so that nobody sits at the head.
- **Lantern Street.** The street of the Lampwrights, in the Tey half. Every
  shop sells light: lanterns, lamps, candles, oil, wicks, glass. At night,
  it is the brightest street in the land.
- **The Hall of the Lampwrights.** The oldest guild hall in the city, at
  the top of Lantern Street. In its inner room, the First Lamp burns.
- **The theaters.** Corrantey loves plays. Three companies play the story
  of the Five each year, in three different ways.

**People.**

- **Perrin Hale,** the master of the Lampwrights. Careful, curious, and a
  little greedy. He is the first person in Book One who can tell the
  party something true about Wick.

**What the party finds.** In chapter 6, the Lampwrights see the mark of
their founder on Wick: the mark of the steward. The First Lamp is in their
hall, and agents of Gaunt want it too. The Crafting and Gathering system
starts here: the guild makes items from the materials that the party
brings.

> **Secret (chapter 6):** The founder of the Lampwrights was Haskel Orme.
> His apprentices brought the First Lamp west after his death. The guild
> knows his mark and his name. It does not know what he did on the last
> night, and it does not know about the lie.

> **Secret (chapter 6):** The Lampwrights know that the days become
> shorter. Their book of sunrise and sunset shows it, year by year, for
> thirty years. The council does not want this known. Lamp oil is very
> good business.

### The Corrin and the Tey

*Two short rivers that reach the sea at Corrantey.*

The Corrin comes from the north-east, the Tey from the east. Both are
short, fast, and full of mills in their last miles. The pilgrim road west
from Saint Ivo's follows the Tey down to the city.

### The Western Sea

*The sea on the west coast.*

Grey, cold, and rich in fish. Ships go up and down the coast and do not go
far out, because nobody knows what is out there and nobody has ever come
back to say. North of Corrantey, the mouth of the Dunn is a maze of sand
and reeds where smugglers land. South of the city, cliffs and small
fishing villages go down the coast toward the hills of Gaunt.

## The East

### The Low Fen

*Marsh and black water in the south-east.*

**Then.** Not a marsh. The richest farmland in Ostrey: long straight
fields of barley and beans, ditches, and dikes along the Brann, with the
market town of Fennick in the middle.

**Now.** In the third spring of the Grey Winter, the Brann broke its dikes
and the water never went back down. Two hundred years later, the farmland
is the Low Fen: reeds taller than a rider, pools of black water, islands
of willow, and the lines of the old dikes, now long low ridges in the
marsh where a person can walk.

- **The fen folk.** A few hundred people live in the Low Fen, on the old
  dikes and in houses on stilts. They catch eels and birds, cut reeds, and
  pole flat boats on secret channels. They are wary of strangers. They
  pay no tithe and no toll, and they like it that way.
- **The lights.** On some nights, small lights move over the water. The
  fen folk call them the lamps of the drowned, and they do not follow
  them.
- **The tower.** From anywhere on the old dikes, a person can see the
  church tower of Fennick, standing out of the water in the middle of the
  fen.

**What the party finds.** Four days off the road from Saint Ivo's, the way
to Fennick. Use the Journeys system: the Low Fen is the hardest ground in
Book One.

### Fennick

*A drowned town in the Low Fen. Chapter 8.*

**Then.** The market town of the south-east, on a small rise in the
farmland, with dikes all around it and a church with a tall square tower.
Fennick grew the best barley and brewed the best ale in Ostrey, and sent
good soldiers to the war.

**Now.** The water covered Fennick in one night, two hundred years ago.
The town is still there, under the black water: streets, houses, the
market square, the dikes that failed. Only the top of the church tower and
the roofs of the highest houses stand above the water. At low water in a
dry summer, a person can walk on the roofs of the market.

The fen folk do not go to Fennick. They say that the people of the town
are still there, that they ring the bell of the church on the night of the
flood each year, and that a soldier stands guard on the tower and calls
out the watch.

**What the party finds.** In chapter 8, a dungeon under the water: the
drowned streets, the flooded houses, and the old church, where a piece of
the Dawnfire still burns. A ghost of Ostrey teaches the party the old
fighting ways, and the Techniques system starts here.

**People.**

- **Sergeant Maud Kettle,** the ghost of a soldier of Ostrey. She was a
  sergeant of the last garrison of the king in Fennick, in the Grey
  Winter. She learned the old fighting ways from soldiers who fought in
  the Long War, and she drowned with her town in the flood. She still
  keeps the watch. She teaches the old fighting ways to anybody who earns
  them.

> **Secret (chapter 8):** The piece of the Dawnfire in the old church of
> Fennick still burns under the water. The flood did not put it out.

> **Secret (chapter 8):** The reason why the Storm agreed to sleep is in
> Fennick. Use the words of the player of the Storm. See the thread in
> chapter 8.
