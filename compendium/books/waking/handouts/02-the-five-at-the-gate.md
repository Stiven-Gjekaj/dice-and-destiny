# The Five at the Gate

*A song of the pilgrim road, as they sing it at the Ferry Bell in Brannock's Ford.*

> When the snow lay deep on Ashby Field
> and the night was black as a well,
> the Unlit King came down from the north
> with a hundred hounds of hell.
>
> *Oh, seven stood at the gate, my love,*
> *and seven did not run.*
> *Seven held the gate till dawn,*
> *and seven woke the sun.*
>
> The Shield stood first, the tall and fair,
> who never felt a wound.
> The Light sang out a holy hymn,
> and every hound was swooned.
>
> The Spark called down the stars of heaven,
> the Storm laughed like a bear.
> The Shadow, who never took a thing
> that was not given fair,
>
> crept up behind the Unlit King
> and cut his black right hand,
> and the king fled back into the dark
> and never more will stand.
>
> *Oh, seven stood at the gate, my love,*
> *and seven did not run.*
> *Seven held the gate till dawn,*
> *and seven woke the sun.*
>
> Now they sleep beneath the holy hill,
> the hill of Saint Ivo,
> and they'll wake when the long night comes again,
> and the cold winds blow.
>
> So light a lamp on the longest night,
> and leave it at your door,
> and the Five will see it as they pass,
> and keep you evermore.

*The last verse says "the Five", and the chorus says "seven". Nobody in the inn has ever noticed.*
