# The Letters of Haskel Orme

*Found in the closed study of the steward, Dawnwatch Keep. Four pages, tied
with a blue ribbon. The ink is brown with age.*

---

## The first letter

To Wenna Orme, at the sign of the Two Keys, Kingsholm.

Dear Wen,

The nineteenth winter of the war, and the coldest yet. The well froze again
last night. The cooks melted snow for the porridge, and it tasted of smoke.

You asked me in your last letter when the war will end. I will tell you the
truth, because you always know when I do not. We cannot kill him. We tried
fire, and he drank it. We tried steel, and the steel went cold in our hands.
I have stopped asking whether we can. Lately I have stopped asking whether
we should.

The council has a new plan. It is my plan, if I am honest, and I am not
proud of it. We will not kill the King. We will put him to sleep.

I tested the crystal on a sparrow. It slept for a year and a day, and when
I cracked the crystal it woke hungry and very annoyed, and it bit me. So the
spell works, and it does no harm. That is more than I can say for anything
else in this war.

Burn this letter. Kiss the children for me.

Your brother,
Haskel

---

## The second letter

To Wenna Orme, at the sign of the Two Keys, Kingsholm.

Wen,

It is done. He sleeps, under the old palace, where the bells used to be.
The crystal held. I sat with him all night to be sure. His face was calm.
I had forgotten that his face could be calm.

Now the council asks for more, and I have agreed. Five more sleepers. The
same spell, the same crystal, here under the keep. If he ever wakes, someone
must wake with him. Someone who remembers what he was before.

I chose the five myself. They are the best of the Dawnwatch who are left.
They are young enough to wait and old enough to understand. They do not
know everything. I could not tell them everything. If they knew, I do not
think that they would lie down in the crystal, and I need them to.

You will hear that the five died bravely in the last battle. Let people say
it. It is kinder than the truth, and it is almost true.

Haskel

## The third letter

*No name on the outside. The seal is a sun inside a square.*

Wen,

The last night. Tomorrow I close the hall, and then there is only the
waiting, and I am too old to do the waiting myself.

So tonight I made a lantern. A small brass one, with a face in the flame.
I gave it a voice, because a hall of sleepers is a lonely place. I taught
it to count, so that it will know how long they slept. I taught it to sing,
badly. I taught it one rule: do not let them oversleep.

I did not teach it his name. I did not teach it mine. A lantern that knows
nothing cannot tell a lie, and I have told enough of those for both of us.

If the five ever read this, and I hope that they do: I am sorry. You will
want the rest of the truth. I did not leave it here. I left it with him,
under Kingsholm, where only the Dawnwatch would think to look.

Haskel
