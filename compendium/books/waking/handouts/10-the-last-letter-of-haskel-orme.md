# The Last Letter of Haskel Orme

*Found in a stone box at the foot of the crystal, under Kingsholm. The box
was sealed with wax. The seal is a sun inside a square. The letter is
dry, and the ink is still dark.*

---

To the five, when you wake.

If you are reading this, then you came down here, and you are standing next
to him. Before you read any further, look at the crystal.

If it is whole, and he is still inside it, then put this letter back in the
box. Go home. Let him sleep. Some truths do the most good when nobody digs
them up, and I would rather you hated me for a mystery than for an answer.

If it is broken, and he is gone, then read the rest. You need it now more
than you need my good name.

---

His name was Garrath.

Sir Garrath, first knight of the sun. The first of the Dawnwatch. He wrote
the oath that you swore, and the motto of the order: *we watch so that the
dawn can wake.* I carved his words over the door of your hall, and I
changed one of them. *We sleep.* He would have hated that.

He was the first to see that the sun was failing. A little weaker each
winter, a little later each spring. The scholars laughed at him. I laughed
at him. He did not laugh. He went north, to the old places, and he came back
with a way to save it: to take the fire of the sun into himself, and keep it
safe, as a man keeps an ember alive in his cupped hands through a long night.

It did not keep. Or it kept, and it kept him too. The fire went out of him
and the dark came in, and the man who came back down the mountain was not
the man who went up it. You know the rest, because you fought in it. Twenty
winters of war against a king with no light in him.

In the villages they started to call him Mal Garrath before the war was
half done. The bad Garrath. It was a soldiers' joke at first. By the end
nobody laughed when they said it, and they said it fast, the way you say a
word that you are afraid of. Mal Garrath. Malgrath. By the time your
children's children say it, I think nobody will remember that it was ever
a man's name.

I made sure of that. I am sorry.

---

We did not defeat him. Nobody pushed him back into the dark. We put him to
sleep in crystal, here, under the bells of the old palace. Then I put you
to sleep with the same spell, in the same crystal, under the keep. Six
sleepers, not five. I never told you about the sixth.

I wrote the history that you will have read. A demon from the dark, a
kingdom that held the line, a victory. I wrote it because a kingdom can
survive a demon. I do not think that it can survive the knowledge that its
best knight became its worst enemy because he loved the sun too much. And
I did not want the next frightened man with a good idea to think of
Garrath as a hero. In the margin of my draft I wrote it plainly, so that
I would not lie to myself: a demon is easier to forgive than a friend.

So I told the world a lie, and I told you only half the truth, and I made
a lantern that knows neither. Be kind to the lantern. It did nothing wrong.

---

Here is what I could not say to your faces.

You did not sleep to guard the world from a stranger. You slept so that,
if he woke, someone of his own order would be awake with him. You never
met him. He founded the watch a generation before you, and he was gone
north before you took the oath. But you swore his words, and you are his
brothers and sisters of the watch. When the rest of us are dust, you will
be the only people left in the land who can look at Malgrath and know
that a knight of the Dawnwatch stands there. I do not know if that is a
weapon. It might be the only one.

And there is one more thing, the worst thing. He was right about the sun.
I have watched it for twenty years now, and he was right. I do not know
what you will find when you wake. I hope that it is a warm spring, and that
I was a frightened old man. But if the days are short, and the winters are
long, then he is not the only one who has to answer that question. You do
too.

I pinned his badge to the crystal. It was the only honest thing that I
could leave him. I left him a lamp too, the first time that I came down
here, so that he would not sleep in the dark. It will go out long before
he wakes. I hope that he does not mind.

Keep the watch. Wake the dawn. Forgive me if you can.

Haskel Orme,
Steward of Dawnwatch Keep,
written at Kingsholm, in the year of the long sleep
