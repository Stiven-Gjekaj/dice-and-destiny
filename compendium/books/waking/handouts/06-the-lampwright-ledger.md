# A page of the first ledger of the Lampwrights

*The page is brown and soft with age. The ink is faded. At the top, drawn
with care, is a small sun inside a square.*

---

**In the shop at Corrin, the first year after the long sleep.**

Col Tully, Esk, and I, Marra Lisle, apprentices of Master Haskel Orme,
open this book.

We came west on the orders of the master. He gave us his tools, his
patterns, and three purses of gold, and he told us to make lamps and to ask
no questions. We ask them anyway, but only in this book.

He also gave us the Lamp.

It is the small one of green glass that he made in the first winter of the
war. It needs no oil and it does not go out. The master said: "Keep it lit.
Keep it where everyone can see it. Nobody steals a thing that the whole town
watches. And if anyone ever comes to put it out, do not let them touch it."

We do not know who would want to put out a lamp. Esk says it is a riddle. I
think the master does not tell riddles.

**Things to remember about the master, so that we do not forget:**

- He hummed when he worked. Always the same song. Badly.
- He made six brass guards from the old patterns, for the hall that we
  will build one day. He put his mark on their heels and said they would
  know his work when they saw it.
- In the last month before we left, he went north to Kingsholm with a lamp
  and came back without it. He was gone three days. When he came back he did
  not hum any more.
- On the last night he did not sleep. He sat in the workshop and made one
  more lamp, a small brass one with a hood. He would not let us see it.
  When we left in the morning, it was gone, and so was he.

We will keep the Lamp lit.

*M. L.*
