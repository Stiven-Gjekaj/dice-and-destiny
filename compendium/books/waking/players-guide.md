# A Guide to Old Ostrey

*This guide shows the world as the Dawnwatch knew it, on the last night
before the long sleep, three hundred years ago. Wick tells it. The GM can
read it aloud before the first session, or the players can read it
themselves. Everything here was true, or was believed to be true, on the
night the crystal closed.*

## A Word from Your Guide

> Ahem. Is this on? I am never sure if I am on. I am a lantern.
>
> Good evening, Sleepers. Or good morning. I have lost track. I am Wick:
> Keeper of the Light, Guardian of the Hall, Official Waker, and now,
> Official Teller of Things. The steward gave me the first three titles. I
> gave myself the fourth. Nobody was here to stop me.
>
> While you slept, I made this guide. I had no hands and no ink, so I
> made it in my head. Then I said it out loud, to the walls, to check that
> it sounded good. Then I said it again, a little better. This is draft
> four hundred and twelve. The rats heard drafts ninety to four hundred
> and eleven. They did not clap.
>
> The steward told me everything in one night, on the night that he made
> me. He talked very fast. He said, "They will be confused when they wake,
> Wick. Remind them who they are." So that is what this is. A reminder.
> Who you are, where you are, and why you are lying in a crystal coffin
> under a hill.
>
> Please hold your questions until the end. I have waited a long time to
> say this, and I will say all of it.

## The Kingdom of Ostrey

> Ostrey is the land between the mountains and the sea. The Grey Teeth
> close it in the north. The Western Sea washes it on the west. In the
> south, the hills go down into the March, and the March goes down to
> lands that nobody in Ostrey cares about very much.
>
> It is a green kingdom, mostly. Sheep on the hills. Barley in the low
> fields. Rivers everywhere, and a ford or a ferry or a bridge on each of
> them, and an old man at each one who wants a coin to let you cross.
>
> A king rules Ostrey from the city of Kingsholm. The king now is Aldwin,
> who has a grey beard, a tired face, and a very good crown. He was a
> young man when the war started. He is not young now. Under the king are
> the lords of the land, who hold the roads and the towns for him, and the
> stewards, who hold his castles when the lords are away. The steward of
> Dawnwatch Keep holds this one. He is the one who made me, so he is my
> favorite.

### How big it is

> From the sea to the eastern border is about eighty leagues, which is two
> hundred and forty miles. From the Grey Teeth to the bottom of the March
> is more. A rider on the king's road makes the trip from Kingsholm to the
> south in a week if the horse is good and the rain is not. A shepherd
> makes it never, because why would a shepherd go to the south.

## The Places of Ostrey

> I will go from north to south, as the sun goes. Well. As the sun goes in
> my head. I have not seen the sun. I am told it is very good.

### The Grey Teeth

> A wall of mountains across the top of the world. Snow on the peaks all
> year. Passes that only the goliath guides know, and only in summer. Past
> the Grey Teeth is the dark country, where nobody lives, or nobody should.
> That is where the Unlit King came from. More about him later. I have
> to build up to him. He is the scary part.

### Caer Dunholm

> An old fortress of Ostrey at the foot of the Grey Teeth, on the high road
> to the passes. It was built to watch the north. In the war, the Unlit
> King took it and made it his seat. Its walls are black now, people say,
> as if a fire burned them from the inside. Nobody goes there. If somebody
> asks you to go there, say no. Say it was my advice.

### Kingsholm

> The capital. The heart of Ostrey. A city of white stone on the River
> Dunn, with the palace on a hill in the middle and the great bridge below
> it. Ten thousand people, maybe more. Bells at every hour. The market by
> the bridge sells everything: wool from the Wolds, eels from the fens,
> wine from the south, and pies, which I am told are the best thing about
> having a mouth.
>
> The king lives in the palace. The great houses of the lords stand around
> it. Below them are the guild halls, the inns, the temples of the Morning
> Flame, and many, many narrow streets where the Shadow learned a trade
> that we do not talk about.

### The River Dunn

> The great river of Ostrey. It comes down from the Grey Teeth, cold and
> fast. It passes Kingsholm, where it is wide and slow and brown. Then it
> runs south-west to the sea. Barges carry grain and stone on it. In the
> spring it floods. Everybody knows that it floods. Everybody builds next
> to it anyway.

### Dawnwatch Keep and the Wolds

> East of Kingsholm are the Wolds: low green hills, sheep, stone walls,
> more sheep, and old standing stones that nobody remembers putting up.
> The wind is always blowing. The sheep do not mind.
>
> On the highest hill of the Wolds is Dawnwatch Keep. You are under it
> now. It is a great fortress of grey stone, with four towers, a hall for
> the knights, a chapel with the sacred flame, an armory, deep cellars,
> and the Hall of Sleepers, which the steward built for you in the last
> year of the war. From the walls you can see half of Ostrey on a clear
> day. The steward says that the keep is the eye of the kingdom. I say
> that I am the eye of the keep. It is a long chain of eyes.

### The River Brann and the Fen

> The River Brann starts in the Wolds, below the keep. It is a small,
> friendly river. It has fords where a cart can cross in summer. It runs
> south and gets lazy and wide, through the flattest, richest farmland in
> Ostrey: long straight fields of barley and beans, with ditches between
> them and dikes of earth along the river to keep the spring floods out.

### Fennick

> The market town of the south-east, in the low farmland by the Brann. It
> stands on a small rise, with dikes all around it and a church with a
> tall square tower that you can see from a day away. The people of
> Fennick grow the best barley in Ostrey, brew the best ale, and catch
> eels in the ditches. They are proud of their dikes. They talk about
> their dikes a lot. Do not ask about the dikes unless you have an
> afternoon.
>
> Fennick sent good soldiers to the war. Fen folk are stubborn. They do
> not run, because the ground is too flat to hide on.

### The Shrine at Kell

> West of the Brann, on a lonely hill, is the shrine at Kell. It is very
> old: a ring of standing stones with a little chapel of the Morning
> Flame inside the ring. Pilgrims climb the hill at the Longest Night and
> keep a candle burning until the dawn. A priest lives there alone. The
> priest is always old. I think they choose them old on purpose.

### The Shrine of Saint Ivo

> South of the Wolds, where the old road goes down toward the March, is
> the shrine of Saint Ivo. It is small: a chapel, a well, a guesthouse for
> pilgrims, and a garden. Saint Ivo was a healer of the Morning Flame. In
> a hard winter long ago, he stayed with the sick of the south when
> everybody else ran away, and he did not catch the sickness, and nobody
> knows why. The sick and the worried go to his well. Most of them feel
> better. Some of them actually are better.
>
> It is a quiet place. A very quiet place. You could go there for a rest
> and nothing would ever happen to you. I like that in a place.

### The Field of the Five

> On the road south of Saint Ivo's shrine, where the hills stop and the
> March starts, there is open land. Grass and wind and nothing else. In
> the war, the army of the king stopped the hounds of the Unlit King
> there, in the snow, at dawn. People call it the Field of the Five now,
> after the song. The Dawnwatch held the center. Some of you were
> there. You will remember it better than I do. I only know the song.

### The March of Gauntry

> The March is the south border of Ostrey: the hard country, with the
> long walls and the watchtowers. The Gauntry family holds it for the
> king. They are soldiers, all of them, the grandmothers too. They ride
> well, they fight well, and they tell you about it at dinner.
>
> Their seat is a fortress on a rock above the south road. The
> Gauntrys are loyal to the crown. They say so often. Some people at court
> think they say it a little too often. The steward says that a loyal
> sword is still a sword.

### Corrin and Tey

> On the west coast, where two short rivers reach the sea, are two towns.
> Corrin is on the Corrin. Tey is on the Tey. The people of Ostrey are not
> very good at names.
>
> Corrin and Tey are about a mile apart, and they hate each other very
> much, in the friendly way of two towns that are about a mile apart. Both
> are ports. Both have fish markets, shipyards, and merchants who say
> that the other town's merchants are thieves. Ships from Corrin and Tey
> go up and down the coast with wool, salt, and the news. If you want to
> know what is happening in Ostrey, ask a sailor in Tey. If you want to
> know what is not happening, ask a sailor in Corrin.

## The Morning Flame

> The people of Ostrey worship the Morning Flame, the god of the dawn.
> The Morning Flame is the light that comes back. Each night, the dark
> comes. Each morning, the light wins again. That is the whole faith, and
> it is a good one, because it is true every day.
>
> The priests of the Morning Flame wear red and gold. They pray at dawn,
> facing east. Their symbol is a sun disk. A temple has a flame on its
> altar that the priests keep burning. If it goes out, it is a very bad
> sign, and the priests do not sleep until they light it again.
>
> People say "Light to you" when they meet. The answer is "And dawn to
> you." When something goes badly, people say "Dark take it." When
> something goes very badly, they do not say anything, because it is bad
> luck to name the dark twice.

### The Dawnfire

> The greatest flame of the Morning Flame is the Dawnfire. It is the holy
> fire of Ostrey. Some priests say that it fell from the first sunrise.
> Some say that the first king lit it from the sun with a mirror. The
> steward says that it is older than both stories, and that the priests
> should argue less.
>
> In the war, the Dawnfire went in front of the army, in a great bronze
> bowl on a wagon. Where it burned, the creatures of the Unlit King could
> not stand. Now it burns in the chapel of Dawnwatch Keep, near you,
> to keep watch while you sleep. While the Dawnfire burns, the Unlit King
> stays in the dark. If it ever goes out, he is coming back.
>
> I am a flame too. A smaller one. I do not like to compare.

### The Longest Night

> Each winter, on the longest night of the year, the people of Ostrey keep
> a vigil. Nobody sleeps. Each house keeps one candle burning until dawn.
> Children try to stay awake and fail. At the first light, everybody goes
> out and shouts at the sun to welcome it back, and then they eat a very
> large breakfast. It is the best night of the year.
>
> The Dawnwatch always kept the Longest Night on the walls of the keep,
> facing north. You were awake for it every year. Now you have slept
> through three hundred of them. That is a lot of breakfasts.

## Daily Life in Ostrey

### Money

> The king makes three coins. The gold coin is a **sun**, with the sun on
> one side and the face of the king on the other. The silver coin is a
> **star**. Ten stars make a sun. The copper coin is a **bit**, because it
> is a bit of a star. Ten bits make a star.
>
> A loaf of bread costs one bit. A room in an inn costs five bits. A good
> sword costs fifteen suns. A good horse costs more than most people earn
> in a year, so most people walk.

### Work and Food

> Most people in Ostrey work the land. In the Wolds, they keep sheep. In
> the river valleys, they grow barley, oats, and beans. By the Brann,
> they catch eels in the ditches. On the coast, they catch fish and argue
> about fish. In the towns, they make cloth, pots, barrels, nails, candles, and lanterns.
> Lantern makers are the most important craft. I may be a little proud
> about that.
>
> People eat bread, porridge, cheese, beans, onions, and, on a good day,
> mutton. They drink ale, because the water in a town is not always safe.
> At the Longest Night, they eat honey cakes. I have never tasted one. The
> steward said they are "fine". The steward was not a poet.

### Law and the Roads

> The king's law is the same everywhere in Ostrey. A lord judges the small
> cases. The king's judges ride from town to town for the big ones. Theft
> costs a hand, in the old law, but most judges take a fine instead. The
> Shadow can tell you more about this. The Shadow knows the law very well,
> from the other side.
>
> The king's road goes from Kingsholm south through the Wolds, past Saint
> Ivo's shrine, to the March. It is paved with stone. Other roads are mud.
> In the war, soldiers guarded the roads. Before the war, everybody did,
> because of wolves.

### The Year

> Spring is lambs and floods. Summer is hay and the fair at Kingsholm,
> with races, songs, and a goose that bites the winner. Autumn is the
> harvest and the slaughter, and the long evenings with the stories.
> Winter is the cold, and the Longest Night, and then the long wait for
> spring.
>
> In the years of the war, the winters were longer. The snow stayed
> until the end of spring. People said that the cold came first, before
> the Unlit King, the way that wind comes before a storm.

### What People Fear

> Wolves, fire, flood, a bad harvest, and the tax man. And since the war:
> the dark. People in Ostrey do not like to be outside after sunset now.
> They hang a lantern by every door. A house with no light at night is a
> house where something bad happened. I approve of the lanterns. I do not
> approve of the reason.

### Songs and Stories

> Everybody in Ostrey knows "The Five at the Gate", a song about the
> Dawnwatch at the Field of the Five. It has nine verses, and the fifth
> verse is wrong, because the man who wrote it was not there. You were there. You
> can tell him. Well. You could have.
>
> Children play "King in the Dark": one child is the king, and hides, and
> the other children carry candles and try to find the king before the
> candles go out. It is a good game. It is not a nice game.

## The Dawnwatch

> And now, the best part. You.

### The Order

> The Dawnwatch is an order of the king's guard. Its knights swear to the
> king and to the Morning Flame. In peace, they watched the north from the
> walls of this keep. In the war, they were the first into each fight and
> the last out of it. Their task was simple: stand at the edge of the
> dark, and hold it until the light comes.
>
> The Dawnwatch marched at night and arrived at dawn. That was their
> fame. An enemy that camped in the dark woke to see the Dawnwatch on the
> hill, with the sun coming up behind them. It is a very good way to
> arrive. I recommend it. I arrive the same way, on a smaller scale.
>
> At the start of the war, there were forty knights of the Dawnwatch.
> There were many more squires, scouts, healers, and mages who served
> with them. The war was long. By the end, there were five.
>
> That is you.

### The Badge

> The badge of the Dawnwatch is a rising sun: a half circle of gold on a
> straight line, with seven rays. It is on your shields, your cloaks, and
> the doors of this keep. The line is the edge of the night. The sun is
> what comes over it.

### The Oath

> Each knight of the Dawnwatch speaks the oath at dawn, on the walls, with
> a hand on the stone. I know it by heart. I say it each morning. Well. I
> say it each time I think that it is morning. It is hard to tell, down
> here.

> *I stand at the edge of the night.*
> *I keep the light when others sleep.*
> *I go where the dark is, and I stay until the dawn.*
> *I do not run. I do not rest. I do not forget.*
> *I swear to wake the dawn.*

> You will see that the second line has a small problem now. You sleep, and
> the others are awake. The steward said that the oath still counts. He
> said that a promise is about what you do, not about who is snoring.

### The Motto

> Above the door of this hall, the steward carved the motto of the last
> watch:
>
> **"We sleep so that the dawn can wake."**
>
> Before the long sleep, the Dawnwatch said, "We watch so that the dawn
> can wake." The steward changed one word, with a chisel, on the last
> night. He said that it was the most important word that he ever carved.
> He was very tired. I think he was also crying a little. Lanterns notice
> these things.

### The Captain, and the Rest

> The Dawnwatch has a captain, who leads in the field and speaks for the
> order at court. The others are equal. Each one has a task: the healer
> keeps the order alive, the mage keeps the old lore, the scout goes
> first, and the muscle goes through the door that the scout found. Each
> knight can do the work of the others in a hurry. Nobody can do it as
> well.

## The War of the Unlit King

> Now I must tell you about him. Dim the lights. Not me. The other lights.
> There are no other lights. Fine.

### Where He Came From

> About forty years before the long sleep, the winters of Ostrey started
> to grow long. Each year, the snow stayed a little later. Each year, the
> sun seemed a little more tired. People blamed the weather. The priests
> blamed sin. They were all wrong.
>
> Twenty years before the long sleep, he came. **Malgrath, the Unlit
> King.** A demon king out of the dark beyond the Grey Teeth, very tall,
> with a crown of black fire that gave no light. Where he walked, flames
> went out. Candles, hearths, torches, all of them. His voice was deep and
> slow, and the people who heard it never forgot it.
>
> He wanted the sun. Not to take it. To put it out. Forever. He wanted a
> world with no dawn, where the dark would never end, and he would be
> king of all of it.

### The Twenty Years

> He took Caer Dunholm first. Then he came down the Dunn with an army out
> of the dark: cold shadows that walked like men, wolves with no eyes,
> hounds of cinder and sulfur, and men and women of Ostrey who heard his
> voice and went to him. Those were the worst. They had friends, and
> families, and names. They gave them up.
>
> For twenty years, Ostrey fought him. The king's army held the line of
> the Dunn. It lost the line of the Dunn. It took it back. Villages
> burned. Fields went to weeds. In the north, the people lived behind
> walls and kept their lanterns lit all night, every night. The lantern
> makers of Ostrey were never so busy.
>
> The Dawnwatch fought in every year of the war. The knights held the
> bridge at Kingsholm when the shadows came over the ice. They carried the
> Dawnfire through the snow to the Field of the Five. They went into
> the dark of the north, again and again, and came back with fewer each time.

### How It Ended

> In the last year of the war, the king gave the order. The whole army
> marched north with the Dawnfire at its front. The Dawnwatch marched at
> night, as always, and arrived at dawn. The Dawnfire blazed like a second
> sun. The army of the Unlit King broke.
>
> Ostrey pushed Malgrath back into the dark, back past the Grey Teeth, to
> the place he came from. The steward of Dawnwatch Keep was there, and the
> steward told me the end himself. "Back into the dark," he said. "Where
> he belongs."
>
> The bells of Kingsholm rang for three days. People danced in the
> streets. The war was over.

### Why You Sleep

> But the king, and the steward, and the wise priests of the Morning
> Flame all knew one thing. A demon king is not a man. He does not die
> of old age. He does not forget. Pushed back into the dark, he waits.
> One day, in ten years or a hundred or a thousand, he will come again.
>
> And on that day, who will remember how to fight him? The old soldiers
> will be dead. The songs will be wrong. Nine verses, and the fifth one
> wrong.
>
> So the king chose the last five of the Dawnwatch, the ones who knew him
> best, to sleep. A spell of the Morning Flame put you in crystal under
> this keep. The Dawnfire burns in the chapel and keeps watch. When it goes
> out, it means that he is coming back, and the crystal opens, and you
> wake. Not old. Not slow. Exactly as you were on the last night, ready
> to do it all again.
>
> It was the steward's spell. The steward's plan. The steward's hall. I am
> very proud of him. He made me on the same night, so that somebody would
> be here when you woke.

### The Last Night

> I remember it. It is the first thing I remember, so it is a very
> important memory to me.
>
> The hall was full of candles. The five of you walked in with your gear,
> and the steward was waiting. You each said goodbye to somebody, or you
> did not, and you will have to tell me about that, because I do not know
> who they were. You each lay down in the crystal. The steward said the
> words of the spell. The crystal closed, slowly, like ice on a pond.
>
> Then the steward put me in that niche in the wall, and lit me, and
> said: "Do not let them oversleep."
>
> And then he left, and I waited.

## You, the Sleepers

> I know each of you. Well, I know about each of you. The steward told me.
> He talked about you the way that a father talks about his children, if
> the children were knights and he was a little afraid of them.
>
> I do not know your names. The steward said that you would tell me
> yourselves. I think he forgot to tell me them. It was a busy night.

### The Shield

> The captain of the Dawnwatch. A human soldier of Ostrey, raised on the
> drill yard, and the best shield in the kingdom. The Shield spoke for the
> order before the king himself, and did not bow too low. The others
> follow the Shield, not because they must, but because the Shield is
> always in front. The captain made a promise on the last night. I saw the
> captain whisper it to somebody at the door of the hall. I did not hear
> it. I would very much like to know.

### The Light

> The healer of the Dawnwatch, a dwarf priest of the Morning Flame. Dwarves
> are rare in the Wolds; this one came down from the holds under the Grey
> Teeth as a young priest, to serve the god of the dawn among people who
> see the sun every day. The Light kept the order alive for twenty years
> of war, and lost very few. When the crystal closed, the Light was
> praying. The steward said, "Look at the face of that one. That one sees
> something." I wonder what it was.

### The Spark

> The mage of the Dawnwatch, an elf, and the youngest knight that the
> order ever took. The Spark knows the old stories and the old spells, and
> remembers more history than the king's own librarians, which is easy,
> because the king's librarians are mostly asleep. On the first day in the
> Dawnwatch, the Spark showed off a spell in front of the whole order. The
> steward told me that story with a very strange face. He did not tell me
> how it ended.

### The Shadow

> The scout of the Dawnwatch, a halfling, and a thief from the narrow
> streets of Kingsholm. The captain caught the Shadow in the act, and
> instead of handing the Shadow to the judge, offered a second chance.
> The Shadow took it, and became the best scout in the order. Mostly. The
> steward said that the Shadow "went straight, in a crooked kind of way."
> On the last night, I am almost certain that something went missing from
> somebody. I am not saying who. I am a lantern. I have no proof.

### The Storm

> The muscle of the Dawnwatch, a goliath from the high passes of the Grey
> Teeth, where the Storm guided travelers over the snow before the war
> came over the mountains. The Storm is strong, loud, and honest, and did
> not want to sleep. The Storm said so to the king, to the steward, to the
> captain, and to me, and I was only one hour old. But at the end, the
> Storm lay down in the crystal with the others. I do not know why. I
> would like to.

### If You Are Somebody Else

> Perhaps you are not one of these five. Perhaps the steward described you
> wrong. He was tired. If you made your own knight of the Dawnwatch, then
> you were still one of the last five, and you still swore the oath, and
> you still lay down in the crystal on the last night. Ask yourself the
> same question that the others ask: who did you leave behind, and what
> did you leave unfinished?

## A Last Word from Wick

> That is all. That is everything. Draft four hundred and twelve. I think
> it went well. I think it was the best one.
>
> One more thing. The steward said one more thing to me, on the last
> night, after you were all asleep. He sat on the floor, there, by the
> door, for a long time. Then he said: "When they wake, Wick, the world
> will be different. Help them anyway."
>
> I did not understand it. I still do not. The world will be exactly the
> same, I think. It has only been a little while.
>
> Hasn't it?
