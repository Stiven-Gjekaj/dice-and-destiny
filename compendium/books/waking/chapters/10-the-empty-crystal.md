# The Empty Crystal

This is the last chapter of Book One. The Dawnwatch goes to Kingsholm, the
old capital of Ostrey, where the Hush gathers for its last rite. The party
breaks the cult and defeats Mother Sallow. Then it goes down into the vault
under the old palace, where the Unlit King slept, and finds the crystal
empty. On the crystal is the badge of the Dawnwatch.

The chapter has two jobs. It gives the group a true victory, the kind that a
group can end a campaign on. And it tells the truth that the whole book has
hidden: Malgrath was Sir Garrath, the first of the Dawnwatch.

|---|---|
| **Levels** | 10 (the characters go to level 11 at the end) |

## Running This Chapter

**Goal:** The party ends the Hush, and learns what the Dawnwatch really is.

Read the whole chapter before you run it. Then read the handout. The last
letter of Haskel Orme is the heart of this chapter, and the players should
read it themselves at the table.

### What really happens

> **GM tip:** This box holds the full truth of Book One. Do not tell it to
> the players. They find it in Scene 6.
>
> Three hundred years ago, Sir Garrath was the first knight of the sun and
> the first of the Dawnwatch. He saw that the sun was dying. He tried to take
> its fire into himself to keep it safe, and the dark took him instead. He
> became the Unlit King. Ostrey could not kill him. Steward Haskel Orme put
> him to sleep in crystal under Kingsholm. Then Orme put five more of the
> Dawnwatch to sleep under Dawnwatch Keep, with the same spell, so that
> someone who remembered Garrath would wake if he woke. Orme wrote the lie
> that the world knows: a demon came from the dark, and Ostrey pushed him
> back.
>
> The villagers called the fallen knight "Mal Garrath", the bad Garrath. In
> three hundred years, the name wore down to "Malgrath".
>
> When the Hush put out the Dawnfire in the chapel of the keep, the crystal
> under Kingsholm cracked. Garrath woke, and he left. He is in the north now,
> in Coldharrow. That is Book Two. Under Kingsholm there is only an empty
> crystal, a guard that he left behind, and the last letter of Haskel Orme.

### What the Hush wants

The Hush knows that its king is awake. It does not know where he went. It
thinks that the vault under Kingsholm is a door: if the cult puts out a
piece of the Dawnfire on the crystal, the king will hear, and the Long
Night will come early, and the sun will go out and burn again, new.

Mother Sallow has gone further than her followers know. She went down into
the vault and drank the dark that the sleeper left in the crystal. It made
her strong. It is also killing her. In this chapter she is **Mother Sallow,
Unbound**, a stronger form of the leader of the Hush.

The rite is on the shortest day of the year. On that day, the Hush puts out
whatever piece of the Dawnfire it holds. If the Hush still has the Ember of
Saint Ivo (see The Hush, earlier in this book), that is the piece. If the
party took the Ember back, the Hush wants the First Lamp or the Fennick
Candle, and it will try to take one from the party in Kingsholm.

### The optional systems in this chapter

No system starts in this chapter. The systems that your table uses stay on.

- **Techniques.** The encounters below use the XP budgets with no change.
  Each Scaling box says what to add when Techniques are on.

- **Bonds.** The last scene is a good moment to spend a Bond, or to form one.

## Scene 1: The Last Council (1 session or less)

**Goal:** Send the party to Kingsholm with the allies that it earned.

### How the party learns of Kingsholm

The party has two or three clues by now. Use the ones that your group found:

- **The last letter in the study** (The Keep Again): the steward left the
  rest of the truth "with him, under Kingsholm."
- **The words of the unmasked agent** (Three Banners): the Hush gathers at
  Kingsholm, in the old capital.
- **The black stone.** Since Three Banners, it hums toward the north-west,
  toward the River Dunn, not toward the far north.

If the players missed all three, the black stone is enough. One night,
the bearer dreams of a cold hall under a broken palace, with bells that do
not ring.

### At the keep

Start at Dawnwatch Keep. The keep is the home of the party now. Let the
players feel it.

> Snow on the Wolds. The keep looks different from the day you woke under
> it. There is glass in the windows of the hall. There is smoke from the
> kitchen. Someone has hung green branches over the Dawn Door for the
> midwinter feast.
>
> Wick sits on the table in the great hall, between the bread and the maps.
> He is quiet tonight. "The shortest day is in nine days," he says. "I
> counted. I always count." His flame is small. "The stone is humming,
> isn't it? I can hear it from here."

Hesper Wren, the steward of the keep, brings the news of the month. Take a
last Stronghold Turn now, if your table uses Strongholds. Do not roll the
event: the event this month is the Hush.

> **GM tip:** This scene is a good place to remind the players of what
> they built. Name the people who live at the keep now. Name the rooms
> that the party built. Name the friends who sent letters. The players
> should feel that they have something to lose, and something to come home
> to.

### Who comes with the party

Each power that trusts the party sends help. Use the renown of the
characters with each faction (Factions and Renown in the Game Master's
Compendium). If your table keeps one renown for the whole party, use it. If
your table does not use Factions, use what happened in Gaunt and in Three
Banners.

| Ally | Who needs what | What comes |
|---|---|---|
| Gaunt | A character is a Member (renown 10) of Gaunt, or the party sided with Gaunt or stopped the war | A company of twenty soldiers of Gaunt (Line Pikemen and Crossbow Sergeants) under Captain Ilse Varro, if she lives. They hold the Kings' Bridge (Scene 3). |
| The Church | A character is a Member of the Church, or the party sided with the Church or stopped the war | Brother Cade and six healers. After each fight in Kingsholm, each character regains Hit Points as if they spent two Hit Point Dice, at no cost. Cade can also tend injuries. |
| The Lampwrights | The party has the First Lamp, or a character is a Friend (renown 3) of the guild | Perrin Hale sends a cart of lamps that burn without oil. In Kingsholm, the party never stands in darkness by accident. The Hush Grey Choir's song still dims them. |
| The keep | The party owns the keep as a stronghold | Followers equal to the keep's Renown come as porters, guides, and guards. They keep the camp. The camp can't be surprised. |

The allies make the road and the camp safer. They do not fight the big
battles. Tell the players this plainly: the soldiers can hold a bridge,
but only the Dawnwatch can go into the dark.

> **GM tip:** If a faction is an enemy of the party, it does not send help.
> It can send a messenger instead, with a short, cold note: "Do what you
> must. We will watch." This is a good moment to show that the war in Three
> Banners changed things.

### Wick before the road

On the last night before the road, Wick asks to talk to the party alone.

> "I want to say something," says Wick, "before we go, in case I don't
> get to say it after." His flame goes very small, and then very bright.
> "The steward made me. I know that now. He made me on the last night, and
> he did not tell me his name, and he did not tell me why. I was angry
> about that. I am still angry about that." He looks at the black stone.
> "But he made me to wake you. And I did. Whatever is in that vault, I want
> you to know that I would do it again."

Give each player a moment to answer Wick. A good answer earns Heroic
Inspiration.

## Scene 2: The Road Along the Dunn (half a session)

**Goal:** A short last journey. Show the world getting darker.

Kingsholm is 3 days from the keep, off the road, along the River Dunn (see
the travel table in the Gazetteer). In midwinter, the road is hard.

If your table uses Journeys, run it as a journey of 3 days. Each character
takes a role (Guide, Scout, Quartermaster, Lookout). Use the Supply Die.
The weather is cold every day. Roll on the table below once each day, in
place of the usual encounter roll.

> On the second morning, the sun comes up late. Wick counts. "Eleven
> minutes," he says. "Eleven minutes later than it should. Last year it was
> four." Nobody answers him.

### Events on the road

Roll a d6 once each day, or choose.

| d6 | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The grey dome.** From a hill, the party sees Kingsholm far away: a dome of grey air over the ruins, like a bowl turned upside down. The sun does not shine inside it. |
| 2 | **Pilgrims going home.** A family of the Church walks south, away from Kingsholm. They saw people in grey go north in long lines. "They didn't speak. Not one word. Not even the children." |
| 3 | **The frozen ford.** The Dunn is frozen at a ford. The ice is thin in the middle. DC 15 Wisdom (Survival) for the Guide to find a safe way; on a failure, one character falls through and gains 1 Exhaustion level from the cold, unless the character has Resistance to Cold damage. |
| 4 | **Hush outriders.** A patrol of the Hush rides south to look for the Dawnwatch (see "Hush Outriders" below). If the Scout succeeded today, the party can avoid them or surprise them. |
| 5 | **A dead lamp.** A shrine of old Ostrey by the road, with an iron lamp on it. The lamp is cold. Somebody pinched out its flame and left a grey ribbon tied to it. |
| 6 | **The stone pulls.** The black stone hums so loudly that each character can hear it. Its bearer knows, without a doubt, that it wants to go home. It points at Kingsholm. |

### Encounter: Hush Outriders (optional, Low)

The Hush sends riders to find the Dawnwatch before it reaches Kingsholm.
They want the black stone and the pieces of the Dawnfire. They fight in a
line on the road and try to take one item and ride away.

- **Budget:** Low, for 4 characters of level 10: 1,600 × 4 = 6,400 XP.
- **Monsters:** 4 Hush Night Wardens (4 × 1,100 = 4,400 XP) and 4 Cultist
  Fanatics (4 × 450 = 1,800 XP), on horses. Total: 6,200 XP.

{{statblock: Hush Night Warden}}

**Tactics.** The wardens Snuff the party's torches and lanterns first (Wick
and the pieces of the Dawnfire stay lit, and this scares them). The
fanatics go for the character who carries the black stone. When half of
them fall, the rest ride north to warn Kingsholm. If one of them escapes,
the Hush at the Kings' Bridge is ready (no surprise in Scene 3).

> **Scaling:** The budget is 1,600 XP for each character.
>
> - **3 characters (4,800 XP):** 3 Hush Night Wardens and 3 Cultist
>   Fanatics (4,650 XP).
> - **4 characters (6,400 XP):** as written (6,200 XP).
> - **5 characters (8,000 XP):** 5 Hush Night Wardens and 4 Cultist
>   Fanatics (7,300 XP).
> - **6 characters (9,600 XP):** 5 Hush Night Wardens, 1 Hush Dusk Priest,
>   and 2 Cultist Fanatics (9,300 XP).
> - **With Techniques on:** add one Cultist Fanatic for each four
>   characters.

**After the fight.** A captured outrider does not speak at first. A DC 15
Charisma (Intimidation) or Charisma (Persuasion) check, or a kind word and
a warm fire, makes the outrider talk. The outrider tells the party about
the rite on the shortest day, and that "Mother has been down to see the
king. She came back different. Her eyes are all black now."

## Scene 3: Kingsholm (1 session)

**Goal:** The party enters the ruins and breaks the outer ring of the Hush.

### The old capital

Kingsholm was the capital of Ostrey. It stands on both banks of the River
Dunn, at a bend where the river runs fast and deep. The palace stood on a
hill on the north bank. Three hundred years of weather, a civil war, and a
long time with nobody to fix the roofs have made it a ruin. Nobody lives
here now. The people of today say that the place is bad luck, and they are
right.

> The grey dome is bigger from close by. It sits over the ruins like a lid.
> At its edge, the snow stops. Inside, there is no snow, no wind, and no
> sun. The light goes grey and then goes out, as if you walk into the
> evening in the middle of the day.
>
> Inside the dome, the city is very quiet. Broken towers. A bridge of white
> stone across a black river. On the hill above it, a palace with no roof,
> and over the palace the bell towers, with no bells in them. Somewhere,
> very far away, many people hum one long note.

**Inside the dome.** It is always night inside the dome. The area is in
darkness, except where there is a light. Nonmagical lights work, but the
Hush puts them out (see the Snuff bonus action of the Hush Night Warden).
Wick, the First Lamp, the Fennick Candle, and the lamps of the Lampwrights
burn normally. Spells that make light work, but Hush Grey Choirs dim them.

> **GM tip:** Make the dark feel heavy, not hopeless. Each time the party
> lights a lamp in Kingsholm, describe how the grey air pulls back from it,
> just a little. The party is the only light in the city. That is the
> image of this chapter.

### Ways in

Ask the players how they want to go in. All three ways lead to the Kings'
Bridge and the palace hill.

| Way | What happens |
|---|---|
| **The front.** Across the Kings' Bridge, in the open. | The Hush sees the party at once. Run "The Kings' Bridge" with no surprise. If Gaunt soldiers came, they hold the far end of the bridge and fight the Cultist Fanatics there: remove the fanatics from the encounter. |
| **Quiet.** Through the drowned streets of the south bank, and over the river on the ice. | A group Dexterity (Stealth) check, DC 15. On a success, the party reaches the bridge from the side and surprises the Hush. On a failure, a sentry hums a warning, and the party fights on the bridge with no surprise. The ice is Difficult Terrain. |
| **In grey.** In the robes of captured outriders. | A DC 16 Charisma (Deception) check by the leader, with Advantage if the party took an outrider alive. The Hush speaks little, so a character who stays silent does well. On a success, the party walks past the bridge and can skip the fight, or start it with surprise. On a failure, the Hush sees the light of Wick under a robe. |

### Places in Kingsholm

Use these places as the party crosses the city. They are in the order in
which the party usually finds them.

#### K1. The Market of Statues

A wide square on the south bank, full of statues of the knights of the sun.
Most of them stand, with snow on their shoulders from before the dome came.
One statue lies face down in the square. Its name was cut off the base with
a chisel, long ago. Only four letters are left: **...RATH**.

| Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Intelligence (History) | 15 | The statue is the oldest in the square, and the base is the biggest. Whoever it was, it was the first knight that the city honored. |
| Wisdom (Perception) | 13 | Someone put a grey ribbon on the fallen statue's hand. The Hush comes here to pray. |
| Intelligence (Investigation) | 18 | On the back of the base, under the moss, the chisel missed one word: **GAR**. The rest is gone. |

Do not explain the statue. Let the players think about it. It pays off in
Scene 6.

#### K2. The Drowned Streets

The streets along the south bank flood each spring. Now they are frozen.
The houses are empty. Grey ribbons hang on many doors, one for each family
that joined the Hush. Some of those families came from the villages that
the party saw in the earlier chapters.

#### K3. The Kings' Bridge

A bridge of white stone, 30 feet wide and 300 feet long, with a gatehouse
at the north end. The Hush holds the bridge. See the encounter below.

#### K4. The Grey Camp

Under the palace hill, on the north bank, the Hush has a camp: a hundred
tents, grey and quiet. Most of the people here are not fighters. They are
the poor, the frightened, and the tired, who believe that the sun is dying
and that only the Unlit King can save it. When Mother Sallow falls, most of
them give up (see Scene 4).

A party that goes through the camp before the throne hall sees the faces
of the Hush. Use this to make the end of the Hush feel like a rescue, not
only a battle.

> A woman sits in front of a tent with a baby in her arms. She hums with
> the others, very softly. When she sees your light, she does not run. She
> looks at it for a long time, as if she has forgotten what it is.

#### K5. The Cathedral of the Sun

The great church of old Ostrey, on the side of the palace hill. The Hush
uses it as a prison. See "The Cathedral" below.

#### K6. The Palace Steps

Four hundred steps up the hill, to the gate of the palace. The steps are in
plain view of the throne hall. The Hush does not guard them. Mother Sallow
wants the party to come up.

#### K7. The Throne Hall

The rite happens here. See Scene 4.

#### K8. The Stair of Bells

Behind the throne, a stair goes down into the hill, under the old bell
towers. At its foot is the vault. See Scene 5.

### Encounter: The Kings' Bridge (Moderate)

> At the north end of the bridge, the grey robes stand in a crowd, close
> together, shoulder to shoulder. They are the ones who hum. As you come
> closer, the note gets louder, and your light gets smaller. In front of
> them stand soldiers in grey mail with no badge. Behind them, on the roof
> of the gatehouse, a priest in grey lifts a fist.

The Hush wants to stop the party here, or at least to slow it down until
the rite is done.

- **Budget:** Moderate, for 4 characters of level 10: 2,300 × 4 = 9,200 XP.
- **Monsters:** 1 Hush Grey Choir (2,300 XP), 2 Hush Night Wardens (2,200
  XP), 1 Hush Dusk Priest (2,900 XP), and 4 Cultist Fanatics (1,800 XP).
  Total: 9,200 XP.

{{statblock: Hush Grey Choir}}

{{statblock: Hush Dusk Priest}}

**The battlefield.** The bridge is 30 feet wide. Its low walls give Half
Cover to a creature that crouches behind them. The river is 40 feet below:
a creature that falls in takes 2d6 Bludgeoning damage from the ice and
must swim (DC 15 Strength (Athletics)) or be swept 60 feet downstream each
round. The gatehouse roof is 20 feet up, and the Dusk Priest stands on it.

**Tactics.**

- The **choir** walks forward along the middle of the bridge, so that its
  song covers the most ground. It uses Hymn of Quiet as soon as it can catch
  three characters in it.
- The **wardens** stand in front of the choir and protect it with Close the
  Ranks.
- The **fanatics** try to get around the party and reach the character who
  carries a piece of the Dawnfire.
- The **priest** uses Snuff the Sun on the biggest group of characters, then
  Hold Person on a fighter, then Word of Night.

**When the bridge falls.** When the priest and the choir drop, the others
run up the hill. They do not fight to the death. The way to the palace is
open.

> **Scaling:** The budget is 2,300 XP for each character.
>
> - **3 characters (6,900 XP):** remove the Dusk Priest, and add 1 Cultist
>   Fanatic (6,750 XP).
> - **4 characters (9,200 XP):** as written (9,200 XP).
> - **5 characters (11,500 XP):** add 1 Hush Night Warden and 1 Cultist
>   Fanatic (10,750 XP).
> - **6 characters (13,800 XP):** add 1 Hush Night Warden and 1 Hush Dusk
>   Priest (13,200 XP).
> - **With Techniques on:** add one Cultist Fanatic for each four
>   characters.
> - **Gaunt soldiers hold the bridge:** remove all the Cultist Fanatics.

### Encounter: The Cathedral (optional, Low)

The Hush keeps prisoners in the cathedral: pilgrims who would not join, two
Lampwright journeymen who came to look for the First Lamp, and anyone else
that the party lost on the way. At midnight on the shortest day, the Hush
plans to "let them see the last dark": to leave them in the vault.

> The great windows of the cathedral are dark, because there is no sun
> behind them. Each window shows a knight of the sun. On the biggest
> window, over the altar, someone broke out the face of the knight, long
> ago, one small piece of glass at a time. Under the window, the Hush has
> tied a hundred grey ribbons.
>
> The prisoners sit in the middle of the floor, tied together. Around them,
> the Hush stands in a ring, with their backs to the prisoners and their
> faces to the doors.

- **Budget:** Low, for 4 characters of level 10: 1,600 × 4 = 6,400 XP.
- **Monsters:** 1 Hush Dusk Priest (2,900 XP), 1 Hush Night Warden (1,100
  XP), 4 Cultist Fanatics (1,800 XP), and 8 Cultists (200 XP). Total:
  6,000 XP.

**Not everyone fights.** The eight Cultists are new to the Hush. They are
scared. When the priest falls, each of them drops their weapon and sits
down. A character can also stop one with words: as an action, a DC 13
Charisma (Persuasion) check makes one Cultist put down its weapon.

> **Teach: not every fight is to the death.** "Most people don't want to
> die. When their leader falls, or when you offer them a way out, they give
> up or they run." In this chapter, the Hush breaks because the people in
> it stop believing. Each Cultist that the party talks down is one more
> person who goes home after Book One.

> **Scaling:** The budget is 1,600 XP for each character.
>
> - **3 characters (4,800 XP):** remove the Night Warden (4,900 XP).
> - **4 characters (6,400 XP):** as written (6,000 XP).
> - **5 characters (8,000 XP):** add 1 Hush Night Warden and 2 Cultist
>   Fanatics (8,000 XP).
> - **6 characters (9,600 XP):** add 2 Hush Night Wardens and 3 Cultist
>   Fanatics (9,550 XP).
> - **With Techniques on:** add one Cultist for each four characters.

**After the fight.** The prisoners thank the party. One of them, an old
pilgrim of the Church, knows the cathedral. She tells the party that "the
broken window was the first knight, the one the old songs don't name. The
old kingdom broke his face out, the stories say. And the Hush ties ribbons
under it, and cries. I never understood that."

## Scene 4: The Throne Hall (1 session)

**Goal:** The victory. The party defeats Mother Sallow, and the Hush breaks.

### The rite

> The throne hall of Ostrey has no roof. Above it, where the sky should be,
> the grey dome turns slowly, like smoke in a jar. The floor is covered with
> people in grey, kneeling in rows, a hundred of them, humming. At the end of
> the hall stands the old throne, cracked down the middle. In front of the
> throne is a table of black stone. On the table lies a small light.
>
> Next to it stands a thin woman in grey. You know her. But her eyes are
> black now, all black, from edge to edge, and when she breathes out, her
> breath is dark, like ink in water.
>
> "Dawnwatch," says Mother Sallow. She does not raise her voice. Every person
> in the hall hears it. "You came. Good. I wanted you to see this."

The small light on the table is the piece of the Dawnfire that the Hush
holds: the Ember of Saint Ivo, if the Hush still has it. If the party took
the Ember back, the table is empty, and Mother Sallow wants the First Lamp
or the Fennick Candle from the party.

### Mother Sallow speaks

Mother Sallow does not attack at once. She talks first. She has waited a
long time to say these things, and she says them to the Dawnwatch, not to
her followers. Play her calm, tired, and sure. She is not cruel. She thinks
that she is the only honest person in the hall.

Use some of these lines, or all of them:

- "The days are shorter. You have seen it. Your little lantern counts it.
  Eleven minutes this year. Twenty next year. The sun is dying, sleepers.
  Nobody in Gaunt or in Saint Ivo's will say it, because there is nothing
  that they can do about it."
- "There was one man who tried to do something about it. Only one. Ostrey
  called him a demon for it, and put him in a box under this hill."
- "He was a man once. Did you know that? A knight. They don't teach that in
  the songs." She smiles. "I went down to see him. The box is empty. I
  drank what he left behind. It is cold, and it is very strong."
- "A fire that goes out can be lit again. A fire that burns down to nothing
  cannot. Let it go out. Let him light it again. That is all the Hush has
  ever wanted."
- "Give me the stone. Give me the light. Kneel with us. You can be the
  first to see the new dawn."

> **GM tip:** Mother Sallow is partly right, and the players may feel it.
> That is good. Do not argue for her, and do not argue against her. If a
> player asks a good question, answer as she would. The one thing that she
> cannot answer is this: "What happens to all the people under the sun
> while it is out?" Her answer is a long silence, and then: "He will know."

### Ways to stop the rite

The rite ends when the piece of the Dawnfire is safe, or when Mother Sallow
falls. The party can do it in several ways at once.

| What the party does | Check | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Takes the piece from the table | DC 16 Dexterity (Sleight of Hand), or just grab it in the fight | The rite stops. Mother Sallow can't put out the piece while a member of the Dawnwatch holds it. She attacks at once. |
| Talks to the kneeling Hush | DC 17 Charisma (Persuasion), as an action, once per round | Ten of the kneeling Hush stop humming and get up. On three successes, the hum breaks: the Hush Grey Choir loses its Song of the Long Night trait for the rest of the fight. |
| Shows them the light | The First Lamp, the Fennick Candle, or Wick, held high | Advantage on the Persuasion check above. Many of the Hush have not seen a clean flame in years. |
| Waits too long | No check | At the end of the third round of talk, Mother Sallow puts out the piece on the table (if there is one), and the fight starts. A piece that she puts out is gone for good. |

> **GM tip:** The party should not lose the Ember of Saint Ivo by accident.
> If the players are slow, let Wick shout: "The light! Somebody get the
> light!" Losing the Ember is a real cost, so it is fine if it happens, but
> only because the players chose to talk too long.

### Encounter: Mother Sallow, Unbound (High)

> Mother Sallow sighs. "Then you will see it from the floor." She opens her
> hand, and the dark pours out of it like water. Around you, the grey robes
> rise.

This is the victory of Book One. Make it big.

- **Budget:** High, for 4 characters of level 10: 3,100 × 4 = 12,400 XP.
- **Monsters:** Mother Sallow, Unbound (7,200 XP), 1 Hush Grey Choir (2,300
  XP), and 2 Hush Night Wardens (2,200 XP). Total: 11,700 XP.

{{statblock: Mother Sallow, Unbound}}

The kneeling Hush are not part of the fight. They scatter to the walls when
it starts. They do not attack the party, and the party should not attack
them.

**The battlefield.** The hall is 120 feet long and 60 feet wide, with no
roof. It is dark, except for the party's lights. The cracked throne is on a
dais, 5 feet high, at the north end. Six broken pillars give Half Cover.
Around the edge of the hall, iron braziers burn with a cold grey fire that
gives no light; a creature that touches one takes 2d6 Cold damage.

**Tactics.**

- **Mother Sallow** opens with The Long Night on the most characters that she
  can catch. Then she uses Word of Ending on a spellcaster and Grey Knife on
  anyone who comes close. She uses Fade to stay in the dark and out of
  reach. She uses Hold Monster on the strongest fighter. She spends her
  legendary actions on Snuff when the party has more than one light.
- **The choir** stays near Mother Sallow, so that its song makes her harder
  to see and her enemies worse at Concentration.
- **The wardens** guard Mother Sallow with Close the Ranks.
- **When Mother Sallow is Bloodied,** her Unbound trait goes off. Describe
  it: the dark bursts out of her like a flock of black birds, and for one
  moment the whole hall is black. Then it is her own voice, very small:
  "Oh. It's taking more than I thought."

> **Scaling:** The budget is 3,100 XP for each character.
>
> - **3 characters (9,300 XP):** Mother Sallow, Unbound and 2 Hush Night
>   Wardens (9,400 XP). No choir.
> - **4 characters (12,400 XP):** as written (11,700 XP).
> - **5 characters (15,500 XP):** add 1 Hush Dusk Priest (14,600 XP).
> - **6 characters (18,600 XP):** add 1 Hush Dusk Priest, 1 Hush Night
>   Warden, and a second Hush Grey Choir (18,000 XP).
> - **With Techniques on:** add one Hush Night Warden for each four
>   characters, or give Mother Sallow 10% more Hit Points (205 in all).

> **GM tip:** If the fight goes very badly, the kneeling Hush can save the
> party without meaning to. One of them, the woman with the baby from the
> Grey Camp, stands up between Mother Sallow and a fallen character and
> says "No." Mother Sallow hesitates for one round. It is the first time
> anybody in the Hush has said no to her.

### The fall of Mother Sallow

When Mother Sallow drops to 0 Hit Points, she does not die at once, unless
the players want her dead. Ask the player who struck the last blow: "Do you
kill her, or do you leave her alive?" Both are fine.

> The dark goes out of Mother Sallow all at once, the way the air goes out
> of a bellows. She sits down on the step of the throne. Her eyes are grey
> again, an ordinary, tired grey. She looks very old.
>
> "It's empty," she says. "Down there. Go and look. Go and look, and then
> tell me who the monster is."

If she lives, Mother Sallow is a broken old woman. She does not fight
again. She can go to the Church in Saint Ivo's, to Gaunt for a trial, or
to the keep as a prisoner. Book Two does not need her.

### The Hush breaks

When Mother Sallow falls, the hum stops. Each creature of the Hush that is
still in the fight drops its weapon or runs. The kneeling Hush look at
each other, as if they have just woken up.

Then the grey dome breaks.

> A sound like a sheet of ice that cracks on a lake. Above the hall, the
> grey splits open, and light falls through the crack. Not lamplight. Not
> the light of a spell. Sunlight, thin and white and winter-cold, the first
> sunlight in Kingsholm for a month.
>
> It falls across the throne and the floor and the faces of the Hush. Some
> of them cover their eyes. Some of them cry. A child somewhere starts to
> laugh, and cannot stop.
>
> "Oh," says Wick, very quietly. "Oh, look at that."

This is the victory. Stop for a moment and let the players enjoy it. The
Hush is broken. Most of its people will go home in the next days, and the
lights will come back on in the villages of the land. Give each player a
moment to say what their character does in the first minute of sunlight.

> **GM tip:** If your group stops here because of time, this is a fine
> place to end a session. Do not go down into the vault in the last ten
> minutes of a session. The truth deserves a fresh start.

The party can take a Short Rest in the throne hall. Brother Cade and his
healers come up from the bridge, if they came to Kingsholm. Nobody
attacks the party here any more.

## When Things Go Wrong

The last chapter of a long campaign carries a lot of weight. Here is what to
do when the dice or the plans go somewhere you did not expect.

### The party loses the fight in the throne hall

A total party defeat in the last chapter is a sad way to end a book. Do
not let it happen by bad luck alone.

- **Mother Sallow does not kill the Dawnwatch.** She wants them to see the
  dark. If every character drops, she has them carried down to the vault and
  left at the foot of the empty crystal, alive, with 1 Hit Point each. Then
  she goes back up to finish the rite. Run Scene 5 and Scene 6 with the
  party alone in the vault, with no gear but what they carry. The Unlit Knight
  is there. When the party comes back up, the rite is half done: the dome is
  darker, and Mother Sallow is waiting. Run the fight again, and give the
  party Advantage on Initiative, because she does not expect them.
- **The allies come.** If Brother Cade, Ilse Varro, or the soldiers of the
  keep came to Kingsholm, they arrive at the end of the third round of a bad
  fight. Each of them takes one Hush Night Warden out of the fight. Do this
  once. The Dawnwatch should finish the fight themselves.

### Mother Sallow puts out the Ember

If the Ember of Saint Ivo goes out, it is gone. The coal turns grey and cold
in the hand, and it crumbles. The Church will grieve, and Abbess Merrow will
never quite forgive the party. Do not undo it. Give it weight:

- The grey dome does not break when Mother Sallow falls. It thins, and the
  sun comes through it weak and pale, like the sun of a long winter.
- In the vault, the empty crystal is colder. The Unlit Knight has 10% more
  Hit Points.
- In the last words, Malgrath says one more line, before the last one: "One
  more light out. Do you feel the cold? I feel it every day."

### The players go to the vault first

Some groups skip the throne hall and look for the Stair of Bells. Let them
try. The stair is behind the throne, so the party must cross the hall, and
Mother Sallow sees them. If they sneak past (a group Dexterity (Stealth)
check against DC 18, with Disadvantage if anyone carries an uncovered light),
they reach the vault first. Run the vault and the letter. Then Mother Sallow
comes down the stair with her guards, and the fight with her happens in the
vault, at the foot of the empty crystal. That is a strong scene too. She
reads the letter over their shoulders, and she laughs, and then she cries.

### The players want to destroy the black stone

After the letter, a player may want to break the black stone, or leave it in
the crystal. Both are fine.

- **Left in the crystal:** the stone stays. The bearer's Mark stays too, but
  it stops growing. The last words still happen: the voice of Malgrath comes
  through the badge of Sir Garrath instead, if a character wears it, or
  through the empty crystal itself, as a whisper up the Stair of Bells.
- **Broken:** the stone does not break. Steel, fire, and spells of level 5 or
  lower do nothing to it. It is a piece of the spell of Haskel Orme, and only
  a greater magic can end it. That is a question for Book Two.

### A character dies

Death in the last chapter matters. If a character dies in Kingsholm, do not
rush to raise them. Brother Cade can cast a spell of raising if the players
want it, but ask first: some players want the death to stand. If it stands,
give the fallen character the first place in the last scene. The voice of
Malgrath speaks their name first. He knew all five of them, once.

## Scene 5: The Stair of Bells (half a session)

**Goal:** The last fight of Book One, against the guard that the sleeper
left behind.

### Down

Behind the cracked throne, a stair goes down into the hill. In the days of
Ostrey, the great bells of the palace hung in the towers above it, and the
ropes ran down through the stair. The ropes are gone. The holes in the
roof of the stair are still there.

> The stair goes down and down. The air gets colder, and drier, and very
> still. The sunlight from the hall does not follow you. After two hundred
> steps, the walls change. They are not rough stone any more. They are
> smooth and carved, and the carving is one you know.
>
> Roots of light, carved in the stone. Lines that go out from one point,
> across the floor and up the walls, like the roots of a tree. You have
> seen this before. You woke up in the middle of it.
>
> At the foot of the stair is a door. Over the door, words are carved in
> the stone: "WE SLEEP SO THAT THE DAWN CAN WAKE."

Let the players react. They know these words: they are over the door of
the Hall of Sleepers, under Dawnwatch Keep, where they woke in the
tutorial. Wick knows them too.

> Wick's flame goes very small. "That's our door," he says. "That's the
> door of our hall. Why is our door here?"

| Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Intelligence (Arcana) | 15 | The carvings are the same spell as in the Hall of Sleepers, the same lines, the same hand. Whoever made the hall under the keep made this one too. |
| Intelligence (History) | 13 | Halls like this were made for the Dawnwatch, and only for the Dawnwatch. Nobody else in Ostrey slept in crystal. |
| Wisdom (Insight), about Wick | 10 | Wick is not only scared. He is starting to understand something, and he does not want to. |

The door is not locked. It opens at a push. Beyond it is the vault.

### The vault

> A round hall of stone, very like the one where you woke. In the middle,
> on a low step, lies one coffin of blue crystal, bigger than yours. The
> crystal is broken open. Not cracked: broken, from the inside, the way an
> egg breaks when the chick comes out.
>
> In front of the broken coffin, a knight stands guard. Black plate,
> black as wet slate. A greatsword, point down, both hands on the hilt. It
> does not move. The light of your lamps goes dim around it, as if the
> knight drinks it.
>
> Then the visor turns toward you.

### Encounter: The Unlit Knight (the end of Act II)

The Unlit Knight is the creature of the end of Act II. It is not Garrath. It
is the dark that the sleeper left behind in the crystal when he woke, in
the shape of the knight that he was, still keeping watch. It does not
speak. It attacks anyone who comes close to the crystal: the Hush too.

- **Budget:** Moderate, for 4 characters of level 10: 2,300 × 4 = 9,200 XP.
- **Monsters:** the Unlit Knight (8,400 XP) and 3 Shadows (300 XP), that
  rise from the corners of the vault on the first round. Total: 8,700 XP.

{{statblock: The Unlit Knight}}

**The battlefield.** The vault is a circle 80 feet across, with a domed
roof 40 feet high. The broken coffin stands on a step 2 feet high in the
middle. The carved lines on the floor are dark. The room is in darkness,
except for the party's lights, and the Unlit trait of the knight makes
those lights dim within 30 feet of it. The light of the First Lamp and the
Fennick Candle is sunlight, so the knight can't dim it.

**Tactics.** The knight fights like a trained knight, not like a monster.
It stands between the party and the crystal. It attacks the closest
character three times each turn, and it uses Parry against the strongest
melee attacker. It uses Eclipse when three or more characters stand close
to it. It uses Dark Step to reach a caster who stays in the dark. It never
flees.

**A way to end it with no fight.** The knight is a watch that never ended.
A character who stands in front of it, with no weapon in hand, and speaks
the oath of the Dawnwatch ("We sleep so that the dawn can wake") makes it
stop for one round. A character who then says the words of the end of a
watch, in their own words ("Your watch is over. We have it now.") and
succeeds on a DC 20 Charisma (Persuasion) check makes it kneel. Its armor
falls empty, and it speaks its Last Words. The Shadows fade. Give full XP
for this.

> **GM tip:** Do not tell the players about this way. Let them find it. A
> player who thinks of the oath on the door has earned the moment. If the
> players have the Dawn Banner, a character who plants it in front of the
> knight has Advantage on the Persuasion check.

> **Scaling:** The budget is 2,300 XP for each character.
>
> - **3 characters (6,900 XP):** the Unlit Knight alone (8,400 XP), with 30
>   fewer Hit Points and no Legendary Resistance. This is a hard fight for
>   three characters. Let them take a Short Rest first.
> - **4 characters (9,200 XP):** as written (8,700 XP).
> - **5 characters (11,500 XP):** the Unlit Knight, 1 Wraith, and 3 Shadows
>   (10,500 XP).
> - **6 characters (13,800 XP):** the Unlit Knight, 2 Wraiths, and 2
>   Shadows (12,200 XP).
> - **With Techniques on:** add one Shadow for each four characters, or
>   give the knight 10% more Hit Points (219 in all).

When the knight falls, its armor falls apart, piece by piece, and it is
empty. From inside the helmet, a voice says: "We sleep so that the dawn can
wake." It is a man's voice. It is tired. It is kind. The players have never
heard it before. They will hear it again in the last scene.

## Scene 6: The Empty Crystal (half a session)

**Goal:** The truth.

### The crystal

When the knight is gone, the party can look at the crystal at last.

> The coffin is bigger than yours, and older. The crystal is thick and
> blue, and it is broken open from the inside. There is nothing in it now.
> Only the shape of a man, pressed into the crystal, the way a body leaves
> its shape in snow.
>
> On the lid, someone has pinned a badge. It is brass, black with age, in
> the shape of a rising sun.
>
> You know that badge. You each wore one. You still do.

The badge is the badge of the Dawnwatch: the same badge that each character
wears (or wore, before the long sleep). It is **the Badge of Sir Garrath**.

{{treasure: The Badge of Sir Garrath}}

### The black stone

The black stone hums so loudly that the whole party can hear it. Its bearer
feels it pull toward the crystal.

> At the edge of the broken lid, there is a small chip, a little gap in the
> crystal, the size of a thumb. The black stone in your hand is the size of
> a thumb. You know, before you try it, that it will fit.

If a character puts the stone in the chip, it fits exactly. The stone is a
piece of this crystal. It broke off when the crystal cracked, and it flew
three days south, to the chapel of the keep, to the Dawnwatch. The hum
stops for a moment. Then it starts again, softer, like something far away
that breathes.

If your table uses Marks, the Mark of the stone's bearer does not grow
here. It aches, like an old scar in the cold. The rules of the stone are
in The Hush, earlier in this book.

> **GM tip:** Let the players put the pieces together themselves: the door,
> the carvings, the badge, the stone. Some groups say "He was one of us"
> before they read the letter. That is the best moment of the chapter. If
> they do, do not confirm it. Just hand them the letter.

### The last letter

At the foot of the coffin is a small box of stone, sealed with wax. On the
wax is the mark of Haskel Orme: a flame inside a ring. Wick knows it at
once. It is the mark that the Lampwrights showed him in Corrantey.

Give the players the handout. Let one player read it aloud to the table,
or let each player read it alone, in silence. Do not hurry this.

{{handout: 10-the-last-letter-of-haskel-orme}}

### After the letter

When the players finish, it is quiet. Let it be quiet.

Then Wick speaks. He says the name slowly, the way you sound out a word in
a language that you are learning.

> "Mal... Garrath." A pause. "Mal Garrath. Malgarrath. Malgrath." Wick's
> flame shakes. "Three hundred years. Three hundred years I sat in the dark
> and counted, and I was afraid of a name, and it was only a man's name
> with a joke in front of it."
>
> He looks up at you. "He made me not to know. So that I couldn't tell you.
> So that I couldn't lie to you." A long pause. "I think he loved you. I
> think that's why he was such a coward about it."

Let each player answer, if they want to. Then give the players time to talk
about what the letter means. Here are the questions that the players
usually ask, and what you can tell them:

| The players ask | What they know now |
|---|---|
| Is the Unlit King really Sir Garrath? | Yes. He was the first knight of the sun and the first of the Dawnwatch. He wrote their oath. |
| Was he evil? | He tried to save the sun, and the dark took him. Whether he is evil now is the question of Book Two. |
| Were we lied to? | Yes. The history that the characters know, and that the world knows, is the story that Haskel Orme wrote. Ostrey did not push him back. It put him to sleep. |
| Why did we sleep? | To wake when he woke, and to be the people who remember who he was. |
| Is the sun really dying? | The letter says that Garrath was right. Wick has counted the days. They are shorter. |
| Where is he? | Gone north. The black stone, when it hums, points to the far north now, to the Grey Teeth, and past them. |

> **Thread (all characters):** Each character slept in crystal to wait for
> Malgrath. Each character now knows that Malgrath slept in crystal too,
> and that he was one of them. Ask each player one question about it.
> Choose the one that fits the character's Before answer from the tutorial:
>
> - **The Shield (a promise):** "You swore the oath that Garrath wrote. Do
>   you still keep it? What does it mean now?"
> - **The Light (a sign from a god):** "Your god sent you a sign before the
>   sleep. Do you think the god knew about Garrath? What does that mean for
>   your faith?"
> - **The Spark (a spell on the first day):** "You learned your first spells
>   in a Dawnwatch that Garrath built. Is there anything of his in your
>   magic?"
> - **The Shadow (one last thing stolen):** "You know what it is to take a
>   thing that isn't yours to save it. Do you understand him, a little?"
> - **The Storm (why you agreed to sleep):** "You didn't want to sleep. Now
>   you know why they needed you to. Would you still have agreed?"
>
> For a character that the players made themselves, use the question with
> the nearest Before answer.

## Ending the Book

**Goal:** Close Book One as a full story. Then open the door to Book Two.

Run this part in the last session, after the vault. Take your time. A group
that stops at level 10 ends its campaign here. A group that goes on to Book
Two needs this part just as much: it is the rest between two acts.

### Out of the dark

> You climb the Stair of Bells into the light. The grey dome is gone. Over
> Kingsholm the sky is pale and cold and clean, and the sun is low in the
> south, a winter sun, small and white.
>
> Below the palace hill, the people of the Hush are taking down their grey
> tents. Nobody tells them to. Somebody has lit a fire in the middle of the
> camp, a plain wood fire, yellow and noisy, and people stand around it
> holding out their hands.

### Where everyone ends up

Tell the players what happens to the people of the book. Choose the lines
that fit your game, and change them to fit what your party did.

| Who | What happens |
|---|---|
| The Hush | It is broken. Most of its people go home. A few go north, to look for their king. Book Two meets some of them again. |
| Mother Sallow | If she lives, she spends her last years in a cell, or a cloister, or the keep. Each morning she asks someone how long the day was. |
| Gaunt and the Church | What the party did in Three Banners decides this. If the party stopped the war, Marshal Ide Gauntry and Abbess Merrow sign a truce at the Ashby stone, and Tobin Gauntry carries it to Saint Ivo's himself. |
| The Lampwrights | Perrin Hale writes the true history of the guild, with the name of Haskel Orme on the first page. |
| Dawnwatch Keep | It holds. Hesper Wren runs it. Each year on the shortest day, the people of the Wolds come up the hill for a feast, and somebody puts a lamp in every window. |
| The pieces of the Dawnfire | The party keeps them, or gives them to people that it trusts. They are the last lights that the Hush could not put out. |
| Wick | Wick stays with the Dawnwatch. He counts the minutes of each day, out loud, every morning. He does not like the numbers. |

### Telling the truth, or keeping it

The party knows a truth that nobody else in the land knows. Ask the players:
"What do you do with it?" There is no wrong answer, and Book Two works with
each one.

- **Tell everyone.** The party tells the Church, Gaunt, and the Lampwrights
  that Malgrath was the first of the Dawnwatch. Some people believe it. The
  Church splits over it. The song about the Dawnwatch gets a new verse, and
  the new verse is not kind.
- **Tell a few.** The party tells only the people it trusts. Perrin Hale.
  Brother Cade. Tobin Gauntry. The secret becomes a burden that a few good
  people carry together.
- **Keep it.** The party does what Haskel Orme did. The players can feel how
  heavy that is. Wick will not lie for them, but he will not tell either.

### The After moment

At the end of the tutorial, each player answered an After question. Now
ask one last question of each player. Choose the one that fits:

- "You made a promise three hundred years ago. You have kept it, in your
  way. What do you promise now?"
- "You stole one last thing on the night before the long sleep. What is the
  most valuable thing that you have now?"
- "Your god sent you a sign. Look at the winter sun. What do you see in it
  now?"
- "Nobody alive remembered the spell from your first day. Who will remember
  you?"
- "You did not want to sleep. You are awake now. What do you want?"

Give Heroic Inspiration for the best answer, as in the tutorial. If your
table uses Bonds, this is the moment to form one last Bond, or to name the
Bond that mattered most.

### If you stop here

Book One is a full story. The Dawnwatch woke, found a world that forgot
them, made a home, broke the Hush, and learned the truth about the enemy
that they slept to fight. That is an ending.

If your group stops here, read the last words below as the end of the
campaign. They are not a cliffhanger. They are the last piece of the truth:
the Dawnwatch and the Unlit King are the same kind of thing. What the
characters do with that is their story, and they can tell it in one
sentence each, around the table, after the last roll. Ask each player:
"Ten years from now, where is your character, and what do they think when
the days get shorter?"

### If you go on

Book Two, The Last Dawn, starts at level 11 with the characters that the
players have now. It starts with the days getting shorter, and with a
question that Book One raised and did not answer: is Garrath right about
the sun? Before you start Book Two, read its first chapter, and give the
characters a month at the keep to rest, heal their injuries, and gain their
scars.

## Rewards

### Experience

This book uses milestone levels. The characters go up a level at the end of
this chapter, whatever XP the fights gave.

### Treasure

- **The Badge of Sir Garrath** (Rare, requires attunement). On the crystal.
  It belongs to the Dawnwatch now.
- **The hoard of the Hush.** The Hush took the gold of its members when they
  joined. In the throne hall, in iron boxes behind the throne, the party
  finds 4,000 GP in coins of every kind, and a chest of silver and jewels
  worth 3,000 GP. Most of it belonged to the people in the Grey Camp. A
  party that gives it back gains renown with the Church and the
  Lampwrights (+2 with each, as a great deed for the people), and the
  thanks of a hundred families. A party that keeps it keeps it.
- **The weapons of the Hush:** 20 sets of grey mail (as chain mail), a cart
  of maces and crossbows. They are good steel, if the keep needs an armory.
- **The Ember of Saint Ivo,** if the party saved it. The Church wants it
  back. A character who returns it gains 5 renown with the Church.
- **Mother Sallow's knife.** A plain grey knife, cold to the touch. It is
  not magic any more. It is the only thing that she asks for, if she lives.

### Renown

If your table uses Factions, each character gains 5 renown (a great deed
that changes the faction's fortunes) with each faction that fought the
Hush with them: Gaunt, the Church, the Lampwrights, or all three.

### The keep

If your table uses Strongholds, the keep gains 1 Renown (maximum 5). The
people of the Wolds know who broke the Hush. Two new followers ask to join
the keep: a veteran of the Kings' Bridge, and one of the Hush who put down
their weapon in the cathedral.

### Scars

If a character carries an injury from this chapter or the last, and it
heals at the keep with rest and care, the character gains its scar. Most
characters end Book One with a scar or two. Ask each player to tell where
their scar came from, in one sentence.

### Level

The characters go to level 11.

## The Last Words

Read this last, after the rewards, when the table is quiet. It is the end
of Book One.

> **GM tip:** This scene is after the Rewards on purpose. The rewards are
> the end of the game. This is the end of the story. Wait until the dice are
> put away. Dim the lights, as you did on the first night of the tutorial,
> when the Dawnwatch slept.

> That night, at the keep, you stand on the wall and look north. The sky
> is clear. The stars are very bright and very cold. Far away, over the
> Grey Teeth, there is a darkness where no stars are.
>
> The black stone in your pocket hums.
>
> A voice speaks in your head. You have heard it before: in the chapel, the
> day you woke, and from inside an empty helmet, under Kingsholm. Deep and
> slow and tired. Kind.
>
> "Brothers. Sisters. You read his letter. Old Haskel always did write a
> good letter." A breath, like a laugh with no joy in it. "He told you that
> I loved the sun too much. He was right. He told you that I failed. He was
> right about that too."
>
> "But look at the sky, and count, as your little lantern counts. The days
> are shorter. The winters are longer. I saw it first. Now you see it too."
>
> "They put me in the crystal so that I would stop. They put you in the
> crystal so that you would be there when I woke. Two halls. One spell.
> One oath. We sleep so that the dawn can wake."
>
> The stone is warm in your hand. It has never been warm before.
>
> "Now you know what we are."
