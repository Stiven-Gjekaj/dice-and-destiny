# Corrantey

The party goes west to the city of merchants. There they find the second
piece of the Dawnfire, a lamp that has burned for three hundred years. They
also find something that Wick did not expect: the name of the person who
made him.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Levels** | 6 to 7 |
| **Time at the table** | About 3 or 4 sessions |
| **Places** | The road west, the city of Corrantey, the hall of the Lampwrights, the Skerrows (sea caves under the mouth of the Tey) |
| **System that starts** | Crafting and gathering |

## Running This Chapter

**Goal:** the party finds the second piece of the Dawnfire, the First Lamp,
and learns the past of Wick.

The chapter has five parts:

1. **The city.** The party arrives in Corrantey and sees how big and rich the
   world has become.
2. **The Lampwrights.** Perrin Hale, the master of the guild of lantern
   makers, sees a small mark on Wick. It is the mark of Haskel Orme, the
   steward of Dawnwatch Keep. Wick learns the name of his maker.
3. **The Skerrows.** The guild offers to make things for the party from rare
   materials. The party goes into the sea caves to gather and harvest them.
   This teaches the Crafting and gathering system.
4. **The offer.** Captain Ilse Varro, the spymaster of Gaunt, asks the party
   to help her take the First Lamp. Or to look away.
5. **Lamplight Night.** On the night of the city festival, Ilse and her
   agents raid the hall. The old brass guardians of the guild wake up. At
   the end, Perrin Hale gives the First Lamp to the party.

### What the Party Knows at the Start

At the end of chapter 5, the party has met Marshal Ide Gauntry and the power
of Gaunt. They know that the Hush hunts the pieces of the Dawnfire. They may
know (from Wick, from Brother Cade, or from the Church) that a second piece
is said to burn in Corrantey, in the hall of the guild that makes lanterns.

If the party needs a reason to go west, use one of these:

- **Wick asks.** "The lantern makers of the west. If anyone can tell me what
  I am made of, it is them. Also, I have a squeak in my hinge. It is
  undignified."
- **The Church sends them.** Abbess Merrow wants the party to bring the
  blessing of the Five Sleepers to the Lamplight festival. She hopes that the
  rich guilds of Corrantey will give money to the Church.
- **The Hush is already there.** A merchant from Corrantey tells the party
  that grey-robed people were seen near the hall of the Lampwrights. "They
  don't buy anything. They just stand and look at the lights."

### The Pieces of the Dawnfire So Far

| Piece | Where | Who has it |
|---|---|---|
| The fire in the chapel of the keep | Dawnwatch Keep | It went out in chapter 1. |
| The Ember of Saint Ivo | Saint Ivo's | The Hush stole it in chapter 4. |
| The First Lamp | Corrantey | The Lampwrights. This chapter. |
| The piece in Fennick | Under the water of the Low Fen | Nobody. Chapter 8. |

> **GM tip:** The Ember may be in a different place at your table, because
> of what happened in chapter 4. That is fine. Change the table to match.
> The important thing for this chapter is simple: the Hush has already put
> out one piece, and it wants all of them.

### Optional Systems at This Level

The party already uses Bonds, Journeys, Marks, and Factions and renown. This
chapter adds Crafting and gathering. Crafting makes characters stronger only
through the items that they make. Count the crafted items of this chapter as
part of its treasure. The Rewards section does this for you.

## Scene 1: The Road West (short)

**Goal:** show the size of the land, and set the tone of a city of
merchants.

The pilgrim road goes west from Saint Ivo's to Corrantey. It takes 5 days.
Run it with the Journeys rules if your table likes them, or tell it in a few
lines. The road is busy: carts of wool, pilgrims who go home, and soldiers of
Gaunt in grey cloaks who ride fast and say nothing.

> On the fifth day the road climbs a long hill, and the wind changes. It
> smells of salt and tar and smoke. At the top you stop.
>
> Below you, two rivers run into the sea. Between them, and on both banks,
> there is a city. It is bigger than any city of old Ostrey. Roofs of red tile
> and grey slate go on and on. Masts stand in the harbor like a forest in
> winter. And everywhere, on the walls, over the doors, along the quays, on
> the masts, there are lamps. It is still afternoon, and the city already
> shines.
>
> "Oh," says Wick, very quietly. "Oh, look at all my cousins."

### What the Characters Remember

The Dawnwatch knew this place as two small towns. Ask a player: "Your
character came here once, three hundred years ago. What was here then?" Let
the player answer. Then show how it changed.

| Old Ostrey (300 years ago) | Corrantey today |
|---|---|
| **Corrin**, a town of wool merchants on the north river, with one stone quay. | The north half of the city. Stone houses, counting halls, and the Council House of the guilds. |
| **Tey**, a fishing village on the south river, built of wood. | The south half. Shipyards, fish markets, rope walks, and narrow streets that smell of tar. |
| A ferry between the two towns. | A wide stone bridge over the mouth of the Corrin, with a lamp on each post. People call it the Long Bridge. |

> **GM tip:** Let the players feel small here. In Brannock's Ford the
> Dawnwatch were strangers with old coins. In Corrantey nobody looks at them
> twice. A city of thirty thousand people has seen stranger things than five
> people in old armor and a talking lantern.

## Scene 2: A City of Lights

**Goal:** the party learns how Corrantey works, and hears about the festival
and the First Lamp.

### The City at a Glance

- **A council of guilds rules the city.** Each guild sends one master to the
  Council House. There is no lord and no king. The guilds argue about
  everything, and they agree on one thing: no army of Gaunt comes inside the
  walls.
- **The Lampwrights are the oldest guild.** They make the lanterns that light
  the streets, the lighthouses on the coast, and the lamps of every ship that
  leaves the harbor. Their master always has a seat at the front of the
  council.
- **Gaunt is in the harbor.** Three tall grey ships of Gaunt sit at anchor
  outside the harbor wall. They say that they are here to trade. Their
  sailors buy very little.
- **Lamplight Night is in four days.** Once each year, every house in the
  city hangs a lamp, and the Lampwrights open their hall. For one night, the
  people of the city can walk in and see the First Lamp, the oldest flame in
  the land.

### What the People Say

When the party walks the streets, eats in a tavern, or asks questions, roll
a d6 or choose.

| d6 | Rumor |
|---|---|
| 1 | "The First Lamp has burned since the time of old Ostrey. No oil. No wick. Nobody knows how. The Lampwrights say that if it goes out, the city falls into the sea." (The first part is true.) |
| 2 | "The Gaunt ships want the Lamp. The marshal wants to put it on a pole at the front of her army, like a flag." (Close to the truth.) |
| 3 | "There are people in grey near the Lamp hall at night. They stand very still and don't talk. My cousin says that a street lamp went out when one of them walked past." (True. They are the Hush.) |
| 4 | "The Five Sleepers? The Church folk say that they are awake. I say that it's a story to sell candles." (The party is standing right there.) |
| 5 | "The brass men in the Lamp hall move at night. My grandmother saw one turn its head." (True. They are the Brass Lamp Wardens.) |
| 6 | "Master Hale of the Lampwrights is the cleverest man in the city, and he knows it. Don't touch anything in his hall." (True.) |

### Checks in the City

| Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Intelligence (History) | 12 | The character remembers the name of a lantern maker who worked for the keep: "Master Orme had apprentices. Three of them, I think. They came from the west coast." |
| Charisma (Persuasion) | 13 | A dock clerk lets the party look at the harbor book. The Gaunt ships came eleven days ago and have unloaded nothing. |
| Wisdom (Perception) | 14 | The character sees a woman in a good grey coat who follows the party for two streets, then is gone. (This is one of the agents of Captain Ilse Varro.) |
| Wisdom (Insight) | 12 | The people of the city are nervous about Gaunt. They smile, and they keep their children inside after dark. |

> **GM tip:** If a character reached a rank with Gaunt in chapter 5, the
> agents of Gaunt in the city already know that character by sight. Ilse
> Varro will use that in Scene 6. If a character has renown with the Church,
> the small chapel of the Five Sleepers on the Tey side gives the party beds
> for free.

## Scene 3: The Hall of the Lampwrights

**Goal:** the party meets Perrin Hale and sees the First Lamp. Perrin finds
the maker's mark on Wick.

### The Hall

The hall of the Lampwrights stands on the Corrin side, near the Long Bridge.
It is the oldest building in the city: a square stone hall with a round tower
at one corner. The tower was a lighthouse once. Now the city has grown past
it.

> The great doors of the hall are open, and warm light pours out. Inside, a
> hundred lanterns hang from the beams on chains: lanterns of brass and tin,
> of red and blue and green glass, round ones, square ones, one in the shape
> of a ship. Apprentices in leather aprons climb ladders to clean them.
>
> At each side of the hall, three tall figures of brass stand against the
> wall, like knights in a chapel. Each one has a lamp where its heart should
> be. The lamps are dark.
>
> At the far end, behind an iron rail, there is a stone plinth. On it stands
> a small, plain lamp of thick green glass on a brass foot. Its flame is no
> bigger than your thumb. It does not flicker.
>
> Wick goes very still in your hands. "That," he whispers, "smells like the
> chapel. Like the fire in the chapel."

An apprentice stops the party at the rail. The hall is open to visitors in
the day, but nobody goes past the rail. If the party asks for the master, or
if Wick says anything at all, the apprentice runs to get him.

### Perrin Hale

Perrin Hale is the master of the Lampwrights. He is in his sixties, thin,
with ink on his fingers and a pair of small round spectacles that he pushes
up his nose when he thinks. He wears a leather apron over a very fine coat.

- **He is proud.** The Lampwrights are the oldest guild in the city, and the
  First Lamp is the oldest flame in the land. He says both things often.
- **He is practical.** He does not believe in the Five Sleepers. "A good
  story. The Church sells it well. I sell lanterns."
- **He loves good work.** Show him something well made and he forgets to be
  proud. A talking lantern made three hundred years ago is the best thing he
  has ever seen.
- **He is afraid.** He knows that Gaunt wants the First Lamp. He knows that
  the council will not fight Gaunt for it. He does not yet know about the
  Hush.

> A thin man in a leather apron comes down the hall fast, wiping his hands on
> a rag. He looks at you. Then he looks at the lantern in your hands, and he
> stops.
>
> "Who made that?" he says. "No. Don't tell me. Let me look." He takes a
> lens out of his apron, bends close to Wick, and turns him gently toward the
> light. Wick opens his mouth to complain, and the man says, "Hush, please. I
> mean, be quiet, please. Sorry. Hold still."
>
> He tilts Wick back. On the bottom of the brass, under three hundred years of
> soot, there is a tiny stamp. A flame inside a ring.
>
> The man takes off his spectacles. His hands shake a little. "That is the
> mark of Master Orme," he says. "That is the mark of the first master. Nobody
> has seen a new piece with that mark for three hundred years."

### The Mark of Haskel Orme

Perrin explains, quickly and with a lot of pride:

- The Lampwrights started with three apprentices of a master called **Haskel
  Orme**. He was a steward of the old kingdom, and the best maker of lamps
  that ever lived. The apprentices came west after the war of the Unlit King,
  and they opened a shop in Corrin.
- Each piece that Orme made has his mark: a flame inside a ring. The guild
  still uses the mark, with a small line under it, to show that a piece comes
  from his school.
- The six brass figures in the hall carry the mark on their heels. So does
  the brass foot of the First Lamp.
- The guild has the old ledgers of the first apprentices. They are in the
  tower, and nobody reads them, because they are written in the old speech
  of Ostrey and nobody can read it well. (The Dawnwatch can. See Scene 4.)

> **GM tip:** Let the players connect the dots. Wick said in the tutorial
> that "the steward" made him on the last night. Now they hear that a
> steward called Haskel Orme was a maker of lamps. A player who remembers
> this should say it aloud. If no player does, Wick does, after a long
> silence: "The steward. My steward. His name was Haskel?"

> **Thread (Wick):** Wick never knew the name of his maker. The steward made
> him on one night, taught him to count and to talk, and told him one thing:
> "Do not let them oversleep." Then the steward closed the door of the Hall
> of Sleepers and never came back. Wick waited three hundred years and never
> knew whom he waited for.
>
> Play this scene slowly. Wick is proud first ("A master! I was made by a
> master!"), then very quiet. If a player asks Wick what he remembers about
> the steward, give Wick these memories, one at a time:
>
> - "He hummed when he worked. Always the same song. Badly."
> - "He smelled of lamp oil and ink. His hands were cold."
> - "He talked to me while he made me. He said sorry. He said it many times.
>   I did not know why. I still don't."
>
> The last memory matters later. Chapter 7 and chapter 10 tell why the
> steward was sorry. Do not explain it now. If a player asks, Wick says, "I
> don't know. I was very new. I thought everyone said sorry to lanterns."

### Checks with Perrin Hale

| Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Charisma (Persuasion) | 12 | Perrin believes that the party are who they say. "A Sleeper. In my hall. The council will never believe me." He offers the party rooms in the guild house. |
| Intelligence (History) | 14 | The character knew Haskel Orme by sight: the steward of Dawnwatch Keep, a quiet man with ink on his hands. Nobody in the Dawnwatch knew that he made lamps. |
| Intelligence (Arcana) | 15 | The First Lamp is not a lamp with a spell on it. The flame is the spell. It is the same kind of fire as the fire in the chapel of the keep: a piece of the Dawnfire. |
| Wisdom (Insight) | 13 | Perrin is scared of something. If the party asks, he admits it: "Gaunt. The marshal sends me letters. Polite ones. Each one is less polite." |

### What Perrin Knows About the First Lamp

- The first apprentices brought the Lamp from the north, in the year of the
  long sleep. The master told them to keep it lit, and to keep it where
  everyone could see it. "Nobody steals a thing that the whole city watches,"
  he wrote.
- The flame needs no oil. It never goes out. Rain, wind, and sea water do not
  touch it. One master tried to put it out with a bucket, two hundred years
  ago, for a bet. The water boiled.
- The guild does not know what the Lamp is for. "It is a lamp. It gives
  light. That is what lamps are for."

If the party tells Perrin what the Lamp really is, a piece of the Dawnfire,
and that the Hush wants to put it out, he goes pale. "Then it is not safe
here. And it is not safe with Gaunt." He asks for one night to think.

## Scene 4: The Guild and the Work

**Goal:** Perrin offers to make things for the party. Teach the Crafting
and gathering system.

The next morning, Perrin has thought about it. He makes the party an offer.

> Perrin Hale meets you in the workshop under the tower. Long benches, small
> forges, glass in every color, and the sound of little hammers. He pushes
> his spectacles up his nose.
>
> "I can't give you the Lamp. Not yet. The council would have my head, and
> the Lamp is the heart of this guild. But I can do something better than
> talk. You are going to fight the people who put out lights. You need lights
> that do not go out." He smiles for the first time. "Bring me the right
> things, and my guild will make them for you."

### The Two Things That Perrin Offers

Perrin offers to make two things. He knows what each one needs.

1. **Potions of Water Breathing.** "You told me that one piece of the old
   fire is under the water, in the fen. You will need to breathe there." The
   potions need Kelp Hearts. Kelp grows in the Skerrows, the sea caves under
   the mouth of the Tey.
2. **A storm lantern.** A lantern that only magic can put out, and that shows
   things that hide in the dark. The guild has made one before, a hundred
   years ago, for the lighthouse on the point. It needs a Storm Pearl, from
   the deep pools of the Skerrows, and a Wisp Spark, which the guild has in
   its stores.

> **GM tip:** The potions are important for chapter 8. Fennick is a drowned
> town, and much of it is under the water. Each character needs a way to
> breathe there. The potions are the easy way. A spell such as Water
> Breathing also works. Help the players see this now, so that they do not
> arrive in Fennick without air.

> **Teach: Crafting and gathering.** Read this to the players, or say it in
> your own words. Keep it short. They learn the rest when they do it.
>
> "With this system, you can harvest parts from the monsters you defeat, and
> gather rare things in the wild. Then a crafter turns them into potions and
> magic items. There are three steps.
>
> **Harvest or gather.** After a fight, one of you can harvest a creature.
> It takes 10 minutes for a Medium or smaller creature, 30 minutes for a
> Large one, and 1 hour for a Huge one. In the wild, you can search for 1
> hour for something that grows or forms there. Either way, you make a check.
> The check depends on the material. The DC depends on how rare it is: 10
> for Common, 13 for Uncommon, 15 for Rare. A success gives you one unit. A
> failure by 5 or more ruins it.
>
> **Follow a recipe.** A recipe names a tool, the materials, the gold, and
> the number of workdays. A workday is 8 hours.
>
> **Make the check.** At the end, the crafter makes a check with the tool,
> against a DC for the rarity of the item. On a success, the item is done. On
> a failure, half the gold and all the time are lost, but you keep the
> materials and can try again."
>
> Then tell them the most important thing: "Crafting doesn't make magic items
> cheap. It gives you control. You choose what you make. And if you harvest
> your own materials, you save a little gold."

### The Recipe Cards

Show the players these two cards. Copy them on paper if you like. The
players can see the costs and plan.

> **Recipe: Potion of Water Breathing** (Uncommon, consumable)
>
> - **Tool:** Alchemist's Supplies
> - **Materials:** 1 Kelp Heart (Uncommon, worth 50 GP)
> - **Gold:** 50 GP
> - **Workdays:** 5
> - **Check:** DC 13 with Alchemist's Supplies
>
> One recipe makes one potion. Each potion needs its own Kelp Heart.

> **Recipe: Storm Lantern of Corrantey** (Rare, permanent)
>
> - **Tool:** Tinker's Tools
> - **Materials:** 1 Storm Pearl (Rare, worth 500 GP) and 1 Wisp Spark
>   (Common, worth 10 GP)
> - **Gold:** 1,100 GP
> - **Workdays:** 40
> - **Check:** DC 15 with Tinker's Tools
>
> The guild has a Wisp Spark in its stores. Perrin sells it at its full value,
> 10 GP. Four masters of the guild work on the lantern together. Each day of
> four crafters counts as four workdays, so the lantern takes 10 days.

{{treasure: Storm Lantern of Corrantey}}

> **GM tip: why the numbers work.** The Storm Lantern is a recipe that this
> book makes with the rules in Making Your Own Recipes, in the Crafting and
> Gathering chapter of the Game Master's Compendium. A Rare item costs about
> 2,000 GP and 50 workdays to craft. The recipe costs 1,610 GP in all (1,100
> GP in gold and 510 GP in materials) and takes 40 workdays. If the party
> harvests the Storm Pearl itself, it pays only 1,110 GP. That is the small
> saving that the system promises: a quarter of the cost, never more.

### Who Does the Work

The guild does the crafting. A master of the Lampwrights has a +7 bonus with
Tinker's Tools, Alchemist's Supplies, and Glassblower's Tools. The masters
roll the check, not the players.

A character who has proficiency with the right tool can work with the
masters. That character counts as one more crafter, so the work goes a
little faster, and the character can make the check in place of a master if
the player wants to. Players like to roll for their own items. Let them.

The party pays the gold. Perrin does not take a fee for the work: "A
Lampwright never charges a Sleeper. I just decided that. It is a new rule."

> **GM tip:** The party may not have 1,100 GP now. That is fine. Perrin
> starts the work when the Storm Pearl arrives, and the party can pay at the
> end of the chapter. After Lamplight Night, the guild pays half of the gold
> as thanks (see Rewards).

### Other Things the Guild Can Make

If the party brings more materials, the guild can make other items from the
recipes in the Crafting and Gathering chapter. The most likely ones:

| Material | Where in this chapter | What the guild can make |
|---|---|---|
| Elemental Mote | Harvest from the Water Elemental in the Skerrows | An Elemental Gem (Water): Jeweler's Tools, 50 GP, 5 workdays, DC 13. |
| Kelp Heart (extra) | The kelp beds of the Skerrows | More Potions of Water Breathing. |
| Wisp Spark | The guild stores, 10 GP | A Lantern of Small Hours: Tinker's Tools, 40 GP, 5 workdays, DC 10. |

The guild can also make ordinary gear, such as oil, lanterns, and flasks of
Alchemist's Fire, at half the price, with the gear rules of the Crafting and
Gathering chapter.

## Scene 5: The Skerrows

**Goal:** the party gathers Kelp Hearts and a Storm Pearl, and harvests a
monster. The first fight of the chapter.

### Getting There

The Skerrows are a maze of sea caves in the black cliffs south of the mouth
of the Tey. At low tide, a path of wet rocks leads from the shore into the
caves. At high tide, the sea fills most of them. The fishers of the Tey side
do not go in. They say that "the old woman of the rocks" lives there, and
that she sings ships onto the reef.

> The tide pulls back, and the path appears: a line of black, weedy rocks
> that runs from the beach into a mouth in the cliff. Inside, the light turns
> green. Water drips from the roof. Pools lie everywhere, clear as glass,
> and in them long ropes of kelp move slowly, though there is no wind.
>
> Somewhere deeper in, someone is singing. It is a woman's voice, old and
> cracked, and the song has no words.

The party has about 6 hours before the tide comes back. Tell the players
this. Each hour of searching uses one of those hours. When the tide returns,
the low caves fill, and the party must leave or swim.

> **GM tip:** The tide gives the players a clock. They can search, fight,
> and harvest, but not all at once and not forever. Keep a count of the hours
> where the players can see it.

### Gathering in the Caves

| Material | Where | Check | DC | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kelp Heart (Uncommon) | The kelp pools near the entrance and in the Long Pool | Wisdom (Survival) | 13 | 1 hour of searching. Each success gives one Kelp Heart. The pools hold 8 hearts in all. A character must go into the water to cut a heart from a living plant. |
| Storm Pearl (Rare) | Only in the Drowned Chapel, the deepest cave (see below) | Wisdom (Survival) | 15 | 1 hour of searching. The pools there hold 2 pearls. |
| Elemental Mote (Uncommon) | Harvest from the Water Elemental after the fight | Intelligence (Arcana) | 13 | 30 minutes (the elemental is Large). |

On a failure by 5 or more, the material is ruined: the kelp heart tears, or
the pearl cracks in the hand.

### Areas of the Skerrows

**S1. The mouth.** A wide cave with a floor of wet rock and shallow pools. The
kelp pools here hold 3 Kelp Hearts. The water is 3 feet deep: Difficult
Terrain for a Medium creature.

**S2. The Long Pool.** A long, narrow cave with a deep pool down the middle,
20 feet deep and very clear. The kelp here holds 5 Kelp Hearts. A character
who dives must hold their breath. A ledge 5 feet wide runs along each side.

**S3. The bone shelf.** A dry cave above the tide line, full of old bones,
broken oars, and the figurehead of a ship (a woman with a lamp). This is
where the hag sleeps. Under her nest of rotten sailcloth, there is a chest
(see Treasure).

**S4. The Drowned Chapel.** The deepest cave, a round chamber with a roof like
a dome. Once, smugglers carved an altar here for luck. Now the sea comes in
through a hole in the floor, and the cave is half water. The light from the
water is blue. The 2 Storm Pearls lie in the deep pool under the altar. This
is where the fight happens.

### The Fight in the Drowned Chapel

The singer is Nan Corley, a sea hag who has lived in the Skerrows for sixty
years. She sings ships onto the reef and takes what washes up. Some years
ago, she bound a water elemental to the pool of the Drowned Chapel with a
broken charm from a wreck. Three merrow serve her for a share of the
drowned.

> The cave opens into a dome of black rock. Half of the floor is water, and
> the water glows a soft blue. On a carved stone altar at the edge of the
> pool sits a woman with grey-green skin and hair like old rope. She is the
> singer. She stops singing and smiles at you with too many teeth.
>
> "Visitors. Visitors with a light." Her eyes go to Wick. "Pretty. I will
> hang it on my wall." She slaps the water with her hand. The pool rises,
> and rises, and a shape of water stands up out of it, taller than a man.
> Behind it, three long, grey shapes slide up onto the rocks.

**Creatures:** one Sea Hag (Nan Corley), one Water Elemental, and three
Merrow.

**The budget:** for 4 characters of level 6, a Moderate fight is 4 × 1,000 =
4,000 XP. The Sea Hag is 450 XP, the Water Elemental is 1,800 XP, and the
three Merrow are 1,350 XP. The total is 3,600 XP, a Moderate fight with room
for the terrain.

**Terrain:**

- The pool is 15 feet deep. A creature in it swims. The elemental and the
  merrow have Swim Speeds; most characters do not, so they swim at half
  Speed.
- The rock floor around the pool is wet and covered in weed. A creature that
  takes the Dash action on it must succeed on a DC 10 Dexterity (Acrobatics)
  check or fall Prone.
- The altar is 3 feet high. It gives Half Cover.

**Tactics:**

- **Nan Corley** stays on the altar. Her Vile Appearance frightens the
  characters who come close, and she uses Death Glare on a Frightened
  character. She hates light. She tries to grab Wick, or to make a character
  drop him. If she drops below half her Hit Points, she slips into the water
  and tries to flee down the hole in the floor.
- **The Water Elemental** uses Whelm to pull characters into the pool and
  drown them. It
  is bound to the pool, and it can't go more than 30 feet from it.
- **The Merrow** attack the characters who stand at the edge of the water and
  try to drag them in.

**The charm:** the elemental is bound by a bronze ship's bell, green with
age, that hangs from a chain over the pool. A character who sees the bell
and succeeds on a DC 13 Intelligence (Arcana) check knows that it holds the
elemental. If a character breaks the chain (AC 15, 10 Hit Points) or takes
the bell, the elemental is free. A free elemental does not attack the party.
It sinks into the pool and goes back to the sea. The party can't harvest an
Elemental Mote from a free elemental.

> **GM tip:** This is a good choice for the players. They can fight the
> elemental and harvest it, or free it and lose the mote. Neither is wrong.
> If they free it, give them a moment of wonder: the water stands still, the
> elemental looks at them for a long second, and then it is just the sea.

> **Scaling:** The budget for a Moderate fight at level 6 is 1,000 XP for
> each character.
>
> - **3 characters (3,000 XP):** remove one Merrow. The total is 3,150 XP.
> - **4 characters (4,000 XP):** use the fight as written. The total is
>   3,600 XP.
> - **5 characters (5,000 XP):** add two Merrow and one Hunter Shark in the
>   pool. The total is 4,950 XP.
> - **6 characters (6,000 XP):** add three Merrow and one Hunter Shark. The
>   total is 5,850 XP.

**After the fight:** the characters can harvest the Water Elemental if it
fell (see the table above). When the elemental drops to 0 Hit Points, it
splashes over the floor, and a small bright stone of hard water is left in
the pool. That is where the harvester works. The Storm Pearls are in the deep
pool, now quiet.

**Treasure:** in the chest in S3, the hag kept what washed up from forty
years of wrecks: 640 GP in mixed coins of Corrantey and Gaunt, a silver
compass that always points to the nearest land (a trinket, worth 50 GP),
and the logbook of a ship called the *Merry Tern*. The last page of the log
says: "Grey passengers. Paid in old gold. They asked us to put out the
lamps."

> **GM tip:** The logbook is a small sign of the Hush. Three months ago, the
> Hush paid a ship to carry its people along the coast with no lights. The
> hag wrecked the ship. If the players ask Perrin about the *Merry Tern*, he
> knows the ship: it left Corrantey for the north, and it never came back.

### The Tide Comes Back

If the party is still in the caves when the 6 hours end, the sea comes back
fast. Each character makes a DC 12 Strength (Athletics) check to wade and
swim out. On a failure, the character takes 2d6 Bludgeoning damage from the
rocks and gains 1 Exhaustion level, but still gets out. Nobody drowns here.
The sea only reminds them that it is bigger than they are.

## Scene 6: The Offer of Gaunt

**Goal:** Captain Ilse Varro makes the party an offer. The players choose a
side, or pretend to.

Run this scene the day after the Skerrows, or any time before Lamplight
Night. Ilse chooses a public place, so that the party can't make trouble: a
fine eating house on the Corrin side, at a table by the window.

### Captain Ilse Varro

Ilse Varro is the spymaster of Gaunt in Corrantey. She works for Marshal Ide
Gauntry, and she is very good at her work.

- **She looks harmless.** A woman of about forty in a good grey coat, with
  short dark hair, reading glasses on a chain, and a cup of tea. She looks
  like a clerk of a counting house. That is the point.
- **She is polite and direct.** She does not lie when the truth works better.
- **She keeps a list.** She knows who owes whom in the city. She knows which
  guild master drinks, which one gambles, and which one has a son in debt.
- **She believes in Gaunt.** She thinks that the land needs one strong ruler,
  and that the marshal is the best one. She thinks that the Hush is a danger,
  and that the First Lamp is safer in Stannard than in a guild hall with an
  open door.

> A woman in a grey coat sits alone at the window table. She has a cup of tea
> and a small book. When you come in, she closes the book and smiles. "Please
> sit. I have ordered for you. The fish here is the best in the city. I am
> Ilse Varro. I work for the marshal, as I think you know. I would like to
> save us all some trouble."

### The Offer

Ilse is clear about what she wants.

- **The marshal wants the First Lamp.** "Gaunt is the heir of old Ostrey. The
  old fire belongs with the heir. It will be safe in Stannard, behind walls
  and soldiers, not on a plinth where any fool in grey can walk up to it."
- **She offers money and friendship.** 2,000 GP, paid in Stannard, and the
  friendship of Gaunt. A character with renown in Gaunt gains 5 renown (a
  great deed for the faction).
- **She asks for one of two things.** Either the party helps her take the
  Lamp on Lamplight Night, when the hall is full and the guards are busy. Or
  the party simply stays away from the hall that night. "I don't need your
  swords. I need you to be somewhere else."
- **She knows about the Hush.** If the party mentions the people in grey, she
  nods. "Yes. My people have seen them. Six or seven, on the Tey side. They
  are why I can't wait. If they put out the Lamp, nobody gets it."

### Checks with Ilse

| Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Wisdom (Insight) | 15 | Ilse tells the truth about the Hush and about the money. She does not say that her agents will hurt anyone who stands in the way. She will not lose sleep over a few dead apprentices. |
| Charisma (Persuasion) | 18 | Ilse agrees to wait one week and to talk to the marshal again. She still raids the hall on Lamplight Night, but with fewer agents (remove the two Spies from the fight in Scene 7). |
| Charisma (Intimidation) | 16 | Ilse smiles. "I am a woman alone with a cup of tea, in a room full of witnesses. Please, go on." It does not work, and she remembers. |
| Charisma (Deception) | 14 | Ilse believes that the party will stay away. On Lamplight Night, she does not expect them in the hall. The party has Advantage on Initiative in Scene 7. |
| Wisdom (Perception) | 13 | Two other tables are hers: a young man reading a letter and an old woman who never drinks her wine. Both are agents. |

### What the Players Choose

- **They refuse.** Ilse pays for the meal and leaves. "That is a pity. I
  liked you." Each character with renown in Gaunt loses 1 renown. The raid
  happens as written.
- **They agree to stay away.** The raid happens without them. Perrin sends an
  apprentice to beg them for help when the alarm sounds, so they can still
  change their minds. If they don't come, the raid succeeds, and Gaunt has
  the First Lamp (see "If Gaunt takes the Lamp", below).
- **They agree to help Ilse.** On Lamplight Night, the party is on the side of
  the raid. The Brass Lamp Wardens wake and attack everyone who doesn't carry
  Wick. Perrin sees the party with the agents of Gaunt. He never trusts them
  again, and he does not come to the keep in chapter 7.
- **They pretend to agree.** A good plan. Let it work if the Deception check
  works.

> **GM tip:** The players may want to fight Ilse here and now. Remind them,
> through Wick if you must, that this is a public room in a city with no
> love for violence, and that the city watch is two streets away. If they
> fight anyway, Ilse uses her Smoke Pellet and escapes, and the party spends
> a night in the cells of the Council House. Perrin pays to get them out, and
> he is not happy.

## Scene 7: Lamplight Night

**Goal:** the agents of Gaunt raid the hall. The Brass Lamp Wardens wake.
The big fight of the chapter.

### The Festival

> At sunset, the city lights up. It starts at the harbor: one lamp on the
> highest mast, then ten, then a hundred. The light runs up the streets like
> water running uphill. Every window has a lamp. Every door. Children carry
> paper lanterns on sticks. On the Long Bridge, every post burns, and the
> river below is full of little floating candles.
>
> The great doors of the hall of the Lampwrights stand open. The people of
> the city walk in, slowly, in a long line, to look at the small green lamp
> on its plinth. Some of them cry. Some of them pray. An old man lifts his
> grandson onto his shoulders so that he can see.
>
> Wick watches all of it from your hands. "They love it," he says. "They
> don't know what it is, and they love it anyway."

Perrin asks the party to stand near the Lamp, if they agreed to help him.
The hall is full of people: two hundred visitors in a slow line, twenty
apprentices, six masters, and four guards of the guild with clubs.

### The Raid

An hour after sunset, the raid starts. Ilse planned it well.

1. **The lights go out.** An agent on the roof cuts the chains of the big
   lanterns over the door. They fall and smash. The front of the hall goes
   dark, and people scream and run.
2. **The agents come in with the crowd.** Ilse and her people wear the plain
   clothes of the city. They push to the rail in the panic.
3. **The crossbows cover them.** Two sergeants with crossbows stand on the
   gallery over the door and shoot anyone who comes near the plinth.
4. **The Wardens wake.** When the first agent touches the iron rail, the six
   brass figures in the hall open their eyes. Their heart-lamps light up with
   a roar. Two of them step down from the wall.

> Glass breaks over the door. The big lanterns fall in a rain of sparks, and
> half of the hall goes black. People scream. The line breaks. In the dark,
> you see shapes push against the crowd, toward the plinth: a woman in a grey
> coat, and people with knives in their hands.
>
> Then a sound like a forge door opening. Along the wall, the brass figures
> lift their heads. Fire blooms in their chests. Two of them step down from
> their places, and the floor shakes.
>
> "Oh," says Wick. "Oh, they're awake. Hello! Hello, brothers! Please don't
> step on me!"

**Creatures:** Captain Ilse Varro, two Crossbow Sergeants (on the gallery),
two Spies, and one Brass Lamp Warden.

Two Wardens step down, but only one of them fights in the area of the
plinth. The other one stands in the door and holds it shut, so that no more
agents come in and nobody goes out. (Use it for the Scaling box.)

**The budget:** for 4 characters of level 6, a High fight is 4 × 1,400 =
5,600 XP. Ilse is 2,300 XP, the two Crossbow Sergeants are 900 XP, the two
Spies are 400 XP, and the Brass Lamp Warden is 1,800 XP. The total is 5,400
XP, a High fight.

### The Wardens and Wick

The Brass Lamp Warden attacks every creature that does not carry an object
with the maker's mark of Haskel Orme. Wick carries that mark. So the Warden
does not attack the character who carries Wick (unless that character
attacks it first), but it does attack the other characters, the agents of
Gaunt, and anyone else in its way. It does not attack the visitors who run.
It only attacks creatures near the plinth or the rail.

This makes the fight a fight with three sides. The Warden is a danger to
both the party and the agents.

> **GM tip:** Let the players find a way to use Wick. A character who holds
> Wick up and stands between the Warden and a friend makes the Warden stop
> and turn away. A clever player can carry Wick to each friend in turn. The
> Warden is old and simple, and Wick is its little brother.
>
> If Wick speaks to the Warden, he can try to order it. Wick makes a
> Charisma check with a +4 bonus, DC 15, as his action in the round (Wick
> acts on the turn of the character who carries him). On a success, the
> Warden stops for 1 round and attacks only the creature that Wick names. On
> a failure, the Warden looks at Wick, and then goes on.

### The Raid, Round by Round

- **Ilse** goes for the Lamp. On her first turn, she reaches the rail. On her
  second turn, she lifts the Lamp from the plinth. (It is not hot. It is warm,
  like a hand.) Then she runs for the side door, which her agents have
  opened. She uses Gaunt Training to Dash. She uses Smoke Pellet when a
  character gets close.
- **The Crossbow Sergeants** shoot from the gallery, 15 feet above the floor.
  The gallery rail gives them Half Cover. A ladder at each end goes up to the
  gallery. One sergeant uses Call the Volley on the first character who
  comes near Ilse.
- **The Spies** protect Ilse. They fight with shortswords and hand
  crossbows, and they use Cunning Action to get in the way.
- **The Warden** walks to the rail and attacks the nearest creature that
  does not carry Wick. It uses Flare on a group.

**If Ilse gets away with the Lamp:** she runs into the streets of the
festival (see the rooftop chase, Scene 8). The party can catch her there.

**If Ilse drops to half her Hit Points or fewer:** she drops the Lamp, uses
Smoke Pellet, and flees. She does not die for the marshal. Her agents
surrender when she is gone.

**If the party captures Ilse:** she does not talk about her work, but she
tells the truth about one thing: "You are not the only ones who want that
fire. The people in grey are out there tonight. I saw them on the bridge."

> **Scaling:** The budget for a High fight at level 6 is 1,400 XP for each
> character.
>
> - **3 characters (4,200 XP):** remove the two Crossbow Sergeants. The
>   total is 4,500 XP. Ilse's agents are a little thin, and the Warden does
>   more of the work.
> - **4 characters (5,600 XP):** use the fight as written. The total is
>   5,400 XP.
> - **5 characters (7,000 XP):** add one Veiled Courier (an agent who came in
>   through the roof) and one more Spy. The total is 6,700 XP.
> - **6 characters (8,400 XP):** add one Veiled Courier, and the second
>   Brass Lamp Warden leaves the door and joins the fight. The total is
>   8,300 XP.

### After the Raid

When the fight ends, the Wardens go back to their places on the wall. Their
heart-lamps go dark one by one. The last one to go dark turns its head and
looks at Wick for a long moment.

> The brass figure climbs back into its place on the wall. The fire in its
> chest sinks to a coal, then to nothing. Before the light goes, its head
> turns, slow and stiff, and it looks at the little lantern in your hands.
>
> Wick does not say anything for a long time. Then: "It knew me. It didn't
> know my name. I didn't know its name. But it knew me."

## Scene 8: Through the Festival (optional)

**Goal:** a short, fast scene. Use it if Ilse escapes with the Lamp, or if
your group wants one more fight with the Hush.

### Option A: The Chase

If Ilse got away with the Lamp, she runs through the festival to a boat at
the Tey quay. The party chases her. Run a quick chase with these steps. Each
step, each character who follows makes the check. A character who fails
falls behind and must catch up with a Dash on the next step.

| Step | What happens | Check | DC |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The crowd on the Long Bridge. Hundreds of people and floating candles. | Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) | 13 |
| 2 | Up a drainpipe onto the roofs of the Tey side. Ilse runs along the ridge. | Strength (Athletics) | 14 |
| 3 | A jump over a narrow street, ten feet, over a line of paper lanterns. | Dexterity (Acrobatics) | 13 |
| 4 | Down to the quay. Where did she go? | Wisdom (Perception) | 15 |

If at least half of the characters succeed at step 4, they catch Ilse at the
boat. She fights for 1 round, then gives up the Lamp and jumps into the
harbor. If they fail, she gets away on the boat, and Gaunt has the Lamp (see
below).

The light of the Lamp makes it easy to follow. Give Advantage on the step 4
check to a character who says "I look for the green light".

### Option B: The Grey on the Bridge

Whether the party has the Lamp or not, the Hush tries its own move on the
same night. Six of them wait on the Long Bridge, where the crowd is thick.
When the Lamp crosses the bridge (in the hands of the party, of Ilse, or of
Perrin, who wants to take it to a safe place), the Hush puts out every lamp
on the bridge at once.

> All the lamps on the bridge go out together, as if someone blew on them.
> The river of little candles below goes dark. In the sudden black, the only
> light is the small green flame of the First Lamp. And around it, out of the
> crowd, come people in grey. They don't shout. They don't run. They just
> come, with their hands open, reaching for the flame.

**Creatures:** one Hush Night Warden, two Cultist Fanatics, and four
Cultists. The Cultists and the Fanatics are new members of the Hush, who
joined in the city.

**The budget:** for 4 characters of level 6, a Low fight is 4 × 600 = 2,400
XP. The Hush Night Warden is 1,100 XP, the two Cultist Fanatics are 900 XP,
and the four Cultists are 100 XP. The total is 2,100 XP, a Low fight. It is
short and fast, after a long night.
