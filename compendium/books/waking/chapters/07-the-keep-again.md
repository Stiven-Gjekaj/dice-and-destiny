# The Keep Again

| | |
|---|---|
| **Levels** | 7 to 8 |
| **Time at the table** | About three sessions (10 to 12 hours) |
| **Places** | The Wolds, Dawnwatch Keep |
| **System that starts** | Strongholds |

## This chapter in short

**Goal:** a home.

The Dawnwatch woke under Dawnwatch Keep and walked out of it into a world
that forgot them. Since then they slept in inns, in tents, and in the guest
rooms of people who wanted something from them. In this chapter they go back
to the keep, and they find the Hush in it.

The Hush hold the keep. They camp in the ruins on the hill, and they dig in
the Hall of Sleepers, where the characters slept for three hundred years.
They smash the crystal coffins and carry away the shards. They also try to
open a door that nobody has opened since the long sleep: the closed study of
Haskel Orme, the steward of the keep.

The characters take back their keep. In the Hall of Sleepers they meet Osk,
the enforcer of the Hush, and Mother Sallow, its leader. Mother Sallow talks
to them, calm and almost kind, and then she slips away. The study opens only
for Wick, because the steward made Wick to be its key. Inside are the first
letters of Haskel Orme, which start to show the lie, and the last war banner
of the Dawnwatch.

Then the keep becomes the home of the party. The Strongholds system starts:
the characters get the keep as a Hold, fix its old rooms, take on a steward,
and play their first Monthly Turn. The first event of the keep is a night
attack by the Hush.

### The scenes

| Scene | What happens | Encounter |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The Road Home | The party hears that the Hush hold the keep, and crosses the Wolds. | None |
| 2. Watchers on the Hill | The party scouts the keep and chooses a way in. | None (a trap is possible) |
| 3. Into the Keep | The party fights the main force of the Hush. | Encounter 1 (Moderate) |
| 4. The Hall of Sleepers | Osk, a Hush Dusk Priest, and Mother Sallow. | Encounter 2 (High) |
| 5. The Steward's Study | Wick opens the study. The letters. | None |
| 6. A Home | Strongholds starts. Hesper Wren and Perrin Hale. | None |
| 7. The Grey Night | The Hush attack the keep at night. | Encounter 3 (Low to Moderate) |

### Before you run this chapter

- Read Strongholds in the Game Master's Compendium. Scene 6 teaches it, and
  the players use it for the rest of the book.
- Check the statblock of Osk in chapter 4. This chapter assumes that Osk is
  about CR 5 (1,800 XP). If his CR is different, change the number of Cultist
  Fanatics in encounter 2 to keep the XP total near the budget.
- Print or copy the handout of the letters of Haskel Orme. You give it to the
  players in Scene 5.
- Look again at the "Before" answers of the players. The Hall of Sleepers is
  where those answers started. Scene 4 uses them.

> **GM tip:** This chapter is about a place that the players already know.
> Use that. Describe the same rooms that they saw in the tutorial, and show
> what changed. The rats are gone. The grease on the Stair of Teeth is new.
> The bowl in the chapel has a grey cloth over it. When players know a place,
> small changes feel big.

### What the Hush want here

The Hush came to the keep about two weeks before the party arrives. Mother
Sallow believes two things:

1. The crystal of the coffins in the Hall of Sleepers is made with the same
   spell as the crystal of her king. A priest of the Hush wrote in his notes:
   "Same crystal. Same hand." She wants the shards to study that spell.
2. The steward hid something in his study that can wake her king fully. She
   calls it "the key of the king's sleep". She is wrong about the key. There
   is no key in the study. The study holds letters, and the letters hold the
   truth.

She also wants the black stone, as she did in chapter 4. If she can take it
here, she takes it.

> **GM tip:** The players must not learn the full truth in this chapter.
> They learn that the steward lied about something, and that the truth is
> under Kingsholm. Chapter 10 gives the rest. If a player guesses the twist,
> do not say yes or no. Say: "Good question. Keep it."

## Scene 1: The Road Home (30 minutes)

**Goal:** Give the party a reason to go back to the keep, and show what the
Hush did to the Wolds.

### The hook

Use one of these hooks, or more than one. Each one leads to the keep.

- **A message from Corrantey.** After chapter 6, Perrin Hale read the rest
  of the oldest ledger of the Lampwrights. One entry says: "The master's study at the keep is
  sealed. Only the light he made can open it." Now a rider of the guild
  catches up with the party. Perrin Hale writes: "A merchant from the Wolds
  says that men in grey hold your keep. I think they want the study. Please
  hurry. I am coming too, as fast as an old cart can go."
- **The black stone.** The black stone hums for a full night. In the hum, the
  bearer hears many small voices, all whispering at once, far away and to the
  east. Wick says: "That way is home."
- **Refugees.** On the road, the party meets shepherds who fled from the
  Wolds (see below).

> Wick is quiet for most of the morning. Then he speaks, very fast, as if
> the words were waiting a long time. "I know it is only a ruin. I know there
> are rats. Well, there were rats. But I lived there for three hundred
> years, four months, and two days. Plus a bit. And now somebody else is in
> it, and they did not ask."

### Across the Wolds

If you use Journeys, run the last day of the trip with it. Brannock's Ford
is one day from the keep, along a sheep track. If you do not use Journeys,
skip to the shepherds.

> The Wolds roll away on each side: low green hills, stone walls, and old
> standing stones with moss on their tops. The sheep are here, but nobody
> keeps them. They stand in the road and stare at you.
>
> In the afternoon you pass a farm. The house is black. The roof is gone. The
> fire is out, and it went out days ago. Nobody is here. In the yard, someone
> pushed every lamp, candle, and lantern of the house into a pile, and broke
> them with a hammer.

The Hush burned the farms of the Wolds to drive the people away from the
keep. They burn by day and leave before dark. A Hush saying goes: "Fire is
for ending things." They break each lamp that they find.

> **Thread (the black stone):** The black stone hums softly from here on,
> and it hums louder as the party gets closer to the keep. Tell the bearer
> each time it changes. It is a clock: when it hums loudly, the Hush are
> near.

### The shepherds

At dusk the party meets a group of shepherds on the road: an old man named
Edric Pole, his two grandsons, and a dog. They carry what they could save.
They are afraid of the party at first, because the party carries a light.

> "Put it out! Put the light out!" The old man waves his stick at you. Then
> he sees your faces in the light of the lantern, and he stops. He looks at
> you for a long time. "No," he says. "No, you are not them. They do not
> carry lights. They hate lights."

Edric knows that the people of the Wolds call the party "the Sleepers from
under the hill". He does not believe it at first. A character who shows the
black stone, or whom Wick names, can make him believe.

| Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Charisma (Persuasion) | 12 | Edric tells what he saw: grey people, about thirty of them, came two weeks ago. They walk at night with no lights. A very big man with them carries a pick. They dig in the hill. |
| Wisdom (Insight) | 13 | Edric is ashamed. He hid in a ditch while his neighbors ran. He wants to help now. If the party asks, he tells them about the well house (see Scene 2). |
| Wisdom (Medicine) | 10 | One grandson has a burned arm. A character who treats it earns the trust of the family. Edric gives the party his good cheese and all his information, with no roll. |
| Intelligence (History) | 15 | The character remembers that the keep had a well, and that the well shaft went down to the armory. The steward used it to send food down to the halls. |

> **GM tip:** Edric is a small character, but he can come back. In Scene 6,
> the keep needs followers. If the party helped him, Edric comes back with
> his grandsons and offers to keep the gate.

If the players do not talk to the shepherds, they still find the keep. They
miss the tip about the well house.

## Scene 2: Watchers on the Hill (30 to 45 minutes)

**Goal:** Let the players scout the keep and choose a way in. Reward a good
plan.

### The keep from outside

> The hill of the keep rises out of the Wolds like the back of a sleeping
> animal. At the top are the walls that you saw on your first day: broken,
> grey, with trees growing through them. Then they were empty.
>
> Now there are tents in the old courtyard. Grey tents, grey cloth hung
> between the walls, and grey banners with no sign on them. People walk
> between the tents. There is no fire, no lamp, not one candle. As the sun
> goes down, the hill goes dark, and the people on it keep walking in the
> dark as if it were day.
>
> Wick's flame shrinks to the size of a fingernail. "They are in my house,"
> he whispers.

The Hush have Grey Sight (the statblock of the Hush Night Warden explains
it), so they do not need lights. Most Hush initiates (Cultists) do not have
Grey Sight. They walk by memory and move slowly at night.

### Scouting

Let each player say how the character scouts. Use this table. Each check
takes about an hour, and a character can try one check. Give one fact for
each success.

| Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Dexterity (Stealth) to get close | 14 | The character counts the Hush: about thirty in all. Twenty are initiates who carry baskets and tools. About ten are soldiers in grey mail (Hush Night Wardens and Cultist Fanatics). |
| Wisdom (Perception) | 13 | Hush soldiers come and go through the white stone door on the hilltop: the Dawn Door. That is the main way in. Four guards stand at it day and night. |
| Wisdom (Survival) | 14 | Tracks lead to the far side of the hill. A landslip opened a crack in the old wall there. The Hush use it to throw out dirt and broken crystal. It leads down into the Stair of Teeth. |
| Wisdom (Insight), watching the camp | 15 | The character sees the pattern of the camp. At dawn, most Hush go inside to sleep. The best time to attack is just after sunrise, when the camp is sleepy. |
| Intelligence (History) or Wick | 12 | The well house. A stone hut in the old courtyard has a shaft that goes 60 feet down into the armory. Wick: "The steward sent soup down that shaft. Cold soup, usually." |
| Intelligence (Investigation), at night | 15 | A cart leaves the hill each night with baskets of broken blue crystal, going north toward the Dunn. The Hush send the shards away. (This is a hint for chapter 10. The cart goes toward Kingsholm.) |

> **Thread (the black stone):** If the bearer of the black stone comes
> within 300 feet of the Dawn Door, the stone hums so loud that the bearer
> must hold it to keep it quiet. Each Hush in the keep feels it too. If the
> bearer comes this close before the attack, add 1 to the alarm (see
> below).

### Three ways in

**The Dawn Door.** The front door. It leads into the Chapel of Dawn, where
the main force of the Hush camps. Four guards stand outside it. A fight
here is loud, and it starts encounter 1 at the door.

**The well house.** A stone hut in the courtyard, with a rotten wooden cover
over the shaft. The shaft is 60 feet deep, with old iron rungs. Most rungs
are good. Each character makes a DC 13 Strength (Athletics) check to climb
down. On a failure, a rung breaks and the character falls the last 20 feet
(2d6 Bludgeoning damage) into the water of the well, which is 10 feet deep.
The splash adds 1 to the alarm. The shaft comes out in the well in the
middle of the armory.

**The broken wall.** On the far side of the hill, the crack in the wall
leads into a tunnel of fallen stone, and then onto the Stair of Teeth, near
the top. This way skips the main force. It goes straight down to the Bell
Gate and up to the Hall of Sleepers. The Hush know the crack, so they put a
trap in it (see "The new teeth" in Scene 3).

> **GM tip:** Each way in is good. None is a trick. The Dawn Door gives a
> straight fight. The well gives surprise. The broken wall gives speed. Let
> the players choose, and then make their choice matter.

### The alarm

Keep a count of the alarm, from 0 to 3. Each of these adds 1:

- A group Dexterity (Stealth) check to get inside fails (DC 13; the group
  succeeds if at least half of the characters succeed).
- A loud noise: a fall, a shout, a spell like Thunderwave.
- The black stone comes close before the attack (see the thread above).

What the alarm does:

| Alarm | Effect |
|---|---|
| 0 | The Hush are surprised: in encounter 1, they have Disadvantage on Initiative. |
| 1 | No surprise. Encounter 1 is normal. |
| 2 | The Hush in the Hall of Sleepers hear the fight. In encounter 2, they are ready, and Mother Sallow starts to leave sooner (see Scene 4). |
| 3 | As alarm 2. Also, two of the initiates in the hall run to the study door with hammers. The party has 3 rounds before they break the hinges of the door frame and damage the door. (The door still does not open. But Wick is very angry.) |

## Scene 3: Into the Keep (45 to 60 minutes)

**Goal:** Fight the main force of the Hush and get to the Hall of Sleepers.

### The keep inside

The players know these rooms from the tutorial. Here is what changed.

**The Chapel of Dawn.** The Hush camp here. Bedrolls lie on the floor. A
grey cloth covers the stone bowl of the Dawnfire, like a shroud on a body.
The crack in the roof is blocked with a tent cloth, so the thin line of
light is gone. The room is dark.

**The armory.** The Hush use it as a store. Baskets of broken blue crystal
stand in rows between the old weapon racks. Picks and shovels lean against
the well.

**The Bell Gate.** The Hush cut the ropes of the three bells. The gate
stands open.

**The Stair of Teeth.** The Hush put new teeth on the stair (see below).

**The Hall of Sleepers.** See Scene 4.

### Where encounter 1 happens

Encounter 1 is the fight with the main force. Where it happens depends on
the way in.

- **The Dawn Door:** at the door and in the chapel. The four guards at the
  door are two Hush Night Wardens and two Cultist Fanatics. The rest of the
  force wakes and joins on the second round.
- **The well:** in the armory. The force is in the chapel, next door, and it
  comes through the big wooden door at the end of the armory. If the alarm
  is 0, the party can choose to attack the sleeping force in the chapel
  instead, with surprise.
- **The broken wall:** the party skips the chapel and goes down the Stair of
  Teeth. The force follows. It catches the party at the Bell Gate, at the
  bottom of the stair, two rounds after the party gets there. The narrow
  gate is a good place to hold. If the party wants, it can go on to the Hall
  of Sleepers first. Then the force arrives in the hall at the start of the
  third round of encounter 2. In that case, use only half of the force
  (round down), so the two fights together are not too much.

### The new teeth

If the party goes down the Stair of Teeth, the trap waits halfway down. The
Hush know the old trap of the steward. They made it again, with new teeth
and fresh grease.

> Halfway down the stair, Wick's light falls on something new: a bright
> copper wire across the steps, and above it, on the old shelf, a new
> barrel. Or it would, if anyone looked.

- **Find it:** DC 15 Wisdom (Perception). A character who remembers the old
  trap from the tutorial has Advantage. Say: "You were here before. You know
  where to look."
- **Disarm it:** DC 15 Dexterity check with Thieves' Tools.
- **If it triggers:** each creature in a 10-foot square makes a DC 15
  Dexterity saving throw, taking 4d10 Piercing damage on a failed save or
  half as much on a success. The square is Difficult Terrain. The barrel
  crashes, which adds 1 to the alarm.

> Wick sniffs. "That is the steward's trap. They stole the steward's trap.
> That is not even their trap."

### Encounter 1: The Main Force

> They come out of the dark with no sound: grey mail, grey hoods, and maces
> that do not shine. Nobody shouts an order. One of them lifts a hand and
> pinches the air, and the torch on the wall dies like a candle under a
> thumb. In the new dark, the grey shapes come on, and you hear only their
> boots.

**Creatures:** 3 Hush Night Wardens (CR 4, 1,100 XP each) and 4 Cultist
Fanatics (CR 2, 450 XP each). The Cultist Fanatics are senior initiates of
the Hush. They wear grey robes, not mail.

**XP:** 3 × 1,100 + 4 × 450 = 5,100 XP. A Moderate fight for four
characters of level 7 (the budget is 4 × 1,300 = 5,200 XP).

{{statblock: Hush Night Warden}}

{{statblock: Cultist Fanatic}}

**Tactics:**

- **Snuff first.** On the first round, each Night Warden uses Snuff as a
  Bonus Action on a torch or a lantern. Wick is magical, so his flame only
  flickers. Describe it: "The grey hand pinches toward Wick. Wick's flame
  bends, shivers... and stays. Wick sticks out a tongue of fire at it."
- **Fight in the dark.** The Wardens have Grey Sight. If the party has no
  light, the Wardens have Advantage against characters who can't see them.
  Wick and the First Lamp from chapter 6 are the answer. Let the players find
  that out.
- **Close the Ranks.** The Wardens stand next to the Fanatics and use their
  Reaction to protect them.
- **No retreat.** The Hush in this force do not flee. They believe that
  their deaths feed the long night. A Fanatic at 10 Hit Points or fewer
  drops its weapon, kneels, and waits in silence. It does not fight again.
  The party can take it prisoner.

> **GM tip:** The Hush are not monsters. They are people who are afraid that
> the world is ending. Play their silence. They do not taunt and they do not
> beg. When one falls, the others do not look. This is scarier than any
> battle cry, and it makes Mother Sallow more interesting in Scene 4.

> **Scaling:** The budget for a Moderate fight at level 7 is 1,300 XP for
> each character.
>
> - **3 characters** (3,900 XP): 2 Hush Night Wardens and 3 Cultist Fanatics
>   (2 × 1,100 + 3 × 450 = 3,550 XP).
> - **4 characters** (5,200 XP): 3 Hush Night Wardens and 4 Cultist Fanatics
>   (5,100 XP).
> - **5 characters** (6,500 XP): 4 Hush Night Wardens and 4 Cultist Fanatics
>   (4 × 1,100 + 4 × 450 = 6,200 XP).
> - **6 characters** (7,800 XP): 5 Hush Night Wardens and 5 Cultist Fanatics
>   (5 × 1,100 + 5 × 450 = 7,750 XP).
>
> If you use other optional systems, make the changes that their chapters
> give, as you did in the earlier chapters.

### After the fight

The party can take a Short Rest in the armory or the chapel. The rest of the
Hush in the camp are initiates (Cultists). They do not fight. They hide in
the tents or run away into the Wolds. Let them go. They are farmers and
clerks who joined a cult because the winters got longer.

If the party took a prisoner, it can ask questions. The prisoner does not
speak at first. The Hush value silence.

| Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Charisma (Intimidation) | 15 | The prisoner says one sentence and no more: "Mother is at the door, and the big one is with her." |
| Charisma (Persuasion) | 14 | The prisoner talks. The Hush dig for shards because "the sleepers and the king were put to sleep by the same hand". Mother Sallow wants the key of the king's sleep, which is behind the door that has no lock. |
| Wisdom (Insight) | 13 | The prisoner is not afraid of death. The prisoner is afraid of the dark that comes after the sun dies, and really believes that it comes soon. |
| Wick talks to the prisoner | No roll | Wick asks why the Hush hate lights. The prisoner answers: "We do not hate them. We are saving them. A fire that burns all night is gone by morning." Wick has no answer, and that bothers him. |

> **Thread (the steward):** "The same hand." If the players ask Wick, he
> says that the steward made the spell of the coffins. "The steward could
> do anything with crystal. He made the coffins, and the coffins worked." He
> does not know of any other crystal. The players can wonder what other
> crystal the steward made.

## Scene 4: The Hall of Sleepers (60 minutes)

**Goal:** A hard fight in the room where the characters woke. Meet Mother
Sallow face to face. Let her escape.

### What they see

> The Hall of Sleepers. You know it: the round stone room, the words above
> the door, WE SLEEP SO THAT THE DAWN CAN WAKE. You woke here.
>
> It is not the same room now. The floor is torn up in long trenches. The
> lines of old light that once ran from the coffins like roots are dug out,
> stone by stone. And the coffins... Your coffins lie broken. Someone took a
> pick to the blue crystal, and the pieces lie in baskets, sorted by size.
>
> A huge man stands in the trench next to the coffin that was yours. He holds
> a pick, and he does not look up. He is breaking the last big piece.
>
> On the far side of the room, next to the niche in the wall where Wick once
> sat, a woman in grey stands in front of a plain stone door. She has her
> hand flat on the door, as if she listens to it. She turns her head and
> looks at you. She smiles.

Let each player look at the broken coffin of the character. Ask one
question to each player: "What do you feel when you see it?" Use the
"Before" answers. A character who said goodbye to someone in the night
before the long sleep can remember that, here.

> **Teach: the story belongs to the players, again.** This is the second
> time that the players stand in this hall. Remind them of the first time.
> "When you woke up here, you told me about [the promise, the stolen thing,
> the sign]. Look at the place where you lay. What do you think about now?"
> Give Heroic Inspiration for the best answer, as in the tutorial.

### Mother Sallow speaks

Mother Sallow does not attack at once. If the party attacks at once, go to
the fight, and let her say her words during the first round, as she moves
away. If the party waits, she talks.

Play her calm, slow, and almost kind. She speaks softly, so that the party
has to be quiet to hear her. She is the opposite of a shouting villain.

> "Sleepers." She says it the way a mother says the name of a child who came
> home late. "Look at what they did to you. Three hundred years in a box, and
> for what? So that a steward could write a pretty story.
>
> "I will not lie to you. I am the only one who will not. The sun is dying.
> Each winter it stands lower. Each summer is a little shorter. Count the
> days, if you do not believe me. Your steward counted them. He knew.
>
> "Give me the stone, and I will leave your hall. You can keep your broken
> beds."

What Mother Sallow wants, in order:

1. The black stone. She asks for it once, politely. She knows that the party
   will say no.
2. Time. She waits for Osk to break the last coffin, and she waits for the
   door. She thinks that the door opens if she listens long enough.
3. The party, if she can have it. She really believes that the Dawnwatch can
   join the Hush, because the Dawnwatch were also made to wait in the dark.

What she says, if the party asks:

- **"Why do you put out lights?"** "Because a light that burns all night is
  gone by morning. We are saving the fire for when it matters."
- **"What is behind the door?"** "The key of the king's sleep. Your steward
  hid it. Or so I believed. Perhaps your lantern knows. Ask your lantern who
  made the lock on this door."
- **"Who is your king?"** "The one who tried to save the sun, and was put in
  a box for it. Like you." She says no more about this. If the players push,
  she only smiles.
- **"Where is the Ember?"** (If the Hush still have the Ember of Saint Ivo
  from chapter 4.) "Safe. Quiet. Asleep, like a good child."

| Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Wisdom (Insight) | 14 | Mother Sallow believes each word that she says. She is not a liar. She is also afraid, and she hides it well. |
| Wisdom (Insight) | 18 | She does not know what is behind the door. She is not sure that the key exists. She came here on a guess, and the guess was wrong, and she knows it. |
| Intelligence (Arcana) | 15 | Grey smoke curls around the hem of her robe. It is a spell that she prepared earlier, ready to take her away. Nobody can stop her from leaving. |
| Charisma (Persuasion) | 20 | She answers one more question truly. She says: "Under Kingsholm there is a room that nobody visits. Go there, if you want to know what your steward did." Then the fight starts. |

> **GM tip:** Mother Sallow does not fight in this chapter, and she has no
> statblock here. She escapes. That is not a trick on the players. It is the
> story: she is the leader of the Hush, and the players meet her at the end
> of Book One in Kingsholm. If the players do something clever to stop her
> (a good grapple, a spell that holds, a door slammed shut), let it cost her
> something. She drops her grey prayer book (see "She leaves" below), or she
> leaves her right shoe in the hand of a grappling Storm. She still escapes.

### Encounter 2: Osk and the Priest

The fight starts when the party attacks, when Mother Sallow gives up on the
stone, or when Osk breaks the last coffin.

> Mother Sallow sighs. "Osk," she says.
>
> The huge man puts down his pick. He climbs out of the trench. He picks up
> a hammer as long as a spear. He still does not look at you. From the dark
> at the edge of the room, a figure in a long grey robe steps out, swinging
> an iron censer. No smoke comes out of it. It breathes in.

**Creatures:** Osk (about CR 5, 1,800 XP), 1 Hush Dusk Priest (CR 7, 2,900
XP), and 4 Cultist Fanatics (CR 2, 450 XP each).

**XP:** 1,800 + 2,900 + 4 × 450 = 6,500 XP. A High fight for four characters
of level 7 (the budget is 4 × 1,700 = 6,800 XP).

{{statblock: Osk}}

{{statblock: Hush Dusk Priest}}

The statblock of the Cultist Fanatic is in Scene 3.

> **GM tip:** Check the CR of Osk in his statblock. If Osk is CR 6 (2,300
> XP), use 3 Cultist Fanatics. If Osk is CR 4 (1,100 XP), use 5 Cultist
> Fanatics. If Osk is CR 7 or more, use no Cultist Fanatics, and give the
> Dusk Priest the role of the second big threat.

**The room.** The Hall of Sleepers is round, 60 feet across. The trenches
are 5 feet wide and 4 feet deep. A creature in a trench has Half Cover
against attacks from outside the trench, and climbing out costs 5 feet of
extra movement. The five broken coffins give Half Cover. The baskets of
crystal shards are Difficult Terrain where they spill.

**Tactics:**

- **Osk** goes for the bearer of the black stone. He does not speak, and he
  does not stop. If the party hides the stone, he goes for the character
  closest to Mother Sallow.
- **The Dusk Priest** opens with Snuff the Sun on the biggest group of
  characters, and puts out their lights. Then it casts Darkness on Wick if it
  can, or on the character who carries Wick. Then it uses Word of Night.
  It uses Dim the Flame on the first attack with Radiant damage.
- **The Fanatics** guard the priest. Two stay next to it. Two try to keep the
  characters away from the study door.

**Mother Sallow leaves.** She leaves at the end of the first round in which
Osk or the Dusk Priest is Bloodied. If the alarm was 2 or more, she leaves
at the end of the first round, whatever happens.

> Mother Sallow looks at the door one last time. Then she looks at you, and
> the look is sad. "You will see," she says. "You will count the days, and
> you will see." The grey smoke around her feet rises like water in a well.
> It covers her to the knees, the waist, the face. Then the smoke falls to
> the floor, and she is not in it.

**Osk** fights to the end. He does not flee and he does not surrender. If a
character spares him at the end (he drops to 0 Hit Points from a blow that
the attacker chooses to make nonlethal, or he is held and cannot fight),
Osk looks at the place where Mother Sallow stood. Then he sits down on the
edge of a trench and waits. He does not speak. What happens to him is the
choice of the players. He does not appear again in Book One.

> **Scaling:** The budget for a High fight at level 7 is 1,700 XP for each
> character. Osk and the Dusk Priest are always in the fight.
>
> - **3 characters** (5,100 XP): Osk, the Dusk Priest, and 2 Cultists
>   (1,800 + 2,900 + 2 × 25 = 4,750 XP).
> - **4 characters** (6,800 XP): Osk, the Dusk Priest, and 4 Cultist
>   Fanatics (6,500 XP).
> - **5 characters** (8,500 XP): Osk, the Dusk Priest, 2 Hush Night Wardens,
>   and 3 Cultist Fanatics (1,800 + 2,900 + 2,200 + 1,350 = 8,250 XP).
> - **6 characters** (10,200 XP): Osk, 2 Hush Dusk Priests, and 2 Hush Night
>   Wardens (1,800 + 5,800 + 2,200 = 9,800 XP).
>
> This fight comes after encounter 1. If the party did not get a Short Rest
> between the two, use the row for one character fewer.

### She leaves

When Mother Sallow is gone, she leaves something behind. Choose one, or use
both if the party stopped her for a moment:

- **Her grey prayer book.** It has no prayers in it. Each page is a list of
  numbers: the length of each day, written down at the same place, year
  after year, for thirty years. The numbers get smaller. On the last page,
  in a different hand, someone drew a plan of a round room "under the hall
  of the king", with a square in the middle labeled "him".
- **A shard.** On the floor where she stood is a small piece of black
  crystal, like the black stone of the party. It is dead. It does not hum.
  If the bearer of the black stone puts the two together, the stone hums
  once, sadly, like a bell with a crack.

> **Thread (the black stone):** When Mother Sallow leaves, the black stone
> stops humming. The hall is quiet. Then, very far away, the bearer hears a
> slow, deep voice: "She does not understand. Not yet. Neither do you." Then
> nothing. Malgrath says nothing else in this chapter.

## Scene 5: The Steward's Study (30 to 45 minutes)

**Goal:** Wick opens the study. The party gets the first letters of Haskel
Orme, and the last banner of the Dawnwatch.

### The door with no lock

> The door is plain grey stone, the same as the wall. It has no handle, no
> hinge, and no keyhole. If you did not know that it was a door, you would
> not see it. The only mark on it is small, at the height of your heart: a
> flame inside a ring.
>
> Next to the door is the niche where Wick sat when you first woke. It is
> just big enough for a lantern. At the back of the niche, a brass bracket is
> set into the stone, shaped like an open hand.

The players saw the mark of the flame inside a ring in chapter 6: it is the
maker's mark of Haskel Orme, the same mark that is on Wick. The players
can try to open the door. Nothing works.

| What the players try | Result |
|---|---|
| Force, magic, a Knock spell, a tool | Nothing. The door is not locked. It is a wall that remembers that it is a door. |
| Intelligence (Arcana), DC 15 | The door is held by an old spell. It waits for one thing: a certain light. |
| Intelligence (Investigation), DC 13 | The bracket in the niche is warm. It is the only warm thing in the room. It is made to hold a lantern of exactly Wick's size. |
| Wick in the niche, but sad and small | The bracket glows a little. The door does not move. |

The answer: Wick must sit in the bracket and burn as bright as he can. In
three hundred years, Wick never burned bright. He was lonely and sad, and
his flame was small. He sat in front of this door for three hundred years,
and it never opened.

Let the players find it. If they need help, Wick finds it himself:

> Wick looks at the niche for a long time. "I sat there," he says. "Right
> there. For three hundred years. The steward put me there on the last
> night, and he said, 'Wait for them, little light. When they come back,
> burn as bright as you like.'" His flame wobbles. "I never did. There was
> nothing to be bright about."

When a character puts Wick in the bracket, ask the players: "What do you
say to Wick?" A kind word, a joke, a memory of the road. Any honest answer
works. Then:

> Wick's flame grows. It grows past the size of a candle, past the size of a
> torch. It fills the niche, and the brass hand closes softly around him.
> White and gold light pours into the Hall of Sleepers, and every shard of
> crystal in the baskets lights up like a star.
>
> In the door, the flame inside the ring glows. Then the stone slides back
> into the wall without a sound.
>
> Wick sits in the brass hand, small again, and very quiet. "He made me to
> open it," he says at last. "I was the key. I was the key the whole time."

> **Thread (Wick):** Wick is moved, and a little hurt. The steward made Wick
> to wait here, but never told him why. Give Wick a moment. If the players
> comfort him, Wick says: "He could have told me. I would have kept the
> secret. I am very good at secrets. I kept the secret of the dogs for a
> whole hour." Wick trusts the party a little more after this, and the
> steward a little less.

### Inside the study

> The study is small and dry. No dust: the air is sealed and still, as if
> the steward stepped out a minute ago. A desk stands under a shelf of books.
> On the desk are a pen, a pot of ink gone to powder, and a neat bundle of
> letters tied with a blue ribbon. A cold hearth. A chair with a worn
> cushion. On the wall hangs a painting of a knight in the blue and gold of
> the Dawnwatch. Someone cut the face out of it with a knife, many years ago.
>
> In the corner stands a long wooden chest with a sun painted on the lid.

**The letters.** Give the players the handout.

{{handout: 07-the-letters-of-haskel-orme}}

The letters are from Haskel Orme to his sister, Wenna Orme. He wrote them
in the last months before the long sleep, and he never sent them. Let the
players read them aloud. Do not explain them. The players can talk about
what they mean as long as they want.

The last of the letters ends with a line that matters: the rest of the
truth is "with him, under Kingsholm". The players do not know who "him" is.
Do not tell them. Wick does not know either.

> **GM tip:** The letters show that the steward lied about something, and
> that he felt bad about it. They do not say what the lie is. If the players
> ask Wick, he says: "The steward did not lie. The steward was the most
> honest person in the keep." Then, after a moment: "Probably." This is the
> first time that Wick is not sure of the steward.

**The painting.** The knight in the painting wears the badge of the
Dawnwatch: a rising sun. The brass plate on the frame is scraped away. Only
two letters are left: "Sir G". A character who makes a DC 15 Intelligence
(History) check knows that the painting is old, older than the Dawnwatch of
the characters, and that the knight was important: he stands in front of
the sun, not under it.

> **GM tip:** The painting shows Sir Garrath, the first of the Dawnwatch,
> who became Malgrath. Do not say this. If the players ask who it is, Wick
> says: "I do not know. The steward never talked about it. He used to sit in
> that chair and look at it." Chapter 10 tells the truth. A player who
> remembers the painting then will have a great moment.

**The chest.** It holds the last war banner of the Dawnwatch, folded with
care, and wrapped in waxed cloth.

{{treasure: The Dawn Banner}}

> The banner is dark blue, with a gold sun. It is faded, and someone patched
> it in three places with careful small stitches. Under the banner is a card
> in the steward's hand: "For the day they come home."

### Other things in the study

Let the players search. Each check takes 10 minutes.

| Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Intelligence (Investigation) | 12 | A drawing of a lantern with a face, with notes: "voice: cheerful", "teach to count", "must not fear the dark". It is the plan of Wick. Wick is delighted and embarrassed at once. |
| Intelligence (Investigation) | 15 | A ledger of the keep. The last page is a list of five names in the steward's hand: the names of the characters. Under each name is a short line. Use the "Before" answers: "Promised to come back." "Took something from the treasury; let it go." Choose a line for each character. |
| Intelligence (Arcana) | 15 | Notes on the crystal spell. The writing is too old and too careful to understand fully, but one note is clear: "The spell holds a sleeper, not a prisoner. It cannot tell the difference. Neither, the gods help me, can I." |
| Wisdom (Perception) | 13 | Under the cushion of the chair: a small brass key. It opens nothing in the study. (It is the key to the strongroom of the keep. In Scene 6, it lets the party build a Vault for half the cost, if they want one.) |
| Intelligence (History) | 18 | A book of the official history of Ostrey, a fresh copy, never read. On the first page, the steward wrote: "Forgive me." |

> **Thread (the steward):** Here is what the players can learn about Haskel
> Orme in this chapter:
>
> - He made the coffins, the crystal spell, the study, and Wick.
> - He loved the Dawnwatch. He wrote their names down and remembered small
>   things about each of them.
> - He wrote something that he was ashamed of, and he asked his sister to
>   forgive him.
> - He knew that the sun was getting weaker (the letters say so).
> - He put the rest of the truth "under Kingsholm", with someone he calls
>   "him".
>
> That is enough. Do not add more. Chapter 10 gives the last letter.
