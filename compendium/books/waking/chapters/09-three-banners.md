# Three Banners

| | |
|---|---|
| **Levels** | 9 to 10 |
| **Time at the table** | 3 or 4 sessions |
| **Places** | The road south from the keep, the village of Wenlow, Ashby Field on the border of Gaunt and Saint Ivo's, the two camps, the Ashby stone |
| **System that starts** | Injuries and scars |

Two armies march to the same field. One flies the grey and steel of Gaunt.
One flies the five gold stars of the Church. Both say that the Dawnwatch
march with them. Neither asked.

In this chapter the characters stand between two powers that both want to
own them, and a third power that wants them both to bleed. They can choose
a side. They can try to stop the war. Whatever they choose, they fight
their first true battle, and they carry its marks for a long time.

## The chapter at a glance

**Goal:** the party chooses a side, or stops a war.

1. **Two banners at the gate.** Riders from Gaunt and from the Church come
   to Dawnwatch Keep on the same day. Each brings a proclamation that says
   the Dawnwatch stand with it.
2. **The burning chapel.** On the road south, the characters catch soldiers
   in Gaunt colors as they burn a chapel. Under the tabards is grey mail.
   (Encounter 1.)
3. **Ashby Field.** The two armies face each other across a shallow valley.
   In the middle stands the Ashby stone, where the kings of Ostrey once
   swore their treaties.
4. **The camp of Gaunt.** Marshal Ide Gauntry, her son Tobin, Captain Ilse
   Varro, and the helpful Lieutenant Roe.
5. **The camp of the Church.** Abbess Merrow, Brother Cade, the quiet
   Brother Ansel, and a young corporal with an old token.
6. **The dead envoy.** In the night, an envoy of Gaunt dies with a Church
   knife in his back. Tomorrow the armies fight. The characters choose.
7. **The battle of Ashby Field.** Three phases: the Grey, the Charge, and
   the Heart. (Encounters 2, 3, and 4, and an optional duel.)
8. **After the battle.** A truce at the stone, or a victory, or a grave.
   The first scars at the fire. A prisoner names Kingsholm.

### What is really going on

Read this part before you run the chapter. The players learn it slowly.

- **The Hush wants a war.** A land at war does not look north. While Gaunt
  and the Church bleed each other, nobody sees the Hush gather in the ruins
  of Kingsholm. And Mother Sallow has a second wish: if the Dawnwatch lead
  two armies into a slaughter, nobody calls them saints again.
- **Two agents start the fire.** Lieutenant Roe is the aide of Marshal
  Gauntry. Brother Ansel is the secretary of Abbess Merrow. Both are
  members of the Hush. Both came to their posts two years ago, with good
  letters and no past.
- **Two false attacks.** A Hush band in Gaunt tabards burned the chapel at
  Wenlow, on the land of the Church. (The characters catch a second band at
  work in Scene 2.) And on the first night at Ashby Field, Roe kills the
  envoy of Gaunt and leaves a Church knife in the body.
- **On the day of the battle,** the Hush brings the grey: a Grey Choir that
  sings a fog over the valley, so that each army thinks the other attacks
  first.

The armies are not evil. Marshal Gauntry wants a strong land, and she
believes that she is the only one who can make it. Abbess Merrow wants the
Sleepers safe from soldiers, and she believes that her prayers are enough
to keep them. Both are proud. Both are a little afraid. The Hush only has
to push.

> **GM tip:** The players do not have to find every clue. Two clues are
> enough to make a good case. If the players are stuck, let Wick ask the
> question that the players forgot: "Why does the Lieutenant always stand
> where the light is worst?"

## Before you start: Injuries

This chapter starts the **Injuries and Scars** system of the Game Master's
Compendium. Read that chapter before the session. It is short.

Injuries are optional, and they change the feel of the game. So talk to
the players before you start, not in the middle of a fight. Say something
like this:

> **Teach: the table agreement.** "From this chapter on, I want to use
> injuries. When an enemy scores a Critical Hit on you, or when you drop to
> 0 Hit Points, you can get a real wound: cracked ribs, a broken arm, a cut
> above the eye. Hit Points come back after a night of sleep. An injury
> stays until it heals. An injury never kills you and never takes you out
> of the game. And when it heals slowly, with rest, it leaves a scar that
> makes you a little tougher. Do we want this?"

Agree on two questions, as the Compendium says:

- **Who can be injured?** The characters and the people who matter. In this
  chapter, that means the Marshal, Tobin, Corporal Lenna, and the two
  agents. Ordinary soldiers and Hush members just fall.
- **Can a Lost Eye be for good?** The player always chooses. Some tables
  leave permanent losses out. Ask.

If the table says no to injuries, run the chapter as it is and skip the
Teach boxes about injuries. The story still works. The battle is still a
battle.

> **GM tip:** This is the right chapter to start injuries. The characters
> are level 9. They are strong, and they have Hit Points to spare. A
> battle with hundreds of soldiers is the place where a hero comes home
> with a limp and a story. And the next chapter is the end of Book One, so
> the players carry their scars into the finale.

## Scene 1: Two Banners at the Gate

**Goal:** Start the war. Show that both sides want the Dawnwatch, and that
neither side asked them.

Run this scene at Dawnwatch Keep, during or right after a monthly turn of
the stronghold. If the characters are somewhere else, the riders find them
there.

> It is a grey morning on the walls of the keep. Hesper Wren comes up the
> stair two steps at a time, out of breath.
>
> "Riders on the road. Two groups. One from the south, one from the west.
> They are racing each other."
>
> From the wall you see them: four riders in grey and steel, under a red
> banner with an iron gauntlet. Behind them, three riders in white, under a
> blue banner with five gold stars. The grey riders reach the gate first.
> The white riders shout at them. Somebody's horse bites somebody else's
> horse.
>
> Wick peers over the edge of the wall. "Oh, good," he says. "Visitors who
> already hate each other. That saves time."

Both groups have the same task: give the Dawnwatch a proclamation, and
bring the Dawnwatch back with them. Give the players the handout now.

{{handout: 09-two-proclamations}}

### The riders

- **The riders of Gaunt** are led by a young officer, Ensign Aldo Varne. He
  is polite and stiff. He has orders: "The Marshal requests that the
  Dawnwatch ride to Ashby Field, as the honor guard of Gaunt." He has a
  horse for each character, already saddled.
- **The riders of the Church** are led by a lay sister, Sister Orla. She is
  warm and loud. She has a letter from Abbess Merrow, sealed with the five
  stars: "Come to us, and stand where all the faithful can see you. Your
  presence alone can stop this."

Both riders say that the other side started it. Both believe it.

### What the riders know

| Question | The rider of Gaunt says | The rider of the Church says |
|---|---|---|
| Why is there a war? | "The Church burned its own chapel at Wenlow and blamed us. Then it closed the pilgrim road to Gaunt traders." | "Gaunt soldiers burned the chapel at Wenlow. We saw the tabards. Now Gaunt sends an army north." |
| Who burned the chapel? | "Monks in disguise. Or a mob of their pilgrims." | "Soldiers in grey and steel. Eleven of them, on foot." |
| Where are the armies? | "At Ashby Field, on the border. Six days to the south." | "At Ashby Field. Ten thousand faithful, the abbess says. Perhaps two thousand." |
| When will they fight? | "When the Marshal decides. Soon." | "When Gaunt attacks. We will not attack first." |
| What do they want from us? | "To ride with the Marshal. Your banner beside hers." | "To stand with the faithful. Your faces, in the light." |

Let the characters ask questions. Use this table for checks.

| Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Wisdom (Insight) on Ensign Varne | 12 | He is afraid. He has never been in a battle. He believes the story about the monks, because his lieutenant told it to him. "Lieutenant Roe saw the reports himself." |
| Wisdom (Insight) on Sister Orla | 12 | She is honest, and she is angry. She saw the burned chapel herself. Something about it bothers her, but she does not want to say it in front of Gaunt. |
| Charisma (Persuasion) on Sister Orla, in private | 14 | "The soldiers at Wenlow did not shout. Gaunt soldiers always shout. And they put out every lamp in the village before they lit the fire. Why would you do that?" |
| Intelligence (History) | 15 | Ashby Field is old. The kings of Ostrey met their rivals there, at a standing stone, to swear treaties. "No blood at the stone" was the rule. In the time of the characters, it had another name: the Field of the Five, where the army of the king stopped the hounds of the Unlit King, in the snow, at dawn. The song "The Five at the Gate" tells of that day, and gets almost all of it wrong. |

> **GM tip:** Sister Orla's detail is the first clue. People who put out
> the lights before they act: the players have seen that before. If no
> player makes the connection, Wick does, very quietly: "They put out the
> lamps first. Who does that?"

### The choice of the road

Both riders want the characters to ride with them, right now. The
characters can:

- **Ride with Gaunt.** Ensign Varne takes the good road. They arrive at the
  Gaunt camp. Give each character +1 renown with Gaunt for the courtesy.
- **Ride with the Church.** Sister Orla takes the pilgrim road. They arrive
  at the Church camp. Give each character +1 renown with the Church.
- **Ride alone, or with both.** The characters go by their own road, and
  the riders follow, still arguing. Nobody gets renown, and nobody is
  offended.

Whichever road they take, the road passes Wenlow. Go to Scene 2.

> **Teach: renown, again.** "Each side keeps track of how much it trusts
> you. That is your renown, from chapter 5. Riding with one side is a small
> service. The big deeds come at Ashby Field." Remind the players of their
> current ranks with Gaunt and with the Church. A character who reaches the
> third rank in one faction is an enemy of the other, so the choice in this
> chapter can cost something.

## Scene 2: The Burning Chapel

**Goal:** Catch the Hush at work. Give the players the first hard proof
that a third hand stirs the war.

The village of Wenlow lies three days south of the keep, on the old road
between Brannock's Ford and Saint Ivo's, on the land of the Church. Its chapel already burned, ten days ago. But the Hush is thorough.
Tonight a second band comes to burn the inn, where the people of Wenlow now
pray, and to leave a few Gaunt badges in the ashes.

> The road comes down into a little valley in the last light of the day.
> Wenlow is twenty houses, a mill, and the black shell of a chapel. Its
> roof is gone. Its walls are scorched.
>
> Then the lights of the village go out. Not all at once: one by one, from
> the north end of the street to the south, like someone walking along and
> pinching each candle.
>
> In the dark, someone screams. A torch flares at the door of the inn.
> In its light you see soldiers in grey and steel tabards, with the iron
> gauntlet of Gaunt on their chests. They are nailing the door shut. People
> are inside.

### Encounter 1: The False Soldiers

The band works fast. Two soldiers nail the door of the inn and pile straw
against it, while the rest watch the street. A priest in a soldier's cloak
stands back in the dark and watches.

- **Creatures:** 3 Hush Night Wardens, 1 Hush Dusk Priest, and 4 Hush
  Shrouds, all in Gaunt tabards.
- **XP:** 3 × 1,100 + 2,900 + 4 × 450 = 8,000 XP. For four characters of
  level 9, this is a **Moderate** encounter (4 × 2,000 = 8,000).
- **The inn:** eighteen villagers are inside. The straw catches fire on
  round 2. On round 4, the inn burns, and each villager still inside is in
  danger. A character next to the door can use an action to break it open
  (Strength (Athletics), DC 15) or pull the boards off (no check, but it
  takes the full action and the Bonus Action).
- **The dark:** the Wardens have used Snuff on every lamp in the street.
  The street is dark, except for the torch at the inn door and the light
  that the characters bring. The Wardens and the priest have Grey Sight,
  and the Shrouds have Darkvision. Light matters here:
  Wick, the First Lamp, and the Storm Lantern all help.

**Tactics:** The Shrouds fight in the street. Two Wardens hold the door of
the inn and use Close the Ranks for each other. The third Warden shoots
from the mill. The Dusk Priest casts Darkness on the brightest light, then
uses Word of Night on the character who carries it. When two Wardens fall,
the priest tries to flee into the dark of the mill race. A Shroud who is
alone gives up.

**What the band carries:** Under each tabard is grey mail with no badge.
Each member carries a stub of black candle, never lit, and a small purse
with Gaunt silver (2d6 × 10 GP in all). The priest has a letter in a code
of short lines and dots. The letter ends with a drawing of a candle with
no flame.

> **Scaling:** The budget for this encounter is Moderate: 2,000 XP for
> each character.
>
> - **3 characters (6,000 XP):** 2 Hush Night Wardens, 1 Hush Dusk Priest,
>   and 2 Hush Shrouds (2,200 + 2,900 + 900 = 6,000).
> - **4 characters (8,000 XP):** as written.
> - **5 characters (10,000 XP):** 4 Hush Night Wardens, 1 Hush Dusk Priest,
>   and 6 Hush Shrouds (4,400 + 2,900 + 2,700 = 10,000).
> - **6 characters (12,000 XP):** 4 Hush Night Wardens, 2 Hush Dusk
>   Priests, and 4 Hush Shrouds (4,400 + 5,800 + 1,800 = 12,000).
>
> **Optional systems:** Techniques started in chapter 8. At level 5 or
> more, add one monster of the lowest CR for each four characters (here,
> one Hush Shroud), or give each Warden and the priest 10% more Hit
> Points. If you also use other optional systems, count each one
> separately, as the Compendium says.

### After the fight

The villagers come out coughing. An old miller, Gaffer Tull, takes off his
hat and stares at the characters for a long time. "You are them," he says.
"The Sleepers. I knew you would be taller."

The people of Wenlow tell the characters what they saw ten days ago: the
same soldiers, the same silence, the same lamps going out. They believed
the tabards. Now they are not so sure.

| Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Intelligence (Investigation) on the bodies | 12 | The tabards are new. The stitching is cheap. Real Gaunt tabards have the badge of the company on the shoulder. These have none. |
| Wisdom (Perception) in the ashes of the old chapel | 14 | A cheap Gaunt badge, pressed into the ash on purpose. Nobody drops a badge face up in the exact middle of a floor. |
| Intelligence (Arcana or Religion) on the coded letter | 16 | The code is simple, once you see it: each line is a word, each dot a letter. It says: "The field in four days. R. and A. are in place. Let them meet in the grey." |
| Charisma (Intimidation or Persuasion) on a captured Shroud | 15 | The prisoner says only: "The sun is dying. You will see. Mother Sallow says so." Then the prisoner says nothing more. |

> **GM tip:** "R. and A." are Roe and Ansel. The players do not know those
> names yet. Let them carry the letter into the camps. When they meet a
> Lieutenant Roe and a Brother Ansel, let them make the connection
> themselves. It is a great moment at the table.

The black stone hums the whole time that the Hush prisoners are near.
Remind the bearer of this. It is useful later.

## Scene 3: Ashby Field

**Goal:** Show the scale of the war. Show the place where the battle will
happen.

Three days after Wenlow, by Saint Ivo's and the good road of Gaunt, the
road comes over a low ridge.

> Below you, the land opens into a long, shallow valley of grass and
> stone walls. A stream runs down the middle, so small that you could jump
> it. On the south slope stands a city of tents in grey and black, with
> thin columns of smoke and the flash of steel. On the north slope stands
> a second city, larger and messier, of white tents and wagons, with a
> hundred blue banners and a sound of singing.
>
> In the bottom of the valley, by the stream, stands one grey stone, taller
> than a rider. Nobody camps near it. A ring of trampled grass shows where
> both armies have sent riders to look at it, and turned back.
>
> Wick goes very quiet. Then he says, "I know that stone. The steward told
> me about it, the night he made me. The kings swore on it. No blood at the
> stone. That was the rule." He pauses. "I do not think anyone here
> remembers the rule."

### The field

Describe the field to the players. They fight here in Scene 7, and they
plan better if they know it.

- **The south slope:** the camp of Gaunt. About 2,000 soldiers: pike
  squares, crossbows, and 300 riders. Neat rows. Good order.
- **The north slope:** the camp of the Church. About 3,000 people: some
  knights of the Church and templars, many levies from the farms and towns
  with spears and old helmets, and a crowd of pilgrims who came to watch
  and pray. Less order. More heart.
- **The Ashby stone:** a standing stone of Ostrey, 12 feet tall, by the
  stream. The old words cut in it are worn almost flat. A character who
  reads the old speech of Ostrey can read them: "HERE THE SWORD SLEEPS."
- **The stream:** 5 feet wide, knee-deep. Difficult Terrain.
- **The stone walls:** old field walls, 3 feet high. They give Half Cover.
- **The hollow:** a dip west of the stone, out of sight of both camps. Fog
  gathers there each morning. (This is where the Hush hides its choir.)

> **Thread (Wick):** Wick never saw this field. But on the night that he
> made Wick, the steward talked about it, the way people talk to a thing
> that they think cannot listen yet. If a player asks, Wick tells it: "He
> stood by that stone once, with a king, to swear a peace. He said the
> words, and the king said them back, and nobody died that day. He was
> very proud. He told me three times." Wick is quiet for a moment. "He
> told me a lot of things. He did not tell me the important ones."

From here the characters go to one camp or both. Most groups visit both.
Run Scenes 4 and 5 in the order that the players choose.

## Scene 4: The Camp of Gaunt

**Goal:** Meet the Marshal, her son, her spymaster, and her helpful aide.
Find clues.

> The camp of Gaunt is quiet. Soldiers clean armor in rows. Sergeants walk
> between them and say little. Every tent has the same shape, the same
> distance from the next one, the same red flag.
>
> In the center is a large tent with its walls rolled up. A tall woman in
> plain steel stands over a table with a map. She is about sixty, with grey
> braids and a face like a closed door. She looks up, and for a moment
> the door opens a little.
>
> "The Dawnwatch," says Marshal Ide Gauntry. "I read about you when I was
> a girl. Sit. Tell me how many of the books were wrong."

### Marshal Ide Gauntry

The Marshal is the most dangerous person in the chapter, because she is
reasonable. Play her calm, curious, and sure. She does not shout. She asks
good questions and listens to the answers.

- **What she wants:** the Dawnwatch at her side tomorrow, in front of both
  armies. If the Dawnwatch ride with Gaunt, the Church levies will break
  and run home, and nobody needs to die. "That is not a threat. That is
  the kindest plan I have."
- **What she believes:** the Church burned its own chapel, and killed her
  envoy (after Scene 6). The priests want the Dawnwatch as statues, and the
  land needs them as soldiers.
- **What she will not do:** attack first without a reason. She has given
  her army that order. She thinks that the Church will give her a reason
  soon.

If the characters met her in chapter 5, she remembers it, and she says
what she thought of them then, honestly.

If Gaunt took the First Lamp in chapter 6, it stands on a pole outside
her tent, behind a guard of four soldiers. She carries it into the battle
as a banner. The characters can win it back: as the price of a truce, as
the prize of the duel, or from Ilse Varro, who never liked the plan.

### Tobin Gauntry

Tobin is her son, twenty years old, in armor that is too new for him. He
stands at the edge of the tent and does not speak while his mother speaks.
Later, he finds the characters alone.

> The young man catches up with you between the tents, out of breath. He
> looks back over his shoulder twice.
>
> "You must stop this," Tobin says. "She thinks it will be quick. It is
> never quick. Half of those people on the other slope are farmers. My
> mother has never met a farmer. She has met maps of them."

Tobin is the key to the third path. He does not know about the Hush. He
knows that his mother trusts Lieutenant Roe more each month, and he does
not like it. "Roe is always there. Roe always agrees with her. Roe told
her about the chapel before the riders even came."

### Captain Ilse Varro

The characters met Ilse in Corrantey in chapter 6. How that went decides
how she greets them now:

- **If they parted as enemies:** she is cold and polite, and she watches
  them. She still talks, because she is a professional.
- **If they made a deal, or parted with respect:** she is almost friendly.
  "You again. I hoped it would be you."

Ilse is suspicious of Roe for her own reasons. She did not write the
report about the chapel. Roe did. And the envoy who dies in Scene 6 was
one of her people. If the characters bring her proof, she becomes their
best ally in the Gaunt camp. She can get them to the Marshal at any hour,
and she can arrest Roe if the proof is good enough.

### Lieutenant Roe

> At the Marshal's shoulder stands a slim man in a grey officer's coat, so
> neat that he looks drawn with a ruler. He bows to you, low and correct.
> "Lieutenant Roe, at your service. If you need anything at all, you ask
> me first."

Roe is helpful, charming, and always present. He offers the characters a
tent, food, a guide. He agrees with whatever the Marshal says, a moment
after she says it. He is a member of the Hush, and he is very good at his
work.

**Clues that Roe gives away** (the characters notice these only if they
look):

- He always stands where the light is worst: at the back of the tent, out
  of the circle of the lamp.
- He flinches from the light of the First Lamp, and he does not look at
  Wick.
- He does not drink the wine. (The Hush keeps a fast before its great
  works.)
- The black stone hums when he is within 30 feet.

| Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Wisdom (Insight) on the Marshal | 13 | She is less sure than she looks. She does not want to kill farmers. She wants someone to give her a way out that does not look like weakness. |
| Wisdom (Insight) on Roe | 18 | He is not afraid of the battle. He is looking forward to it. A character who beats the DC by 5 or more also notices that he has never once said the word "light". |
| Wisdom (Perception) in Roe's tent | 15 | No lamp at all. A stub of black candle in a boot, never lit. |
| Intelligence (Investigation) of the reports on the Marshal's table | 14 | The report about Wenlow is in Roe's hand, and it is dated one day before the chapel burned. Someone wrote it before the fire. |
| Charisma (Persuasion) with the Marshal | 16 | She agrees to wait one more day before she attacks, if the characters bring her proof of a third hand. (Only with real evidence: the letter from Wenlow, the dated report, or a witness.) |
| Charisma (Deception) with Roe | 15 | He believes that the characters will ride with Gaunt. He relaxes, and he says one thing too many: "Then the field will be grey for both sides tomorrow." |

> **GM tip:** Do not let the players accuse Roe in front of the Marshal
> with no proof. If they do, the Marshal sides with her aide, and the
> characters lose 2 renown with Gaunt. Roe smiles. Then he moves his plan a
> day earlier. (The battle starts at dawn, and the dead envoy of Scene 6
> happens tonight.)

## Scene 5: The Camp of the Church

**Goal:** Meet the Abbess, an old friend, a quiet secretary, and a young
soldier who carries the Shield's past. Find clues.

> The camp of the Church sings. Everywhere you look, people sing: hymns
> around the cook fires, marching songs at the spear drills, and one old
> woman who sings the same verse to a goat, over and over. Pilgrims crowd
> the paths. Children run between the tents with wooden swords and argue
> about which of them is the Shield.
>
> When the first person sees your faces, the singing stops. Then it
> starts again, louder, and very much worse, because everyone sings a
> different song.

The characters cannot walk anywhere in this camp without a crowd. People
want to touch their cloaks, show them a sick child, or ask them which side
of the bed the Light sleeps on. This is funny, and it is also a little
frightening.

### Abbess Merrow

> The Abbess receives you in a great white tent full of candles. She is
> old and small and very bright, with the five gold stars sewn on her
> sleeves. She takes the hands of the nearest character in both of hers.
>
> "You came," she says. "I knew you would. They cannot fight in front of
> you. Nobody can. You only have to stand on the hill and let them see
> you."

Abbess Merrow wants the characters as living saints. That has not changed
since chapter 3. Now she has a plan for it: the characters stand on the
north slope in the morning, under the banner of the Five, and Gaunt
loses its nerve.

- **What she wants:** the Dawnwatch on the hill, in white, in the light.
- **What she believes:** Gaunt burned Wenlow. Gaunt sent an envoy with
  threats. The Sleepers belong to the faithful, because the faithful kept
  their memory for a hundred and sixty years.
- **What she will not do:** attack first. "We are the Church. We do not
  attack." She does not see that three thousand people with spears are an
  attack, to the other side.

### Brother Cade

Brother Cade thought that the characters were impostors, in chapter 3.
Now he is their friend, and he is worried.

> Brother Cade finds you outside the tent and walks you away from the
> crowd, fast. "Do not let her put you on that hill," he says. "If you
> stand there in white, Gaunt sees an army with five heroes in front. They
> will charge the hill to take you. Merrow thinks you stop a war. You
> start it."

Cade is the key to the third path in this camp, as Tobin is in the other.
He also does not trust Brother Ansel. "He came to us two years ago with a
letter from a house of prayer that I cannot find on any map. He writes all
her letters now. She signs them without reading."

### Brother Ansel

> Behind the Abbess, at a writing desk in the shadow of the tent wall,
> sits a pale young monk with ink on his fingers. He does not get up. He
> nods to you once, a very small nod, and goes back to his writing.

Ansel is quiet, polite, and almost invisible. He wrote the letter of the
Church in the handout, and the Abbess signed it. He is the one who
suggested the call for spears. He is a member of the Hush.

**Clues that Ansel gives away:**

- His desk is the only place in the tent with no candle. He writes in the
  light of the candles of others.
- He trims the wicks of the candles near him very short, so that they
  give almost no light.
- The black stone hums when he is within 30 feet.
- His sandals are wet with the dew of the hollow west of the stone. He

### The last night

The Marshal and the Abbess both send for the characters before dawn. Both

to the battle in Scene 7.

the charge from becoming a massacre.

**Path 2: With the Church.** The characters stand on the hill with the

## Scene 7: The Battle of Ashby Field

**Goal:** The first true battle. Teach injuries. Let the characters'
choice decide the shape of the day.

### How to run the battle

Do not try to run five thousand soldiers. The armies are the weather. The
characters fight in the places where the battle turns. The battle has
three phases, and each phase is one encounter:

1. **The Grey:** at dawn, the Hush choir sings a fog over the valley. The
   characters fight the Hush in the fog. (Encounter 2.)
2. **The Charge:** the armies move. The characters fight soldiers, or stand
   between them. (Encounter 3.)
3. **The Heart:** the characters find the agents and the choir in the
   hollow, and break the Hush. (Encounter 4.)

Between the phases comes **a lull**. The armies pull back to form again,
the wounded crawl to the rear, and the characters have about one hour.
They can take a Short Rest in each lull. Remind the players of that. It is
the only rest that they get today.

### The Toll

Keep a number called **the Toll** for the day. It starts at 0. It shows how
many people die on Ashby Field. Each phase tells you when the Toll goes up.
At the end of the battle, the Toll tells you how the day ends (see Scene
8). You can tell the players that you keep it, or keep it secret. If you
tell them, say: "This is how many people die today. Lower is better."

### Running Injuries in the battle

> **Teach: injuries.** Use this box the first time that an enemy scores a
> Critical Hit on a character, or a character drops to 0 Hit Points. Stop
> the fight for one minute.
>
> "That was a big hit. You can get an injury now. First you choose. Take
> the injury, and roll on the Injury table. Or shake it off: you gain one
> level of Exhaustion instead, and no injury."
>
> If the player takes the injury: "Roll a d20. Because you dropped to 0
> Hit Points, add 5. The highest is 20." (Add the 5 only for a drop to 0
> Hit Points, not for a Critical Hit alone.) Read the injury from the
> table in the Injuries and Scars chapter. For example, an 11 is Cracked
> Ribs, a 16 is a Broken Arm, and an 8 is a Cut Above the Eye.
>
> "Each injury says what it does, and how it heals. Healing magic gives you
> Hit Points back, but it does not fix the injury. A Minor injury heals
> after a Long Rest. A Serious one needs several Long Rests with care, or a
> Lesser Restoration spell. A Lasting one needs a month of rest, or strong
> magic."
>
> Keep it short. Then go on with the fight.

Remember these rules during the battle:

- **One injury for each hit.** A Critical Hit that also drops the character
  to 0 Hit Points gives one injury, not two.
- **Critical Hits give an injury only once in each turn,** however many of
  them land on a character.
- **Three at most.** A character carries at most three injuries. If a
  fourth comes, the player chooses which injury it replaces. The old one
  heals with no scar.
- **Exhaustion adds up.** A character who shakes off two injuries has two
  levels of Exhaustion. Tell the player what that means on the sheet. A
  Long Rest removes one level.
- **The people who matter can be injured too.** If the Marshal, Tobin, or
  Lenna drops to 0 Hit Points or takes a Critical Hit, roll an injury for
  them. A Marshal with a Broken Arm after the battle is a strong picture.

> **GM tip:** The first injury at your table is a story moment. Describe
> it: the crack of a rib, the arm that does not lift, the blood in the eye.
> Then let the character show it in play. A fighter with Cracked Ribs who
> still holds the line is the reason to use this system.

### Phase 1: The Grey (Encounter 2)

> The dawn comes up red over the east ridge. On both slopes, horns blow.
> The lines form: grey squares of pikes on the south slope, a crowd of
> spears under blue banners on the north slope. Between them, by the
> stream, the stone stands alone.
>
> Then you hear it. A hum, low and wide, as if the ground itself sings with
> its mouth closed. It comes from the west, from the hollow. And with the
> hum comes the fog. Grey, thick, cold. It pours out of the hollow and
> across the valley floor like milk across a table. In a minute you cannot
> see the stone. In two, you cannot see your hand.
>
> Out of the grey, on both slopes, men begin to shout. "They are coming!"
> "They are attacking!" Nobody is attacking. Not yet.

The fog comes from the Grey Choirs, who walk inside it and sing. Under its
cover, Hush Night Wardens in Gaunt tabards shoot crossbows at the Church
line, and Wardens in Church white shoot at the Gaunt line. In a few
minutes, both armies charge.

Wherever the characters are, two choirs and their guards come out of the
fog near them: the Hush wants the Dawnwatch busy, or dead.

- **Creatures:** 2 Hush Grey Choirs and 3 Hush Night Wardens.
- **XP:** 2 × 2,300 + 3 × 1,100 = 7,900 XP. For four characters of level 9,
  this is a **Moderate** encounter (8,000).
- **The grey:** the fog is magical. The whole area is Heavily Obscured
  beyond 10 feet, except within the Bright Light of a light source. The
  Hush have Grey Sight and see 30 feet in it. The Song of the Long Night of
  each choir turns Bright Light within 30 feet of it into Dim Light, so
  the closer the choirs come, the darker it gets. Sunlight, such as the
  light of the First Lamp or the Fennick Candle, is not dimmed by the fog,
  but the songs still dim it to Dim Light.
- **Ending the grey:** the fog ends when both choirs are destroyed, or when
  a creature casts a spell of level 3 or higher that makes light or wind in
  the fog (such as Daylight or Gust of Wind cast with a slot of level 3 or
  higher). A Dispel Magic from the Storm Lantern also ends it, in a
  60-foot radius around the lantern.

**Tactics:** The choirs move into the characters' spaces and attack with
their knives and cudgels, and they use Hymn of Quiet as often as they can,
on spellcasters first. The Wardens stay at the edge of the light, shoot,
and use Snuff on torches and lanterns. A Warden next to a choir uses Close
the Ranks for the choir.

**The Toll:** if the fight lasts more than 5 rounds, the armies meet in the
fog before the characters can stop it. The Toll goes up by 1. If the
characters end the grey in 3 rounds or fewer, the armies see that nobody
attacked them, and they halt in confusion. The Toll does not go up, and
the characters can call out to the lines (see Phase 2).

> **Scaling:** The budget for this encounter is Moderate: 2,000 XP for
> each character.
>
> - **3 characters (6,000 XP):** 1 Hush Grey Choir and 3 Hush Night
>   Wardens (2,300 + 3,300 = 5,600).
> - **4 characters (8,000 XP):** as written.
> - **5 characters (10,000 XP):** 2 Hush Grey Choirs and 5 Hush Night
>   Wardens (4,600 + 5,500 = 10,100).
> - **6 characters (12,000 XP):** 3 Hush Grey Choirs and 4 Hush Night
>   Wardens (6,900 + 4,400 = 11,300). Each choir sings from a different
>   side.
>
> **Optional systems:** with Techniques on, add one Hush Night Warden for
> each four characters, or give each creature 10% more Hit Points.

### The first lull

> The grey thins. Through it you see the field again, and it is worse than
> the fog. The lines are broken and tangled. Pikes lie in the grass. A horse
> with no rider walks in circles by the stream. On both slopes, officers
> scream at their soldiers to form again, to form again, to form.
>
> For a moment, nobody fights. Everyone breathes.

The characters have about one hour before the Charge. They can take a Short
Rest, tend injuries (a Healer's Kit or a Wisdom (Medicine) check, as the
injuries say), and talk to their side. On the stop-the-war path, this is
the moment to ride to the stone.

> **GM tip:** Tending an injury now does not heal it. Most injuries need a
> Long Rest after the care. But the care is what counts later, so a
> character who tends a friend in the lull is doing real work. Let a
> player who rolls the Medicine check describe it.

### Phase 2: The Charge (Encounter 3)

The armies form again, and this time they move for real. Where the
characters are depends on their path. Run one of the three versions.

#### The Gaunt version (the characters stand with the Church)

> On the south slope, the grey squares turn as one. Then the riders come
> out between them, three hundred lances, and start down the hill at a
> walk. Then a trot. The ground shakes under your boots.
>
> In the front rank of the levy, right next to you, a young woman with a
> corporal's knot sets the butt of her spear in the earth. Her hands
> shake. She holds on.

The charge of Gaunt hits the Church line where the characters stand. The
characters fight the point of the charge. If they break the point, the
charge breaks with it, and the riders behind wheel away.

- **Creatures:** 1 Shield-Wall Captain, 2 Mounted Lancers (each on a
  Warhorse), 5 Line Pikemen, and 2 Crossbow Sergeants.
- **XP:** 1,800 + 2 × (700 + 100) + 5 × 200 + 2 × 450 = 5,300 XP. For four
  characters of level 9, this is a **Low** encounter (5,200), but the
  characters also have a task.
- **The task:** keep Corporal Lenna and her squad alive. Lenna uses the
  Line Pikeman statblock. The lancers ride at her squad first. If Lenna
  drops to 0 Hit Points, roll an injury for her, and she is out of the
  fight.
- **Breaking the point:** when the Shield-Wall Captain and both lancers
  are down, the rest of the point falls back, and the charge turns.

**The Toll:** if Lenna's squad breaks (Lenna and two other Pikemen of the
levy drop), the Toll goes up by 1. If the characters break the point in 4
rounds or fewer, the Toll does not go up.

#### The Church version (the characters ride with Gaunt)

> The Marshal raises her sword. The horns of Gaunt answer. You ride down
> the hill at the front of three hundred lances, and the wind is in your
> face, and the Church line grows bigger and bigger: farmers, children,
> old men, and in the middle of them, a knight in shining plate on foot,
> with a sword that burns like the sun.
>
> Somewhere in the front rank, a young woman with a corporal's knot sets
> her spear. Her hands shake. She holds on.

The characters lead the charge into the Church line. The Marshal wants a
quick break. A quick break is a massacre, unless the characters make it a
surrender. They must beat the knights that hold the line, so that the
levies can give up without being cut down.

- **Creatures:** 1 Templar of the Sun, 1 Mounted Lancer of the Church on a
  Warhorse, 4 Line Pikemen, and 2 Crossbow Sergeants.
- **XP:** 2,900 + (700 + 100) + 4 × 200 + 2 × 450 = 5,400 XP. A **Low**
  encounter for four characters, with a task.
- **The task:** win without slaughter. Each character can say, when an
  enemy Pikeman drops to 0 Hit Points, that the blow knocks it out and
  does not kill it. If the characters end the fight with the Templar
  beaten or surrendered, the levy lays down its spears.
- **Lenna:** she is one of the four Pikemen. If the Shield knows who she
  is, the Shield sees her in the line. Give the Shield's player the
  choice: what does the Shield do?

**The Toll:** if the characters kill more than half the Pikemen, or if
Lenna dies, the Toll goes up by 1. If the levy surrenders, the Toll does
not go up.

#### The stone version (the characters stop the war)

> You ride down into the valley as the lines move. You reach the stone at
> the same moment as the first riders of Gaunt on one side and the first
> knights of the Church on the other. You plant yourselves between them,
> with the stream at your boots and the stone at your backs.
>
> For one long moment, both lines slow. They see you. Then someone on the
> south slope shouts, "It is a trick of the priests!" and someone on the
> north slope shouts, "Gaunt has taken them!" and both lines come on.

The characters hold the stone between the armies. They do not have to beat
the soldiers. They have to stop them for 3 rounds, until the leaders see
what happens and call their lines back. The soldiers of both sides attack
the characters, and sometimes each other.

- **Creatures:** from Gaunt, 1 Shield-Wall Captain and 2 Line Pikemen; from
  the Church, 1 Templar of the Sun and 2 Line Pikemen.
- **XP:** 1,800 + 400 + 2,900 + 400 = 5,500 XP. A **Low** encounter for
  four characters, with a task.
- **The task:** stand at the stone for 3 rounds. At the end of each round,
  if at least one character stands within 10 feet of the stone and has not
  dropped to 0 Hit Points in that round, mark one round of holding. After
  3 rounds of holding, the horns of both sides blow the halt, and the
  soldiers pull back.
- **The two sides fight each other too:** on its turn, each soldier
  attacks the nearest enemy, character or soldier of the other side. The
  Captain and the Templar attack the characters first.
- **The Dawn Banner:** if a character plants the Dawn Banner at the stone,
  the soldiers of both sides see it. Each soldier must succeed on a DC 13
  Wisdom saving throw at the start of its turn, or it does nothing that
  turn but stare. (The banner of the Dawnwatch, at the stone of peace, in
  front of both armies: the old soldiers know what it means.) After the
  first round, the Captain and the Templar are immune to this.
- **Words at the stone:** a character within 10 feet of the stone can use
  an action to shout to the lines. Charisma (Persuasion or Intimidation),
  DC 15. On a success, mark one extra round of holding. A character who
  shouts the old words, "No blood at the stone!", has Advantage.

**The Toll:** if the characters hold for 3 rounds, the Toll does not go
up. If they fail to hold, the lines crash together at the stone, and the
Toll goes up by 2.

> **Scaling (all three versions):** The budget for this encounter is Low:
> 1,300 XP for each character. The task makes it harder than its XP.
>
> - **3 characters (3,900 XP):** remove one Mounted Lancer on its horse
>   and three Line Pikemen, or, in the stone version, both Pikemen of
>   Gaunt and one of the Church (each version is then about 3,900 to
>   4,900 XP).
> - **4 characters (5,200 XP):** as written.
> - **5 characters (6,500 XP):** add one Mounted Lancer on a Warhorse
>   (800) and one Crossbow Sergeant (450), so about 6,500 XP.
> - **6 characters (7,800 XP):** add two Mounted Lancers on Warhorses and
>   two Crossbow Sergeants (2,500), so about 7,800 XP. In the stone
>   version, put one lancer and one sergeant on each side.
>
> **Optional systems:** with Techniques on, add one Line Pikeman for each
> four characters, or give the Captain and the Templar 10% more Hit
> Points.

### The optional duel: the Marshal

Use this on the Church path, or on any path if the Shield, or another
character, challenges the Marshal. Marshal Ide Gauntry rides at the front
of her army. She is proud, and the old books of Ostrey that she loves are
full of champions who fought for their armies.

> Through the dust, a rider in plain steel comes straight at you, with
> two guards in tall helmets behind her. She reins in ten feet away and
> lifts her visor. It is the Marshal. Her face is grey with dust and very
> calm.
>
> "The books say that the Dawnwatch never lost a fight," she says. "I
> would like to know if the books are wrong."

The characters can fight her with her Royal Guards, or one character can
take a duel, one against one, with the guards and the armies watching.

{{statblock: Marshal Ide Gauntry}}

- **The full fight:** Marshal Ide Gauntry and 2 Royal Guards. The guards
  protect her: the Marshal is their charge, for their Sworn Protector and
  Take the Blow features. **XP:** 3,900 + 2 × 2,300 = 8,500 XP, a

- **The duel:** one character against the Marshal alone. She fights to

  goes up by 1.
- **Injuries:** the Marshal is a person who matters. If a Critical Hit lands
  on her, roll an injury. She carries it for the rest of the book.

> **Scaling:** The budget for the full fight is Moderate: 2,000 XP for
> each character.

> - **4 characters (8,000 XP):** as written (8,500).
> - **5 characters (10,000 XP):** the Marshal, 2 Royal Guards, and 1

### The second lull

> The charge breaks, or halts, or turns. The horns blow again, ragged now.
> Between the lines, the grass is torn into mud. The stream runs brown.
> The wounded call for water in two accents.
>
> And from the west, from the hollow, the hum begins again. Louder.

The characters have about one hour again. A Short Rest, care for the
wounded, and a plan. Everyone who has found a clue now knows where to go:
the hollow. If the characters do not see it, Brother Cade or Captain Ilse
Varro brings it to them: "Ansel ran to the hollow." "Roe is gone from the
command tent. His horse went west."

### Phase 3: The Heart (Encounter 4)

> The hollow is a bowl of grass and grey rocks, a quarter of a mile west
> of the stone, out of sight of both camps. The fog here never left. It
> pours over the rim like water over a dam.
>
> In the middle of the bowl, around a ring of black candles that burn with
> no light at all, stand the singers: rows of grey hoods, swaying. In front
> of them stand two figures who do not sing. One wears the grey coat of a
> Gaunt officer. One wears the brown robe of a monk of the Church. Both
> have taken off their masks of politeness, and their faces are calm and
> happy.
>
> Lieutenant Roe lifts a hand to you, like a friend. "You came," he says.
> "Good. Mother wanted you to see this."
>
> Brother Ansel only smiles.

The two agents are Hush Dusk Priests. Here they lead the last song of the
choir, the song that would put the whole valley in the grey for the rest
of the day, until the armies have killed each other in the dark. If the
characters break the Heart, the fog over Ashby Field lifts for good.

- **Creatures:** 2 Hush Dusk Priests (Lieutenant Roe and Brother Ansel),
  1 Hush Grey Choir, and 2 Hush Night Wardens.
- **XP:** 2 × 2,900 + 2,300 + 2 × 1,100 = 10,300 XP. For four characters of
  level 9, this is a **High** encounter (4 × 2,600 = 10,400). This fight
  can drop a character. That is the point.
- **The hollow:** the whole bowl is in the magical fog of Phase 1. It is
  Heavily Obscured beyond 10 feet, except in Bright Light. The ring of
  black candles is 20 feet across. While at least one priest stands in the
  ring, the fog cannot be ended by the spells that end it in Phase 1.
- **The candles:** the black candles stand in four groups around the ring,
  each in one 5-foot square. A character can use an action to knock over
  one group. When all four groups are down (four actions), the ring breaks, and the fog in the
  hollow ends at the end of that turn.
- **Grey rocks:** they give Half Cover.

**Tactics:** The choir comes straight at the characters, into their spaces,
and sings Hymn of Quiet at the spellcasters. The Wardens guard the ring
and use Close the Ranks for the priests. Both priests open with Snuff the
Sun on the brightest group of characters. Then Roe casts Hold Person on the
strongest fighter, and Ansel casts Silence on the spellcasters. They use
Word of Night on a character who carries a light. A priest who is Bloodied
fights on: they do not fear death. They believe that the sun dies anyway.

**If the Marshal had proof** (see Scene 6), Ilse Varro has Roe in chains.
Only Ansel is here. Use 1 Hush Dusk Priest and 3 Hush Night Wardens
(2,900 + 3,300 = 6,200 XP, plus the choir 2,300, so 8,500 XP: Moderate).

**The Toll:** if the fight lasts more than 6 rounds, the armies blunder
into each other again in the fog. The Toll goes up by 1. If the characters
break the ring of candles before the priests fall, the Toll does not go up,
whatever the length of the fight.

> **Scaling:** The budget for this encounter is High: 2,600 XP for each
> character.
>
> - **3 characters (7,800 XP):** 2 Hush Dusk Priests and 2 Hush Night
>   Wardens (5,800 + 2,200 = 8,000). No choir; the priests sing the song
>   themselves.
> - **4 characters (10,400 XP):** as written.
> - **5 characters (13,000 XP):** 2 Hush Dusk Priests, 1 Hush Grey Choir,
>   and 4 Hush Night Wardens (5,800 + 2,300 + 4,400 = 12,500).
> - **6 characters (15,600 XP):** 2 Hush Dusk Priests, 2 Hush Grey Choirs,
>   and 4 Hush Night Wardens (5,800 + 4,600 + 4,400 = 14,800).
>
> **Optional systems:** with Techniques on, add one Hush Night Warden for
> each four characters, or give the two priests 10% more Hit Points.

### The end of the battle

> The last black candle falls. The hum stops in the middle of a note, as
> if someone shut a door on it. And the fog lifts: not slowly, like a
> morning mist, but all at once, like a sheet pulled off a table.
>
> From the hollow you can see the whole valley. Two armies stand on two
> slopes, tired and bloody and blinking in the sun. Between them, the
> grass is churned and red. The stone stands where it always stood.
>
> For the first time today, nobody shouts.

## Scene 8: After the Battle

**Goal:** End the war, or end the day. Give the injuries their weight and
the first scars their story. Point at Kingsholm.

### How the day ends

Look at the Toll, and at the path that the characters took.

| Toll | What happened | The end of the day |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Almost nobody died. A few dozen wounded. | A true truce. The Marshal and the Abbess meet at the stone that evening, with the characters between them. |
| 1 to 2 | Some dead on both sides. Many wounded. | A hard truce. The leaders meet, but they do not shake hands. The war stops for now. |
| 3 to 4 | Hundreds dead. | A bitter end. The side that the characters helped holds the field. The other side goes home and does not forgive. |
| 5 or more | A slaughter. | The valley is full of graves. The leaders agree to stop only because nobody can fight any more. The land remembers this day, and it remembers that the Dawnwatch were here. |

Each path also changes the end:

- **With Gaunt:** if the Toll is 2 or lower, the Church levies surrendered
  and went home alive. The Marshal holds Ashby Field and the pilgrim road.
  She offers the characters a place at her side, for good.
- **With the Church:** if the Toll is 2 or lower, the charge broke on the
  hill, and Gaunt pulled back to the south. The Abbess calls it a miracle,
  in front of everyone. She calls the characters saints, in front of
  everyone. Brace for the songs.
- **Stop the war:** if the Toll is 2 or lower, both armies stood down. The
  Marshal and the Abbess meet at the stone. Neither is happy. Both are
  alive.

### The truce at the stone

If the leaders meet (a Toll of 4 or lower), run this scene.

> At sunset, the Marshal walks down the south slope with Tobin and Ilse
> Varro. The Abbess walks down the north slope with Brother Cade, leaning
> on his arm. They meet at the Ashby stone. The two armies watch from the
> slopes, thousands of faces in the low red light.
>
> Nobody knows what to say first. They both look at you.

The characters can speak first, or let the leaders speak. If the
characters bring the agents (alive or dead), the evidence from Wenlow, and
the black candles of the hollow, the truth comes out in front of both
leaders. The Marshal goes very pale when she sees Roe. The Abbess cries
when she sees Ansel, and then she is very angry.

| Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Charisma (Persuasion) to make a lasting peace | 15 (12 with the evidence of both agents) | The Marshal and the Abbess swear on the stone, in the old words, that there will be no war between Gaunt and Saint Ivo's while the Hush lives. The pilgrim road opens to Gaunt traders. |
| Charisma (Persuasion) to make Gaunt leave the field | 13 | The Marshal agrees to go home, "for now". |
| Charisma (Persuasion) to make the Church send the levies home | 12 | The Abbess agrees, and she adds, without being asked, that she was wrong to call for spears. |
| Intelligence (History) | 13 | The character knows the old words of the treaty of the stone, and teaches them to the leaders. They say them back. Wick says them too, very quietly, at the same time. |

> **GM tip:** A peace that both sides sign is the best end for the story,
> but not the only good one. If the players took a side, let that side
> win, and let the cost show. Book Two remembers what happened here.

**Tobin:** whatever the end, Tobin finds the characters after the truce.
He thanks them, or he does not. If his mother was injured in the duel or
the charge, he sits by her tent all night. He says one thing, to the
character who did the most to stop the war: "When I am Marshal, there will
be no more of this. I promise." (Promises are a theme in this chapter. The
Shield's player may have something to say.)

### The prisoner

If either agent is alive, the characters can question them. If both are
dead, a captured Hush Night Warden from the hollow takes their place. The
prisoner is calm. The Hush fears nothing that the characters can do.

> The prisoner sits in the dirt with chained hands and looks at you
> without hate. "You won a field," the prisoner says. "Good for you. It
> does not matter. While you played with soldiers, Mother went north. They
> are all gathering at Kingsholm. In the old capital, where he slept. When
> the last light goes out there, you will understand. Everyone will
> understand."

| Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Charisma (Intimidation) | 17 | The prisoner says more than they meant to: "Mother Sallow went down to his bed and drank the dark that he left in the crystal. Now she will put out the coal there, and the long night comes early." |
| Charisma (Persuasion) | 15 | The prisoner speaks almost kindly: "The sun is dying. You know it. You have seen the days. He tried to save it once. He will try again. We help him. What do you help?" |
| Wisdom (Insight) | 14 | The prisoner believes every word. And the prisoner is not sure that the characters are wrong, either. |

The prisoner says "the coal": if Brother Ansel stole the Ember (see "The
Ember in the camp", Scene 5), the prisoner means the Ember, and it is in
Kingsholm now. If the party keeps the Ember, the prisoner means the pieces
of the Dawnfire in general.

> **GM tip:** This scene points at chapter 10. Make sure that the players
> leave with three facts: the Hush gathers at Kingsholm; Mother Sallow
> leads it; and "he" slept there. Let the black stone hum loud when the
> prisoner says "Kingsholm".

### The fire: Injuries and scars

That night, wherever the characters camp (with the army they helped, or
alone by the stone), give the players a quiet scene at the fire. This is
the time to look at what the battle left on each character.

> The camp is very quiet. Somewhere a man sings to his horse. Somewhere
> else a woman laughs, because she is alive and could not help it. You sit
> at your own fire and count what you lost.

Go around the table. For each character who has an injury, ask the player
two questions:

1. "How does it feel tonight?"
2. "Who tends it?"

Then teach the rest of the system.

> **Teach: healing and scars.** "Your injuries heal with time and care.
> A Minor injury heals when you finish a Long Rest. Some need someone to
> tend them first: a Healer's Kit, or a Wisdom (Medicine) check. Most
> Serious injuries need three Long Rests, each after someone tends it. A
> Broken Arm needs seven. A Lasting
> injury needs a month of rest. Magic can heal an injury at once: a Lesser
> Restoration spell ends a Serious one, and stronger spells end a Lasting
> one.
>
> "But here is the thing. When an injury heals slowly, with rest and care,
> and not with magic, you get its scar. A scar is small and permanent. A
> Cut Above the Eye leaves a Dueling Scar. Cracked Ribs leave Knit Bone. A
> Broken Arm leaves a Set Bone. Each scar gives you a small benefit, and a
> story to tell at the fire. You can have as many scars as your
> Proficiency Bonus. So you choose: heal fast with magic and keep nothing,
> or heal slow and keep the scar."

Let the players decide for each injury. The characters have a few weeks of
travel and rest before chapter 10, if they want them (see the end of this
chapter). Most Minor and Serious injuries can heal with care in that time.

> **GM tip:** The choice between magic and time is the heart of this
> system. Do not push. Some players love a scar. Some want their character
> whole for the finale. Both are good choices. The cleric with Lesser
> Restoration is now very popular; let that be a fun problem.

### The field healer

Among the wounded of the Church (or among the prisoners, on the Gaunt
path) works an old field healer, Sister Hild. She has sewn up soldiers of
both sides all day, and she does not care which slope they came from.

> The old woman does not look up from the leg that she sews. "Sit," she
> says. "You are next. No, do not tell me you are fine. Everyone says that.
> Sit."

Sister Hild tends one injury of each character for free, and she does it
well (count it as care, and the Medicine check succeeds). When the
characters leave, she gives them her needle.

> "Take it. It has sewn up ten thousand fools, and you will need it more
> than I will. I am going home to my garden." She presses the horn case
> into your hand. "Slow is better. Let them heal slow. Scars remind you."

### Renown

### Toward Kingsholm

> In the morning, the armies begin to pack. Wagons roll south. Pilgrims
> walk north, singing again, more quietly. On the stone, someone has
> scratched a new line under the old worn words. It is the date, and five
> small marks, one for each of you.
>
> Wick looks north, past the hills, toward the river and the old capital.
> His flame is very small.
>
> "Kingsholm," he says. "The steward smelled of river water and cold
> stone, the night he made me. I always wondered where he had been." A
> pause. "I think I know now."

Kingsholm is three days north-west of the keep, over the Wolds and up the
River Dunn, off the road. The characters can go straight there, or rest first at Dawnwatch
Keep. A few weeks at the keep let injuries heal, a monthly turn of the
stronghold run, and the allies of this chapter come to help (see chapter
10). The Hush does not wait forever, but it waits long enough.
