# Three Banners

Two armies march to the same field. One flies the grey and steel of Gaunt.
One flies the five gold stars of the Church. Both say that the Dawnwatch
march with them. Neither asked.

In this chapter the characters stand between two powers that both want to
own them, and a third power that wants them both to bleed. They can choose
a side. They can try to stop the war. Whatever they choose, they fight
their first true battle, and they carry its marks for a long time.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Levels** | 9 to 10 |
| **Time at the table** | 3 or 4 sessions |
| **Places** | The road south from the keep, the village of Wenlow, Ashby Field on the border of Gaunt and Saint Ivo's, the two camps, the Ashby stone |
| **System that starts** | Injuries and scars |

## The Chapter at a Glance

**Goal:** the party chooses a side, or stops a war.

1. **Two banners at the gate.** Riders from Gaunt and from the Church come
   to Dawnwatch Keep on the same day. Each brings a proclamation that says
   the Dawnwatch stand with it.
2. **The burning chapel.** On the road south, the characters catch soldiers
   in Gaunt colors as they burn a chapel. Under the tabards is grey mail.
   (Encounter 1.)
3. **Ashby Field.** The two armies face each other across a shallow valley.
   In the middle stands the Ashby stone, where the kings of Ostrey once
   swore their treaties.
4. **The camp of Gaunt.** Marshal Ide Gauntry, her son Tobin, Captain Ilse
   Varro, and the helpful Lieutenant Roe.
5. **The camp of the Church.** Abbess Merrow, Brother Cade, the quiet
   Brother Ansel, and a young corporal with an old token.
6. **The dead envoy.** In the night, an envoy of Gaunt dies with a Church
   knife in his back. Tomorrow the armies fight. The characters choose.
7. **The battle of Ashby Field.** Three phases: the Grey, the Charge, and
   the Heart. (Encounters 2, 3, and 4, and an optional duel.)
8. **After the battle.** A truce at the stone, or a victory, or a grave.
   The first scars at the fire. A prisoner names Kingsholm.

### What Is Really Going on

Read this part before you run the chapter. The players learn it slowly.

- **The Hush wants a war.** A land at war does not look north. While Gaunt
  and the Church bleed each other, nobody sees the Hush gather in the ruins
  of Kingsholm. And Mother Sallow has a second wish: if the Dawnwatch lead
  two armies into a slaughter, nobody calls them saints again.
- **Two agents start the fire.** Lieutenant Roe is the aide of Marshal
  Gauntry. Brother Ansel is the secretary of Abbess Merrow. Both are
  members of the Hush. Both came to their posts two years ago, with good
  letters and no past.
- **Two false attacks.** A Hush band in Gaunt tabards burned the chapel at
  Wenlow, on the land of the Church. (The characters catch a second band at
  work in Scene 2.) And on the first night at Ashby Field, Roe kills the
  envoy of Gaunt and leaves a Church knife in the body.
- **On the day of the battle,** the Hush brings the grey: a Grey Choir that
  sings a fog over the valley, so that each army thinks the other attacks
  first.

The armies are not evil. Marshal Gauntry wants a strong land, and she
believes that she is the only one who can make it. Abbess Merrow wants the
Sleepers safe from soldiers, and she believes that her prayers are enough
to keep them. Both are proud. Both are a little afraid. The Hush only has
to push.

> **GM tip:** The players do not have to find every clue. Two clues are
> enough to make a good case. If the players are stuck, let Wick ask the
> question that the players forgot: "Why does the Lieutenant always stand
> where the light is worst?"

## Before You Start: Injuries

This chapter starts the **Injuries and Scars** system of the Game Master's
Compendium. Read that chapter before the session. It is short.

Injuries are optional, and they change the feel of the game. So talk to
the players before you start, not in the middle of a fight. Say something
like this:

> **Teach: the table agreement.** "From this chapter on, I want to use
> injuries. When an enemy scores a Critical Hit on you, or when you drop to
> 0 Hit Points, you can get a real wound: cracked ribs, a broken arm, a cut
> above the eye. Hit Points come back after a night of sleep. An injury
> stays until it heals. An injury never kills you and never takes you out
> of the game. And when it heals slowly, with rest, it leaves a scar that
> makes you a little tougher. Do we want this?"

Agree on two questions, as the Compendium says:

- **Who can be injured?** The characters and the people who matter. In this
  chapter, that means the Marshal, Tobin, Corporal Lenna, and the two
  agents. Ordinary soldiers and Hush members just fall.
- **Can a Lost Eye be for good?** The player always chooses. Some tables
  leave permanent losses out. Ask.

If the table says no to injuries, run the chapter as it is and skip the
Teach boxes about injuries. The story still works. The battle is still a
battle.

> **GM tip:** This is the right chapter to start injuries. The characters
> are level 9. They are strong, and they have Hit Points to spare. A
> battle with hundreds of soldiers is the place where a hero comes home
> with a limp and a story. And the next chapter is the end of Book One, so
> the players carry their scars into the finale.

## Scene 1: Two Banners at the Gate

**Goal:** Start the war. Show that both sides want the Dawnwatch, and that
neither side asked them.

Run this scene at Dawnwatch Keep, during or right after a monthly turn of
the stronghold. If the characters are somewhere else, the riders find them
there.

> It is a grey morning on the walls of the keep. Hesper Wren comes up the
> stair two steps at a time, out of breath.
>
> "Riders on the road. Two groups. One from the south, one from the west.
> They are racing each other."
>
> From the wall you see them: four riders in grey and steel, under a black
> banner with a white gauntlet. Behind them, three riders in white, under a
> blue banner with five gold stars. The grey riders reach the gate first.
> The white riders shout at them. Somebody's horse bites somebody else's
> horse.
>
> Wick peers over the edge of the wall. "Oh, good," he says. "Visitors who
> already hate each other. That saves time."

Both groups have the same task: give the Dawnwatch a proclamation, and
bring the Dawnwatch back with them. Give the players the handout now.

{{handout: 09-two-proclamations}}

### The Riders

- **The riders of Gaunt** are led by a young officer, Ensign Aldo Varne. He
  is polite and stiff. He has orders: "The Marshal requests that the
  Dawnwatch ride to Ashby Field, as the honor guard of Gaunt." He has a
  horse for each character, already saddled.
- **The riders of the Church** are led by a lay sister, Sister Orla. She is
  warm and loud. She has a letter from Abbess Merrow, sealed with the five
  stars: "Come to us, and stand where all the faithful can see you. Your
  presence alone can stop this."

Both riders say that the other side started it. Both believe it.

### What the Riders Know

| Question | The rider of Gaunt says | The rider of the Church says |
|---|---|---|
| Why is there a war? | "The Church burned its own chapel at Wenlow and blamed us. Then it closed the pilgrim road to Gaunt traders." | "Gaunt soldiers burned the chapel at Wenlow. We saw the tabards. Now Gaunt sends an army north." |
| Who burned the chapel? | "Monks in disguise. Or a mob of their pilgrims." | "Soldiers in grey and steel. Eleven of them, on foot." |
| Where are the armies? | "At Ashby Field, on the border. Four days to the south." | "At Ashby Field. Ten thousand faithful, the abbess says. Perhaps two thousand." |
| When will they fight? | "When the Marshal decides. Soon." | "When Gaunt attacks. We will not attack first." |
| What do they want from us? | "To ride with the Marshal. Your banner beside hers." | "To stand with the faithful. Your faces, in the light." |

Let the characters ask questions. Use this table for checks.

| Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Wisdom (Insight) on Ensign Varne | 12 | He is afraid. He has never been in a battle. He believes the story about the monks, because his lieutenant told it to him. "Lieutenant Roe saw the reports himself." |
| Wisdom (Insight) on Sister Orla | 12 | She is honest, and she is angry. She saw the burned chapel herself. Something about it bothers her, but she does not want to say it in front of Gaunt. |
| Charisma (Persuasion) on Sister Orla, in private | 14 | "The soldiers at Wenlow did not shout. Gaunt soldiers always shout. And they put out every lamp in the village before they lit the fire. Why would you do that?" |
| Intelligence (History) | 15 | Ashby Field is old. The kings of Ostrey met their rivals there, at a standing stone, to swear treaties. "No blood at the stone" was the rule. |

> **GM tip:** Sister Orla's detail is the first clue. People who put out
> the lights before they act: the players have seen that before. If no
> player makes the connection, Wick does, very quietly: "They put out the
> lamps first. Who does that?"

### The Choice of the Road

Both riders want the characters to ride with them, right now. The
characters can:

- **Ride with Gaunt.** Ensign Varne takes the good road. They arrive at the
  Gaunt camp. Give each character +1 renown with Gaunt for the courtesy.
- **Ride with the Church.** Sister Orla takes the pilgrim road. They arrive
  at the Church camp. Give each character +1 renown with the Church.
- **Ride alone, or with both.** The characters go by their own road, and
  the riders follow, still arguing. Nobody gets renown, and nobody is
  offended.

Whichever road they take, the road passes Wenlow. Go to Scene 2.

> **Teach: renown, again.** "Each side keeps track of how much it trusts
> you. That is your renown, from chapter 5. Riding with one side is a small
> service. The big deeds come at Ashby Field." Remind the players of their
> current ranks with Gaunt and with the Church. A character who reaches the
> third rank in one faction is an enemy of the other, so the choice in this
> chapter can cost something.

## Scene 2: The Burning Chapel

**Goal:** Catch the Hush at work. Give the players the first hard proof
that a third hand stirs the war.

The village of Wenlow lies two days south of the keep, on the land of the
Church. Its chapel already burned, ten days ago. But the Hush is thorough.
Tonight a second band comes to burn the inn, where the people of Wenlow now
pray, and to leave a few Gaunt badges in the ashes.

> The road comes down into a little valley in the last light of the day.
> Wenlow is twenty houses, a mill, and the black shell of a chapel. Its
> roof is gone. Its walls are scorched.
>
> Then the lights of the village go out. Not all at once: one by one, from
> the north end of the street to the south, like someone walking along and
> pinching each candle.
>
> In the dark, someone screams. A torch flares at the door of the inn.
> In its light you see soldiers in grey and steel tabards, with the white
> gauntlet of Gaunt on their chests. They are nailing the door shut. People
> are inside.

### Encounter 1: The False Soldiers

The band works fast. Two soldiers nail the door of the inn and pile straw
against it, while the rest watch the street. A priest in a soldier's cloak
stands back in the dark and watches.

- **Creatures:** 3 Hush Night Wardens, 1 Hush Dusk Priest, and 4 Cultist
  Fanatics (SRD), all in Gaunt tabards.
- **XP:** 3 × 1,100 + 2,900 + 4 × 450 = 8,000 XP. For four characters of
  level 9, this is a **Moderate** encounter (4 × 2,000 = 8,000).
- **The inn:** eighteen villagers are inside. The straw catches fire on
  round 2. On round 4, the inn burns, and each villager still inside is in
  danger. A character next to the door can use an action to break it open
  (Strength (Athletics), DC 15) or pull the boards off (no check, but it
  takes the full action and the Bonus Action).
- **The dark:** the Wardens have used Snuff on every lamp in the street.
  The street is dark, except for the torch at the inn door and the light
  that the characters bring. The Hush have Grey Sight. Light matters here:
  Wick, the First Lamp, and the Storm Lantern all help.

**Tactics:** The Fanatics fight in the street. Two Wardens hold the door of
the inn and use Close the Ranks for each other. The third Warden shoots
from the mill. The Dusk Priest casts Darkness on the brightest light, then
uses Word of Night on the character who carries it. When two Wardens fall,
the priest tries to flee into the dark of the mill race. A Fanatic who is
alone gives up.

**What the band carries:** Under each tabard is grey mail with no badge.
Each member carries a stub of black candle, never lit, and a small purse
with Gaunt silver (2d6 × 10 GP in all). The priest has a letter in a code
of short lines and dots. The letter ends with a drawing of a candle with
no flame.

> **Scaling:** The budget for this encounter is Moderate: 2,000 XP for
> each character.
>
> - **3 characters (6,000 XP):** 2 Hush Night Wardens, 1 Hush Dusk Priest,
>   and 2 Cultist Fanatics (2,200 + 2,900 + 900 = 6,000).
> - **4 characters (8,000 XP):** as written.
> - **5 characters (10,000 XP):** 4 Hush Night Wardens, 1 Hush Dusk Priest,
>   and 6 Cultist Fanatics (4,400 + 2,900 + 2,700 = 10,000).
> - **6 characters (12,000 XP):** 4 Hush Night Wardens, 2 Hush Dusk
>   Priests, and 4 Cultist Fanatics (4,400 + 5,800 + 1,800 = 12,000).
>
> **Optional systems:** Techniques started in chapter 8. At level 5 or
> more, add one monster of the lowest CR for each four characters (here,
> one Cultist Fanatic), or give each Warden and the priest 10% more Hit
> Points. If you also use other optional systems, count each one
> separately, as the Compendium says.

### After the Fight

The villagers come out coughing. An old miller, Gaffer Tull, takes off his
hat and stares at the characters for a long time. "You are them," he says.
"The Sleepers. I knew you would be taller."

The people of Wenlow tell the characters what they saw ten days ago: the
same soldiers, the same silence, the same lamps going out. They believed
the tabards. Now they are not so sure.

| Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Intelligence (Investigation) on the bodies | 12 | The tabards are new. The stitching is cheap. Real Gaunt tabards have the badge of the company on the shoulder. These have none. |
| Wisdom (Perception) in the ashes of the old chapel | 14 | A cheap Gaunt badge, pressed into the ash on purpose. Nobody drops a badge face up in the exact middle of a floor. |
| Intelligence (Arcana or Religion) on the coded letter | 16 | The code is simple, once you see it: each line is a word, each dot a letter. It says: "The field in four days. R. and A. are in place. Let them meet in the grey." |
| Charisma (Intimidation or Persuasion) on a captured Fanatic | 15 | The prisoner says only: "The sun is dying. You will see. Mother Sallow says so." Then the prisoner says nothing more. |

> **GM tip:** "R. and A." are Roe and Ansel. The players do not know those
> names yet. Let them carry the letter into the camps. When they meet a
> Lieutenant Roe and a Brother Ansel, let them make the connection
> themselves. It is a great moment at the table.

The black stone hums the whole time that the Hush prisoners are near.
Remind the bearer of this. It is useful later.

## Scene 3: Ashby Field

**Goal:** Show the scale of the war. Show the place where the battle will
happen.

Two days after Wenlow, the road comes over a low ridge.

> Below you, the land opens into a long, shallow valley of grass and
> stone walls. A stream runs down the middle, so small that you could jump
> it. On the south slope stands a city of tents in grey and black, with
> thin columns of smoke and the flash of steel. On the north slope stands
> a second city, larger and messier, of white tents and wagons, with a
> hundred blue banners and a sound of singing.
>
> In the bottom of the valley, by the stream, stands one grey stone, taller
> than a rider. Nobody camps near it. A ring of trampled grass shows where
> both armies have sent riders to look at it, and turned back.
>
> Wick goes very quiet. Then he says, "I know that stone. The steward told
> me about it, the night he made me. The kings swore on it. No blood at the
> stone. That was the rule." He pauses. "I do not think anyone here
> remembers the rule."

### The Field

Describe the field to the players. They fight here in Scene 7, and they
plan better if they know it.

- **The south slope:** the camp of Gaunt. About 2,000 soldiers: pike
  squares, crossbows, and 300 riders. Neat rows. Good order.
- **The north slope:** the camp of the Church. About 3,000 people: some
  knights of the Church and templars, many levies from the farms and towns
  with spears and old helmets, and a crowd of pilgrims who came to watch
  and pray. Less order. More heart.
- **The Ashby stone:** a standing stone of Ostrey, 12 feet tall, by the
  stream. The old words cut in it are worn almost flat. A character who
  reads the old speech of Ostrey can read them: "HERE THE SWORD SLEEPS."
- **The stream:** 5 feet wide, knee-deep. Difficult Terrain.
- **The stone walls:** old field walls, 3 feet high. They give Half Cover.
- **The hollow:** a dip west of the stone, out of sight of both camps. Fog
  gathers there each morning. (This is where the Hush hides its choir.)

> **Thread (Wick):** Wick never saw this field. But on the night that he
> made Wick, the steward talked about it, the way people talk to a thing
> that they think cannot listen yet. If a player asks, Wick tells it: "He
> stood by that stone once, with a king, to swear a peace. He said the
> words, and the king said them back, and nobody died that day. He was
> very proud. He told me three times." Wick is quiet for a moment. "He
> told me a lot of things. He did not tell me the important ones."

From here the characters go to one camp or both. Most groups visit both.
Run Scenes 4 and 5 in the order that the players choose.

## Scene 4: The Camp of Gaunt

**Goal:** Meet the Marshal, her son, her spymaster, and her helpful aide.
Find clues.

> The camp of Gaunt is quiet. Soldiers clean armor in rows. Sergeants walk
> between them and say little. Every tent has the same shape, the same
> distance from the next one, the same black flag.
>
> In the center is a large tent with its walls rolled up. A woman in plain
> steel stands over a table with a map. She is about fifty, with grey hair
> cut short and a face like a closed door. She looks up, and for a moment
> the door opens a little.
>
> "The Dawnwatch," says Marshal Ide Gauntry. "I read about you when I was
> a girl. Sit. Tell me how many of the books were wrong."

### Marshal Ide Gauntry

The Marshal is the most dangerous person in the chapter, because she is
reasonable. Play her calm, curious, and sure. She does not shout. She asks
good questions and listens to the answers.

- **What she wants:** the Dawnwatch at her side tomorrow, in front of both
  armies. If the Dawnwatch ride with Gaunt, the Church levies will break
  and run home, and nobody needs to die. "That is not a threat. That is
  the kindest plan I have."
- **What she believes:** the Church burned its own chapel, and killed her
  envoy (after Scene 6). The priests want the Dawnwatch as statues, and the
  land needs them as soldiers.
- **What she will not do:** attack first without a reason. She has given
  her army that order. She thinks that the Church will give her a reason
  soon.

If the characters met her in chapter 5, she remembers it, and she says
what she thought of them then, honestly.

If Gaunt took the First Lamp in chapter 6, it stands on a pole outside
her tent, behind a guard of four soldiers. She carries it into the battle
as a banner. The characters can win it back: as the price of a truce, as
the prize of the duel, or from Ilse Varro, who never liked the plan.

### Tobin Gauntry

Tobin is her son, twenty years old, in armor that is too new for him. He
stands at the edge of the tent and does not speak while his mother speaks.
Later, he finds the characters alone.

> The young man catches up with you between the tents, out of breath. He
> looks back over his shoulder twice.
>
> "You must stop this," Tobin says. "She thinks it will be quick. It is
> never quick. Half of those people on the other slope are farmers. My
> mother has never met a farmer. She has met maps of them."

Tobin is the key to the third path. He does not know about the Hush. He
knows that his mother trusts Lieutenant Roe more each month, and he does
not like it. "Roe is always there. Roe always agrees with her. Roe told
her about the chapel before the riders even came."

### Captain Ilse Varro

The characters met Ilse in Corrantey in chapter 6. How that went decides
how she greets them now:

- **If they parted as enemies:** she is cold and polite, and she watches
  them. She still talks, because she is a professional.
- **If they made a deal, or parted with respect:** she is almost friendly.
  "You again. I hoped it would be you."

Ilse is suspicious of Roe for her own reasons. She did not write the
report about the chapel. Roe did. And the envoy who dies in Scene 6 was
one of her people. If the characters bring her proof, she becomes their
best ally in the Gaunt camp. She can get them to the Marshal at any hour,
and she can arrest Roe if the proof is good enough.

### Lieutenant Roe

> At the Marshal's shoulder stands a slim man in a grey officer's coat, so
> neat that he looks drawn with a ruler. He bows to you, low and correct.
> "Lieutenant Roe, at your service. If you need anything at all, you ask
> me first."

Roe is helpful, charming, and always present. He offers the characters a
tent, food, a guide. He agrees with whatever the Marshal says, a moment
after she says it. He is a member of the Hush, and he is very good at his
work.

**Clues that Roe gives away** (the characters notice these only if they
look):

- He always stands where the light is worst: at the back of the tent, out
  of the circle of the lamp.
- He flinches from the light of the First Lamp, and he does not look at
  Wick.
- He does not drink the wine. (The Hush keep a fast before their great
  works.)
- The black stone hums when he is within 30 feet.

| Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Wisdom (Insight) on the Marshal | 13 | She is less sure than she looks. She does not want to kill farmers. She wants someone to give her a way out that does not look like weakness. |
| Wisdom (Insight) on Roe | 18 | He is not afraid of the battle. He is looking forward to it. A character who beats the DC by 5 or more also notices that he has never once said the word "light". |
| Wisdom (Perception) in Roe's tent | 15 | No lamp at all. A stub of black candle in a boot, never lit. |
| Intelligence (Investigation) of the reports on the Marshal's table | 14 | The report about Wenlow is in Roe's hand, and it is dated one day before the chapel burned. Someone wrote it before the fire. |
| Charisma (Persuasion) with the Marshal | 16 | She agrees to wait one more day before she attacks, if the characters bring her proof of a third hand. (Only with real evidence: the letter from Wenlow, the dated report, or a witness.) |
| Charisma (Deception) with Roe | 15 | He believes that the characters will ride with Gaunt. He relaxes, and he says one thing too many: "Then the field will be grey for both sides tomorrow." |

> **GM tip:** Do not let the players accuse Roe in front of the Marshal
> with no proof. If they do, the Marshal sides with her aide, and the
> characters lose 2 renown with Gaunt. Roe smiles. Then he moves his plan a
> day earlier. (The battle starts at dawn, and the dead envoy of Scene 6
> happens tonight.)

## Scene 5: The Camp of the Church

**Goal:** Meet the Abbess, an old friend, a quiet secretary, and a young
soldier who carries the Shield's past. Find clues.

> The camp of the Church sings. Everywhere you look, people sing: hymns
> around the cook fires, marching songs at the spear drills, and one old
> woman who sings the same verse to a goat, over and over. Pilgrims crowd
> the paths. Children run between the tents with wooden swords and argue
> about which of them is the Shield.
>
> When the first person sees your faces, the singing stops. Then it
> starts again, louder, and very much worse, because everyone sings a
> different song.

The characters cannot walk anywhere in this camp without a crowd. People
want to touch their cloaks, show them a sick child, or ask them which side
of the bed the Light sleeps on. This is funny, and it is also a little
frightening.

### Abbess Merrow

> The Abbess receives you in a great white tent full of candles. She is
> old and small and very bright, with the five gold stars sewn on her
> sleeves. She takes the hands of the nearest character in both of hers.
>
> "You came," she says. "I knew you would. They cannot fight in front of
> you. Nobody can. You only have to stand on the hill and let them see
> you."

Abbess Merrow wants the characters as living saints. That has not changed
since chapter 3. Now she has a plan for it: the characters stand on the
north slope in the morning, under the banner of the Five, and Gaunt
loses its nerve.

- **What she wants:** the Dawnwatch on the hill, in white, in the light.
- **What she believes:** Gaunt burned Wenlow. Gaunt sent an envoy with
  threats. The Sleepers belong to the faithful, because the faithful kept
  their memory for a hundred and sixty years.
- **What she will not do:** attack first. "We are the Church. We do not
  attack." She does not see that three thousand people with spears are an
  attack, to the other side.

### Brother Cade

Brother Cade thought that the characters were impostors, in chapter 3.
Now he is their friend, and he is worried.

> Brother Cade finds you outside the tent and walks you away from the
> crowd, fast. "Do not let her put you on that hill," he says. "If you
> stand there in white, Gaunt sees an army with five heroes in front. They
> will charge the hill to take you. Merrow thinks you stop a war. You
> start it."

Cade is the key to the third path in this camp, as Tobin is in the other.
He also does not trust Brother Ansel. "He came to us two years ago with a
letter from a house of prayer that I cannot find on any map. He writes all
her letters now. She signs them without reading."

### Brother Ansel

> Behind the Abbess, at a writing desk in the shadow of the tent wall,
> sits a pale young monk with ink on his fingers. He does not get up. He
> nods to you once, a very small nod, and goes back to his writing.

Ansel is quiet, polite, and almost invisible. He wrote the letter of the
Church in the handout, and the Abbess signed it. He is the one who
suggested the call for spears. He is a member of the Hush.

**Clues that Ansel gives away:**

- His desk is the only place in the tent with no candle. He writes in the
  light of the candles of others.
- He trims the wicks of the candles near him very short, so that they
  give almost no light.
- The black stone hums when he is within 30 feet.
- His sandals are wet with the dew of the hollow west of the stone. He
  walks there each morning, alone, "to pray".

| Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|

### Corporal Lenna

Somewhere in the camp of the Church, the characters meet a young soldier
of the levy of Saint Ivo's. Run this when it feels right: at a drill, at a
cook fire, or when the crowd presses in.

> A young woman in a leather jerkin pushes through the crowd with her
> spear held sideways, like a gate, to clear a path for you. "Back, back.
> Let them breathe." She has a corporal's knot on her shoulder and a stubborn
> chin. When the crowd thins, she turns to you, suddenly shy.
>
> Around her neck, on a leather cord, hangs an old thing. Too old for her.

> **Thread (the Shield):** In the first chapter, the player of the Shield
> answered the question: "The night before the long sleep, you made a
> promise to someone. Who was it, and what did you promise?" Read that
> answer again before this scene.
>
> Corporal Lenna is a descendant of that person. Her family name is the
> family name of the person of the promise. The thing on the cord is the
> token of the promise, or a thing that came from it: a ring, a button
> from the Shield's coat, a carved wooden horse, a coin bent in half.
> Choose the thing that fits the answer.
>
> Lenna does not know who the Shield is, at first. She knows a family
> story: "My great-great-something grandmother waited for a soldier who
> went to sleep under a hill. He promised her that he would come back. He
> never did. We keep this, so that somebody remembers him."
>
> Some ideas for the promise:
>
> - **"I promised to come back."** The family waited, then stopped
>   waiting, then made it a story. Lenna wears the token for luck.
> - **"I promised to protect them."** The family became soldiers, all of
>   them, for ten generations, because of the promise. Lenna is the latest.
>   She is afraid, and she is too proud to say it.
> - **"I promised to keep a secret."** The family kept something hidden,
>   and does not know why. Lenna carries a sealed note that nobody in the
>   family ever opened.
> - **"I promised to say goodbye properly."** The family has an old
>   letter, never sent, in the Shield's hand.
>
> Now the promise has a face, and that face is in the front rank of the
> Church levies tomorrow. If the war starts, Lenna stands where the cavalry
> of Gaunt charges. Give the Shield a reason to care which way this goes.
>
> If the Shield is on the side of Gaunt, the thread still works: Lenna is
> on the other side of the field. If no player plays the Shield, use the
> nearest question of a character the players made.

Lenna stays with the levy. She does not leave her squad, even for the
Dawnwatch. "I made a promise too," she says, if anyone asks her to.
