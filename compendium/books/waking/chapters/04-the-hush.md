# The Hush

| | |
|---|---|
| **Levels** | 4 to 5 |
| **Time at the table** | About 2 to 3 sessions (8 to 12 hours) |
| **Places** | Saint Ivo's, the hills to the west, the shrine at Kell |
| **System that starts** | Marks |
| **Thread** | None. The black stone is the thread of this chapter. |
| **People** | Mother Sallow, the leader of the Hush; Osk, her enforcer; Brother Cade; Garret Pell, if the Hush took him |

On the night after the Waking Service, the grey folk come out of the dark.
They steal the Ember of Saint Ivo from the high altar, and they try to take
the black stone from the party. They have a name: the Hush, the cult of
the Unlit King. They believe that the sun is dying, and that only their
king can save it.

The party follows them west, off the road, to an old shrine of Ostrey on
a hill: the shrine at Kell. There, Mother Sallow tries to put out the
Ember, and she needs the black stone to do it. At the end of the night,
the party learns the worst thing about the stone: the Unlit King can feel
where it is.

This chapter ends Act I.

This chapter has two jobs:

1. **Bring the Hush into the open.** Until now, the grey folk were a
   rumor. Now they have a name, a leader, and a goal.
2. **Teach Marks.** The black stone has had no rules since the tutorial.
   Now it marks the character who carries it, and the Mark grows each time
   the character uses the stone.

## Running This Chapter

### What happens

1. **The night of the theft (Scene 1).** The Hush attack the Saints' House
   to take the black stone, while other members steal the Ember from the
   cathedral. This is the first fight.
2. **The morning after (Scene 2).** The town is in shock. Brother Cade
   gives the grey folk a name. The party finds the trail west.
3. **The weight of the stone (Scene 3).** The black stone wakes. It marks
   its bearer. Teach Marks here.
4. **The hills (Scene 4).** Two days off the road, with the Journeys rules.
   The Hush can feel the stone, so they set an ambush. Osk comes for it.
   This is the second fight.
5. **The shrine at Kell (Scene 5).** A ring of standing stones on a hill,
   with a small chapel inside it. The Hush camp there. The party scouts
   it and finds a way in.
6. **The rite (Scene 6).** Mother Sallow tries to put out the Ember. A
   shadow steps out of the black stone. This is the last fight of Act I.
7. **The voice in the stone (Scene 7).** The Unlit King speaks. Mother
   Sallow escapes. The party carries the Ember home, and Act I ends.

### The Hush

The Hush is the cult of the Unlit King. Use these facts when the party
meets its members and asks questions.

- **It is about thirty years old.** It started when the days started to
  become shorter. Few people noticed the days. The Hush noticed.
- **It believes that the sun is dying,** and that only the Unlit King can
  save it. Its members are partly right, and that makes them dangerous.
  They are not monsters. They are frightened people with a terrible
  answer.
- **It speaks little.** Its members talk with signs of the hand: Hush
  Sign. They take a vow of quiet when they join.
- **It wears grey** and puts out lights. A grey circle is tattooed inside
  the wrist of each member.
- **It looks for the pieces of the Dawnfire,** to put them out and wake its
  king. It put out the piece in the chapel of Dawnwatch Keep, on the night
  the Dawnwatch woke. The Ember of Saint Ivo is the next.
- **It wants the black stone.** Mother Sallow knows that the stone is a
  piece of the king's own crystal. She needs it for her rite.

The Hush has three tiers. Their statblocks are in this chapter and in the
Creatures appendix.

| Tier | Creature | CR | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hush Snuffer | 1/2 | A new member with a brass snuffer on a pole. |
| 2 | Hush Shroud | 2 | A priest of the Hush in a grey veil. It prays in silence. |
| 3 | Hush Lightless | 4 | A warrior who drank the black water at the secret rite. Its eyes have no white. |

### The pace

The Hush have a day's start. The party can catch them at Kell on the
second night if it travels fast, or on the third night at a normal pace.
It does not matter much: Mother Sallow waits for the party. She needs the
black stone, and she knows that the party will bring it to her. The real
clock is in Scene 6.

> **GM tip:** Tell the players, through Cade or Wick, that the Hush took
> the Ember to put it out. That is the stake: the first piece of the
> Dawnfire that they ever saw, the light that knew them. Do not tell them
> that Sallow is waiting for them. Let them work it out from the trail:
> it is very easy to follow. Too easy.

## Scene 1: The Night of the Theft (45 minutes)

**Goal:** The Hush come out of the dark. The first fight of the chapter.

This scene happens on the night after the Waking Service, or the night
after that, if the party left the town for a day. The party sleeps in the
Saints' House, next to the Abbey. It is a fine stone house of two floors,
with a garden behind it and a servant who sleeps in the kitchen.

> It is the middle of the night. The town is asleep after three days of
> feast.
>
> The black stone wakes you. It hums so hard that it rattles on the table
> beside the bed, like a cup in an earthquake.
>
> Downstairs, the lamp in the hall goes out. Then the one on the stair.
> Then the one outside your door.
>
> In the dark, a floorboard creaks.

The bearer of the black stone, and any character who is on watch, wakes at
once. The others wake when the first one shouts, or when the fight starts.

### The attack

The Hush want the black stone. They know where it is: the stone glows in
the dark for them, like a lamp. A **Hush Lightless** leads them. With it
are **Hush Snuffers**. They come in through the garden door and up the
stair.

{{statblock: Hush Lightless}}

| Party size | Monsters | XP |
|---|---|---|
| 4 characters | 1 Hush Lightless, 4 Hush Snuffers | 1,500 |

> **Scaling:** This is a Moderate fight for level 4 (375 XP for each
> character).
>
> - **3 characters (1,125 XP):** 1 Hush Lightless, 1 Hush Snuffer (1,200
>   XP).
> - **4 characters (1,500 XP):** as in the table.
> - **5 characters (1,875 XP):** 1 Hush Lightless, 1 Hush Shroud, 3 Hush
>   Snuffers (1,850 XP).
> - **6 characters (2,250 XP):** 1 Hush Lightless, 2 Hush Shrouds, 2 Hush
>   Snuffers (2,200 XP).

The statblocks of the Hush Snuffer and the Hush Shroud are in Scene 7 of
chapter 3, and in the Creatures appendix.

### The map

- The upper floor has a landing 10 feet wide and three bedrooms. The stair
  is 5 feet wide and 20 feet long. One Snuffer at the top of the stair
  blocks it.
- The ground floor has a hall, a big room with a long table, and a kitchen
  that opens to the garden.
- **It is dark.** The Hush put out every lamp. Only Wick gives light,
  unless a character lights something. A Hush Lightless has Darkvision and
  deals extra Necrotic damage in Dim Light and Darkness.
- **The windows** of the bedrooms open on the garden, 15 feet below.

### Running the fight

- **Round 1:** Two Snuffers come up the stair. The Lightless follows. Two
  Snuffers wait in the garden, under the window of the stone bearer.
- **The goal is the stone.** A Snuffer that reaches the stone takes it as
  a Utilize action and runs for the garden. If a Snuffer holds the stone,
  the others cover its escape.
- **The Hush flee** when the Lightless falls, or when they have the stone
  and reach the garden wall. Nobody stops a running Snuffer in the dark
  streets. If the Hush escape with the stone, they take it to Kell, and the
  party must take it back there. See "If the Hush have the stone" in
  Scene 6.
- **The servant** of the house, an old man called Wat, hides under the
  kitchen table and prays very loudly.

> **GM tip:** The stone is the most important thing in the room, and the
> players may not know it yet. If a player says "I grab the stone" or "I
> hold onto it", reward that. If nobody thinks of it, let the first
> Snuffer that reaches the room look straight at it, not at the
> characters. Players notice when the enemy ignores them.

### The bells

When the fight ends, the bells of the cathedral start to ring. Not the
slow bells of prayer. The fast bells of fire, or war. The whole town
wakes. A priest runs past the Saints' House with a lamp, crying: "The
Ember! The Ember is gone!"

## Scene 2: The Morning After (40 minutes)

**Goal:** Give the grey folk a name. Find the trail. Bring Cade along.

### The cathedral

> The high altar is empty. The gold cage of the Ember lies on the floor,
> its bars cut. Around the altar, every candle in the cathedral is out:
> not blown out, but pinched, a thousand black wicks. On the floor, in the
> wax, are the prints of bare feet and soft shoes.
>
> The Abbess stands in front of the empty altar. She has not slept. She
> looks ten years older than she did last night. "Two hundred years," she
> says. "Two hundred years, and it is gone in one night. In the week that
> you woke."

The Abbess does not blame the party. She is too frightened. She asks them
to bring the Ember back. "You are the Sleepers. Who else can I ask?" She
offers whatever help the Church can give: horses, food, two priests. The
party can take them or not.

### What the party can find

| What a character does | Check | DC | What the character learns |
|---|---|---|---|
| Looks at the cage | Intelligence (Investigation) | 10 | The bars were cut with a fine saw. The thieves had time. They came from below. |
| Follows the prints | Wisdom (Survival) | 12 | The prints go to the stair behind the altar, down into the old shrine, to the well of Saint Ivo. A rope hangs down the well. |
| Goes down the well | Strength (Athletics) | 12 | At the water, 60 feet down, an old tunnel of Ostrey runs out of the well to the river. Someone used it last night. A boat was tied at the end. |
| Asks the guards on the walls | Charisma (Persuasion) | 10 | A guard on the west wall saw a cart with no lamp on the road west, after midnight, going fast. |
| Searches a dead or captured Snuffer | Wisdom (Perception) | 10 | A grey circle tattooed inside the wrist. A brass snuffer. A string of grey beads with one black bead. A scrap of paper with three words in an old hand: KELL. THIRD NIGHT. |
| Compares the snuffer from the well (chapter 3) | None | None | It is the same as the snuffers of the Hush. Somebody came down the well days ago, to find the way. |

> **GM tip:** The trail is easy. That is on purpose. Mother Sallow wants
> the party to follow, with the stone. A character who makes a DC 15
> Wisdom (Insight) check, after the party has three clues, feels it: "This
> is too easy. They want us to come." Good. The party goes anyway. The
> Ember is there.

### Brother Cade names them

Cade comes to the cathedral with a book under his arm, out of breath.

> "The Hush," says Cade. "I knew I had read it." He opens the book. It is
> a book of reports from priests on the roads, from the last thirty years.
> "Grey folk. No lamps. Signs with the hands. A cult of the Unlit King,
> they say. It started when I was a boy. Nobody believed it. Small things:
> a shrine with its candles out, a priest who heard grey folk singing
> without sound. I wrote it down. I always write it down." He looks at the
> empty altar. "I did not believe it either."

Cade knows the facts in "The Hush" at the start of this chapter, except
for the black stone and the rite. He knows that Kell is an old shrine of
Ostrey on a hill, two days west, off the road: a ring of standing stones
with a chapel inside. Nobody goes there. "The shepherds say the stones are
cold even in summer."

Cade goes with the party. If he believes them (5 Proof in chapter 3), he
says so plainly: "I was wrong about you. I will not be wrong about them."
If he does not, he says: "I still do not know what you are. But I know
what they are. I am coming."

> **GM tip:** Cade is an ally in this chapter. He is a CR 2 priest with
> *Cure Wounds* and *Bless*. He is not part of the encounter budgets. In a
> party of 3, he fights beside the party. In a party of 5 or 6, he stays
> back and keeps the horses, or he guards Pell. In every case, the players
> should not need him to win. He is there for the talk, the heal at the
> right moment, and the scene at the end.

### If the Hush took Garret Pell

If the Hush took Pell in chapter 3, his troupe comes to the party in the
morning. Tilly Ashe has a note that the Hush left on the stage: grey
paper, with one line. "The false sleeper waits at Kell for the true ones."
Now the party knows two reasons to go.

## Scene 3: The Weight of the Stone (30 minutes)

**Goal:** Teach Marks. The black stone marks the character who carries it.

This scene happens on the morning after the theft, before the party leaves
Saint Ivo's, or at the first camp on the road. It starts when the bearer
of the black stone picks it up.

> The stone is cold, as always. Then it is not. It is warm, like a hand,
> and for a moment you feel it move in your palm, like a heart.
>
> You see a long, cold dark. Crystal on every side. A slow, deep breath,
> very far away, in and out, once each hour. Then it is gone. The stone is
> cold again.
>
> Wick looks at you. His flame is very small. "You went grey," he says.
> "Just for a moment. Your whole face went grey."

The Ember flared next to the stone at the Waking Service. Something woke
in the stone that night. Now it marks its bearer.

### Before you start

Marks are an optional system. Read Running Marks in the Game Master's
Compendium, and the Marks chapter of the Player's Compendium, before this
session. Then ask the table the three questions from Before You Start:

- Are hidden Marks allowed? This chapter uses an **open** Mark. The
  player sees all of its rules. If your table loves secrets, you can keep
  the track behind your screen instead, and describe its signs.
- Can the story put a Mark on a character with no choice? Here, the
  player chooses who carries the stone. That is the choice.
- Which themes are off the table? This Mark is about cold, the dark, and
  a voice that wants something. If a player does not want that, the party
  can skip the Mark: see "If your group does not use Marks" below.

> **Teach: Marks.** "A Mark is a lasting change to your character, from a
> blessing, a curse, or a power you used. It gives you something and it
> takes something. It has a track from 0 to 5. Each time its trigger
> happens, the track grows by 1, but only once each scene. At some
> numbers, the Mark changes. At 5, you choose: give in to it, or break
> free."

### Who carries the stone

The stone marks the character who carries it for one day. Let the players
choose. It can be the character who picked it up in the tutorial, or
someone else.

- **A character can refuse to carry it.** Then someone else must. The
  stone cannot be left behind: the Hush can feel it, and they would take
  it.
- **Wick cannot carry it.** If a player tries, Wick's flame goes out for a
  moment, and Wick screams. He is fine. He is not happy.
- **Cade will not carry it.** "I have read about cursed things," he says.
  "I have never read a good ending."

### The Curse of the Black Stone

Give the player the handout. It is the Mark card for the stone, with the
track.

{{handout: 04-the-black-stone}}

The full rules of the stone are in its entry in the Treasures appendix.
Here is the short form, to explain at the table.

{{treasure: The Black Stone}}

| Part | The black stone |
|---|---|
| **Kind** | An open Curse. The bearer chose to carry the stone, but the stone chose what it does. |
| **Benefit** | Darkvision 60 feet (or 30 feet more). Once each Short or Long Rest, as a Bonus Action, *draw on the stone* for 1 minute: your weapon attacks and cantrips deal an extra 1d6 Necrotic damage, and magical Darkness doesn't stop your Darkvision. |
| **Cost** | A member of the Hush within 1 mile knows the direction to you. You have Disadvantage on saves against the Charmed and Frightened conditions from the Hush. Touching a piece of the Dawnfire deals 1d6 Radiant damage to you. |
| **Grows** | Each time you draw on the stone. |
| **At 2** | You dream of cold crystal. Once between Long Rests, you can learn the direction to the nearest piece of the Dawnfire within 10 miles. The extra damage becomes 1d8. |
| **At 4** | Your shadow falls a moment late. The Hush think that you are one of them until you attack. You have Disadvantage on attack rolls in the Bright Light of a piece of the Dawnfire. |
| **Crisis (5)** | Give in, or break free. See the full entry. |
| **Ending** | Hold the stone in the light of a piece of the Dawnfire from dusk to dawn, and take the burn every hour. |

> **Teach: the track.** "The track starts at 0. Each time you draw on the
> stone, it grows by 1, but only once in each scene. It never goes down by
> itself. You decide when to use the stone. The stone decides what it does
> to you."

> **Teach: the crisis.** "When the track reaches 5, you choose. Give in, and
> the stone gives you much more power, but at some moments, I tell you
> what your character does. Or break free: the stone cracks, the Mark
> ends, and you keep a small scar. Your character is always yours, unless
> you choose otherwise."

### The cost is the story

The first line of the cost matters most: **the Hush can feel the stone.**
Say it plainly to the players: "As long as you carry it, the grey folk can
find you." This is why the Hush found the Saints' House in the dark. It is
why the trail to Kell is so easy. And at the end of this chapter, the party
learns that the Hush is not the only one who can feel it.

### Pacing the Mark

Running Marks suggests one threshold every two or three sessions. The
stone grows only when the bearer chooses to draw on it, so the player
controls the pace. In this chapter, the bearer can use it in each fight.
That is up to three points before the end of chapter 4. That is fine: it
puts the first threshold in this chapter, and the second in chapter 6 or
7.

> **GM tip:** Do not push the player to use the stone. Offer it. In a hard
> moment of a fight, the stone hums, and the bearer feels that it would
> help. "The stone is cold in your hand. It wants to help. Do you let it?"
> That is all. The choice is the story.

### Power and encounters

Running Marks says that a Mark before its crisis is worth about an
Uncommon magic item. One Mark in a party of four or five does not change
the encounters. Build them as written. If a second character takes a Mark
later in the book, build encounters as if the party had one more
character.

### If your group does not use Marks

Skip the Teach boxes. The stone still hums, still draws the Hush, and
still burns near the Dawnfire. It gives no benefit and has no track. The
story of this chapter works the same.
