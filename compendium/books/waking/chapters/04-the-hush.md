# The Hush

| | |
|---|---|
| **Levels** | 4 to 5 |
| **Time at the table** | About 2 to 3 sessions (8 to 12 hours) |
| **Places** | Saint Ivo's, the hills to the west, the shrine at Kell |
| **System that starts** | Marks |
| **Thread** | None. The black stone is the thread of this chapter. |
| **People** | Mother Sallow, the leader of the Hush; Osk, her enforcer; Brother Cade; Garret Pell, if the Hush took him |

On the night after the Waking Service, the grey folk come out of the dark.
They steal the Ember of Saint Ivo from the high altar, and they try to take
the black stone from the party. They have a name: the Hush, the cult of
the Unlit King. They believe that the sun is dying, and that only their
king can save it.

The party follows them west, off the road, to an old shrine of Ostrey on
a hill: the shrine at Kell. There, Mother Sallow tries to put out the
Ember, and she needs the black stone to do it. At the end of the night,
the party learns the worst thing about the stone: the Unlit King can feel
where it is.

This chapter ends Act I.

This chapter has two jobs:

1. **Bring the Hush into the open.** Until now, the grey folk were a
   rumor. Now they have a name, a leader, and a goal.
2. **Teach Marks.** The black stone has had no rules since the tutorial.
   Now it marks the character who carries it, and the Mark grows each time
   the character uses the stone.

## Running This Chapter

### What happens

1. **The night of the theft (Scene 1).** The Hush attack the Saints' House
   to take the black stone, while other members steal the Ember from the
   cathedral. This is the first fight.
2. **The morning after (Scene 2).** The town is in shock. Brother Cade
   gives the grey folk a name. The party finds the trail west.
3. **The weight of the stone (Scene 3).** The black stone wakes. It marks
   its bearer. Teach Marks here.
4. **The hills (Scene 4).** Two days off the road, with the Journeys rules.
   The Hush can feel the stone, so they set an ambush. Osk comes for it.
   This is the second fight.
5. **The shrine at Kell (Scene 5).** A ring of standing stones on a hill,
   with a small chapel inside it. The Hush camp there. The party scouts
   it and finds a way in.
6. **The rite (Scene 6).** Mother Sallow tries to put out the Ember. A
   shadow steps out of the black stone. This is the last fight of Act I.
7. **The voice in the stone (Scene 7).** The Unlit King speaks. Mother
   Sallow escapes. The party carries the Ember home, and Act I ends.

### The Hush

The Hush is the cult of the Unlit King. Use these facts when the party
meets its members and asks questions.

- **It is about thirty years old.** It started when the days started to
  become shorter. Few people noticed the days. The Hush noticed.
- **It believes that the sun is dying,** and that only the Unlit King can
  save it. Its members are partly right, and that makes them dangerous.
  They are not monsters. They are frightened people with a terrible
  answer.
- **It speaks little.** Its members talk with signs of the hand: Hush
  Sign. They take a vow of quiet when they join.
- **It wears grey** and puts out lights. A grey circle is tattooed inside
  the wrist of each member.
- **It looks for the pieces of the Dawnfire,** to put them out and wake its
  king. It put out the piece in the chapel of Dawnwatch Keep, on the night
  the Dawnwatch woke. The Ember of Saint Ivo is the next.
- **It wants the black stone.** Mother Sallow knows that the stone is a
  piece of the king's own crystal. She needs it for her rite.

The Hush has three tiers. Their statblocks are in this chapter and in the
Creatures appendix.

| Tier | Creature | CR | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hush Snuffer | 1/2 | A new member with a brass snuffer on a pole. |
| 2 | Hush Shroud | 2 | A priest of the Hush in a grey veil. It prays in silence. |
| 3 | Hush Lightless | 4 | A warrior who drank the black water at the secret rite. Its eyes have no white. |

### The pace

The Hush have a day's start. The party can catch them at Kell on the
second night if it travels fast, or on the third night at a normal pace.
It does not matter much: Mother Sallow waits for the party. She needs the
black stone, and she knows that the party will bring it to her. The real
clock is in Scene 6.

> **GM tip:** Tell the players, through Cade or Wick, that the Hush took
> the Ember to put it out. That is the stake: the first piece of the
> Dawnfire that they ever saw, the light that knew them. Do not tell them
> that Sallow is waiting for them. Let them work it out from the trail:
> it is very easy to follow. Too easy.

## Scene 1: The Night of the Theft (45 minutes)

**Goal:** The Hush come out of the dark. The first fight of the chapter.

This scene happens on the night after the Waking Service, or the night
after that, if the party left the town for a day. The party sleeps in the
Saints' House, next to the Abbey. It is a fine stone house of two floors,
with a garden behind it and a servant who sleeps in the kitchen.

> It is the middle of the night. The town is asleep after three days of
> feast.
>
> The black stone wakes you. It hums so hard that it rattles on the table
> beside the bed, like a cup in an earthquake.
>
> Downstairs, the lamp in the hall goes out. Then the one on the stair.
> Then the one outside your door.
>
> In the dark, a floorboard creaks.

The bearer of the black stone, and any character who is on watch, wakes at
once. The others wake when the first one shouts, or when the fight starts.

### The attack

The Hush want the black stone. They know where it is: the stone glows in
the dark for them, like a lamp. A **Hush Lightless** leads them. With it
are **Hush Snuffers**. They come in through the garden door and up the
stair.

{{statblock: Hush Lightless}}

| Party size | Monsters | XP |
|---|---|---|
| 4 characters | 1 Hush Lightless, 4 Hush Snuffers | 1,500 |

> **Scaling:** This is a Moderate fight for level 4 (375 XP for each
> character).
>
> - **3 characters (1,125 XP):** 1 Hush Lightless, 1 Hush Snuffer (1,200
>   XP).
> - **4 characters (1,500 XP):** as in the table.
> - **5 characters (1,875 XP):** 1 Hush Lightless, 1 Hush Shroud, 3 Hush
>   Snuffers (1,850 XP).
> - **6 characters (2,250 XP):** 1 Hush Lightless, 2 Hush Shrouds, 2 Hush
>   Snuffers (2,200 XP).

The statblocks of the Hush Snuffer and the Hush Shroud are in Scene 7 of
chapter 3, and in the Creatures appendix.

### The map

- The upper floor has a landing 10 feet wide and three bedrooms. The stair
  is 5 feet wide and 20 feet long. One Snuffer at the top of the stair
  blocks it.
- The ground floor has a hall, a big room with a long table, and a kitchen
  that opens to the garden.
- **It is dark.** The Hush put out every lamp. Only Wick gives light,
  unless a character lights something. A Hush Lightless has Darkvision and
  deals extra Necrotic damage in Dim Light and Darkness.
- **The windows** of the bedrooms open on the garden, 15 feet below.

### Running the fight

- **Round 1:** Two Snuffers come up the stair. The Lightless follows. Two
  Snuffers wait in the garden, under the window of the stone bearer.
- **The goal is the stone.** A Snuffer that reaches the stone takes it as
  a Utilize action and runs for the garden. If a Snuffer holds the stone,
  the others cover its escape.
- **The Hush flee** when the Lightless falls, or when they have the stone
  and reach the garden wall. Nobody stops a running Snuffer in the dark
  streets. If the Hush escape with the stone, they take it to Kell, and the
  party must take it back there. See "If the Hush have the stone" in
  Scene 6.
- **The servant** of the house, an old man called Wat, hides under the
  kitchen table and prays very loudly.

> **GM tip:** The stone is the most important thing in the room, and the
> players may not know it yet. If a player says "I grab the stone" or "I
> hold onto it", reward that. If nobody thinks of it, let the first
> Snuffer that reaches the room look straight at it, not at the
> characters. Players notice when the enemy ignores them.

### The bells

When the fight ends, the bells of the cathedral start to ring. Not the
slow bells of prayer. The fast bells of fire, or war. The whole town
wakes. A priest runs past the Saints' House with a lamp, crying: "The
Ember! The Ember is gone!"

## Scene 2: The Morning After (40 minutes)

**Goal:** Give the grey folk a name. Find the trail. Bring Cade along.

### The cathedral

> The high altar is empty. The gold cage of the Ember lies on the floor,
> its bars cut. Around the altar, every candle in the cathedral is out:
> not blown out, but pinched, a thousand black wicks. On the floor, in the
> wax, are the prints of bare feet and soft shoes.
>
> The Abbess stands in front of the empty altar. She has not slept. She
> looks ten years older than she did last night. "Two hundred years," she
> says. "Two hundred years, and it is gone in one night. In the week that
> you woke."

The Abbess does not blame the party. She is too frightened. She asks them
to bring the Ember back. "You are the Sleepers. Who else can I ask?" She
offers whatever help the Church can give: horses, food, two priests. The
party can take them or not.

### What the party can find

| What a character does | Check | DC | What the character learns |
|---|---|---|---|
| Looks at the cage | Intelligence (Investigation) | 10 | The bars were cut with a fine saw. The thieves had time. They came from below. |
| Follows the prints | Wisdom (Survival) | 12 | The prints go to the stair behind the altar, down into the old shrine, to the well of Saint Ivo. A rope hangs down the well. |
| Goes down the well | Strength (Athletics) | 12 | At the water, 60 feet down, an old tunnel of Ostrey runs out of the well to the river. Someone used it last night. A boat was tied at the end. |
| Asks the guards on the walls | Charisma (Persuasion) | 10 | A guard on the west wall saw a cart with no lamp on the road west, after midnight, going fast. |
| Searches a dead or captured Snuffer | Wisdom (Perception) | 10 | A grey circle tattooed inside the wrist. A brass snuffer. A string of grey beads with one black bead. A scrap of paper with three words in an old hand: KELL. THIRD NIGHT. |
| Compares the snuffer from the well (chapter 3) | None | None | It is the same as the snuffers of the Hush. Somebody came down the well days ago, to find the way. |

> **GM tip:** The trail is easy. That is on purpose. Mother Sallow wants
> the party to follow, with the stone. A character who makes a DC 15
> Wisdom (Insight) check, after the party has three clues, feels it: "This
> is too easy. They want us to come." Good. The party goes anyway. The
> Ember is there.

### Brother Cade names them

Cade comes to the cathedral with a book under his arm, out of breath.

> "The Hush," says Cade. "I knew I had read it." He opens the book. It is
> a book of reports from priests on the roads, from the last thirty years.
> "Grey folk. No lamps. Signs with the hands. A cult of the Unlit King,
> they say. It started when I was a boy. Nobody believed it. Small things:
> a shrine with its candles out, a priest who heard grey folk singing
> without sound. I wrote it down. I always write it down." He looks at the
> empty altar. "I did not believe it either."

Cade knows the facts in "The Hush" at the start of this chapter, except
for the black stone and the rite. He knows that Kell is an old shrine of
Ostrey on a hill, two days west, off the road: a ring of standing stones
with a chapel inside. Nobody goes there. "The shepherds say the stones are
cold even in summer."

Cade goes with the party. If he believes them (5 Proof in chapter 3), he
says so plainly: "I was wrong about you. I will not be wrong about them."
If he does not, he says: "I still do not know what you are. But I know
what they are. I am coming."

> **GM tip:** Cade is an ally in this chapter. He is a CR 2 priest with
> *Cure Wounds* and *Bless*. He is not part of the encounter budgets. In a
> party of 3, he fights beside the party. In a party of 5 or 6, he stays
> back and keeps the horses, or he guards Pell. In every case, the players
> should not need him to win. He is there for the talk, the heal at the
> right moment, and the scene at the end.

### If the Hush took Garret Pell

If the Hush took Pell in chapter 3, his troupe comes to the party in the
morning. Tilly Ashe has a note that the Hush left on the stage: grey
paper, with one line. "The false sleeper waits at Kell for the true ones."
Now the party knows two reasons to go.

## Scene 3: The Weight of the Stone (30 minutes)

**Goal:** Teach Marks. The black stone marks the character who carries it.

This scene happens on the morning after the theft, before the party leaves
Saint Ivo's, or at the first camp on the road. It starts when the bearer
of the black stone picks it up.

> The stone is cold, as always. Then it is not. It is warm, like a hand,
> and for a moment you feel it move in your palm, like a heart.
>
> You see a long, cold dark. Crystal on every side. A slow, deep breath,
> very far away, in and out, once each hour. Then it is gone. The stone is
> cold again.
>
> Wick looks at you. His flame is very small. "You went grey," he says.
> "Just for a moment. Your whole face went grey."

The Ember flared next to the stone at the Waking Service. Something woke
in the stone that night. Now it marks its bearer.

### Before you start

Marks are an optional system. Read Running Marks in the Game Master's
Compendium, and the Marks chapter of the Player's Compendium, before this
session. Then ask the table the three questions from Before You Start:

- Are hidden Marks allowed? This chapter uses an **open** Mark. The
  player sees all of its rules. If your table loves secrets, you can keep
  the track behind your screen instead, and describe its signs.
- Can the story put a Curse on a character with no choice? Here, the
  players choose who carries the stone. That is the choice.
- Which themes are off the table? This Mark is about cold, the dark, and
  a voice that wants something. If a player does not want that, the party
  can skip the Mark: see "If your group does not use Marks" below.

> **Teach: Marks.** "A Mark is a lasting change to your character: a
> blessing, a curse, or the taint of a Sin. It gives you something and it
> takes something. It has a track from 0 to 5. Each time its trigger
> happens, the track grows by 1, but only once each scene. At some
> numbers, the Mark changes. At 5, you choose: give in to it, or break
> free."

### Who carries the stone

The stone marks the character who carries it for one day. Let the players
choose. It can be the character who picked it up in the tutorial, or
someone else.

- **A character can refuse to carry it.** Then someone else must. The
  stone cannot be left behind: the Hush can feel it, and they would take
  it.
- **Wick cannot carry it.** If a player tries, Wick's flame goes out for a
  moment, and Wick screams. He is fine. He is not happy.
- **Cade will not carry it.** "I have read about cursed things," he says.
  "I have never read a good ending."

### The Curse of the Black Stone

Give the player the handout. It is the Mark card for the stone, with the
track.

{{handout: 04-the-black-stone}}

Here is the full entry of the stone. It is also in the Treasures appendix.
The table after it is the short form, to explain at the table.

{{treasure: The Black Stone}}

| Part | The black stone |
|---|---|
| **Kind** | An open Curse. The bearer chose to carry the stone, but the stone chose what it does. |
| **Benefit** | Darkvision 60 feet (or 30 feet more). Once between rests, as a Bonus Action, *draw on the stone* for 1 minute (you regain the use when you finish a Short or Long Rest): your weapon attacks and cantrips deal an extra 1d6 Necrotic damage, and magical Darkness doesn't stop your Darkvision. |
| **Cost** | A member of the Hush within 1 mile knows the direction to you. You have Disadvantage on saves against the Charmed and Frightened conditions from the Hush. Touching a piece of the Dawnfire deals 1d6 Radiant damage to you. |
| **Grows** | Each time you draw on the stone. |
| **At 2** | You dream of cold crystal. Once between Long Rests, you can learn the direction to the nearest piece of the Dawnfire within 10 miles. The extra damage becomes 1d8. |
| **At 4** | Your shadow falls a moment late. The Hush think that you are one of them until you attack. You have Disadvantage on attack rolls in the Bright Light of a piece of the Dawnfire. |
| **Crisis (5)** | Give in, or break free. See the full entry. |
| **Ending** | Hold the stone in the light of a piece of the Dawnfire from dusk to dawn, and take the burn every hour. |

> **Teach: the track.** "The track starts at 0. Each time you draw on the
> stone, it grows by 1, but only once in each scene. It never goes down by
> itself. You decide when to use the stone. The stone decides what it does
> to you."

> **Teach: the crisis.** "When the track reaches 5, you choose. Give in, and
> the stone gives you much more power, but at some moments, I tell you
> what your character does. Or break free: the stone cracks, the Mark
> ends, and you keep a small scar. Your character is always yours, unless
> you choose otherwise."

### The cost is the story

The first line of the cost matters most: **the Hush can feel the stone.**
Say it plainly to the players: "As long as you carry it, the grey folk can
find you." This is why the Hush found the Saints' House in the dark. It is
why the trail to Kell is so easy. And at the end of this chapter, the party
learns that the Hush is not the only one who can feel it.

### Pacing the Mark

Running Marks suggests one threshold every two or three sessions. The
stone grows only when the bearer chooses to draw on it, so the player
controls the pace. In this chapter, the bearer can use it in each fight.
That is up to three points before the end of chapter 4. That is fine: it
puts the first threshold in this chapter, and the second in chapter 6 or
7.

> **GM tip:** Do not push the player to use the stone. Offer it. In a hard
> moment of a fight, the stone hums, and the bearer feels that it would
> help. "The stone is cold in your hand. It wants to help. Do you let it?"
> That is all. The choice is the story.

### Power and encounters

Running Marks says that a Mark before its crisis is worth about an
Uncommon magic item, and that a Mark after the bearer gives in is worth
about a Rare one. One Mark in a party does not change the encounters.
Build them as written. If the party later carries more Marks than it has
characters, build encounters as if the party had one more character. If
two or more characters give in to a Mark, also use monsters of 1 CR more
where you can.

### If your group does not use Marks

Skip the Teach boxes. The stone still hums, still draws the Hush, and
still burns near the Dawnfire. It gives no benefit and has no track. The
story of this chapter works the same.

## Scene 4: The Hills (1 hour)

**Goal:** A short journey with the Journeys rules. The Hush come for the
stone on the road. The second fight.

Kell is two days west of Saint Ivo's, off the road, in the low hills
between the River Brann and the River Dunn. There is no road. There is a
sheep track for half a day, and then only the hills.

### The journey

Use the Journeys rules again, as in chapter 2. This time, do not teach
them. Ask the players to choose their roles and their pace, and go.

| Day | Where | Navigation DC | Foraging DC | Event |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The sheep track, then open hills | 12 | 12 | The ambush (at night) |
| 2 | The high hills, the ring on the skyline | 12 | 12 | Kell |

The hills use the Hill biome of the Journeys chapter in the Game Master's
Compendium: its weather table and its encounter table. If the roll gives
an encounter, use the table there, or use one of these:

- Shepherds who left the high pastures early this year. "The stones of
  Kell are cold," they say. "Colder every year. And there are grey folk up
  there now. They pay for sheep in old coins."
- A flock of crows that follows the party all day, at a distance. In the
  evening they fly west, toward Kell. If the bearer draws on the stone,
  the crows turn and look at the bearer, all together.
- An old milestone of Ostrey, fallen in the grass: KELL 12 MILES. A
  character with the Compass of the Old Road finds the line of the old
  road under the grass. The guide has Advantage for the rest of the day.

> **GM tip:** If the party took horses from the Abbey, it can travel at a
> Fast pace with no Disadvantage for the guide and the scout on day 1,
> because the horses know the track. Off the track, on day 2, the horses
> are slower than feet on the steep ground. Use the normal rules.

### The ambush

The Hush can feel the stone. On the first night, they know exactly where
the party camps. Mother Sallow sends Osk to take the stone and bring it to
her.

If the lookout succeeded, the camp can't be Surprised, and the lookout sees
the grey shapes on the hillside first. If the scout succeeded on day 1,
the party knows that someone followed them all day. Give the party a round
to get ready.

> The fire is low. The hills are black, and the sky is full of stars. The
> stone hums, and it does not stop.
>
> Then the fire goes out. Not low. Out, as if a hand closed over it. In
> the starlight, grey shapes come over the top of the hill, quiet as snow.
> In the middle of them walks a man so big that he blocks the stars. He
> carries an iron maul, and he makes no sound at all.

This is **Osk**, the enforcer of Mother Sallow. With him are **Hush
Snuffers**.

| Party size | Monsters | XP |
|---|---|---|
| 4 characters | Osk, 4 Hush Snuffers | 1,500 |

> **Scaling:** This is a Moderate fight for level 4 (375 XP for each
> character).
>
> - **3 characters (1,125 XP):** Osk, 1 Hush Snuffer (1,200 XP).
> - **4 characters (1,500 XP):** as in the table.
> - **5 characters (1,875 XP):** Osk, 1 Hush Shroud, 3 Hush Snuffers
>   (1,850 XP).
> - **6 characters (2,250 XP):** Osk, 2 Hush Shrouds, 2 Hush Snuffers
>   (2,200 XP).

### Running the ambush

- **The camp** is in a hollow between two hills, 40 feet across. The fire
  is in the middle. The horses or the mule are tied at one side.
- **It is dark.** The Snuffers put out the fire at once. Starlight is Dim
  Light only at the top of the hills. In the hollow, it is Darkness,
  except for Wick.
- **Osk wants the bearer.** He walks straight at the bearer of the stone.
  He uses Carry Off to grab the bearer, and then he runs west with the
  bearer under one arm, at his full Speed. The Snuffers try to stop the
  others from following.
- **To get free,** the bearer makes a Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity
  (Acrobatics) check against the escape DC on Osk's statblock, as an
  action. Allies can attack Osk while he runs, or grab the bearer back.
- **Osk does not die here.** He returns in chapter 7. When
  he drops below a quarter of his Hit Points, he drops the bearer and
  runs into the dark, very fast and very quiet. If the party chases him,
  he is gone at the top of the hill. If a character drops him to 0 Hit
  Points with a melee attack, the character can choose to knock him out
  instead: then he wakes in the night and is gone, with the ropes cut.
  Nobody saw him go. The Snuffers flee when Osk flees.

> **GM tip:** This is a good fight for the bearer to draw on the stone for
> the first time. The bearer is in the dark, under one arm of a giant. The
> stone hums. "It would help," you say. "Do you let it?"

### After the ambush

A captured Snuffer does not talk. A character who makes a DC 15 Charisma
(Intimidation) or Charisma (Persuasion) check gets one answer in Hush
Sign, with the hands: a circle, and a hand that closes over it. A
character who knows the signs from chapter 3 reads it: "The light goes
out." If Cade is there, he says the prayer for the dying over the dead
Snuffers, every one of them. "They were somebody's children," he says.

## Scene 5: The Shrine at Kell (45 minutes)

**Goal:** Show the end of the trail. Let the party scout, plan, and find a
way in.

> On the second evening, you see it from far away: a ring of standing
> stones on the top of a bare hill, black against a red sky. Inside the
> ring is a small chapel of grey stone with no roof. Around the foot of
> the hill, a dozen grey tents. No fires. No lights at all.
>
> As the sun goes down, the stones of the ring do not get dark. They are
> already dark. It is as if they drink the light.
>
> "I know this place," says Wick, very quietly. "The steward talked about
> it. There was a fire here once. Like the one in the chapel at home."

### What Kell is

Three hundred years ago, the shrine at Kell was a ring of standing stones
with a small chapel inside it. The Dawnwatch know it: soldiers went there
to pray before a long march. In the chapel was a bowl of stone with a
small fire in it that never went out.

That fire was a piece of the Dawnfire. Ostrey gave it to Kell, as it gave
the Ember to the shrine of Saint Ivo. In the Broken Years, after the Grey
Winter, the shrine was empty, and the fire went out. Nobody knows how. The
stones have been cold since then.

Mother Sallow chose Kell for this reason. "A dead bowl drinks a living
flame," she tells her people. Her rite needs three things: the Ember on
the dead bowl of Kell, the black stone near it, and her voice.

> **GM tip:** The players may now guess that there were many pieces of the
> Dawnfire, in many shrines, and that the Hush hunts them. That is right.
> Let them guess. The ledger of the Hush, in Scene 7, confirms it.

### The camp of the Hush

| Area | What is there |
|---|---|
| **The tents** | A dozen grey tents at the foot of the hill, on the east side. Most are empty: the Hush are on the hill. Three Snuffers guard them. In one tent, the Hush keep Garret Pell, if they took him. |
| **The path** | A path goes up the hill from the tents, 300 feet, to a gap in the ring. Two Snuffers stand at the gap. |
| **The ring** | Thirteen standing stones, each as tall as two men, in a circle 120 feet across. The stones are cold to the touch, even in the sun. |
| **The chapel** | In the middle of the ring. Four walls of grey stone, 30 feet by 20 feet, with no roof and one door on the east side. Inside is the dead bowl, on a low altar. |

The other sides of the hill are steep and bare, with grass and loose
stones. A character can climb them with a DC 12 Strength (Athletics) or
Dexterity (Stealth) check, to come into the ring from the west, far from
the gap.

### Ways in

Let the players plan. These are good plans. Each one works.

| Plan | Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sneak up the west side at night | Dexterity (Stealth), for each character | 12 | On a success for all, the party reaches the ring unseen. Skip the guards at the gap. In Scene 6, the party acts in a surprise round, with the Hush Surprised. |
| Free Pell from the tents first | Dexterity (Stealth) | 12 | The three Snuffers at the tents do not see the party. Pell is tied up, gagged, and very glad. He can walk. He cannot fight. |
| Fight the guards at the gap | None | None | Two Snuffers. A Low fight for level 4. When the fight starts, Mother Sallow knows. She starts the rite at once. |
| Walk up the path openly, with the stone | None | None | The guards let the party pass. Mother Sallow waits in the chapel. She wants to talk first. See "The talk" in Scene 6. |
| Use the stone as bait | Charisma (Deception) | 13 | One character carries the stone up the path, and the others go up the west side. The Hush watch the stone, not the hill. The others get Advantage on their Stealth checks. |

> **GM tip:** The bearer cannot sneak up on the Hush. They feel the stone.
> If the bearer goes with the sneaking group, the group fails, unless the
> bearer gives the stone to someone else for this scene, or stays behind.
> But the stone must be within 30 feet of the bowl for the rite. Point it
> out to a clever player: "Sallow needs the stone. What if she does not get
> it?" A party that keeps the stone far away makes the rite fail. Then
> Sallow sends everything she has to get it. See Scene 6.

## Scene 6: The Rite (1 hour)

**Goal:** The last fight of Act I. A fight with a clock. Save the Ember.

> The chapel has no roof. Above it, the stars are hidden by a cold grey
> haze that was not there a moment ago. The air inside the ring is still,
> and very quiet. No wind. No crickets. Your own breathing sounds loud.
>
> In the chapel, on the low altar, the Ember of Saint Ivo lies in the
> black bowl of Kell. Its gold light is weak and flickers, like a candle
> in a draft. Around the altar kneel grey figures, with their hands over
> their mouths.
>
> A small woman in grey stands at the altar. She turns to you, and her
> face is kind, and very tired, like a nurse at the end of a long night.
> You saw her in the square at Saint Ivo's, with no candle.
>
> "Oh, good," says Mother Sallow. "You brought it."

{{statblock: Mother Sallow}}

### The talk

If the party comes in openly, or if anyone speaks before the fight, Mother
Sallow talks first. She is calm, gentle, and sure. She believes every
word. Play her as the most reasonable person on the hill.

> "Have you looked at the sun, Sleepers? Really looked? It rises later
> every year. It sets earlier. The winters are longer. The children of
> Brannock's Ford will not see the harvest in thirty years. Your old
> kingdom saw it coming, three hundred years ago, and it did nothing. It
> lit little fires in little bowls, and it went to sleep." She touches the
> Ember, very gently. "I am going to wake the only one who ever tried to
> save it."

- **She tells the truth as she knows it.** The sun is dying. The Unlit King
  wants to save it. The pieces of the Dawnfire keep him asleep. Each light
  that goes out brings him closer to waking. A character who makes a DC 15
  Wisdom (Insight) check knows that she believes all of it.
- **She does not know who the king was.** She calls him "the king" or "the
  Unlit". If a character asks, she smiles. "Does it matter who he was? It
  matters what he will do."
- **She wants the stone.** "Give it to me, and walk away. Nobody needs to
  be hurt. Your friend in the north will thank you." She does not explain
  what she means by that.
- **She cannot be talked out of it.** A character can make her doubt for a
  moment, with a very good argument and a DC 20 Charisma (Persuasion)
  check. On a success, she hesitates: the party acts first in the fight,
  with Advantage on its Initiative rolls. She does not stop.

> **GM tip:** Mother Sallow is the face of the enemy for the rest of Book
> One. Make her memorable. She is not cruel. She is kind to her people,
> polite to the party, and absolutely sure. The scariest villain is the
> one who is half right. Do not let the players kill her: she escapes in
> Scene 7. Her statblock is here so that you can run her spells and her
> escape.

### The rite

When the talk ends, or when the fight starts, Mother Sallow begins the
rite. The rite is a clock.

> **Teach: a fight with a clock.** "This is not only a fight. Mother
> Sallow is doing something at the altar. Each round, it gets closer. I
> will show you how close." Draw three boxes on the paper. Fill one at the
> end of each round in which the rite goes on.

- **The rite needs three things:** the Ember on the dead bowl, the black
  stone within 30 feet of the bowl, and Mother Sallow's voice. She spends
  her action each round on the rite. She uses her Bonus Action (Put Out
  the Light) and her Reaction (Into the Dark) as usual.
- **At the end of each round** in which all three things are true, fill one
  box.
- **When the third box fills,** the Ember goes dark. See "If the Ember goes
  dark" below.

The party can stop the rite. Each of these works. Tell the players none
of them. Let Wick or Cade guess one, if the players are stuck.

| Way | How |
|---|---|
| **Take the Ember.** | A creature next to the altar takes the Ember out of the bowl as a Utilize action. The bearer of the black stone takes 1d6 Radiant damage if it touches the Ember. The rite stops while the Ember is out of the bowl. |
| **Keep the stone away.** | The stone is more than 30 feet from the bowl. The rite does not advance. But the Long Shadow goes with the stone (see below). |
| **Silence Mother Sallow.** | She has the Incapacitated condition, can't speak (*Silence*, a gag while Grappled, a Shush of her own), or is more than 30 feet from the bowl. |
| **Light the stone.** | The bearer holds the stone in the Ember's Bright Light, within 20 feet of it. The rite does not advance this round, and the Long Shadow fades (see below). |

### The Long Shadow

At the end of the first round, Mother Sallow speaks a word that no
character can hear. The stone in the bearer's hand goes ice cold. And the
bearer's own shadow stands up.

> Your shadow peels off the ground. It stretches, up and up, taller than a
> door, in the shape of a knight in old armor with a crown of black fire.
> It moves a moment late, the way a shadow should. It turns its empty face
> toward you, and toward the stone in your hand.

The **Long Shadow** is the shadow of the Unlit King, called out of his own
crystal, through the stone. It fights for the rite. It can't move more
than 120 feet from the stone.

{{statblock: The Long Shadow}}

| Party size | Monsters | XP |
|---|---|---|
| 4 characters | The Long Shadow, 2 Hush Snuffers | 2,000 |

> **Scaling:** This is a High fight for level 4 (500 XP for each
> character). Mother Sallow is not in the budget: she spends her actions
> on the rite, and she escapes. Count her only if she attacks.
>
> - **3 characters (1,500 XP):** the Long Shadow alone (1,800 XP). It
>   starts with 20 fewer Hit Points.
> - **4 characters (2,000 XP):** as in the table.
> - **5 characters (2,500 XP):** the Long Shadow, 1 Hush Shroud, 2 Hush
>   Snuffers (2,450 XP).
> - **6 characters (3,000 XP):** the Long Shadow, 1 Hush Lightless, 1 Hush
>   Snuffer (3,000 XP).
>
> The Long Shadow is CR 5, above the party's level. It is strong in the
> dark and weak in the light. A party that uses the light wins this fight.
> A party that does not use the light may lose it.

### The map

- **The chapel** is 30 feet by 20 feet, with the altar at the west end and
  the door at the east end. Its walls are 10 feet high. The roof is gone.
- **The ring** around the chapel is open grass. The stones give Half
  Cover. The gap is on the east side.
- **The light.** The Ember on the bowl gives only Dim Light, in a 10-foot
  radius, while the rite goes on. When a creature takes the Ember out of
  the bowl, it flares to its full light: Bright Light for 20 feet and Dim
  Light for 20 feet more. Wick gives his usual light. Mother Sallow uses
  Put Out the Light each round to make Wick's light Dim.
- **The kneeling Hush** are not in the fight. They are new members of the
  Hush who pray with their hands over their mouths. They flee in the
  first round in which a character attacks one of them.

### Running the fight

- **Light is everything.** The Long Shadow has Resistance to Bludgeoning,
  Piercing, and Slashing damage in Dim Light or Darkness, and
  Disadvantage on attack rolls in Bright Light. It has Vulnerability to
  Radiant damage. Point this out with Wick: "It does not like me! Hold me
  up! Higher! Oh, it really does not like me."
- **The Ember ends the shadow.** When the stone is in the Bright Light of
  the Ember, the Long Shadow fades into the floor and is gone. This is the
  best way to win the fight, and the most dramatic. The bearer must hold
  the stone near the Ember. It burns.
- **The black stone.** The bearer can draw on the stone. If the bearer does
  it now, describe the Long Shadow turning its head toward the bearer, as
  if it heard a friend.
- **Mother Sallow** can cast *Hold Person* once, on the character who
  reaches the altar first. She does not fight to the death. When she takes
  damage while she is in Dim Light or Darkness, she uses Into the Dark,
  and she is gone. Osk waits for her outside the ring.

### If the Ember goes dark

If the third box fills, the Ember goes dark. Do not make this the end.

> The gold light shrinks to a red spark. Then to nothing. The coal in the
> bowl is black. Mother Sallow closes her eyes, and for a moment she looks
> very happy, and very sad.
>
> Far away in the north, something breathes in.

Mother Sallow uses Into the Dark at once, and she is gone. The Long Shadow
stops, bows to the bearer, and sinks into the ground. The fight is over.

But the Ember is not dead. A piece of the Dawnfire is hard to kill. At
dawn, when the sun touches the black coal, a red spark wakes in it. Over
seven days in the sun, it grows back to its gold. Until then, it has none
of its properties. The Hush failed. It was very close.

### If the Hush have the stone

If the Hush took the stone in Scene 1 or Scene 4, Mother Sallow has it at
the altar, and the Long Shadow rises from her own shadow. It is tied to
the stone in her hand. Run the fight the same way, with one change: the
party must take the stone back from her, or force her to use Into the
Dark. When she flees, she drops it: the stone burns her hand at the last
moment, and she screams, the only sound she makes in the whole book. The
stone goes back to the party. It always comes back.

## Scene 7: The Voice in the Stone (20 minutes)

**Goal:** The end of Act I. The Unlit King speaks. The party learns that he
can feel where the stone is.

When the Long Shadow fades, or when the Ember goes dark, the ring is very
quiet. Then the stone speaks.

> The stone in your hand hums, one long low note, and the voice comes. You
> heard it once before, on the hill above the keep. It is deep and slow,
> like a tired man at the end of a long watch. It does not shout. It
> speaks as if it sits next to you at a campfire.
>
> "Sleeper."
>
> A pause, like a breath.
>
> "You carry a piece of me. I feel it, as you feel your own hand in the
> dark. Wherever you take it, I know where you are. I knew at the keep. I
> knew at the ford. I know now."
>
> Another pause.
>
> "You were always the careful ones. Be careful now. We kept the same watch
> once. It was a long one."
>
> The stone is cold, and quiet. The haze in the sky is gone. The stars are
> very bright.

> **GM tip:** This is one of only three times that Malgrath speaks in Book
> One. Do not add to it. Do not explain it. Every word is true, and the
> players cannot yet understand why. If a player asks Wick what he meant
> by "the same watch", Wick says, too fast: "He means the long night. He
> is the Unlit King. He is a liar. Everybody knows he is a liar." Wick
> believes it.

### Mother Sallow's last word

If Mother Sallow did not flee already, she flees now, with Into the Dark.
Before she goes, she looks at the bearer of the stone, and she smiles, as
if she is proud of a child.

> "You see?" she says. "He knows where you are. He always will. You can
> run anywhere in the land, and he will know, and I will know." Her voice
> is soft and kind. "I will see you again, Sleeper. Keep it warm for him."
>
> Then she steps back into the shadow of a standing stone, and she is not
> there.

If the party searches, they find the tracks of Osk going down the west
side of the hill, very fast, and the marks of a woman's feet next to them.
The trail ends at a stream. Mother Sallow and Osk return in chapter 7.

### What the party finds at Kell

After the fight, the party can search the chapel and the tents.

- **The Ember of Saint Ivo**, in the bowl, gold again (or dark, if the rite
  finished). The bearer of the stone cannot carry it without the burn. Let
  another character carry it. It is warm in the hand. Wick leans toward it
  all the way home.
- **Garret Pell**, if the Hush took him, in a tent at the foot of the hill.
  He is cold, hungry, and very, very grateful. On the way home, he starts
  to write a new play. It is called "The True Sleepers". He asks each
  character what they would like their actor to look like.
- **The Kell Warden's Shield.** Under the altar, in the dust, lies a round
  shield of oak and bronze with a rising sun on its face. The wardens of
  the shrine carried it three hundred years ago. When the Hush are near,
  the sun on the shield glows.
- **The grey ledger.** In the tent of Mother Sallow, under her blanket, is
  a small book bound in grey cloth. Most of it is prayers, in a hand that
  is small and neat. The last page is a list. Give the players the
  handout.

{{handout: 04-the-grey-ledger}}

The grey ledger is the first proof that the Hush hunts the pieces of the
Dawnfire, one by one. It names two more: the lamp of the lantern makers in
Corrantey, and the church in drowned Fennick. These are the hooks of Act
II.

> **GM tip:** Let the players read the list and work it out. The keep: the
> fire in the chapel of the tutorial, out. Kell: dead since the Broken
> Years. Saint Ivo: the Ember, which they just saved. Two more are left.
> Wick says: "If the fire in the chapel kept him asleep, and this one, and
> those ones... how many are left before he wakes?" Nobody knows. That is
> the question for Act II.

### Home to Saint Ivo's

The party walks back to Saint Ivo's in two days. Do not use the Journeys
rules for the way home, unless the players want to. The road is quiet.
The Hush are gone.

> The bells of Saint Ivo's ring for you before you reach the gate. The
> whole town comes out to see the Ember come home. The Abbess comes out
> barefoot, and she takes the Ember in both hands, and she weeps.
>
> Later, when the crowd is gone, Brother Cade sits with you on the steps of
> the cathedral. He does not say anything for a long time. Then: "They will
> come back. For the Ember. For the stone. For you." He looks up at the
> sky, at the sun going down. It is early, for the season. "And it is
> getting dark sooner, is it not? I did not want to see it. I see it now."

### Who keeps the Ember

The Abbess wants the Ember back on the high altar, with a new cage and
twenty guards. The party may want to keep it, to protect it from the Hush.
Both are good choices. Let the players decide, and let it matter.

- **The party gives it back.** The Abbess is deeply grateful. The Church
  remembers this in chapter 5.
- **The party keeps it.** A character can attune to it. The Abbess is hurt,
  and afraid, but she cannot stop the Sleepers. "Then guard it," she says,
  "as we did, for two hundred years." The Church remembers this too.
- **The bearer of the stone** may want to use the Ember to end the Mark,
  from dusk to dawn, in its light. Let the player know that it is possible.
  The full entry of the black stone has the ending. The stone stays whole
  and cold, so the Hush still want it, and the Unlit King still feels it.

> The Dawnwatch woke into a world that forgot them. In a few weeks, they
> learned its songs, saw their own faces painted wrong, and became saints.

## Rewards

The characters can end this chapter with these things.

- **The Kell Warden's Shield**, from the altar at Kell (Scene 7). It is an
  Uncommon magic item.
- **The Ember of Saint Ivo**, if the party keeps it. It is an Artifact, a
  piece of the Dawnfire. If the party gives it back, the Church owes it a
  great debt.
- **The black stone** and its Mark. It is an Artifact. Its bearer carries
  the Curse of the Black Stone.
- **The grey ledger** of the Hush, with the list of the pieces of the
  Dawnfire.
- **Money and gear:** the tents of the Hush hold 60 GP in old coins of
  Ostrey (the Hush pay with them; nobody knows where they find them),
  three Potions of Healing, and twelve brass snuffers. The Abbess gives a
  purse of 250 GP when the Ember comes home, or when the party promises to
  guard it.
- **Friends:** Brother Cade believes the party fully now, whatever the
  Proof was. He is their friend for the rest of the book. Garret Pell owes
  them his life, if they saved him.

### Level up

The characters go to level 5.

They go up at the next Long Rest. Level 5 is the biggest jump in the game:
martial characters get a second attack, and spellcasters get spells of
level 3. Chapter 5 is written for that jump. Give the players time, and
tell them: "Act II starts now. The world will hit harder."
