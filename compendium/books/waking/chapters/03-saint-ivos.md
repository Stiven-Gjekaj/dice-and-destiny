# Saint Ivo's

| | |
|---|---|
| **Levels** | 3 to 4 |
| **Time at the table** | About 2 to 3 sessions (8 to 12 hours) |
| **Places** | The town of Saint Ivo's: the Church of the Five, the old shrine under it, the Relic Market, the Players' Yard, the Abbey |
| **System that starts** | None. This chapter is social. |
| **Thread** | The Light: a sign from a god |
| **People** | Abbess Merrow, who wants the party as living saints; Brother Cade, who thinks that they are impostors; Garret Pell, the leader of a troupe of actors who say that they are the Dawnwatch |

The Dawnwatch arrive at the feast of their own church. The town prays to
them, sells their bones, paints their faces wrong, and sings their songs.
And this year, a troupe of actors stands in front of the cathedral and
says: "We are the Five Sleepers. We are awake."

The party must prove who they are. On the last night of the feast, the
Church carries out its holiest relic, the Ember of Saint Ivo, a coal that
never goes cold. It glows near the true Sleepers. When it glows for the
party, they become the most famous people in the land, and everybody who
wants to use them starts to make plans.

This chapter has two jobs:

1. **Meet the Church, and the people who want to use the Dawnwatch.** The
   Abbess wants living saints. Brother Cade wants the truth. Garret Pell
   wants an audience. The Hush, in the shadows, wants something else.
2. **Bring in the Ember of Saint Ivo**, the first piece of the Dawnfire
   that the party finds. Chapter 4 starts when the Hush steals it.

## Running This Chapter

### What happens

The feast lasts three days. The scenes follow the days, but the party can
do most of them in any order.

| Day | What happens in the town | Scenes |
|---|---|---|
| The day of arrival | The feast opens. The relics go in procession. The party sees the troupe claim to be the Sleepers. | 1, 2 |
| The second day | The market, the play, the Abbess, Brother Cade. The party looks for proof. | 3, 4, 5, 6 |
| The second night | The Hush puts out the lights in the Players' Yard. | 7 |
| The third day | The proof is weighed. Perhaps a trial at the gate. | 8 |
| The third night | The Waking Service. The Ember glows. | 9 |

### Proof

The heart of this chapter is a simple tracker: **Proof**. It counts how
many good reasons the Church has to believe the party. Keep it on a piece
of paper that the players can see.

> **Teach: a tracker for a goal.** "You want the Church to believe you.
> Each good proof that you find goes on this paper. When the paper has
> enough, the Abbess lets you near the Ember." This is not a rule of the
> Compendiums. It is a tool for this chapter. It shows the players that a
> social goal has steps, as a fight has turns.

| Proof | Where the party can get it | Scene |
|---|---|---|
| Corvin Lisle's book, and the thing that the Shadow stole | Corvin is at the feast. He tells the Abbess what his book says. | 3 |
| The questions of Brother Cade | The party answers his questions about old Ostrey better than the archives do. | 4 |
| The window of the Light | The sign of the Light's god is in a window, made two hundred years later. | 5 |
| The guest book of the old shrine | The names of the Dawnwatch, in their own hands, in the old shrine under the cathedral. | 6 |
| The old guards answer the old greeting | The guards of Ostrey under the cathedral know the party. Brother Cade sees it. | 6 |
| Garret Pell confesses | The party saves Pell from the dark, and he tells the truth. | 7 |
| Wick | Wick speaks to the Abbess, and tells her things that only a lamp of Dawnwatch Keep knows. | 3 |

- **3 Proof:** the Abbess lets the party stand next to the Ember at the
  Waking Service (Scene 9).
- **5 Proof:** Brother Cade believes the party too. He stands with them in
  chapter 4.
- **Fewer than 3 Proof** on the third day: the Abbess offers the old trial
  at the gate (Scene 8). If the party holds the gate, it gets 1 Proof and
  stands at the service, with Cade watching.

> **GM tip:** Do not make the party find all seven. There is more proof
> than the party needs. A group that loves to talk finds it in Scenes 3
> and 4. A group that loves dungeons finds it in Scene 6. A group that
> loves fights finds it in Scene 7. Let each group take its own way.

### The town of Saint Ivo's

Saint Ivo's is a town of about four thousand people inside a wall of yellow
stone. During the feast, it holds three times that many. Three hundred years
ago, it was the shrine of Saint Ivo, a healer of the Morning Flame: a chapel, a
well, and a guesthouse in a green valley. The Dawnwatch stopped here many
times. Now the shrine is under the floor of the cathedral, and the town
grew around it.

| Place | What it is |
|---|---|
| **The Church of the Five** | The cathedral: a church as big as a castle, built over the old shrine. It has five towers, and the tallest has a gold sun on top. Inside, the five Sleepers lie in painted stone, with the wrong faces. The Ember of Saint Ivo lives on the high altar, in a cage of gold. |
| **The old shrine** | Under the cathedral. The old chapel, the well of Saint Ivo, and the bones of the saints of the Church. Most of it is closed. |
| **The Abbey** | The house of the Abbess and the priests, next to the cathedral. Brother Cade has a small room full of books at the top of it. |
| **The Relic Market** | A square of stalls that sell holy things: candles, badges, little sleeping figures of wood, and bones. Dace has a stall here. |
| **The Players' Yard** | The big yard of the inn called the Lamb and Lantern. Pell's Players perform here each night of the feast. |
| **The Pilgrims' Hall** | Where the old guesthouse stood. Pilgrims sleep on straw for free. The party can sleep here, or pay 5 SP each night for a room at the Lamb and Lantern. |

### The people

- **Abbess Merrow** is the head of the Church. She is about fifty, tall,
  with a soft voice and a hard mind. She believes in the Sleepers. She
  also knows that Gaunt grows strong, that the Church has no army, and
  that a living saint is worth ten thousand soldiers. She wants the party
  as living saints: on her balcony, blessing pilgrims, under her
  protection. She is not evil. She is a politician who prays.
- **Brother Cade** is a priest and the keeper of the archives. He is about
  forty, broad, tired, and honest to a fault. He has exposed a hundred
  false saints and false relics, and he thinks that the party are number
  one hundred and one. He believes in the true Sleepers too much to let a
  fake one pass. He becomes an ally, but not easily.
- **Garret Pell** is the leader of Pell's Players, a troupe of six actors.
  He is about forty, handsome, loud, and good at his job. He plays the
  Shield. This year, he told the town that his players are the Five
  Sleepers, awake at last. He is not a bad man. He thought that it was a
  good show. It got out of hand.

### Why now: the Ember glows

A week ago, on the night the Dawnwatch woke, the Ember of Saint Ivo
flared. For two hundred years it was a coal that glowed a dull red. That
night, it burned gold, and it has not stopped. The Church calls it a
miracle: "The Sleepers wake!"

Garret Pell heard the news on the road and saw his chance. He arrived on
the first day of the feast and told the crowd that his troupe was the
Five, awake, come to their church. Half the town believes him. The Abbess
does not, but she has not said so. She waits to see what is useful.

The Ember flared because the Dawnfire in the chapel of the keep went out,
and because the Dawnwatch woke. Nobody in Saint Ivo's knows this. Do not
explain it.

## Scene 1: The Feast of the Sleepers (30 minutes)

**Goal:** Show the town, the feast, and the faith. Let the players feel
what their names mean to people.

> The gate of Saint Ivo's is open, and the road goes in like a river into
> the sea. Pilgrims push and sing. Children sit on shoulders. Stalls sell
> hot bread, little wooden Sleepers, and candles as tall as a man. Every
> house has five lamps in the window. Every door has five white ribbons.
>
> Over the noise, the bells of the cathedral ring and ring. Down the main
> street comes a procession: priests in grey and blue, boys with incense,
> and on the shoulders of eight strong men, a gold cage on a gold pole.
> In the cage is a coal as big as a walnut. It burns like a little sun.
>
> The crowd goes down on its knees as it passes. "The Ember," says a woman
> next to you, with tears on her face. "Look how it shines. They are
> waking. They are waking."

The party can kneel or not. If a character is within 30 feet of the
Ember as it passes, the Ember flares, brighter for a moment. Most people
think that it is the wind. Wick's flame leans toward the Ember, like a
plant toward the sun.

> **GM tip:** Wick does not know why he leans. "It is warm," he says. "It
> is the warmest thing I ever saw. Can we follow it? Just a little?" This
> is the first piece of the Dawnfire that the party sees. Wick reacts to
> each one. Do not explain it.

### The faith of the town

Show these things during the day. Choose two or three.

- **The statues.** Inside the gate stand five statues of the Sleepers, as
  tall as two men. They are young, beautiful, and calm. One is a woman
  with a sword. Another is a scholar with a book and a halo of stars. A
  character who looks at them finds one detail that is right: the Storm's
  statue has the right tattoo, or the Shadow's has the right boots. The
  rest is wrong.
- **The prayer.** Pilgrims touch the feet of the statues and pray: "Sleep
  well, and wake for us." The feet of the statues are worn smooth.
- **The children.** A choir of children in white sings "The Five at the
  Gate". They sing the chorus with "seven".
- **The name day.** A family with a new baby calls her after one of the
  Sleepers: the name of a character. "For luck," they say.

> **GM tip:** A character whose name is spoken in a prayer, given to a
> baby, or carved on a statue may feel strange: proud, sad, or angry. Ask
> the player: "Your name is on this baby. How does your character feel?"
> There is no wrong answer.

### Gaunt at the feast

Late on the first day, a column of soldiers rides through the gate: twenty
riders in red coats, with black gauntlets on their shields. The crowd goes
quiet. At their head rides a young officer with a cold face: **Captain
Joss Marrick** of Gaunt. He leads the honor guard that the Marshal sent to
the feast.

The honor guard camps outside the Abbey. It is polite. It is also twenty
armed soldiers of a foreign state in the middle of the holy town. Everyone
watches them. The captain watches the party. If the party has the sealed
letter from the dead horse (chapter 2, encounter 7 of the old road), they
can give it to him or to the Abbess. Either one is grateful.

> **GM tip:** Captain Marrick is the seed of chapter 5. He reports to the
> Marshal. In this chapter, he does nothing but watch, ask polite
> questions, and write letters. At the end of the chapter, he makes the
> party an offer.

## Scene 2: Pell's Players (40 minutes)

**Goal:** Meet the troupe that says that it is the Dawnwatch. Make it
funny. Make it a problem.

On the first night, the Players' Yard is packed. A wooden stage stands at
one end, under a painted cloth of the night sky. A sign over the stage
says: THE FIVE SLEEPERS, AS THEY TRULY WERE. AND AS THEY TRULY ARE.

Give the players the playbill.

{{handout: 03-pells-players}}

> A drum rolls. Five figures walk onto the stage in silver armor and white
> cloaks. The crowd roars. The tallest of them, a handsome man with a gold
> beard, lifts his sword to the sky.
>
> "People of Saint Ivo's!" he cries. "Three hundred years we slept beneath
> the holy hill. Three hundred years we dreamed of you. And now, as the
> Ember blazes, we have come home!"
>
> The crowd goes mad. Flowers fly onto the stage. An old woman faints.
>
> Next to you, Wick whispers: "Who is that? Why is he so shiny? None of you
> were ever that shiny."

### The play

After the speech, the troupe performs "The Five at the Gate", the battle
of Ashby Field. Describe it in short pieces, and let the players react.
Each piece has a mistake.

- **The Shield** (Garret Pell) holds the gate alone against a hundred
  hounds, made of men in fur. He never takes a wound. He gives a speech of
  ten minutes in the middle of the fight.
- **The Light** is a beautiful girl with a harp. She heals people by
  singing.
- **The Spark** throws fireworks. Real fireworks. A man in the front row
  loses his hat.
- **The Shadow** steals the crown of the Unlit King and gives it to the
  poor.
- **The Storm** is a big man in a bear skin who roars. He has two lines.
  Both are "Rrraaagh!"
- **The Unlit King** is a man on stilts with a black crown and a cloak of
  black feathers. The children throw cabbages at him. He loves it.

> **GM tip:** Let each player watch their own character on the stage. Ask:
> "The actor who plays you does this. What do you think of it?" This is
> the funniest moment of the chapter. Then, when the laughter is over, let
> one true moment land: at the end, the actors lie down in five coffins of
> painted wood, and a boy sings the last verse of the song alone, very
> well. The yard goes quiet. Some people cry. Wick cries, too, in his way:
> his flame goes small and blue.

### Backstage

After the play, the party can go behind the stage. Pell's Players are
taking off their armor, which is made of painted leather and tin. They are
tired, happy, and a little drunk.

Garret Pell is friendly and very charming. He signs a playbill for anyone
who wants one. If the party says that they are the real Dawnwatch, Pell
laughs. Then he stops laughing, because he is a good actor, and he can see
when other people are not acting.

| What the party does | Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asks Pell why he says that he is a Sleeper | Wisdom (Insight) | 12 | Pell is scared. He did not expect the Abbess to take him seriously. Now he cannot stop, or the crowd will tear him apart. |
| Tells Pell the truth and asks him to stop | Charisma (Persuasion) | 15 | Pell believes them, in private. He says that he cannot stop in public. "If I say I lied, on the feast of the Sleepers, they will hang me from the tower." He asks for time. |
| Threatens Pell | Charisma (Intimidation) | 12 | Pell is scared, but the five other actors stand with him. The yard's two bouncers come over. Pell says nothing useful. |
| Talks to the other actors | Charisma (Persuasion) | 10 | The actor who plays the Shadow, a young woman called Tilly, says that Pell got a letter before the feast. It said that the Sleepers would wake this week, and that he should "be ready". It was not signed. The paper was grey. |
| Shows the badge of the Dawnwatch | None | None | Pell knows it. The real badge has seven rays. His costumes have five, "because there are five of them". He goes pale. |

The grey letter is from the Hush. The Hush wanted to know if the Sleepers
had woken, and where they were. Pell is bait: the Hush watches the
famous "Sleepers" to see who comes to fight them, or to join them. The
party will see the Hush in Scene 7.

### What Pell wants

Pell wants to get out of this alive, and famous. If the party offers him a
way out (a confession that makes him look good, a new play, a part in
their story), he takes it. If the party saves him in Scene 7, he confesses
in front of the whole town, and that is 1 Proof.

## Scene 3: The Abbess (40 minutes)

**Goal:** Meet the Church. Let the Abbess make her offer. Start the proof.

On the second morning, a young priest finds the party. "The Abbess asks
for the pleasure of your company," he says. "She heard about the song at
the Ferry Bell. And about the dead at the ford. And about a lamp that
talks."

The Abbess received news from the road. Corvin Lisle, the priests on the
road, and Dace (who talks to everybody) all told her stories. She wants to
see the party before anyone else does.

> The Abbess's room is high and white, with one window that looks down on
> the square. A fire burns. On the wall hangs a painting of the Five
> Sleepers, asleep. Under it, a tall woman in grey and blue writes a
> letter. She finishes the sentence before she looks up.
>
> "So you are the second group of Sleepers to wake this week," says Abbess
> Merrow. "It is a busy week for miracles. Please, sit. Tell me why I
> should believe you, and not the man with the gold beard."

### What the Abbess wants

Play the Abbess as kind, calm, and always three steps ahead. She does not
say what she thinks. She asks questions and listens. Use these points.

- **She wants it to be true.** She prayed for the Sleepers all her life.
- **She needs it to be true.** Gaunt grows. Its honor guard sits outside
  her door. The Marshal calls Gaunt "the heir of Ostrey". If the Sleepers
  stand on the balcony of the cathedral, nobody can say that Ostrey's heir
  is anyone but the Church.
- **She cannot afford a mistake.** If she names a false saint, Gaunt
  laughs, and the faithful stop listening to her.

So she asks for proof. She explains the Waking Service: on the last night
of the feast, she carries the Ember out to the people. "It has burned gold
for a week. If you are the Sleepers, it will know you. I will let you stand
beside it, if you give me reason enough to risk it. Bring me proof. Three
good reasons, by the third day."

> "And if I believe you," says the Abbess, "you will never want for
> anything again. A house. A guard. The love of every soul in this land.
> You will stand on my balcony at dawn, and the pilgrims will weep. Is
> that not what the Sleepers were for?"

A character who makes a DC 14 Wisdom (Insight) check understands the
offer: the Abbess wants the party as her saints, in her house, under her
rules. She will be a kind keeper. She will also be a keeper.

### Proof in this scene

Two pieces of proof are possible here.

**Wick.** If Wick talks to the Abbess, she is fascinated. Wick can tell her
things about the keep, the steward, and the old shrine. A character must
help Wick to stay on the point: a DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion) check. On a
success, Wick tells her a detail that she can check in the archives: the
number of steps in the old chapel of Saint Ivo (thirty-one), or the name of
the guest master of the shrine in the year of the sleep (Brother Alys,
who had one leg and kept bees). Wick never saw the shrine. He says so:
"The steward told me. The steward told me everything. He had a lot of
long nights." The Abbess sends for Brother Cade to check it. **1 Proof** when Cade finds it in the archives, in Scene 4.

**Corvin Lisle.** Corvin is at the feast to buy relics. If the party asks
him, he comes to the Abbess with his book. He shows her the page of the
thing that the Shadow stole, "taken by a thief of the Dawnwatch on the
night of the sleep". If the party still has the thing, or if Corvin has
it, the Abbess is impressed. **1 Proof.** If the party has no stolen thing,
Corvin can still confirm that their coins, badges, and gear are of the
last year of Ostrey, "and not a copy, my lady, I would stake my name on
it". Then the Proof needs a DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion) check from a
character to make Corvin speak with full confidence.

> **Thread (the Shadow), continued:** If the Shadow kept the stolen thing
> in chapter 2, Corvin asks again here, with a better offer: 600 GP, and a
> place in his collection with a card that says "Returned by the thief,
> after three hundred years". The player chooses again.

## Scene 4: Brother Cade (40 minutes)

**Goal:** Meet the man who does not believe. Let the party answer hard
questions. Give the party a job that leads under the cathedral.

Brother Cade finds the party, or the Abbess sends them to him. His room is
at the top of the Abbey: a small room with one window, full of books,
papers, and boxes of false relics with labels in his neat hand: "Finger of
the Light (goat)". "Tear of the Spark (glass)". "Shadow's lockpick (nail,
from the cart of Dace, the third one this year)".

> A broad man in a grey robe stands up from his desk. He has ink on his
> fingers and a mace on his belt, and he looks very tired.
>
> "Brother Cade," he says. "Keeper of the archives. My job is to find out
> what is true." He looks at each of you for a long moment. "You are the
> hundred and first. Last year, a man in Corrantey said he was the Storm.
> He was a very large baker. Sit down. I have questions."

{{statblock: Brother Cade}}

### The questions

Cade asks the party about old Ostrey. He has the archives of the Church:
two hundred years of notes, stories, and copies of copies. Many of them are
wrong. When the party gives the true answer, it often does not match the
archives. That is the point.

Ask three or four of these questions. Each one is a short exchange. The
party knows the true answers. Let the players answer in character.

| Cade asks | What the archives say | The truth |
|---|---|---|
| "How many of the Dawnwatch were there?" | Seven, as in the song. | Forty at the start of the war. Five at the end. |
| "What were the colors of the Morning Flame?" | White and gold. | Red and gold. |
| "Where did the Five sleep?" | Under the holy hill of Saint Ivo's. | Under Dawnwatch Keep, in the Wolds. |
| "What did the soldiers of the king say at the gate?" | "The Sleepers keep you." | "Light to you." "And dawn to you." |
| "Who was the guest master of the shrine?" | No name. | Brother Alys. One leg, and he kept bees. |
| "What was carved over the door of the Hall of Sleepers?" | "Here sleep the Holy Five." | "We sleep so that the dawn can wake." |

> **GM tip:** Cade is very good at his job. When the party answers against
> the archives, he does not say "you are wrong". He writes the answer down,
> and he goes very quiet. A character who makes a DC 12 Wisdom (Insight)
> check sees that each true answer worries him more than a lie would. A
> liar learns the song. These people argue with the song.

After the questions, Cade goes to the oldest shelf in his room. He takes
down a book with a cracked spine: the diary of a pilgrim from the year
after the sleep, the oldest thing in the archives. He checks two of the
party's answers in it. They match. He does not say anything for a long
time.

**1 Proof** if the party answers at least three questions with the truth.
If Wick told the Abbess a detail in Scene 3, Cade checks it here too, and
it matches.

### Zone of Truth

If the party asks Cade to test them with magic, or if he decides to, he
casts *Zone of Truth* in his room. The party can tell the truth inside it.
Cade is not convinced.

> "The spell tells me that you believe what you say," says Cade. "It does
> not tell me that it is true. I once put a man in this zone who believed
> he was a teapot. He was very sincere."

The spell gives no Proof by itself. It does make Cade respect the party a
little: they did not refuse the test. Pell refused it, twice.

### Cade's job

At the end of the scene, Cade gives the party a job. He does not trust
them, but he thinks that they might be useful.

> "Someone is selling real bones at the feast," says Cade. "Human bones.
> Old ones. Not from the hill in the north. From under my feet." He
> points at the floor. "Our ossuary is under the cathedral, around the old
> shrine. The deep part is older than the Church: an old crypt of Ostrey.
> We found it, and we sealed it. Something is going in and out of it. I want to know what. And I want it stopped."
>
> He looks at you. "If you are the Sleepers, the old shrine was your
> shrine. You know the way. Show me."

Cade has the keys to the old shrine. He comes with the party (Scene 6).
If the party met Dace in chapter 2, they already know who sells the bones.
If not, a character who asks in the Relic Market (DC 10 Charisma
(Persuasion)) learns that Dace has a stall there with "the best bones on
the road".

## Scene 5: The Window (20 minutes)

**Goal:** The thread of the Light. A sign from three hundred years ago,
in a window made two hundred years after it.

> **Thread (the Light):** In the tutorial, the player of the Light said what
> sign the character's god sent when the crystal closed. In the Church of
> the Five, that sign is in a window of colored glass, made two
> hundred years after the sleep.

Tell the player before the session: "Your sign comes back this week."

This scene happens any time the party visits the cathedral. The Light
sees the window first, or another character points it out.

> The cathedral is full of light and the smell of wax. High on the north
> wall, among windows of the Five Sleepers and saints with sad eyes, one
> window is different. It has no people in it. It shows only one thing,
> large and bright in red and gold glass.
>
> It is the sign that you saw when the crystal closed over your face. The
> exact sign. Every detail.

Use the words of the player. If the Light saw "a white bird that flew into
the sun", the window shows a white bird in front of a gold sun, with its
wings exactly as the Light remembers. If the sign was a sound or a smell,
the window shows its source: a bell, or a field of the flower.

### What the window is

A small brass plate under the window says:

> THE WINDOW OF THE SIGN. MADE IN THE YEAR 196 BY MASTER ULRIC GLASS, WHO
> SAW THIS IN A DREAM ON THE LONGEST NIGHT, AND WAS TOLD: "MAKE THIS, FOR
> ONE WHO SLEEPS."

Nobody in the cathedral knows what the window means. The priests call it
"the Mystery Window". Pilgrims pray to it for dreams. A character who asks
a priest (or Cade) learns that Master Ulric made only this window, and
that he died the next winter. He wrote one more line in his notes: "It was
a promise."

> **GM tip:** Do not explain the window. The god of the Light sent a sign
> to the character three hundred years ago, and a sign to a glassmaker two
> hundred years later, so that the sign would be waiting. Why? That is a
> question for the Light's player to carry. If the player wants an answer,
> give one sentence and no more: the character feels, very strongly, that
> the god has not forgotten.

### The choice

The player of the Light chooses what to do with it.

- **Tell the Church.** The Light can tell a priest, the Abbess, or Cade
  that the window shows the sign. If the Light describes a detail that is
  hidden from the floor (Cade climbs a ladder to check), it is **1
  Proof**. The Abbess is delighted. From now on, the pilgrims pray to the
  window as "the Window of the Light", and they stare at the character.
- **Keep it private.** The Light says nothing. The character can come
  back to the window alone, at night, and pray. Give the character Heroic
  Inspiration. The window stays a mystery for everybody else.

## Scene 6: Under the Cathedral (1 to 1.5 hours)

**Goal:** A short dungeon under the town. The first fight of the chapter.
Two pieces of proof. A first look at the Ember up close.

Cade takes the party down a stair behind the high altar. He carries a lamp
and his mace, and he says little. If the party sent Dace to Saint Ivo's in
chapter 2, Cade has questions about that too.

### The map of the old shrine

The old shrine of Saint Ivo is under the floor of the cathedral. The
Church built the cathedral over it. Around the shrine lies an old crypt of
Ostrey. The Church found the crypt and made its upper galleries into an
ossuary, to keep the bones of the faithful near the saint. It has four
parts.

| Area | What it is |
|---|---|
| **1. The bone galleries** | Long passages with walls made of skulls and bones, stacked neatly. Candles in niches. The Church still uses this part. |
| **2. The deep ossuary** | The deep galleries of the old crypt of Ostrey. People hid here in the Grey Winter. The Church found them later and sealed them with an iron gate. Dace found a way in. So did something else. |
| **3. The well of Saint Ivo** | A round room around the old well. The well goes down to water, 60 feet below. |
| **4. The old chapel** | The chapel of the shrine, as it was three hundred years ago. Closed with a door of stone. Two guards of Ostrey stand at the door. |

### 1. The bone galleries

> The stair goes down into a long passage. Its walls are made of bones:
> thigh bones stacked like firewood, and rows of skulls between them,
> looking out. Candles burn in some of the skulls. The air is cold and
> dry, and smells of wax.
>
> Cade lifts his lamp. "Forty thousand of the faithful," he says. "They
> wanted to sleep near the Sleepers." He glances at you. "You will
> understand, I am sure."

At the end of the galleries is the iron gate of the deep ossuary. It is
locked, and Cade has the key. Next to it, low in the wall, a gap where
bones were pulled out and put back badly. A character who makes a DC 12
Wisdom (Perception) check sees it. Fresh footprints go in and out of it. A
character who makes a DC 12 Wisdom (Survival) check knows that one set of
prints is from a man in good boots (Dace), and the other prints are bare
feet, with long nails.

### 2. The deep ossuary

> Behind the gate, the bones are not stacked. They lie in heaps. The
> skulls have no candles. Some of the bones have marks on them, small and
> neat, like the marks of teeth.
>
> Cade stops. "The Grey Winter," he says, very low. "Three years with no
> summer. The town hid down here from the cold. Not all of them came up."

**The truth:** In the Grey Winter, the starving people of the shrine hid in
the old crypt. Some of them ate the dead. They became ghouls. Years later,
the Church found the crypt, saw what was in it, sealed it with an iron
gate, and forgot them. They sleep in the dark, and they
wake when something warm comes near. Dace came near.

> **GM tip:** This scene is dark in tone. Keep it short and spooky, not
> gory. The ghouls were people, a long time ago. Cade says a prayer for
> them before the fight, if there is time.

### The fight in the ossuary

{{statblock: Ghoul}}

In the largest gallery, the party finds Dace, up to his knees in bones,
with a sack and a candle. He is not alone. Behind him, in the dark, grey
shapes move.

> Dace looks up. He sees you. He opens his mouth to say something clever.
> Then he sees what stands behind him, and he says nothing at all.
>
> Grey shapes come out of the heaps of bones. They were people once. They
> are thin, and naked, and their mouths are wrong. The biggest of them
> wears the rags of a priest's robe. It smells like a grave that was opened
> on a hot day.

The leader is a **Ghast**. Long ago, it was the cook of the old shrine.
It still wears the rags of a robe. With it are **Ghouls**.

- **The map:** a long gallery 50 feet by 20 feet, with heaps of bones.
  The heaps are Difficult Terrain. A creature behind a heap has Half

- **The light:** the gallery is dark. Cade's lamp and Wick give light.
  The ghouls stay out of Bright Light in the first round, and come in from
  the sides.
- **Dace** is a **Commoner**. He hides behind the party. On his first

- **The paralysis.** A Ghoul's claws can give the Paralyzed condition. If

  bones. Remind the players that the condition ends at the end of the
  character's next turn.

**After the fight:** Dace confesses everything, again. He found the gap in
the wall last year, when he was looking for a quiet place to sell bones to
a priest. He has taken a sack of bones each feast since then. Cade takes
his sack and his name. "You will sweep the bone galleries," says Cade,
"every day, for a year. You will learn their names."

### 3. The well of Saint Ivo

> At the center of the old shrine is a round room with a low stone well.
> The rope is long gone. The stones of the well are worn smooth by the
> hands of hundreds of years of pilgrims. A cool breath of air comes up
> from below, with a smell of clean water.

The Dawnwatch drank from this well. A character who makes a DC 10 Wisdom
check remembers it: the water was cold and sweet, and the guest master
gave every soldier a cup and a blessing. A character who drinks from it now
regains 1d4 Hit Points. It is not magic. It is good water, and a good
memory.

A character who looks down the well, with light, sees something on a
ledge 20 feet down: a small brass snuffer on a long pole, like the ones
priests use to put out candles. It is new. It does not belong to the
Church. A character who climbs down (DC 12 Strength (Athletics) check) can
bring it up.

> **GM tip:** The snuffer belongs to the Hush. A snuffer of the Hush came
> down here last week, to look for the way to the Ember from below, and
> dropped it. Nobody in this chapter can say whose it is. Cade has never
> seen one like it. If the bearer of the black stone holds the snuffer,
> the stone hums. Keep the snuffer. Chapter 4 explains it.

### 4. The old chapel

The door of the old chapel is a slab of stone with the sun of Ostrey on it.
Two figures stand on each side of the door, in full plate of Ostrey, with
the sun on their breastplates and halberds in their hands. They have stood
here for three hundred years. Nobody is inside the armor.

> Cade stops at a safe distance. "The guards of the shrine," he says. "They
> were here before the Church. The Church took the Ember out of the
> chapel about a hundred and fifty years ago and sealed the door. Nobody
> has opened it since. When anyone comes near, they speak in the old tongue, and when
> nobody understands, they push them back. We gave up asking."
>
> As you come near, the two helmets turn, together, with a sound of old
> iron. A voice comes out of both of them, in the speech of your own time.
>
> "Light to you."

Every character knows the answer: "And dawn to you." It is the greeting of
Ostrey. If a character gives it, the guards lift their halberds, step
aside, and kneel. The door of the chapel opens by itself.

> Behind you, Cade's lamp shakes. He looks at the kneeling armor, and then
> at you. He opens his mouth, and closes it. Then he says, very quietly:
> "And dawn to you." He has read the words a hundred times. He has never
> heard them answered.

**1 Proof**, because Cade saw it.

If the party attacks the guards, or answers wrongly, the guards fight. They
are two **Animated Armor**. They stop at once if a character gives the
answer.

{{statblock: Animated Armor}}

> **Scaling:** If the guards fight, it is a Low fight for level 3 (150 XP
> for each character). Two Animated Armor (400 XP) fit 3 or 4 characters.
> For 5 or 6 characters, a third guard steps out of a niche (600 XP).

### Inside the old chapel

> The chapel is small and plain, and nothing in it has moved in a hundred
> and fifty years. Four benches. A bowl of stone on a low altar, black inside
> with old soot. A shelf of books. On the wall, a faded painting of the
> Morning Flame, in red and gold.
>
> It is exactly as you remember it. Except for the dust. Except for
> everything.

- **The bowl on the altar** is where the Ember of Saint Ivo burned until
  about year 150. Then the Church moved it upstairs, into the cathedral,
  and sealed the chapel. A character who makes a DC 12 Intelligence (Religion) check
  understands: the shrine kept a piece of a holy fire here, given by the
  king after the war. The Dawnwatch remember this bowl with an ordinary
  fire in it, the fire of the evening prayer. The coal came later, while
  they slept.
- **The book on the shelf** is the guest book of the shrine. Give the
  players the handout.

{{handout: 03-the-guest-book}}

The last page of the guest book has the names of the Dawnwatch, written in
their own hands on their last visit, the week before the sleep. Ask each
player to write the character's name on the handout, as the character
wrote it then. Brother Alys wrote a line under the names in his own hand.

The guest book is **1 Proof**. It is the best proof in the chapter. Cade
takes it to the Abbess himself.

> **GM tip:** Let the players look at their own names. Ask one of them:
> "You wrote this a week before the sleep. What were you thinking about
> that day?" Then let Cade speak. He stands at the door, with his lamp,
> and he says: "I am sorry. I was wrong. I will say so to the Abbess." He
> does not say it easily. He means it.

If the party now has 5 Proof or more, Cade believes them fully. If not,
he believes that they are not liars, which is not the same thing. He wants
to see the Ember.

## Scene 7: Lights Out (45 minutes)

**Goal:** The second fight of the chapter. The grey folk act. The party
saves Garret Pell, or fails to.

This scene happens on the second night, during the play in the Players'
Yard. Run it after Scene 6 if you can, so that the party has the snuffer
from the well. If the party is not at the play, a child runs to find them:
"Master Pell asks for you. He says please. He says it twice."

> The yard is full again, three hundred people shoulder to shoulder. The
> lamps on the stage burn bright. Garret Pell lifts his sword and starts
> his speech.
>
> Then a lamp at the back of the yard goes out. Then the one next to it.
> Then a whole row, one after the other, quick and quiet, like someone
> walking along the wall and pinching each flame.
>
> The crowd laughs, a little. Part of the show? Then the stage lamps go
> out, all at once, and the yard is black, and somebody screams.
>
> In the dark, one light still burns: the small orange flame of Wick.

The grey folk are here. They are **Hush Snuffers**, led by a **Hush
Shroud**. They want Garret Pell. They believe that he is a Sleeper, and
they want to take him to their leader. They put out every lamp in the
yard. They cannot put out Wick, because Wick is magic. Wick is very proud
of this, and very scared.

> **Scaling:** This is a Moderate fight for level 3 (225 XP for each
> character).
>

### The map

- The yard is 80 feet by 60 feet. The stage is at the north end, 5 feet
  high, 30 feet wide. The inn is on the east side. A back gate is on the
  south side, 70 feet from the stage. A cart with a grey cover waits
  outside the gate.
- **The crowd.** In the first two rounds, the crowd fills the yard. Each
  square of the yard that is not the stage is Difficult Terrain. In round
  3, the crowd has run into the inn and the street, and the yard is clear.
- **The dark.** All the lamps are out. The yard is Darkness, except for
  Wick's light: Bright Light for 30 feet and Dim Light for 30 feet more,
  around the character who carries him. The Snuffers have Advantage on
  Stealth in the dark, and they deal extra Cold damage to targets in Dim
  Light or Darkness.
- **Relight the lamps.** A character next to a lamp can light it as an
  action, with a tinderbox, or with a cantrip such as *Light* or
  *Produce Flame*. A lit lamp gives Bright Light for 15 feet. On its next
  turn, a Snuffer may use its Snuff to put it out again. This is a good
  fight to teach the value of light.

### Running the fight

- **Round 1:** The Shroud casts *Darkness* on the stage. Two Snuffers
  climb up and grab Pell. They tie his hands. The other Snuffers attack
  anyone who comes near the stage, or anyone who carries a light.
- **Round 2:** The two Snuffers carry Pell toward the back gate, at half
  their Speed (15 feet each round). The Shroud uses Shush on a spellcaster.
- **Round 3 and later:** The Snuffers with Pell keep going. When they
  reach the gate, they throw him in the cart and drive away.
- **The goal is Pell, not the party.** The Hush flees when the Shroud
  falls, or when Pell is free. Its members do not fight to the death. A Snuffer
  who is caught says nothing at all.
- **The black stone** hums all through the fight, loud enough for its
  bearer to feel it in the teeth. A Snuffer that sees the stone stops for
  a moment and stares at it. If you want, one Snuffer leaves Pell and tries
  to grab the stone. Chapter 4 makes this the main goal of the Hush.

> **GM tip:** The players do not know who these people are. Do not name
> the Hush in this chapter. If a character asks a captured Snuffer who it
> serves, it says one sentence, softly: "The sun is dying, and only the
> king can save it." Then it says nothing more, ever. On its wrist, inside,
> is a small tattoo: a grey circle. The party learns the name of the Hush
> in chapter 4.

### If Pell is saved

Garret Pell sits on the edge of the stage with a cup of wine in his
shaking hands. He looks at the party for a long time.

> "They came for me," he says, "because they thought I was you." He
> laughs, and it is not his stage laugh. "I wanted to be you. For one
> feast. I think I am cured."

The next morning, Pell stands on the stage in front of a crowd and tells
the truth: that his players are actors, that he lied, and that the real
Sleepers saved him from the dark. He makes it a very good speech. It is
the best performance of his life, and he knows it. **1 Proof.**

Pell gives the party his most precious thing: the **Mask of the Fifth
Sleeper**, the mask that the actor of the Fifth Sleeper wears in the last
scene of the play. "Every pilgrim on the road touched it for luck," says
Pell. "Some of the luck stayed. I think it should go home."

{{treasure: Mask of the Fifth Sleeper}}

### If Pell is taken

The cart drives off into the night with Pell. The town is in an uproar:
somebody stole a Sleeper. The Abbess is furious and frightened. Without
Pell's confession, the party has one less way to get Proof.

Pell is not dead. The Hush takes him to the shrine at Kell, and the party
finds him there in chapter 4. Tilly Ashe, the actress who plays the
Shadow, gives the party the Mask of the Fifth Sleeper. "Bring him back,"
she says. "He is an idiot, but he is our idiot."

## Scene 8: The Trial at the Gate (optional, 30 minutes)

**Goal:** A last way to get Proof, for a party that has less than 3 on the
third day.

Use this scene only if the party has fewer than 3 Proof on the morning of
the third day. If the party has 3 or more, skip it.

> "There is an old rite," says the Abbess. "Older than the Church. When a
> soldier of the old kingdom claimed to be of the King's Watch, the
> captains tested the claim at a gate. Hold the gate of the cathedral
> until the bell has rung five times. If you are the Five at the Gate,
> it should not be hard."

The trial is a contest, not a battle. The party holds the great door of
the cathedral, which is 10 feet wide, against Brother Cade and the guards
of the Abbey. The party wins if at least one character still stands in
the doorway at the end of round 5.

- **Nobody dies.** The guards use clubs, and the party must use the flat
  of their blades. A creature that drops to 0 Hit Points has the
  Unconscious condition and is Stable. Spells that deal damage are allowed, but the Abbess frowns
  at fire.
- **The crowd watches.** Captain Marrick of Gaunt watches too, with great
  interest.

## Scene 9: The Waking Service (30 minutes)

**Goal:** The Ember knows the Dawnwatch. The town knows them too. Every
power in the land starts to make plans.

On the third night of the feast, the whole town stands in the square in
front of the cathedral, with candles. The cathedral doors open. The
Abbess comes out with the Ember in its gold cage, on its gold pole, and
the bells ring.

The party stands where the Proof put it: next to the Abbess, at the front,
or at the back of the crowd.

> The Abbess lifts the Ember high. Ten thousand candles answer it. She
> speaks, and her soft voice carries over the whole square.
>
> "For two hundred years, this light has burned for the Sleepers. For two
> hundred years, we asked it: when? This week, it answered." She turns,
> slowly, with the Ember held out in front of her. She turns toward you.
>
> The Ember flares.
>
> It is not a glow. It is a dawn. Gold light pours out of the little coal
> and over the square, over the faces of ten thousand people, over the
> cathedral and its tallest tower and the sun on top of it. It is warm, like
> the first warm day of spring. For one moment, there is no night at all.
>
> In the hands of a character, Wick's flame turns gold, and leans toward
> the Ember so hard that the lantern tips over.

The square is silent. Then somebody starts to cry, and then everybody
does, and the bells ring and ring.

### What each character feels

Go around the table. Tell each player one thing.

- **The bearer of the black stone** feels the stone go cold. Very cold,
  so cold that it hurts, and it hums, a long low note of anger. When the
  bearer comes within 5 feet of the Ember, the stone burns against the
  skin. Nobody else notices. Chapter 4 gives the stone its rules.
- **The Light** feels warm all through, as at the moment of the sign.
- **The Shield, the Storm, the Spark, and the Shadow** each remember one
  dawn from the war: a dawn after a long night, when they were still
  alive and did not expect to be.
- **Wick** says, very quietly, so that only the party hears: "I know that
  light. I do not know how I know it. I think I was made from something
  like it."

> **GM tip:** Wick's flame reacts to the Dawnfire. Nobody knows why in
> Book One, not even Wick. Book Two tells it. This moment is a hint. Do not
> explain it. Do not let the players dwell on it, either: the crowd pushes
> forward, and the moment is gone.

### Living saints

The Abbess kneels in front of the party, with the Ember in her hands. All
of Saint Ivo's kneels with her. Brother Cade kneels too, if the party has
5 Proof. If not, he stands at the back, with his arms folded, and he looks
at the Ember, not at the party.

> "The Sleepers are awake," says the Abbess. "Wake well, and see the
> morning."

Ten thousand voices answer: "And see the morning."

From now on, the party is famous. Everybody in Saint Ivo's knows their
faces. Pilgrims follow them in the street, ask for blessings, and touch
their cloaks. Children follow them everywhere. The Abbess gives them the
Saints' House, a fine stone house next to the Abbey, with servants, and
asks them to bless the pilgrims from her balcony at dawn tomorrow.

> **GM tip:** Let the players enjoy this, and let them feel the cost. A
> living saint cannot buy a drink in peace. A living saint cannot leave the
> house at night without a crowd. The Abbess means well, and she means to
> keep them. The players will want to leave soon. Chapter 4 gives them a
> reason.

### The offer from Gaunt

Late that night, Captain Joss Marrick of Gaunt comes to the Saints' House.
He bows, very correctly.

> "The Marshal of Gaunt sends her greetings to the heroes of Ostrey," he
> says. "Gaunt is the heir of Ostrey. It honors the soldiers of Ostrey.
> When you are rested from your long sleep, and when the Church can spare
> you, the Marshal asks you to visit her at Stannard. She has much to show
> you." He smiles, a little. "She has an army. The Church has candles."

The captain leaves before anyone can answer. This is the seed of chapter
5. The party does not have to answer now.

### The watcher in the crowd

When the party goes back to the Saints' House, after the service, the
bearer of the black stone feels it hum, sharp and sudden.

> At the edge of the square, where the candles end, a small woman in grey
> stands alone. She is old, with a kind face, like somebody's grandmother.
> She is the only person in the square with no candle. She looks at you,
> and she smiles, warmly, as if she is very happy to see you.
>
> Then she lifts one hand and pinches out a candle that somebody left on a
> step. She walks away into the dark.

This is **Mother Sallow**, the leader of the Hush. She came to see the
Sleepers for herself, and the Ember. She has seen enough. If the party
follows her, she is gone: the street is empty, and the cold of the stone
fades. Chapter 4 starts the next night.
