# Brannock's Ford

| | |
|---|---|
| **Levels** | 2 to 3 |
| **Time at the table** | About 2 sessions (8 hours) |
| **Places** | The sheep track from Dawnwatch Keep, the village of Brannock's Ford, the barrow on Ferry Hill, the old road of Ostrey to Saint Ivo's |
| **System that starts** | Journeys |
| **Thread** | The Shadow: one last thing stolen |
| **People** | Tamsin, the old ferry woman; Dace, the pedlar of bones; Corvin Lisle, an antiquary |

The Dawnwatch walk down from the keep into a world that forgot them. In
the first village, their coins are three hundred years old, their accent
sounds like a church play, and the song about them has every fact wrong.
A pedlar sells their bones to pilgrims. Then the characters take the old
road south, three days to Saint Ivo's, and learn to travel in a land with
no king and no maps that they know.

This chapter has two jobs:

1. **Show the three hundred years.** Every scene in the village has one
   small thing that is wrong, old, or new. Let the players find them. Do
   not explain them all.
2. **Teach Journeys.** The road to Saint Ivo's is the first long trip. It
   teaches the four roles, the weather, the encounter roll, and the supply
   die.

## Running This Chapter

### What happens

1. **The sheep track (Scene 1).** The characters walk down from Dawnwatch
   Keep. The land has changed. They reach a village at dusk.
2. **Brannock's Ford (Scene 2).** The village has never heard of them, and
   it has heard of them all its life. The party meets Tamsin at the ferry,
   pays with old coins, and hears a song about the Dawnwatch at the inn.
3. **The bone market (Scene 3).** In the morning, Dace sells "bones of the
   Five Sleepers" to pilgrims on their way to the feast at Saint Ivo's.
4. **Tamsin's questions (Scene 4).** Tamsin knows more than she says. Her
   family kept the last watch at the keep, two hundred years ago.
5. **The dead at the ford (Scene 5).** Dace took his bones from the barrow
   on Ferry Hill. That night, the barrow wants them back. This is the
   first fight of the chapter.
6. **Into the barrow (Scene 6).** The party can fight the old warden of the
   barrow, or talk to it. It knows the badge of the Dawnwatch.
7. **The collector (Scene 7).** Corvin Lisle, an antiquary from
   Corrantey, sees the thing that the Shadow stole, and he offers a lot of
   money for it. This is the thread of the chapter.
8. **The old road (Scenes 8 to 10).** Three days of travel on the old road
   of Ostrey, with the Journeys rules. The second fight happens on the
   road. At the end of the third day, the party sees Saint Ivo's.

### The tone

Keep it light, as the tutorial was. The characters are the most famous
people in the land, and nobody knows their faces. That is funny. Let it be
funny. Under the jokes, let one or two moments land hard: a grave with a
name that a character knows, or a child who asks if the Sleepers will save
them from the short winters.

### What the people of today know

Everybody in Brannock's Ford knows these things. A character learns them
by asking almost anyone. No roll is needed.

- **Ostrey fell two hundred years ago.** The last king died with no heir in
  the Grey Winter, three years with almost no summer. "Old Ostrey" is a
  phrase for anything very old.
- **The Five Sleepers** are saints. They sleep under a hill "somewhere in
  the north", and they will wake when the Unlit King returns. The Church
  of the Five Sleepers prays for that day.
- **Saint Ivo's** is a town three days south on the old road. The Church
  has its seat there. This week is its big feast, so pilgrims are on the
  road.
- **Gaunt** is a state of soldiers further south. Its lords say that they
  are the true heirs of Ostrey. Everybody else says that they say a lot of
  things.
- **The winters are longer.** The old people say so. The young people say
  that the old people always say so.

> **GM tip:** The characters may tell the village who they are. Let them.
> Nobody believes it. A man who says "I am one of the Five Sleepers" in a
> village on the pilgrim road is a drunk, a liar, or an actor. The people
> laugh, or they ask for a blessing as a joke. Proof comes in chapter 3.

### Today and old Ostrey

The Dawnwatch know old Ostrey. Use these differences in every scene. They
show three hundred years with no explanation.

| Then (what the characters know) | Now (what they find) |
|---|---|
| "Light to you." "And dawn to you." | "Wake well." "And see the morning." The greeting comes from the Sleepers. |
| Gold suns, silver stars, copper bits | Thin new coins from Corrantey and Gaunt. An old sun is an antique. |
| The Morning Flame, priests in red and gold | The Church of the Five Sleepers, priests in grey and blue. The Morning Flame is a saint's day. |
| Kingsholm, the capital | A ruin in the north that nobody visits. |
| The road south was paved and kept by wardens | The road is broken in places, and nobody keeps it. |
| The Dawnwatch, five tired soldiers | The Five Sleepers, five saints with halos and the wrong faces |

## Scene 1: The Sheep Track (20 minutes)

**Goal:** Start the chapter. Show the changed land. Let the characters
talk about what they saw at the Dawn Door.

If you start a new session here, read the end of the tutorial again, or
let the players tell it in their own words. Then:

> The hill below the keep is steep and green. Sheep watch you come down
> with no interest at all. A track goes down between them, cut by a
> thousand small hooves. It goes south, toward the smoke of the village.
>
> Where the road of the keep once ran, paved and wide enough for two
> wagons, there is a line of grass a little greener than the rest. You
> find one stone of it. It has the sun of Ostrey on it, worn almost flat.
>
> Wick is quiet for a long time. Then he says, very small: "They paved
> that road the year I was made. It took them all summer."

The walk takes the rest of the day. It is about 24 miles, on a sheep
track, in the low hills of the Wolds. Do not use the Journeys rules for
this walk. It is the walk to the start of the journey.

### On the way

Choose one or two of these moments, or let the players talk.

- **The standing stones.** A ring of old stones on a hilltop. A character
  who makes a DC 10 Intelligence (History) check remembers it: the
  Dawnwatch camped here on the way to the war. Somebody carved a name in
  the largest stone. It is the name of a person from a character's Before
  answer, or the name of a soldier of the Dawnwatch who died.
- **The shepherd.** A boy of ten with a crook and a dog. He says "Wake
  well" and stares at the armor. If a character says "Light to you", the
  boy laughs: "You talk like the priest at the feast play."
- **The sky.** The sun sets earlier than the characters expect. A
  character who makes a DC 12 Wisdom (Survival) check knows it: for this
  time of year, the day is almost an hour short. Nobody in the village will
  agree.

> **GM tip:** If the players ask Wick about the shape in the cloud at the
> end of the tutorial, Wick does not want to talk about it. "The Unlit King
> is far away in the north," he says. "Very far. Very, very far. Please
> walk faster."

### The black stone

The black stone is cold all day. Each time a character touches it, it hums,
a low note that the character feels in the teeth. It has no rules yet.
Chapter 4 gives them. If a player asks what it does, the honest answer is:
"It hums. You do not know why."

## Scene 2: Brannock's Ford (45 minutes)

**Goal:** Show the three hundred years. Meet Tamsin. Learn what the land
knows about the Dawnwatch.

> At dusk, the track comes down to a river. On the far bank is a village:
> forty houses of grey stone with roofs of reed, a mill, a small church
> with a bell, and an inn with a lamp over the door. The river is wide
> here, and slow. A line of stepping stones crosses it, half under the
> water, and a flat ferry boat waits on a rope.
>
> An old woman sits on the ferry with a pipe. She watches you come down
> the bank. She takes the pipe out of her mouth.
>
> "Well," she says. "That is a lot of armor for a sheep track."

This is **Tamsin**, the ferry woman. She is about seventy, small, brown as
a nut, with very sharp eyes. She is kind, and she asks too many questions.
Play her as everybody's grandmother, and also as a judge.

### The ferry

The river is high after rain, so the stepping stones are under the water.
A character can wade across (DC 10 Strength (Athletics) check; on a
failure, the character falls in and arrives wet and cold), or pay Tamsin.
The ferry costs "a copper each, and a story for the boat."

When a character pays with an old coin, Tamsin looks at it for a long time.

> She turns the coin in the last of the light. It is a gold sun of Ostrey,
> heavy and bright, with the face of a king that you knew.
>
> "Now where," says Tamsin, very softly, "would a person get a coin like
> that? My granddad had one. He kept it in the wall. It was the only gold
> he ever owned."

She does not take it. "That is worth more than my boat. Give me a copper
when you have one, and tell me the story now." She ferries the party
across for a story. If the story is good, she gives the storyteller
Heroic Inspiration, as the Teach box in Scene 1 of the tutorial explains.

### The village

Brannock's Ford is about two hundred people. It is not on the map of old
Ostrey: Brannock, a ferryman, built the first house a hundred and fifty
years ago, after the bridge of Ostrey fell. The characters know the river,
but not the village.

Show these things as the party walks to the inn. Choose two or three.

- **The church** has a painted board over its door: five figures in white
  with gold halos, asleep in a row. Their faces are all the same face, a
  calm young man with yellow hair.
- **The well** has a stone from the bridge of Ostrey in its wall. It has
  the sun of Ostrey on it, upside down.
- **A woman** hangs a bundle of herbs over her door. "For the long nights,"
  she says. "It keeps the dark out." She has never heard of the Hush.
- **Children** play a game called "Sleepers". Five children lie down. One
  child is the Unlit King. He creeps up and taps one of them, who must
  stay asleep. The last one awake wins.

### The inn: the Ferry Bell

> The inn is warm and loud and smells of smoke and beer. A fire burns in a
> big stone hearth. Above it, someone painted the Five Sleepers on the
> plaster, asleep in their crystal coffins. In the painting, the coffins
> are made of gold, and an angel stands over each one with a sword.
>
> The room goes quiet when you come in. Then a farmer laughs. "Look, lads,"
> he says. "The feast play came early this year."

The inn is full of farmers, two boatmen, and a family of pilgrims on their
way south. The innkeeper is a big, red, nervous man named Hob. A room
costs 5 SP for each character, with supper. Hob takes old coins, but a
character who makes a DC 10 Wisdom (Insight) check sees that he is very
happy about it. An old sun is worth five times its weight to the right
buyer, and Hob knows a buyer (Scene 7).

### What the characters learn at the inn

Let the characters talk to the people in the room. Use the list in "What
the people of today know" for the easy answers. For more, use this table.

| What a character does | Check | DC | What the character learns |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buys a round and asks for news | Charisma (Persuasion) | 10 | The feast at Saint Ivo's starts in four days. A troupe of actors is there, and they play the Five Sleepers. "They are very good. The Shield cries real tears." |
| Listens to the room | Wisdom (Perception) | 12 | Two boatmen talk low about "the grey folk" who bought a boat last month and paid in old coins. They went up the river at night with no lamp. |
| Asks about the keep in the north | Charisma (Persuasion) | 12 | Nobody goes there. The sheep go there. "My nan said the Sleepers sleep under that hill. My nan also said a frog was her husband." |
| Asks about Ostrey | Intelligence (History) | 10 | The character hears the story of the fall and understands it: the Grey Winter, the king with no heir, the civil war. The character knows that the order of events is right, but the people in it are wrong. |
| Asks about the shorter days | Wisdom (Insight) | 12 | Everybody jokes about the long winters. Nobody likes the joke. Two farmers lost their harvest last year to an early frost. |
| Tells the truth about who they are | Charisma (Persuasion) | 20 | Nobody believes it, on any roll. On a 20 or more, one pilgrim child believes it, and she stays close to the party all night. Her name is Wenna. She wants to know if the Unlit King is real. |

> **GM tip:** The DCs are low. This scene is not a puzzle. It is a window.
> Give the information freely. Save the rolls for when a player wants to
> be clever.

### The song

When the room is full, a fiddler stands on a bench. The room cheers. He
plays "The Five at the Gate", and everybody sings the chorus.

Give the players the handout.

{{handout: 02-the-five-at-the-gate}}

The song tells of the battle at Ashby Field, when the army of the king
stopped the hounds of the Unlit King in the snow, at dawn. The characters
were there. Almost every fact in the song is wrong:

- The song says that there were seven of them. There were five, at the
  end.
- The song says that they fought the Unlit King himself, and cut off his
  hand. They fought his hounds. The king was not there.
- The song says that the Shield "never felt a wound". Ask the player of
  the Shield where the scar from Ashby Field is.
- The song says that the Shadow "never took a thing that was not given".
  Look at the player of the Shadow.
- The song says that they sleep "beneath the holy hill of Saint Ivo".
  They slept under Dawnwatch Keep.

> **GM tip:** Let each player point out one mistake in character. If a
> character corrects the fiddler out loud, the room boos, and an old man
> shouts: "Were you there, then?" This is the best moment of the scene.
> Let the players enjoy it.

If a character plays an instrument and sings the true version, give that
character Heroic Inspiration. The room goes quiet. Nobody believes it, but
nobody forgets it either.

### Wick at the inn

Wick is a talking lantern, and talking lanterns are not common in this
land. If Wick talks in the inn, the room panics, then laughs, then buys
him a drink that he cannot drink. Somebody offers 20 GP for "the trick
lamp". If the party wants Wick to stay hidden, Wick must make a DC 12
Charisma check to keep quiet during the song. He has a +0 to the roll. He
fails on purpose if the song insults the steward.

## Scene 3: The Bone Market (30 minutes)

**Goal:** Meet Dace. Show how the land uses the Five Sleepers. Start the
mystery of the bones.

In the morning, a cart stands on the green in front of the church. It has
a painted sign: DACE, TRUE RELICS OF THE ROAD. A crowd of pilgrims stands
around it.

> The man on the cart is thin and quick, with a long nose, a long coat,
> and a voice like warm honey. He holds up a small box of glass. Inside is
> a brown bone as long as a finger.
>
> "The smallest finger," he says, "of the left hand of the Shield of the
> Dawnwatch, the bravest of the Five! Taken from the holy hill by a
> pilgrim who saw a light in the dark and dug where it pointed! Touch the
> glass, friends, and feel the courage go into you! Two silver. Two
> silver for courage that lasts a lifetime!"

This is **Dace**, the pedlar of bones. He goes to Saint Ivo's for the
feast every year, and he sells relics on the way. He is a liar and a
thief, and he is very good at both. He is not cruel. He believes that he
sells hope, and that hope is worth two silver.

### Dace's relics

Dace sells these things. A character who makes a DC 10 Intelligence
(Investigation) or Wisdom (Medicine) check knows that each one is a fake.

| Relic | Price | The truth |
|---|---|---|
| The smallest finger of the Shield | 2 SP | A sheep bone. |
| A lock of the hair of the Light | 5 SP | Horse hair, dyed. |
| A page from the spellbook of the Spark | 1 GP | A page from a farm almanac, in old letters. The Spark can read it. It is about turnips. |
| The tooth of the Storm | 3 SP | A real human tooth. Old. See below. |
| The lockpick of the Shadow | 4 SP | A bent nail. |
| A bone of the Unlit King, "taken from the black north at great cost" | 10 GP | A real human thigh bone, very old, with a bronze ring around it. See below. |

The tooth and the thigh bone are real, and very old. A character who
makes a DC 12 Wisdom (Medicine) check knows that they came from a grave.
A character who makes a DC 12 Intelligence (History) check knows the
bronze ring: soldiers of Ostrey put a ring like this on the bones of a
road warden, to keep the warden at the job after death.

### What the players can do

- **Expose him.** A character can call Dace a liar in front of the crowd.
  This is a Charisma (Persuasion) or Charisma (Intimidation) check against
  Dace's Charisma (Deception) of +5. On a success, the crowd turns on Dace,
  and he packs his cart in a hurry. On a failure, the crowd turns on the
  party: "Who are you, to call a good man a liar? Are you a Sleeper,
  then?" The pilgrims laugh, and Dace laughs loudest.
- **Prove it.** A character can show a real thing: the badge of the
  Dawnwatch, a hand with all its fingers. Give Advantage on the check above.
- **Buy something.** Dace is happy to sell. He gives a discount to anyone
  in armor: "for the cause".
- **Talk to him alone.** Dace is easy to talk to. He likes clever people.
  A character who makes a DC 12 Wisdom (Insight) check sees that the
  question "where do the bones come from?" makes him nervous.

### Where the bones come from

Dace digs his real bones out of the old barrow on Ferry Hill, a mile north
of the village. The barrow is the grave of the wardens who kept the road of
Ostrey. Dace dug there last week. He took a tooth, a thigh bone, and three
rings of bronze. He did not take the sword, because it was in the hand of
a body that sat up.

Dace tells this only if a character makes a DC 15 Charisma (Intimidation)
or Charisma (Persuasion) check, or if the dead come to the village (Scene
5). If the party threatens him, he tells the truth at once. He is not
brave.

> **GM tip:** Dace returns in chapter 3. Do not let the party kill him or send him to prison here. If they want justice,
> let him run. He takes the road south, with the pilgrims. He will be in
> Saint Ivo's before them, selling bones at the feast.

## Scene 4: Tamsin's Questions (20 minutes)

**Goal:** Give the party a friend in the new world. Give a clue that the
keep was not always empty.

Tamsin finds the party when they are alone: at the ferry, on the bench in
front of the inn, or at breakfast. She sits down without asking. She
brings bread and cheese.

> "I have a question," says Tamsin. "I have a lot of questions, but I will
> start with one." She points her pipe at the badge on your coat: the
> rising sun, with seven rays. "Where did you get that?"

Tamsin asks one question after another, and each question is a little
sharper. Let the players answer as they want. They can lie. Tamsin knows a
lie when she hears one (her Wisdom (Insight) is +4).

- "Where did you sleep last night?"
- "Why do you talk like the priest at the feast play?"
- "Why does your lamp have a face?"
- "How old are you? No. How old are you really?"

### What Tamsin knows

If the characters tell her the truth, Tamsin believes them. She is the only
person in the village who does. She says so in one sentence and then she
cries, quietly, for about ten seconds. Then she tells them this:

> "My family kept that keep. Not the Dawnwatch, mind. The doors. The roof.
> The well. My great-great-granny was the last of us to do it. When the
> Grey Winter came, the steward's people were all dead, and the soldiers
> were gone, and she kept the door shut for three years on her own. Then
> she came down the hill and married a ferryman. She said the Sleepers were
> under the hill, and that somebody had to remember. So we remembered.
> Nobody believed us. My granddad had a coin like yours in the wall."

Tamsin has two gifts, if the party earns her trust:

- **The coin from the wall.** Her granddad's old gold sun. She gives it
  to the character who told her the truth. "It should go back to its own
  time."
- **A ferry token.** A bone disk carved with a boat. Any ferry on the River
  Brann carries the party for free if they show it. It is not magic. Every
  ferryman on the river knows Tamsin.

> **GM tip:** Tamsin is the first person who believes the Dawnwatch. Make
> her matter. If a player's After answer was "everything is gone", Tamsin
> is the proof that something remained. Let that player see it.

### Tamsin's warning

Before she leaves, Tamsin says one more thing:

> "Grey folk came through last month. Five of them, in grey cloaks, with
> no lamp, at night. They bought my cousin's boat and paid in old coins,
> like yours. They asked if anyone had come down from the north hill. Not
> if anyone would. If anyone had." She looks at you. "Somebody knew you
> were waking before you did."

The grey folk are the Hush. The party meets them in chapter 4. Do not name
them here. If the party asks what the grey folk wanted, Tamsin does not
know. If the bearer of the black stone touches it while Tamsin talks about
them, it hums.

## Scene 5: The Dead at the Ford (40 minutes)

**Goal:** The first fight of the chapter. Show that the past is not quiet.

This scene happens on the second night in Brannock's Ford, or on the first
night if the party wants to leave in the morning. It does not matter if
the party learned where Dace got his bones. The dead come anyway.

> In the middle of the night, a dog barks. Then every dog in the village
> barks. Then they all stop at once.
>
> Fog lies on the river. In the fog, on the far bank, a blue light comes
> down the hill, slow and steady, like a lantern carried by someone who is
> not in a hurry. Then a second light. Then the shapes under them: tall,
> thin shapes in old helmets, that walk into the river and do not stop.
>
> The bell of the church starts to ring.

The dead of the barrow on Ferry Hill want their bones back. They walk
across the ford, through the water, and up into the village. They go to
the place where the bones are: Dace's cart on the green, or the room of a
character who bought the tooth or the thigh bone.

The dead are **Skeletons** in the rusty helmets of Ostrey road wardens. A
**Warhorse Skeleton** carries the warden captain's banner. Two **Barrow
Lights** float over them and give them their blue light.

> **Scaling:** This is a Moderate fight for level 2 (150 XP for each
> character).
>
> - **3 characters (450 XP):** 2 Skeletons, 1 Warhorse Skeleton, 2 Barrow
>   Lights (400 XP).
> - **4 characters (600 XP):** as in the table (500 XP). Add 2 Skeletons
>   if the party is strong (600 XP).
> - **5 characters (750 XP):** 4 Skeletons, 2 Warhorse Skeletons, 3 Barrow
>   Lights (700 XP).
> - **6 characters (900 XP):** 6 Skeletons, 2 Warhorse Skeletons, 4 Barrow
>   Lights (900 XP).

### The map

- The village green is 60 feet across, with the church on one side and
  the inn on the other. Dace's cart stands in the middle.
- The ford is 60 feet north of the green. The dead come from there, two
  rounds after the bell starts.
- Fog covers the green. It is Lightly Obscured. Wick's light pushes the
  fog back: in Wick's Bright Light, the fog has no effect.
- Villagers run out of their houses with lamps and pitchforks. In the first
  round, three villagers stand in the way of the dead.

### Running the fight

- **The dead want the bones.** A skeleton attacks a creature that stands
  between it and the bones. It does not chase a creature that steps aside.
  A creature that carries a stolen bone is always its first target.
- **Give back the bones.** A character can throw a stolen bone or ring to
  the dead as a Utilize action. One skeleton picks it up and walks back to
  the river. It is out of the fight. If the dead get all the stolen things
  back, the rest of them stop, turn, and walk home. A character who
  makes a DC 12 Intelligence (History) or Wisdom (Religion) check knows that
  this will work. Wick knows it too, if a player asks him: "They are road
  wardens. They want their things, and then they want to go back to work."
- **The villagers.** A villager is a **Commoner**. A skeleton hits a
  villager that does not move. Give the players a reason to protect them:
  little Wenna from the inn runs out to see the Sleepers fight.
- **The bell.** The priest of the village rings the church bell for the
  whole fight. A character who makes a DC 12 Intelligence (Religion) check
  knows that the bell does nothing to the dead. It makes the villagers
  feel better.

> **Teach: the fight with a goal.** This is the first fight where the
> enemies want something other than to kill. Say: "Look at what the dead
> do. They walk past people who step aside. What do they want?" A good
> answer can end a fight with no damage at all.

### After the fight

If the party won by force, the bones of the dead lie on the green, and the
village is very grateful and very scared. If the party gave back the
bones, the dead walk back across the river, and the village is very
grateful and even more scared.

Either way, Dace confesses. He stands in his nightshirt on the green, in
front of the whole village, and tells where the bones came from. He says
that a body in the barrow sat up when he touched its sword. "It looked at
me. It had no eyes. It looked at me anyway."

> "Somebody has to go up there," says Tamsin, "and put things right. And
> somebody," she looks at Dace, "has to carry the bones."

## Scene 6: Into the Barrow (45 minutes)

**Goal:** A short dungeon, a fight or a talk, and the unique treasure of
the chapter.

The barrow is on Ferry Hill, a mile north of the village, on the far side
of the river. It is a long, low mound of earth with a door of stone at one
end. Dace comes along if the party makes him. He carries the bones and
complains the whole way.

> The barrow is a long green hill with a mouth of stone. Someone dug a
> hole in the side of it, not long ago, and filled it in badly. The great
> stone door at the end stands open a hand's width. Cold air comes out of
> it, and a smell of earth and old iron.
>
> Over the door is a carving that you know: a road, a sun over the road,
> and words in the old letters. KEEP THE ROAD.

A character who makes a DC 10 Intelligence (History) check knows the
words. They are the motto of the road wardens of Ostrey. The wardens kept
the roads safe, and they were buried by the road, so that they could keep
it after death. The Dawnwatch marched past barrows like this one a hundred
times.

### The rooms of the barrow

The barrow is dark. It has three rooms in a line. The ceiling is low: 7
feet. Characters who are tall must stoop.

**1. The passage.** A stone passage 30 feet long and 5 feet wide. On each
side, in niches, lie the bones of wardens, each with a bronze ring on one
leg. Three niches are empty: Dace's work. A character who makes a DC 12
Wisdom (Perception) check sees the trap before it works.

- **The trap: the warden's gate.** A flagstone in the middle of the
  passage sinks when a creature steps on it. A bronze grille drops behind
  the creature and closes the passage. The grille is not a danger. It
  keeps thieves in until the warden comes. A character can lift it with a
  DC 15 Strength (Athletics) check, or open its lock with Thieves' Tools
  and a DC 13 Dexterity check. The grille opens by itself when the warden
  of the barrow lets the party go.
- A character who puts the stolen bones back in their niches, with the
  rings, makes the barrow quiet. The next room is easier: see "The talk"
  below.

**2. The hall of shields.** A round room 20 feet across. Bronze shields
hang on the walls, green with age. Each shield has the name of a warden
and a road: "Aske, the north road. Tobry, the Wolds road." Under the
shields are grave goods: cups and pins of bronze, beads of amber, and old
coins, worth 40 GP in all.

> **GM tip:** If a character takes the grave goods, the warden in room 3
> knows it at once. Its Grave Goods trait gives it Advantage against that
> character. Let the players decide. Do not warn them, but let Wick say,
> "Are we sure about this? I have a bad feeling about this. I have bad
> feelings about most things, but this one is special."

**3. The tomb of the captain.** A long room 30 feet by 15 feet. At the far
end, a stone chair. In the chair sits a dead warden in bronze armor, with
a sword across its knees and a brass compass on a chain around its neck.

> The dead warden in the chair lifts its head. There is nothing in its
> helmet but dark. A cold voice comes out of the dark, in the old speech
> of Ostrey, the speech of your own time. It is the first time since you
> woke that you hear it.
>
> "Who walks my road?"

This is the warden captain, **Aske**, a **Barrow Thane**. Aske died in the
war of the Unlit King, a few years before the Dawnwatch slept, and it has
kept this road ever since. It is not evil. It is very, very tired, and it
has a job.

### The talk

Aske speaks the old speech of Ostrey. The characters speak it too. It is
the first time since the tutorial that somebody talks to them like a
person of their own time. Let it matter.

If the stolen bones are back in their niches, Aske starts calm. If the
bones are not back, or if a character carries grave goods, Aske starts
angry. Use the table.

| What the party does | Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shows the badge of the Dawnwatch | None | None | Aske knows it. It stands up. "The sun with seven rays. The king's own watch. Is the war over? Is the road safe?" |
| Tells Aske the truth: three hundred years, the king is gone | Charisma (Persuasion) | 12 | Aske believes it. It is quiet for a long time. Then it asks one question: "Who keeps the road now?" |
| Lies to Aske that the road is kept | Charisma (Deception) | 15 | On a success, Aske is at peace and lies down. The compass stays on its neck. On a failure, Aske knows, and it fights. |
| Promises to keep the road | None | None | See "The promise" below. |
| Threatens Aske, or attacks | None | None | Aske fights. See "The fight". |
| Brings Dace forward to say sorry | Charisma (Persuasion), made by Dace with Advantage if a character helps | 10 | Aske looks at Dace for a long time. "Thief. You will carry no more bones." Dace never sells a real bone again. He still sells sheep bones. |

### The promise

If a character promises to keep the road, Aske takes the compass from its
neck and holds it out.

> "Then keep it," says Aske. "This was the compass of my road. It knows
> every road that Ostrey built. Some of them are still there, under the
> grass. When you walk them, think of the wardens. That is all we ever
> wanted. Somebody to think of the road."
>
> The dead captain sits down in its chair. The cold goes out of the room.
> The grille in the passage opens with a rusty sound. Somewhere, far away,
> you think you hear a horn.

Aske gives the party the **Compass of the Old Road**. The Treasures
appendix has its full entry. The compass helps the guide on the journey
to Saint Ivo's, so let the party have it before they leave.

{{treasure: Compass of the Old Road}}

### The fight

If the talk fails, or if the party attacks, Aske stands and draws its
sword. Two **Skeletons** (their statblock is in Scene 5) climb out of the
niches in the walls of the room.

{{statblock: Barrow Thane}}

| Party size | Monsters | XP |
|---|---|---|
| 4 characters | 1 Barrow Thane, 2 Skeletons | 800 |

> **Scaling:** This is a High fight for level 2 (200 XP for each
> character). The Barrow Thane is CR 3, above the party's level, so play
> it with care.
>
> - **3 characters (600 XP):** the Barrow Thane alone (700 XP). It starts
>   with 15 fewer Hit Points, and it doesn't use Oath of the Barrow.
> - **4 characters (800 XP):** as in the table.
> - **5 characters (1,000 XP):** the Barrow Thane, 2 Skeletons, 2 Barrow

> - **6 characters (1,200 XP):** the Barrow Thane, 4 Skeletons, 2 Barrow

- **It can still stop.** At the start of each round, a character can try

## Scene 7: The Collector (20 minutes)

**Goal:** The thread of the Shadow. The past comes back as a treasure, and
the player chooses what to do with it.

> **Thread (the Shadow):** In the tutorial, the player of the Shadow said
> what the character stole on the night before the long sleep. It is still
> in the character's pocket. Today, it is a treasure of old Ostrey, and a
> collector wants it.

Tell the player before the session: "Your stolen thing comes back this
week." The How to Run This Book section explains why.

This scene happens on the morning after the barrow, at the Ferry Bell. A
fine coach with two grey horses stands in the yard. Hob the innkeeper sent
word about the old coins, and a buyer came at once.

> A man sits at the best table in the inn with a cup of tea and a book.
> He is about fifty, round and neat, in a green coat with silver buttons.
> His spectacles are on a ribbon. When you come in, he stands up and
> bows, very low.
>
> "Corvin Lisle, of Corrantey," he says. "I buy old things. I am told that
> you have some. I am told," and his eyes shine, "that you have quite a
> lot."

**Corvin Lisle** is an antiquary. He collects things from old Ostrey, and
he sells some of them to the rich families of Corrantey. He is on his way
to the feast at Saint Ivo's, to buy relics. He is honest, in his way: he
never pays less than a thing is worth to him. He is also a little mad
about old things. He touches an old coin like a man touches a sleeping
cat.

### The coins

Corvin pays 5 GP for each old gold sun. He offers this at once. The party
has 15 suns from the armory in the tutorial, and maybe Tamsin's coin. He
pays 1 GP for an old silver star, and 1 SP for a copper bit.

### The stolen thing

When the Shadow (or any character) shows Corvin the thing that the Shadow
stole, Corvin sits down very suddenly.

> He does not touch it. He opens his book to a page near the back, and he
> turns the book around so that you can read it. It is a list, in neat
> handwriting: THINGS OF OSTREY THAT ARE LOST. Halfway down the page is a
> drawing of the thing in your hand.
>
> "It went missing," says Corvin, "on the night the Dawnwatch went to
> sleep. The story says a thief of the Dawnwatch took it. Everybody says
> that the story is a joke." He looks up at you. "Where did you get it?"

Use the words of the player. If the Shadow stole a silver comb from a
duchess, the book has a drawing of the comb, and the duchess has a page of
her own. The family of the duchess is gone, or it is a family of today
that has forgotten her. Do not change the answer of the player. Change the
book.

Corvin offers 300 GP for the thing, at once. If a character bargains with
him (Charisma (Persuasion) or Charisma (Deception), DC 15), he goes up to
500 GP. He has a letter of credit from a bank in Corrantey, and enough gold
in his coach for half the price.

### The choice

The player of the Shadow chooses. Give the scene time. Each choice is a
good story.

- **Sell it.** The party has money for the journey, a mule, and good gear.
  Corvin is happy. He gives his card: a white card with a lamp on it. "If
  you find more, send word to Corrantey." He goes south to the feast.
- **Keep it.** Corvin is sad, but polite. He asks the party to think about
  it, and he says that he will be at the feast in Saint Ivo's. He will ask
  again, with a better offer.
- **Give it back.** The player may want to return the thing to its owner.
  Corvin can tell the party who the owner's family is today, if any. This
  is a good seed for a side trip later in the book.

> **GM tip:** Corvin's book is useful in chapter 3. The party must prove
> who they are to the Church. A thing that was "stolen by a thief of the
> Dawnwatch" on the night of the sleep, in the hand of a halfling in old
> armor, is very good proof. If the party keeps the thing, Corvin can tell
> the Abbess what it is. If the party sold it, Corvin still has it, and he
> still has the book.

If no character has a stolen thing, Corvin finds something else: the
badge of the Dawnwatch, the Scroll of the Last Watch, or Wick himself.
Corvin offers 1,000 GP for Wick. Wick is insulted that it is so little.

## Scene 8: Ready for the Road (30 minutes)

**Goal:** Teach the Journeys system before the trip starts: the roles, the
pace, and the supply die.

The party has a reason to go south: the feast at Saint Ivo's, the Church
that prays to them, the troupe that plays them, Corvin, Dace, and the grey
folk who asked about the north hill. Saint Ivo's is three days south on
the old road of Ostrey, 72 miles. If the players do not want to go, let
Tamsin push them: "You want to know what the world made of you? It is all
in Saint Ivo's. Go and look."

> **Teach: Journeys.** "This is a long trip, so we use the Journeys rules.
> Each day of travel has four steps. I roll the weather. Each of you takes
> a role and makes one check. I roll to see if you meet something on the
> road. Then I roll your supply die, to see how long the food lasts. It
> takes a minute for each day, and the road becomes part of the story."

Then explain the four roles. Each character can take one role each day.
Give each player the Journeys page of the Player's Compendium, or say it
in your own words:

| Role | The check | On a success |
|---|---|---|
| **Guide** | Wisdom (Survival) against the Navigation DC of the day | The party keeps its way. On a failure, it gets lost. |
| **Scout** | Wisdom (Perception) or Dexterity (Stealth), DC 12 | If there is an encounter, the party sees it first. It can avoid it, or start the fight with the other side Surprised. |
| **Quartermaster** | Wisdom (Survival) against the Foraging DC of the day | The party finds food and water. Do not roll the supply die today. |
| **Lookout** | Intelligence (Nature), DC 12 | The party knows tomorrow's weather tonight, and the camp can't be surprised tonight. |

> **Teach: the rules of the roles.** "A role that nobody takes simply
> fails. One character can take two roles, but the second roll has
> Disadvantage. Choose for tomorrow now."

With the pregens, these roles fit well:

- **The Shadow** is a good scout (Perception and Stealth).
- **The Storm** is a mountain guide. The Storm is a good guide or
  quartermaster.
- **The Spark** is a good lookout (Nature, if the player chose it, or
  Intelligence).
- **The Light** is a good quartermaster (Wisdom).
- **The Shield** can take any role. A role with no bonus still has a
  chance.

Wick cannot take a role. "I am a lantern," he says. "I am a very good
lantern. That is my role." In a party of three, Wick can give a +2 bonus
to the lookout's check, because he never sleeps.

### The pace

The party chooses a pace each day:

| Pace | Miles each day | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Fast | 30 | The guide and the scout have Disadvantage. An encounter happens on a 1 or a 2. |
| Normal | 24 | No effect. |
| Slow | 18 | The guide and the scout have Advantage. |

At a Normal pace, the trip takes three days. At a Slow pace, it takes four.
At a Fast pace, it takes a little more than two days, so the party arrives
in the morning of the third day.

### The supply die

> **Teach: the supply die.** "Your food and water is one die, from a d4 to
> a d12. At the end of each day of travel, I roll it. On a 1 or a 2, it
> gets one size smaller: d12, then d10, then d8, then d6, then d4. If a
> d4 rolls a 1 or a 2, the food is gone."

The party starts with no supplies. In Brannock's Ford, the party can buy
them:

- **Each step up the die costs 5 GP for each traveler.** For four
  characters, a d4 costs 20 GP, a d6 costs 40 GP, and so on. Wick does not
  eat.
- **Without a mule or a cart, the largest die is a d10.** Hob sells a mule
  for 8 GP. Tamsin lends the party her old mule, Patience, for free if they
  helped the village in Scene 5. "Send her back with the pilgrims after the
  feast. She knows the way. She knows it better than you do."
- **Tamsin gives a d4 for free** if the party told her the truth in Scene 4.
  It is bread, cheese, apples, and a jar of honey. Then the party buys the
  steps above it.

> **GM tip:** The old coins matter here. If the party sold its suns to
> Corvin (Scene 7), it can buy a d8 or a d10 easily. If it did not, it may
> start with a d4 or a d6 and depend on the quartermaster. Both are fine.
> The road is three days, and the land is not empty. A party that runs out
> of food buys more from a farm for twice the price.

## Scene 9: The Old Road (1 to 2 hours)

**Goal:** Run three days of travel with the Journeys rules. Fight one
encounter. Show the land between the village and the town.

The old road of Ostrey runs south along the River Brann for the first day,
crosses a moor where the road is broken for the second day, and joins the
pilgrim road for the third day. Each day has a Navigation DC, a Foraging
DC, and one event that always happens. Roll the weather, the roles, the
encounter, and the supply die as the Journeys rules say.

| Day | Where | Navigation DC | Foraging DC | Event |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The road along the river | 5 | 8 | The milestone |
| 2 | The broken road across the moor | 12 | 12 | The bridge and the toll |
| 3 | The pilgrim road | 5 | 8 | The pilgrims, and Saint Ivo's |

The first and third days use the Roads and Farmland biome of the Journeys
chapter. The second day is open moor and grass, like the Grassland biome,
but harder to find the way on, because the road is gone under the grass.

> **GM tip:** If the guide has the Compass of the Old Road, the guide has
> Advantage on the check on each day. On day 2, that is the difference
> between a good day and a lost one. Point it out. The dead warden's gift
> already helps.

### Weather on the old road

Roll a d6 each morning, or use the lookout's roll from the night before.
It is autumn. The days are short.

| d6 | Weather | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clear and cold | No effect. A good day to make up lost time. |
| 2 | Rain | The road is mud. Travel at a Normal pace counts as Difficult Terrain (the party goes half as far), unless the party goes at a Slow pace. |
| 3 | Fog in the morning | The area is Lightly Obscured until midday. The scout has Disadvantage. |
| 4 | Wind from the north | Cold and loud. Wisdom (Perception) checks that rely on hearing have Disadvantage. Fires are hard to light. |
| 5 | Early frost | The night is very cold. A traveler with no blanket or cold-weather clothes makes a DC 10 Constitution saving throw at dawn. On a failure, the traveler gains 1 Exhaustion level. |
| 6 | Golden day | No effect. The country is beautiful, and the quartermaster has Advantage. |

> **Teach: weather.** "The weather lasts until the next morning. It can
> change what you do. In the rain, do you go slower, or do you get muddy
> and go less far?"

### Encounters on the old road

Roll a d6 each day. On a 1 (or a 1 or a 2 at a Fast pace), roll a d8 on
this table. If the scout succeeded, the party sees the encounter first.
It can avoid it, or start a fight with the other side Surprised.

| d8 | Encounter |
|---|---|
| 1 | Four **Wolves** (CR 1/4) follow the party at a distance all day. The early frosts drove them out of the hills. At night, they test the camp. If the lookout failed, they attack the mule. |
| 2 | A family of pilgrims with a broken cart. A character who fixes the wheel (DC 12 Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity check with tools) is invited to share their food. Do not roll the supply die today. |
| 3 | A **Barrow Light** (CR 1/2) floats over the road at night. It follows the party for a mile and then goes out. If the party fought Aske, it is his. If they made peace, it is a stranger, and it attacks the first creature that goes near it. |
| 4 | At night, the lookout sees a line of figures in grey crossing the moor, far away, with no lamp. They do not come near. The black stone hums. In the morning, there are footprints in the frost, going south. |
| 5 | A **Giant Boar** (CR 2) in the oak wood by the road. It does not like the mule. A character who makes a DC 12 Wisdom (Animal Handling) check calms it. |
| 6 | A **Priest Acolyte** (CR 1/4) and two **Guards** (CR 1/8) from Saint Ivo's, sent to meet the pilgrims and keep the road. They ask for news, and they warn the party about "bandits who say they are tax men" on the moor. |
| 7 | Two **Swarms of Ravens** (CR 1/4) over a dead horse by the road. The horse has a saddle of Gaunt, with a black gauntlet on red. Its rider is gone. A letter in the saddlebag is addressed to "the Abbess, Saint Ivo's". It is sealed. |
| 8 | A **Scarecrow Watcher** (CR 1) stands in a field by the road. A farmer made it to keep thieves out of the turnips. It cannot tell a thief from a traveler who picks one apple. |

> **GM tip:** An encounter is not always a fight. Entries 2, 4, 6, and 7
> are people, signs, and places. The letter in entry 7 is a seed for
> chapter 5: it is from Marshal Ide Gauntry to the Abbess, about the
> feast. Its wax seal is the gauntlet. If the party opens it, it is polite
> and cold: "Gaunt will send a guard of honor for the Feast of the
> Sleepers. Gaunt honors the Five as the heroes of Ostrey, whose true heir
> Gaunt is." Nothing more.

### Getting lost

On day 2, the road is gone under the grass, and the guide may fail. When
the guide fails, the party is lost.

- **The party travels the day's distance, but in the wrong direction.**
  On the moor, it goes west, toward the hills, away from the river.
- **The next morning, the guide repeats the check with Advantage.** On a
  success, the guide knows the party went wrong, and roughly where it is.
  The party loses one day.
- **If the guide failed by 5 or more,** the party does not even know that
  it is lost until the next check succeeds. Describe the moor as if all is
  well. Then describe the hills in the west, where the hills should not be.

> **Teach: getting lost.** "You are lost. You do not know it yet. Or you
> do: the river was on your left this morning, and now it is nowhere. What
> do you do?" A lost day costs one more roll of the supply die. That is the
> real price.

### Day 1: The milestone

On the first day, the road is good. It follows the River Brann south,
past farms and orchards. The harvest is late, and the farmers are worried.
At midday, the party passes an old milestone of Ostrey by the road.

> The stone is as tall as a child, and green with moss. On one side are
> the words: KINGSHOLM 75 MILES. On the other side: SAINT IVO 60 MILES.
> Under the words, someone carved the sun of Ostrey, and someone else,
> much later, carved five little sleeping figures, with halos.
>
> Wick looks at the stone for a long time. "Seventy-five miles to the
> capital," he says. "I always wanted to see the capital. The steward said
> he would take me one day." He is quiet. Then: "I suppose it is a ruin
> now. I suppose everyone in it is dead." Then, much smaller: "I suppose
> the steward is dead."

> **GM tip:** This is Wick's first real sad moment. Let it breathe. Do not
> fill the quiet. If a player comforts Wick, Wick tells one story about the
> steward: how Haskel Orme sat up all night to make him, the last night
> before the sleep, and sang to him while he worked. The steward could not
> sing. Wick loved it. This makes chapter 7 hit harder.

### Day 2: The bridge and the toll

On the second day, the old road crosses a moor called the Wending. The
road is broken: its stones are gone under the grass, or pulled up for
walls. At midday, the road comes to a stream in a deep gully. The old
bridge of Ostrey still stands, one stone arch, cracked but whole.

On the bridge, a man in a red coat sits on a barrel. A sign on a pole
says: GAUNT ROAD TOLL. SLEEPERS' TITHE. 1 GP A HEAD.

These are bandits. They are not from Gaunt, and nobody in Gaunt sent
them. The red coats came from a dead soldier. The tithe goes in their
pockets.

> "Wake well, friends!" says the man on the barrel. He has a good smile and
> a bad sword. "The road toll of the Marshal of Gaunt, for the upkeep of
> this fine old road. One gold a head. For the Sleepers, you understand.
> The mule goes free."

The party can pay, talk, or fight.

| What the party does | Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pays | None | None | The bandits let them cross, and laugh behind them. If the party meets the Priest Acolyte of encounter 6 later, the acolyte is angry: "You paid them? Now they will stay all winter." |
| Calls the lie | Wisdom (Insight) | 10 | The red coat is too big for the man, and the gauntlet badge is sewn on upside down. |
| Talks them out of it | Charisma (Persuasion) | 15 | The leader is impressed. He lets the party go for free, "this once". |
| Scares them off | Charisma (Intimidation) | 13 | Two of the bandits run. The rest fight. Take two Bandits away from the encounter. |
| Says that the road belongs to Ostrey and the Dawnwatch keeps it | Charisma (Intimidation) | 13, with Advantage if the party has the Compass of the Old Road | The bandits laugh, and then they see the badge of the Dawnwatch. One of them touches two fingers to his eyelids, the sign of the Sleepers. The rest fight, but they flee when their leader falls. |

### Day 3: The pilgrims

On the third day, the old road joins the pilgrim road from the west, and
the road is full. Pilgrims walk south in families and groups, with
banners, songs, and carts. Some walk with bare feet for penance. They all
go to the feast. The party is one group among many.

Choose two or three of these meetings.

- **Dace** is on the road with his cart, if he got away. He waves. He has
  new bones. "Sheep," he says, before anyone asks. "I am a changed man."
- **A pilgrim mother** asks the party to bless her baby. "You look like

  It is a coincidence. The mother does not think so.
- **A group of pilgrims** sing "The Five at the Gate". All of it. Twice.
- **An old man** walks with a stick, very slowly. He is going to the feast

  asked too," he says. "And his."
- **A cart** with the painted sign: PELL'S PLAYERS. THE FIVE SLEEPERS, AS
  THEY TRULY WERE. The cart is empty. The troupe went ahead.

## Scene 10: Saint Ivo's (10 minutes)

**Goal:** End the chapter. Show the town of the Church, and lead into
chapter 3.

> At the end of the third day, the road comes over a low hill, and there it
> is.
>
> You knew the shrine of Saint Ivo: a chapel, a well, and a guesthouse in a
> green valley, where tired soldiers stopped for a night. It is gone. In
> its place is a town of a thousand roofs, inside a wall of yellow stone.
> In the middle of the town stands a church so big that the town looks

The chapter ends here. Chapter 3 starts at the gate of Saint Ivo's, the

- **Money:** up to 75 GP for the old suns from Corvin Lisle (Scene 7), and
  up to 500 GP more if the Shadow sold the stolen thing. The tolls at the

  are worth 40 GP, if the party took them.
- **A Potion of Healing**, from the barrel on the bridge (Scene 9).
- **A friend:** Tamsin believes the party. Her ferry token carries them
  across the River Brann for free, for the rest of their lives.
- **A name in a book:** Corvin Lisle knows that something strange is going
  on. In chapter 3, he is a witness, if the party wants one.

> **Teach: the system stays.** "You now know the Journeys rules. Each time

### Level up

The characters go to level 3.

They go up at the next Long Rest, in Saint Ivo's. Level 3 is a big level:
each character chooses a subclass. Give the players time. The class
chapters of the Player's Compendium give the subclasses and their
features. Help each player of a pregen choose one that fits the character:
the Shield, for example, fits a subclass that protects its friends.
