# Fennick Under the Water

| | |
|---|---|
| **Levels** | 8 to 9 |
| **Time at the table** | Two or three sessions |
| **Places** | The Low Fen, and the drowned town of Fennick |
| **System that starts** | Techniques |

Two hundred years ago, the water came to Fennick. It came in one night, and
it never left. Today only the top of the bell tower stands above the black
water of the Low Fen. Under the water, the town is still there: the market,
the houses, the watch house, and the old church. In the church, a candle
still burns.

That candle is the third piece of the Dawnfire. The party goes down to get
it. The Hush comes too.

This chapter is a dungeon. It is also a school. A ghost on the bell tower,
Sergeant Maud Kettle, has drilled her drowned squad every morning for two
hundred years. She sees the Dawnwatch and she sees new recruits. She teaches
them the old fighting ways of Ostrey: the Techniques.

## How to use this chapter

- **The goal:** the party finds the Fennick Candle, the third piece of the
  Dawnfire, and keeps it out of the hands of the Hush.
- **The structure:** a journey of four days (Scene 1), the bell tower and
  Maud Kettle (Scenes 2 and 3), the drowned town as a dungeon with keyed
  areas (Scene 4), and the church, where three sides fight for the candle
  (Scene 5). Scene 6 is the end of the watch.
- **The new system:** Techniques. Maud teaches it in Scene 3. Read the
  Techniques chapter in the Player's Compendium and Running Techniques in
  the Game Master's Compendium before you run this chapter. The Teach boxes
  give you the short form.
- **Water:** most of this chapter is under water. The box "Fighting under
  the water" in Scene 4 has the rules on one page. Copy it, or keep this
  page open.
- **The thread:** the Storm. The reason why that character agreed to sleep
  is in Fennick. See the Thread box in area F6.

### What the GM knows

- **Fennick** was a busy market town of Ostrey on a small rise in the
  farmland by the River Brann. There was no fen then. A great dike of earth
  and stone kept the river out. Sluice gates in the dike let
  the water go in and out with the seasons.
- **The flood** came about two hundred years ago, in the Grey Winter, during
  the civil war after the last king of Ostrey died. A rebel army camped on
  the dry land below the dike. A lord of the other side gave the order to
  open the sluices and drown the army. The water drowned the army. It also
  drowned Fennick, with all its people. Nobody warned them. Only the watch
  saw it, and only one sergeant rang the bell.
- **The church** of Fennick kept a piece of the Dawnfire on its altar, as
  the shrines of Ostrey did. The piece is a thick white candle that never
  grows shorter. It burned on under the water. It still burns.
- **The Grief of Fennick** is the scream of the whole town at the moment of
  the flood. It never stopped. It became a ghost, and it clings to the only
  warm thing left in Fennick: the candle.
- **Maud Kettle** was a sergeant of the last garrison of the king in
  Fennick, in the Grey Winter. Her squad kept the watch of the town. She
  died on the bell tower as she rang the alarm. Her duty was to keep the watch until the
  all-clear. Nobody ever rang the all-clear. So she stays.
- **The Hush** follows the party. A creature that carries a piece of the
  crystal of the Unlit King can feel where the First Lamp is from 1 mile
  away (see the First Lamp in chapter 6). A priest of the Hush and her
  soldiers come behind the party, and they reach the church at the worst
  moment.

### If the party does not have the First Lamp

If the party left the First Lamp in Corrantey, the Hush follows the black
stone instead. A priest of the Hush can feel it, as chapter 4 tells. The
result is the same: the Hush arrives in Scene 5.

## Scene 1: Into the Low Fen (30 to 60 minutes)

**Goal:** Bring the party to Fennick. Show the fen. Pay off the Potions of
Water Breathing from chapter 6.

### The hook

Use the hook that fits your campaign. Each of them works.

- **Wick remembers.** On the night that he made Wick, the steward named the
  shrines of the Dawnfire out loud, one by one, as he worked. With two pieces
  found (the Ember of Saint Ivo and the First Lamp), Wick counts on his tiny
  fingers, which he does not have. "Saint Ivo's. The lantern makers.
  And... Fennick. The candle of Fennick. He said they had eels there. He
  said it as if eels were a joke that I would understand one day."
- **The guild ledger.** In chapter 6, the old books of the Lampwrights list
  the shrines that got a piece of the Dawnfire. One line reads "Fennick, a
  candle, for the church on the island." A later hand added: "Drowned."
- **The Church.** Abbess Merrow, or Brother Cade, tells the party that
  pilgrims who go into the Low Fen see a light under the water near an old
  tower. The Church calls it "the Drowned Saint" and tells pilgrims not to
  go.

> Wick looks at the map for a long time. Then he says, very quietly: "The
> whole town? Under the water? But the steward said that the dike was the
> biggest thing in Ostrey. Bigger than a dragon. He said that twice."

### The road

Fennick is four days from Saint Ivo's, off the road, through the Low Fen.
Run the trip with the Journeys rules from chapter 2. Use the Swamp biome
(navigate DC 14, forage DC 12).

> The road ends at the last farm. After that, there is only the fen: reeds
> taller than a rider, black water, grey sky, and paths of mud that move
> when you step on them. Frogs sing. Something big slides into the water
> behind you, and the frogs stop.

> **GM tip:** Do not let the journey take the whole session. Roll the roles
> once each day, tell the result in two sentences, and play one encounter
> on the trip at most. The party is level 8. A Swamp encounter from the
> table is a short break, not a threat. The hermit on stilts is a good one:
> the hermit can tell the party about the tower.

### Old Ada Ferrow, the eel catcher

On the second day, the party meets the one person who still goes near
Fennick. Ada Ferrow is an old eel catcher with a flat boat, a long pole, and
a pipe that she never lights. She lives on a platform of reeds in the middle
of the fen. She speaks slowly and she laughs at her own jokes.

> A thin boat comes out of the reeds. In it sits an old woman with a pole,
> a basket of live eels, and a cold pipe in her teeth. She looks at your
> boots, your weapons, and your lantern. The lantern looks back.
>
> "Huh," she says. "Talking lamp. My mother said she saw one of those once.
> She said it was rude to her."
>
> "I am never rude," says Wick. "I am honest."

Ada Ferrow knows the way to Fennick. She does not go into the town. She
will take the party to the edge of the old dike in her boat for a few
coins, or for news, or for a good story.

| Check | DC | Result |
|-------|----|--------|
| Charisma (Persuasion) | 12 | Ada takes the party by boat. The last day of the trip needs no guide roll. |
| Wisdom (Insight) | 14 | Ada is scared of the town. She lost her brother there, twenty years ago. He went down for "the light under the water" and did not come up. |
| Intelligence (History) | 15 | The character knows the dike of Fennick. Ostrey built it in the time of the old kings. It was strong. It did not break by itself. |
| Charisma (Performance) | 13 | A good story makes Ada laugh so much that she gives the party a gift: a coil of eel-skin rope, 100 feet long, that does not rot in water. |

What Ada knows:

- "The tower sticks out of the water like a finger. At dawn you can hear a
  woman shout at it. Shout orders. Like a soldier."
- "There's a light under the water by the tower. A pale one. My brother
  went for it. The water took him."
- "Don't touch the drowned. They hold on. They don't let go."
- "Grey people came through last month. They asked about the light too. I
  told them nothing. They didn't say thank you." (These are scouts of the
  Hush.)

### Potions of Water Breathing

In chapter 6, the Lampwrights could brew Potions of Water Breathing from
the Kelp Hearts that the party gathered. Now remind the players that they
have them. If the party did not make them, give them one more chance:

- Ada Ferrow knows a deep pool where kelp grows in the fen. It is fresh
  water, but the old Ostrey priests planted sea kelp there for the shrine.
  A character can gather one Kelp Heart there with an hour of work and a DC
  13 Wisdom (Survival) check (see Crafting and Gathering in the Game
  Master's Compendium). A character with Alchemist's Supplies can brew a
  Potion of Water Breathing from it in five workdays, so this works only if
  the party camps first.
- Spells such as *Water Breathing* also work, of course.
- And the Fennick Candle, once the party has it, lets the whole party
  breathe water (Scene 5).

> **GM tip:** The party can explore Fennick without water breathing. It is
> only much harder. A character can hold its breath for a number of minutes
> equal to 1 plus its Constitution modifier (minimum 30 seconds). The roofs
> and the tower give places to breathe. Do not let the lack of a potion stop
> the adventure. Let it make the players clever.

## Scene 2: The Bell Tower (15 minutes)

**Goal:** Show the drowned town. Meet Maud Kettle.

### Arrival at dawn

Time the arrival so that the party sees the tower at dawn. If the party
arrives at another time, they camp on the dike, and Maud's drill wakes them.

> The dike is a long, low hill of grass in the middle of the water. On one
> side, the fen. On the other side, a lake of black, still water, a mile
> wide. In the middle of the lake stands a stone tower. Its top is twenty
> feet above the water. Under the surface, if you look hard, you see the
> shapes of roofs, chimneys, and streets, all green and soft with weed.
>
> The sun comes up. And from the top of the tower, a voice shouts across
> the water.
>
> "SQUAD! FALL IN! On the double, you lazy, waterlogged, good-for-nothing
> fish! Spears UP!"
>
> On the tower, a grey shape in old armor walks up and down. Below her, on
> the stones of the tower, stand six figures. They are wet. They are very
> pale. One of them holds his spear upside down.

Wick's flame goes small. "That," he whispers, "is a ghost. And those are
the dead. And that one is holding his spear wrong."

### The tower

The tower is the bell tower of the church of Fennick. The church is under
it, under the water. The top of the tower has an open stone room with three
arches, a floor of wet planks, and one great bronze bell with three ropes.
A ledge runs around the outside of the tower, five feet above the water.
That is where the drowned squad stands for the drill.

The party can reach the tower by boat (Ada Ferrow's, or one they make), by
swimming (about half a mile from the dike), or over the roofs of the town under the water. The
roofs are 5 to 10 feet under the surface. A character can walk on them
with the water to the chest.

| Check | DC | Result |
|-------|----|--------|
| Wisdom (Perception) | 13 | Deep under the water, near the foot of the tower, a pale light glows. It does not move. |
| Intelligence (Religion) | 14 | The tower is a bell tower of a church of Ostrey. The altar of such a church stands under the tower, so that the bell rings over the flame. |
| Wisdom (Survival) | 12 | The safest way in a boat is along the old main street. The roofs there are deep, and no chimneys stick up. |
| Intelligence (Arcana) | 15 | The ghost is bound to the tower by a duty, not by a curse. A duty can end. |

## Scene 3: Sergeant Maud Kettle (45 to 60 minutes)

**Goal:** Teach Techniques. Let the players learn them in a fight that
cannot kill them.

### The sergeant

{{statblock: Sergeant Maud Kettle}}

Play Maud as a loud, tired, funny sergeant who loves her squad and hates how
bad they are. She shouts. She calls everyone "recruit". She never asks a
question that she does not answer herself. Under the shouting, she is very
sad, and very proud.

> The ghost turns as you come close. She is a short, broad woman in the
> half plate of the old watch of Ostrey, with a halberd and a face like a
> fist. Water runs off her and never reaches the floor.
>
> "Halt! Who goes there? Friend or flood?" She looks at you. She looks at
> your weapons. She looks at the lantern, and her face changes.
>
> "That's a Dawnwatch lamp. Steward Orme's work. We had one like it in the
> watch house, with the same little mark." She looks at you again, longer.
> "No. No, you're not. You can't be. The Dawnwatch went to sleep. Everybody
> knows that. My granny was there when they told the town."

What Maud believes:

- It is the morning after the flood. Every morning is the morning after the
  flood. She knows that she is dead. She does not know how long ago.
- The squad is "the third watch of Fennick". She drills them because the
  watch is not over. "Nobody rang the all-clear, recruit. You don't go off
  watch until the all-clear."
- The light under the tower is "the candle". She guards it. "The priest
  said keep the candle safe. The priest's gone. The candle's still there.
  So I keep it safe."
- Something else guards it too. She calls it "the Weeping". She does not
  go near it. "It's all of them, recruit. Everybody. All at once. You don't
  fight that. You can't."

When the party says how long it has been, Maud is quiet for a long moment.

> "Two hundred years." She leans on her halberd. "Two hundred years of
> drill. And Hobb still holds his spear like a broom." Then she straightens
> up. "Right. Doesn't matter. Watch is a watch. What do you want, Dawnwatch?"

| Check | DC | Result |
|-------|----|--------|
| Charisma (Persuasion) | 14 | Maud trusts the party. She tells them about the Weeping, and that it "goes quiet for a moment when the bell rings". |
| Charisma (Intimidation) | 18 | Maud laughs. "You think you can scare me? I'm DEAD." She likes the party a little more for trying. |
| Wisdom (Insight) | 12 | Maud is not angry. She is lonely, and she is ashamed. She thinks the flood was her fault, because she did not see it soon enough. |
| Intelligence (History) | 16 | The character remembers that the king kept a garrison in Fennick, to guard the dike. Its soldiers kept the watch of the town. In the time of the characters, they were good soldiers. |
| A Dawnwatch oath, or a salute of the old army (no roll) | | Maud salutes back, very straight, and calls the character "sir" or "ma'am" from now on. |

> **GM tip:** Maud is not a quest giver who stands still. She wants
> something too. She wants to know if her squad can still fight. She
> wants to see the Dawnwatch fight. And under all of it, she wants to go
> off watch. Let the players find that last thing on their own.

### "Show me what you've got"

When the party asks about the candle, or the town, or the Weeping, Maud says
the same thing.

> "You want to go down there? Into my town? With the Weeping in the
> church?" She looks you up and down. "Then show me what you've got,
> recruits. Fall in."

She watches the party fight one of her squad, or she simply watches them
stand. Then she shakes her head.

> "Strong. Fast. Brave. And you fight like farmers with good swords. No
> footwork. No drill. Every swing is a new idea." She spits, and the spit
> vanishes before it lands. "In my day, a recruit learned the old ways
> first. The stances. The moves. The things your body does before your
> head wakes up. I'll teach you. Nobody's left to stop me."

### Teaching Techniques

Stop the story here and teach the system. Keep each Teach box under one
minute. Let the players choose while they talk to Maud.

> **Teach: Techniques.** "From now on, each of you has Techniques. A
> technique is a trick that you have practiced until you do it without
> thinking: a way to stand, a move in the middle of a fight, or a way to
> bend a spell. Every character gets them, whatever the class."

> **Teach: technique points.** "You have technique points equal to your
> Proficiency Bonus. At level 8, that is 3 points. At level 9, it goes up
> to 4. Each technique costs points. You get all your points back when you
> finish a Short Rest or a Long Rest."

> **Teach: learning techniques.** "You know a number of techniques equal to
> your Proficiency Bonus plus 1. At level 8, that is 4 techniques. Choose
> them now from the Techniques chapter. When your Proficiency Bonus goes
> up, you learn one more. Each time you gain a level, you can swap one
> technique for another. Some techniques say 'level 5+' or more. You must
> be that level to learn them."

> **Teach: the three types.** "A **Stance** is a way of standing. You
> enter it, usually with a Bonus Action, and you pay its cost then. It
> lasts 1 minute, or until you enter another stance, or until you are
> Incapacitated. You can be in only one stance at a time. A **Maneuver**
> is one move, at the moment it names: when you hit, as a Reaction, and so
> on. You can choose a 'when you hit' maneuver after you know that you hit.
> A **Spell Shaping** changes a spell as you cast it with a spell slot. You
> need Spellcasting or Pact Magic to learn one. A spell with a shaping
> can't also use Metamagic."

> **Teach: one at a time.** "You can use only one technique on each of your
> turns, and one more as a Reaction between your turns. Entering a stance
> counts as your technique for that turn. A stance that you entered earlier
> doesn't count again."

> **Teach: the technique DC.** "If a technique asks a creature to make a
> saving throw, the DC is your technique DC: 8 plus your Proficiency Bonus
> plus your Strength or Dexterity modifier. You choose which one each time.
> A Spell Shaping uses your spell save DC instead."

Help the players choose. Each player reads the list and picks four. Maud
helps, in her own way. She recommends the old favorites of the watch of
Ostrey. All of them are in the Techniques chapter of the Player's
Compendium.

| Maud says | Technique | For whom |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| "Stand in front of your friends. That's the job." | Guardian's Stance | The Shield, or anyone in the front line |
| "They run at you, you put the point out. They stop running." | Set Against the Charge | Anyone with a long weapon |
| "A shield's a weapon too, recruit. Hit them with it." | Shield Rattle | Anyone with a Shield |
| "Push. Push them off the wall. Into the water." | Driving Strike | Strong fighters, the Storm |
| "Don't block with your face. Turn the blade." | Turn the Blade | Everyone. Maud teaches this one first. |
| "You lot with the books. Guard your focus like a flank." | Spellguard Stance | The Spark, the Light, any caster |
| "Run. Don't get hit. Run some more." | Flowing Water Stance | The Shadow, or anyone fast |
| "When they're bleeding, finish it. Don't be nice." | Finish Them (level 5+) | Anyone who hits often |

> **GM tip:** Do not let the choice take twenty minutes. If a player cannot
> choose, give them Turn the Blade, Guardian's Stance, Driving Strike, and
> one more that fits the character. They can swap one at each level. Tell
> the casters that the Spell Shapings are on their list too, after the
> stances and maneuvers. Maud does not know any of those. "Magic tricks.
> Ask a wizard."

Give the players the drill card. Maud chalks it on the wall of the tower
with a finger of ice. It is a reminder of the rules, in the words of an old
sergeant.

### Encounter: The Drill (Low)

Maud wants to see the new tricks at once. She calls her squad and fights the
party herself. This is a sparring fight. Nobody dies.

> "Right! Squad, form up on the ledge. Recruits, on the planks. This is a
> drill. A drill is not a fight. Nobody dies in my drill. Nobody dies in my
> drill AGAIN, Hobb." She lifts her halberd. "Begin!"

**Creatures:** Sergeant Maud Kettle and four Fennick Drowned of her squad.

{{statblock: Fennick Drowned}}

**XP:** for 4 characters at level 8, the Low budget is 1,000 × 4 = 4,000
XP. Maud (1,800) and three Fennick Drowned (3 × 450 = 1,350) make 3,150 XP.
With Techniques on, add one monster of the lowest CR for each four
characters: one more Fennick Drowned (450). Total: 3,600 XP. That is a Low
fight, and that is the point. It is a lesson, not a threat.

**The rules of the drill:**

- Maud's "Only Sparring" trait applies to her and to her squad. A creature
  that the squad drops to 0 Hit Points drops to 1 Hit Point instead.
- The drowned don't drag anyone into the water. Maud forbids it. ("NO
  DROWNING. We talked about this.")
- The drill ends when Maud or all of her squad drop to 0 Hit Points, or
  after five rounds. A drowned squad member that drops to 0 Hit Points
  stands up again after the drill. It is already dead.
- If a character uses a technique, Maud shouts what it was: "Turn the
  Blade! GOOD!" If a character does not use one in a round, she shouts at
  them: "That was a swing, recruit, not a technique! Again!"

**Tactics:** Maud uses her own techniques to show how they work. In round 1,
she uses Driving Strike on the first character that she hits, and pushes
them toward the edge of the planks (not into the water). When a character
hits her hard, she uses Turn the Blade as a Reaction and says so out loud.
She uses On Your Feet on her own squad when one of them falls Prone. The
drowned are slow and clumsy. Two of them hit each other by mistake.

> **Teach: the technique on the turn.** On the first turn of each player,
> ask: "Do you want to use a technique this turn? Remember: one on your
> turn, and one as a Reaction." When a player uses one, check the cost,
> mark the points, and describe it big. When Maud uses Turn the Blade,
> show the players that the same rules apply to her.

> **Teach: Short Rests matter more now.** After the drill, Maud lets the
> party rest on the tower. Say: "Techniques come back on a Short Rest. Use
> them in every fight. You get them back when you catch your breath."

> **Scaling:** The Low budgets are 3,000 (3 characters), 4,000 (4), 5,000
> (5), and 6,000 (6). With Techniques on, the extra Fennick Drowned is
> already in each line below for 4 or more characters.
>
> - **3 characters:** Maud and two Fennick Drowned. 1,800 + 900 = 2,700 XP.
> - **4 characters:** Maud and four Fennick Drowned. 1,800 + 1,800 = 3,600
>   XP.
> - **5 characters:** Maud and six Fennick Drowned. 1,800 + 2,700 = 4,500
>   XP.
> - **6 characters:** Maud and all six Fennick Drowned of her squad, and
>   Maud has 10% more Hit Points (107). 1,800 + 2,700 = 4,500 XP. The drill
>   is still a lesson. It does not have to fill the budget.

### After the drill

> Maud lowers her halberd. Her squad stands around you, dripping and pale.
> One of them gives you a thumbs up. His thumb falls off. He picks it up.
>
> "Not bad," says Maud. "Not good. Not bad." From her, that is a medal.
> "All right, Dawnwatch. You want to go down into my town, you'll need to
> know the way. Listen close. I'll say it once. I'll say it twice, because
> you're recruits."

Maud tells the party what she knows about the town. Give the players the
list below in her words. She also tells them the most important thing.

> "The bell. When we rang the all-clear in the old days, we pulled it
> three times, quick, all three ropes together. One, two, three, in one
> breath. That told the town the water had gone down." She looks at the
> three ropes. "I rang the alarm, that night. I never rang the all-clear.
> Couldn't. Needs three pairs of hands. And the water never went down."

This is the bell trick. The party does not know yet that it matters. It
matters in Scene 5.

What Maud tells the party about the town:

- **The market** (F3): "Busy. The drowned go there every morning. They
  still try to buy fish. Don't stop and talk. They hold on."
- **The hall of the dike wardens** (F4): "The wardens kept the sluice keys.
  And their books. If you want to know who opened the gates, it's in the
  books." (She does not say it with any feeling at all.)
- **The watch house** (F5): "My house. The armory. The roll of my squad.
  Take what you need. Bring back what you don't."
- **The sluice gate** (F7): "Big wheel. Opens the gates, closes the gates.
  If you could turn it, you'd let some water out of the church. Haven't
  got the hands. Or the hands are on the wrong side of being alive."
- **The graveyard and the church** (F8 and F9): "Stay out of the church.
  The Weeping's there. And the candle. You want the candle, you get past
  the Weeping. You can't fight it. I've seen people try."

## Scene 4: The Drowned Town (2 to 3 hours)

**Goal:** A dungeon. Let the party explore at their own pace, find the
truth of the flood, and get ready for the church.

### Fighting under the water

Read this box before you run this scene. Copy it for the players if they
want it.

> **Teach: under the water.** These are the rules for a fight under water,
> from the core rules. Teach them the first time the party goes under.
>
> - **Swimming.** A creature without a Swim Speed spends 1 extra foot of
>   movement for each foot that it swims. Fennick is calm water, so no
>   Strength (Athletics) check is needed, except to swim against the
>   current at the sluice gate (F7).
> - **Melee attacks.** A creature without a Swim Speed has Disadvantage on
>   melee attack rolls with a weapon under water, unless the weapon deals
>   Piercing damage (a Dagger, a Spear, a Trident, a Rapier, and so on).
> - **Ranged attacks.** A ranged attack roll with a weapon under water
>   misses at once beyond the weapon's normal range. Inside the normal
>   range, it has Disadvantage.
> - **Fire.** A creature or object that is fully under water has
>   Resistance to Fire damage.
> - **Breath.** A creature can hold its breath for a number of minutes
>   equal to 1 plus its Constitution modifier (minimum 30 seconds). When
>   it runs out of breath, it gains 1 Exhaustion level at the end of each
>   of its turns. When it can breathe again, it removes the Exhaustion
>   levels that it gained this way. A Potion of Water Breathing, the
>   *Water Breathing* spell, or the Fennick Candle (Scene 5) stops all of
>   this.
> - **Light in Fennick.** This one is not a core rule. It is a rule of
>   this town: the water is dark and full of silt, so light from a lantern
>   or a spell reaches half its usual distance under the water. Wick's
>   light is the same. The light of the First Lamp is sunlight, and it is
>   not reduced.

> **GM tip:** The watch house (F5) has spears and tridents in a dry chest.
> Tell the players this before they go into the market. A fighter with a
> longsword or a mace who has Disadvantage on every attack is no fun. A fighter who
> finds a good spear in the armory of the old watch is a story.

### The town in short

The town sits on a low island inside the dike. The water over the streets
is 15 to 25 feet deep. The roofs are 5 to 10 feet under the surface. The
tower and the church are in the middle, on a small hill.

| Area | Name | What is there |
|------|------|---------------|
| F1 | The bell tower | Maud and her squad. A safe place to rest above the water. |
| F2 | The roofs | A path over the roofs. Slippery. |
| F3 | The market square | The drowned at the market. An encounter. |
| F4 | The hall of the dike wardens | The books, and the order that drowned the town. |
| F5 | The watch house | The armory, the roll of the squad, Maud's own room. |
| F6 | The house on Lantern Row | The house of the Storm thread. |
| F7 | The sluice gate | The great wheel. An encounter. Lowers the water in the church. |

The party can go in any order. A good order is F2, F3, F5, F4, F6, F7, F8,

> **GM tip:** Keep the pace up. Give each area two or three sentences of
> read-aloud text, one choice, and one thing to find. Do not describe every
> house. The town has hundreds of houses. Most of them are only mud, fish,

### F1. The bell tower

Described in Scenes 2 and 3. Maud stays here. The party can come back to
rest. The tower top is dry, and Maud keeps watch. A Short Rest here is safe.
A Long Rest is safe too, but the drowned squad snores. ("They don't
breathe," says Wick. "How do they snore?")

The bell has three ropes. A creature can pull one rope with the Utilize
action. The bell rings once for each rope that is pulled. When three ropes
are pulled in the same round, that is the all-clear. See Scene 5.

### F2. The roofs

> Under the water, the roofs of Fennick are a road of green stone and black
> slate. Weed grows on them in long, slow ribbons. Small silver fish hide in
> the chimneys. When you step on a roof, a cloud of mud rises up and
> swallows your feet.

A character can walk on the roofs with the water to the chest or the neck.
The roofs are Difficult Terrain. Between the houses are gaps of 5 to 10
feet, down to the street, 20 feet below.

### F3. The market square

> The street opens into a wide square. Under the green water, the stalls
> still stand: a fish stall, a bread stall, a stall of pots. At each one
> stands a figure. They are pale and bloated, with weed in their hair. One
> holds up a fish that is only bones. One counts coins that are not there.
> One turns slowly toward you. Then all of them turn toward you.
>
> "Fresh," says the drowned woman at the fish stall, in a voice full of
> water. "Fresh today. Come closer."

The drowned of Fennick still do what they did on the last morning of their
lives. They are not evil. They are lonely, and they are hungry for warmth.
They want company. They take it by force.

**Talking first.** A character who acts like a customer can try to walk
through the market without a fight.

| Check | DC | Result |

| Charisma (Deception) | 15 | The character plays a buyer and pays with a real coin. The drowned at that stall lets the character pass. A group of three characters who each succeed can cross the market. |
| Charisma (Persuasion) | 17 | The character talks to the drowned like a neighbor. They listen. They stop. Then one of them reaches out to touch the warm face. The encounter starts with the party in the middle of the square, but the drowned are Surprised. |

### Encounter: The Market of the Drowned (Low)

**Creatures:** Six Fennick Drowned, one Swarm of Bloodleeches, and one
Spearjaw Gar. All of them are in the Original Bestiary of the Game Master's
Compendium, except the drowned, which are in this chapter.

- The drowned stand at the stalls. They move toward the nearest warm
  creature. They grab and drag. A drowned that grabs a character tries to
  swim it down into the deep street, 10 feet below the square.
- The Swarm of Bloodleeches lives in the gutter on the north side. It
  comes out on round 2, when blood is in the water.
- The Spearjaw Gar is a fish as long as a boat. It hides in the broken
  window of the weavers' hall (it is hidden at the start). It attacks the
  first character who swims, not walks.

**XP:** for 4 characters at level 8, the Low budget is 4,000 XP. Five
drowned (2,250), one Swarm of Bloodleeches (450), and one Spearjaw Gar
(450) make 3,150 XP. With Techniques on, add one more drowned (450). Total:
six drowned, 3,600 XP. A Low fight, but a messy one in the water.

**Terrain:**

- The stalls give Half Cover.
- The square is 15 feet under the water. The deep street on the south side
  is 25 feet under the water and very dark.
- A broken well in the middle of the square is a shaft 40 feet deep. A
  creature that is dragged into it is 40 feet under the surface and in
  darkness.

**Tactics:** The drowned do not use smart tactics. They come in a slow wave,
two or three at each character. They hold on with Clutching Hands and swim
their prey down and away. Their trait Still Here makes them hard to put
down in the water. Radiant damage (the First Lamp, a cleric's spell) puts
them down for good. Let the players find that out.

> **GM tip:** If the party has the First Lamp, the drowned hate and want its
> light at the same time. They come toward it, and then they stop at the
> edge of the Bright Light, and they moan. Use that. It is a good moment
> for the players to see what the Lamp is.

> **Scaling:** The Low budgets are 3,000 (3 characters), 4,000 (4), 5,000
> (5), and 6,000 (6). The extra monster for Techniques is in each line for
> 4 or more characters.
>
> - **3 characters:** four drowned, one Swarm of Bloodleeches, one
>   Spearjaw Gar. 1,800 + 450 + 450 = 2,700 XP.
> - **4 characters:** six drowned, one swarm, one gar. 2,700 + 450 + 450 =
>   3,600 XP.
> - **5 characters:** six drowned, two swarms, two gars. 2,700 + 900 + 900
>   = 4,500 XP.
> - **6 characters:** eight drowned, two swarms, two gars. 3,600 + 900 +
>   900 = 5,400 XP. Bring the drowned in waves of two each round, so that
>   you can run them.

**Treasure:** At the money changer's stall, under a stone, is a lead box with
120 GP in old coins and a silver scale that weighs true (worth 25 GP). On
the drowned fishwife is a necklace of river pearls (worth 100 GP).

### F4. The hall of the dike wardens

> A tall stone hall with a bronze door, half open, stuck in the mud. Over
> the door, carved in the stone: a dike, a wave, and a key. Inside, the
> water is very still. Shelves of rotten books line the walls. In the
> middle stands a table of black oak, and on it, under a cover of glass, a
> book. The glass held. The book is dry.

The dike wardens were the officers who kept the dike and the sluices. The
glass case kept their last book dry for two hundred years. The case is
sealed with wax. A character who opens it under the water ruins the book in
1 minute, unless the character carries the case up to the air first. The
case is heavy (60 pounds) but it floats once it is free of the table.

| Check | DC | Result |
|-------|----|--------|
| Intelligence (Investigation) | 13 | The character sees that the case is sealed, and that the book will be ruined in the water. The party knows to take it up first. |
| Strength (Athletics) | 14 | The character frees the case from the table and swims it up. |
| Intelligence (History) | 15 | The character knows the seal on the last page: it is the seal of a lord of the civil war, not of the king. Ostrey had no king at that time. |

The book is the log of the wardens. Most of it is about the water level,
the eels, and the price of stone. The last three pages tell the story of the
last night. Read it to the players, or tell it in your own words.

> *The 3rd day of the deep frost. The rebel host is camped on the low
> ground east of the dike, some four thousand. We closed the gates and set
> the watch.*
>
> *The 4th day. A rider came with a written order and his lordship's seal.
> The order says to open all the gates at the turn of the tide, "to deny
> the low ground to the enemy". I said the water will come over the island.
> The rider said the island is to be cleared first. Nobody has come to
> clear it.*
>
> *The 4th day, night. We have asked for the order to be confirmed. The
> rider says it is confirmed. His men stand at the wheel. They have the
> keys. I have sent the boy to the watch house to tell Sergeant Kettle.
> God keep us.*

The rest of the page is blank.

> **GM tip:** This is the truth of the flood. It is not a monster. It is a
> person who gave an order. Do not name the lord. In the land today, many
> families say that they come from the lords of old Ostrey, and the name
> would only start an argument at the table. What matters is this: nobody
> warned Fennick, and Maud knows it. If the party shows the book to Maud,
> she reads it, and then she sits down on the floor of the tower for a long
> time. "The boy never came," she says at last. "I saw the water first. I
> rang the bell. That's all. I thought I was late."

Showing the book to Maud changes something. She does not feel guilty
anymore. She is only angry, and anger is easier to carry. In Scene 6, this
matters: she can go off watch in peace.

**Treasure:** In a drawer of the table is the silver key of the sluice gate
(F7). With it, the wheel is much easier to turn (see F7).

### F5. The watch house

> A squat building with thick walls and narrow windows, next to the old town
> gate. A bell hangs over the door, smaller than the one in the tower, green
> with age. Inside, the water is cold. Spears stand in a rack on the wall,
> straight and neat, as if someone still cleans them every day.

Someone does. Maud's squad comes here after the drill each morning and
cleans the spears. They are clean. They are also two hundred years old.

**What is here:**

- **The armory.** A chest of tarred oak, still dry inside. It holds six

- **The roll of the squad.** A slate board on the wall with the names of

  names. Six have a line through them: the ones Maud lost in the flood and

  Back at..." It ends there.
- **The town roll.** A big book in a lead box, with the names of every

### F6. The house on Lantern Row

> Lantern Row is a short street of tall, narrow houses near the church. The
> houses are painted, or they were: blue, yellow, red. Now they are all the
> same green. On each door is a small iron lantern, the sign of the street.
> All of them are dark. One house is bigger than the others, with a carved
> door, and the door is shut.

This is the house of the Storm thread. Ostrey paid each family of a sleeper
when the Dawnwatch went into the crystal. The price that the Storm asked
for was a house in Fennick, for the people that the Storm cared about. Use
the answer of the player to tell what is here.

> **Thread (the Storm):** In the tutorial, the player of the Storm answered
> the "Before" question: "You did not want to go into the crystal. Why did
> you agree at the end?" The answer is here, in Fennick. Use what the
> player said. Choose one of these, or mix them.
>
> - **A house.** The Storm agreed to sleep because Ostrey promised to look
>   after someone: a sibling, a child, an old friend, a whole family. That
>   promise was this house. Over the door is carved the name of the family
>   and a small sun, the sign of a Dawnwatch family. Inside, the table is
>   still laid. On the wall is a painting, faded and green, of the person
>   that the Storm cared about, and beside it a younger person who has the
>   same eyes. The family lived. They had children. Then the water came.
> - **A letter.** In a jar sealed with wax, on the shelf over the fire, is
>   a letter. The person that the Storm agreed to sleep for wrote it forty
>   years after the sleep, as an old person. "You will wake one day, and I
>   will not be here. I want you to know that it worked. We had a good
>   life. You gave us that." The jar has a stamp of the Lampwrights on its
>   lid. They kept the letters of the Dawnwatch families and sent them to
>   the shrines.
> - **A grave.** The graveyard (F8) has a stone with the name of the
>   person. It is not a stone of the flood. It is older. The person died
>   long before the flood, old, in bed. On the stone: "Who waited, and was
>   not sorry."
> - **A child of the family.** On the roll of the squad (F5), one of the
>   six lost names has the family name of the person. A descendant of the
>   person stood in the watch of Fennick, and died with Maud. Maud
>   remembers them. "Good soldier. Too brave. Always the first on the
>   wall." If the party can calm the Grief (Scene 5), the Storm hears that
>   name in its hundred voices, and it is quiet.
>
> Give the player a real moment here. Let them say what the character does.
> If the player is happy, give that player Heroic Inspiration.

**Treasure:** If the house is the house of the Storm's family, a chest under
the stairs holds the old family savings: 250 GP in old coins, a silver
brooch with a small sun (worth 50 GP), and a pair of Boots of the
Winterlands that the family kept "for when you come back". The Storm
traveled the high passes. The family remembered.

### F7. The sluice gate

> The great dike rises out of the water on the east side of the town. Here,
> against its inner wall, stands the sluice house: a square tower of stone
> with three iron gates in its base. The gates are open. They have been
> open for two hundred years. Black water moves through them slowly, in
> and out with the fen. Above, half out of the water, an iron wheel as big
> as a cart stands on a stone platform. Rust covers it like red moss.

The wheel works the gates. With the gates open, the water inside the dike is
as high as the fen outside. If the party closes two of the gates and opens
the drain gate under the church, the water in the church drops by 10 feet.
That makes the fight in Scene 5 much easier (see there).

Things in the sluice house have lived here a long time, and they do not want
the wheel to turn.

### Encounter: The Sluice Gate (Moderate)

**Creatures:** Two Chuuls and two Giant Crocodiles. With Techniques on, add
one more Chuul.

- The crocodiles lie on the platform under the wheel, half out of the
  water. They look like logs. They are not.
- The chuuls live in the dark tunnel of the drain under the platform. They
  come out when anything touches the wheel, or when a creature swims in
  the current near the gates.

**XP:** for 4 characters at level 8, the Moderate budget is 1,700 × 4 =
6,800 XP. Two Chuuls (2 × 1,100 = 2,200) and two Giant Crocodiles (2 ×
1,800 = 3,600) make 5,800 XP. With Techniques on, add one Chuul (1,100).
Total: 6,900 XP. A Moderate fight.

**Terrain and hazards:**

- **The current.** Near the open gates, the water pulls. A creature that
  starts its turn within 10 feet of a gate makes a DC 13 Strength
  (Athletics) check. On a failure, the current pulls it 10 feet toward the
  gate. A creature pulled into a gate is stuck against the bars and has
  the Restrained condition until it uses an action to pull free (DC 13
  Strength (Athletics)).
- **The platform.** The platform under the wheel is above the water. It is
  slick with weed. It is Difficult Terrain.
- **The wheel.** See below. A creature at the wheel is on the platform,
  and the crocodiles are there too.

**Turning the wheel:** Closing the gates takes three successful Strength
(Athletics) checks, DC 18, each one an action. With the silver key from the
hall of the dike wardens (F4), the DC is 14: the key opens the lock that
holds the wheel. Two creatures can work the wheel together; each one makes
its own check. After the third success, the gates close with a great sound,
and the drain under the church opens. Over the next 10 minutes, the water in
the church drops by 10 feet.

> **GM tip:** Turning the wheel in the middle of the fight is a great
> moment. One character turns, the others hold off the chuuls. Let the
> players plan it. A Chuul that grabs the character at the wheel is a good
> scare. If the party clears the room first, they can turn the wheel at
> their own pace with no checks at all. Only the key matters then: without
> it, three DC 18 checks still apply, but a failure only costs time.

> **Scaling:** The Moderate budgets are 5,100 (3 characters), 6,800 (4),
> 8,500 (5), and 10,200 (6). The extra Chuul for Techniques is in each line
> for 4 or more characters.
>
> - **3 characters:** one Chuul and two Giant Crocodiles. 1,100 + 3,600 =
>   4,700 XP.
> - **4 characters:** three Chuuls and two Giant Crocodiles. 3,300 + 3,600
>   = 6,900 XP.
> - **5 characters:** four Chuuls and two Giant Crocodiles. 4,400 + 3,600
>   = 8,000 XP.
> - **6 characters:** four Chuuls and three Giant Crocodiles. 4,400 + 5,400
>   = 9,800 XP.

**Treasure:** In the drain tunnel, among the bones of the chuuls' meals, is
the body of a man in old fen leathers, with a rusty knife and a locket. In
the locket is a lock of hair and a scratched name: TAM FERROW. This is the
brother of Ada Ferrow (Scene 1), who came for the light twenty years ago.
The bones also hold 180 GP in coins of today, a Potion of Greater Healing,
and a Lantern of Small Hours that still burns on its last drop of oil.

### F8. The graveyard

> Around the hill of the church lies the old graveyard. The stones lean in

>
> In the side of the hill, between two big tombs, is a low iron door. It

**The mud.** The ground of the graveyard is deep mud under the water. It is

check or falls into the mud.

**The crypt door.** The iron door leads into the crypt under the church.
A narrow stair goes up from the crypt into the church (F9), behind the

Grief and has Advantage on Initiative.

## Scene 5: The Church of Fennick (60 to 90 minutes)

**Goal:** The climax of the chapter. The party gets the candle. The Grief
guards it. The Hush comes to put it out.

### The church

> The church of Fennick is long and dark under the water. Its tall windows
> are broken, and weed hangs in them like curtains. The roof is half gone,
> and the water above is grey with morning light. Rows of benches lean in
> the mud. Fish move between them like the last people at a service.
>
> At the far end, on a stone altar, a candle burns.
>
> It is a thick white candle in a plain iron dish. Its flame is small and
> steady and gold. It burns under the water as if the water was air. Around
> it, the water is warm. Weed grows toward it. Every fish in the church
> stays near it.
>
> And around it, something moves. A cold grey shape, bigger than a horse,
> drifts in a slow circle around the altar. Faces come and go in it. A man.
> A child. An old woman. A dog. A girl with a ribbon. They open their
> mouths, all at once, and they scream with no sound.

{{statblock: Grief of Fennick}}

The Grief does not attack at once. It circles the candle. It attacks any
creature that comes within 30 feet of the altar, or that attacks it, or
that brings a light into the church that is not the candle. (The First
Lamp, Wick, or any spell of light makes it attack at once. It wants no
other warm thing near its candle.)

### The water in the church

The church is 30 feet deep under the water at the doors, and the altar
stands on a step, 25 feet deep.

- **If the party turned the wheel at the sluice gate (F7):** the water is
  10 feet lower. The top of the nave is now air. The benches and the altar
  are still under the water, but the party can swim up to breathe at any
  time, and a character on the top of the old organ loft (20 feet above
  the floor) is out of the water. This also gives ranged attacks a clear
  line from the loft.
- **If not:** the church is fully under the water. The rules of "Fighting
  under the water" apply everywhere.

### The Hush arrives

Start this when the party first comes close to the candle, or when the fight
with the Grief begins.

> A sound comes through the water: a low hum, many voices, with the mouths
> closed. Grey shapes come in through the broken windows on the north side.
> Divers, with weighted belts and grey hoods, and in the middle a woman in
> a long grey robe with an iron censer on a chain. The censer gives out
> black smoke that does not mix with the water. Where it goes, the light
> goes out.
>
> She looks at the candle. She looks at you. She lifts a hand, palm down,
> the sign of the Hush.
>
> "Let it go," she says, and her voice is in your head, not your ears. "It
> has burned long enough. Everything that burns must rest."

The black stone hums so loud that its bearer feels it in the teeth.

The Hush has drunk potions of water breathing, and the priest wears a grey
amulet that does the same. They want one thing: to put the candle out. The
priest needs to use Snuff the Sun with the candle inside the Sphere four
times, or to hold the candle in her hand for 1 minute. Each time, the candle flickers lower, and the Grief howls.

### Encounter: Three Sides at the Altar (High)

{{statblock: Hush Dusk Priest}}

{{statblock: Hush Night Warden}}

**Creatures:** the Grief of Fennick, one Hush Dusk Priest, and two Hush
Night Wardens. The Grief fights everyone who comes near the candle,
including the Hush. The Hush fights the party, and the Grief when they must.

**XP:** for 4 characters at level 8, the High budget is 2,100 × 4 = 8,400
XP. The Grief (3,900), the priest (2,900), and one Night Warden (1,100)
make 7,900 XP. With Techniques on, add one more Night Warden, the monster of
the lowest CR (1,100). Total: 9,000 XP. That is over the High budget, but
the Grief and the Hush also fight each other, so the party never faces all
of it at once. This fight is hard. It is the climax of the chapter.

**The three sides:**

- **The Grief** attacks the nearest creature to the candle, whatever its
  side. On each of its turns, roll a d6. On a 1 to 3, it attacks a member
  of the party. On a 4 to 6, it attacks a member of the Hush. If no one is
  within 30 feet of the altar, it circles and waits. It uses Call the
  Drowned on each turn: the drowned of the graveyard come in through the
  windows, one each round, from round 2. They are part of the Grief, not
  extra monsters, so they do not count for the XP. Use the statblock of
  the Fennick Drowned. No more than three are in the church at one time.
- **The Hush** goes for the candle. The Night Wardens hold the party off
  with Close the Ranks and their maces. The priest casts Darkness on the
  party first, then Silence on the casters, then goes to the altar and uses
  Snuff the Sun as soon as it recharges. When the Grief attacks the
  priest, the wardens turn to protect her.
- **The candle.** Track the candle. It starts at full flame. Each time the
  priest uses Snuff the Sun with the altar in the Sphere, the flame
  drops one step: full, low, a spark. A fourth time puts it out. If the
  priest holds the candle for 1 minute (10 rounds), it goes out too. A
  character who takes the candle from the altar ends all of this: the
  candle is safe in a hand that cares for it. To take it, a character must
  reach the altar and use the Utilize action. The Grief attacks that
  character on its next turn, always.

**The bell trick:** Maud told the party about the all-clear. If three
creatures pull the three ropes of the bell in the tower (F1) in the same
round, the bell rings the all-clear over the town. The Grief loses its trait
The Bell: it no longer regains 10 Hit Points each turn. It also stops for a
moment and turns its hundred faces up toward the sound. It has the
Incapacitated condition until the end of its next turn.

How the party can do it:

- Three characters leave the fight, swim up to the tower, and pull the
  ropes together. The top of the tower is 50 feet above the church floor,
  straight up through the hole of the bell rope in the vault. A character
  with water breathing swims up in two or three turns.
- Maud can pull one rope. If the party asked her, she is at the tower,
  ready. Her squad can pull a second rope if the party showed them the
  drill card, and if a character at the tower uses an action to shout the
  order (a DC 12 Charisma (Intimidation) or (Persuasion) check). So one
  character with Maud and one drowned is enough.
- A spell or a trick that pulls a rope from far away also counts (for
  example *Mage Hand*).

> **GM tip:** The bell trick is not a secret door. It is a reward for
> players who listen. If they forget it, Maud shouts from the tower after
> round 3: "The BELL, recruits! The all-clear! Three ropes!" If the party
> did not meet Maud as a friend, the Grief simply fights on, and the party
> needs more damage. The fight is still winnable.

**Calming the Grief:** The party can end the Grief without destroying it. A
character who holds the town roll from the watch house (F5), or the warden's
book from F4, can use an action to read names aloud to the Grief, or to tell
it the truth of the flood. Make a DC 16 Charisma (Persuasion) or
Intelligence (Religion) check. After three successes, the Grief stops. Its
faces turn calm, one by one, and it sinks into the floor of the church like
mist. It is gone. If the all-clear has rung, each success counts as two.

> **Scaling:** The High budgets are 6,300 (3 characters), 8,400 (4), 10,500
> (5), and 12,600 (6). The extra monster for Techniques is in each line for
> 4 or more characters. Because the Grief and the Hush fight each other too,
> the lines for 3 and 4 characters are a little over the budget on paper.
> That is on purpose.
>
> - **3 characters:** the Grief and the priest. 3,900 + 2,900 = 6,800 XP.
> - **4 characters:** the Grief, the priest, and two Night Wardens. 3,900 +
>   2,900 + 2,200 = 9,000 XP.
> - **5 characters:** the Grief, the priest, and three Night Wardens. 3,900
>   + 2,900 + 3,300 = 10,100 XP.
> - **6 characters:** the Grief, the priest, and four Night Wardens, and
>   the Grief has 10% more Hit Points (156). 3,900 + 2,900 + 4,400 =
>   11,200 XP.
>
> **If the party is in trouble:** Maud comes down from the tower. She is
> bound to the tower, but the church is under the tower, and she decides
> that counts. She takes the place of one Night Warden in the fight and
> fights it, and she shouts techniques at the party.

### When the candle is safe

The fight ends when the Hush is beaten or flees and the Grief is destroyed or
calmed. A Hush that loses its priest flees through the windows. A captured
warden says nothing at all, ever. A captured priest says only this:

> "You can carry the light. You cannot carry the sun. It is dying, little
> sleepers. Ask your lantern. Ask the old man who made it what he did."

On the priest is a letter in a code of the Hush. A DC 15 Intelligence
(Investigation) check breaks it. It says: "When the third light is out,
come north. Mother calls the choir to the old city. The king's bed is
waiting." This points to Kingsholm (chapter 10), and to the gathering of
the Hush there.

When a character takes the candle:

> The candle is warm in your hand. Not hot. Warm, like a cup of tea, like
> a hand. The water around you changes. You breathe in, and it is not
> water anymore. It is cold, clean air. All around you, the people of the
> party gasp, and laugh, and breathe.
>
> Wick looks at the candle. Then he looks at it again. "Oh," he says, in a
> very small voice. "Oh. It's like looking in a mirror. A very old mirror.
> Hello. Hello, cousin."

{{treasure: The Fennick Candle}}

> **GM tip:** Wick does not know that he is the last true piece of the
> Dawnfire. Nobody knows that in Book One. But Wick feels something when he
> is near the pieces. Play it as a small, warm, strange feeling. Do not
> explain it. Book Two explains it.

## Scene 6: Off Watch (20 minutes)

**Goal:** End the chapter on a good, sad moment. Give Maud her rest.

Back on the tower, Maud waits. If the party rang the all-clear, she heard
it. If they did not, they can ring it now, with no fight, with Maud on one
rope.

> The bell rings: one, two, three, quick, in one breath. The sound goes out
> over the black water, over the drowned roofs, over the reeds of the fen,
> and far away a flock of birds rises into the morning.
>
> On the ledge, the drowned squad stops. One by one, they put their spears
> down. They look up at the sun. Then, one by one, they are not there
> anymore.
>
> Maud Kettle stands very still. She is fainter now. You can see the sky
> through her.
>
> "All clear," she says. "That's the all-clear." She takes off her old
> helmet, and under it her hair is grey. "Two hundred years. Good. That's
> enough. That's quite enough."

Maud gives her whistle to the character that she liked best in the drill, or
to the character who used the most techniques.

> She holds out a dented tin whistle on a leather cord. "Here. For the
> drill. Your lot needs a sergeant, and I'm going off watch. Blow it when
> they're tired. They'll remember they have one more in them." She looks
> at the lantern. "And you. Lamp. Look after them. They're good. They're
> not good. They're not bad."
>
> "I will," says Wick. His voice is thick, which is strange, because he is
> a flame.

{{treasure: Maud Kettle's Whistle}}

What else Maud does, if the party has done these things:

- **If the party read her the warden's book (F4):** she says: "Tell them.
  Whoever the land is now. Tell them who opened the gates. Not his name.
  Just that somebody did it, and the town didn't drown by itself."
- **If the party found her family's house next to the Storm house (F6):**
  she asks, very quietly, what they saw. Whatever the party says, she
  nods. "Good. Good. I'll go see them now."
- **If the Storm thread was the descendant in her squad:** she salutes the
  Storm. "Your family stood the wall, Dawnwatch. Last on the wall. Same as
  you."

Then she is gone. The tower is quiet. The bell rings once more by itself,
very softly, and stops.

### Ada Ferrow

If the party found the locket of Tam Ferrow in the sluice (F7), they can
bring it to Ada on the way back. She takes it, looks at it for a long time,
and puts it in her shirt. She says nothing. Then she lights her pipe, for
the first time in twenty years.

### The way out

With the candle, the Low Fen is easier. Its light keeps the swamp creatures
away, and the party can cross water that would stop other travelers. The
trip back to Saint Ivo's is four days. Dawnwatch Keep is four days more,
by the old road through Brannock's Ford. Run it with
one journey roll for each day, or skip it.

> **GM tip:** If the party has Dawnwatch Keep as a stronghold (chapter 7),
> this is a good moment for a Stronghold Turn. News comes from the keep:
> the Hush was seen on the Dunn road, going north. Many of them. That leads
> to chapter 9 and, later, to chapter 10.
