# Gaunt

| | |
|---|---|
| **Levels** | 5 to 6 |
| **Time at the table** | About 3 sessions (12 hours) |
| **Places** | The good road south, the border village of Wendle, Stannard: the Gate of the Five, the Marshal's Hall, the College of War, the drill fields |
| **System that starts** | Factions and renown |
| **Thread** | The Spark: a spell on the first day |
| **People** | Marshal Ide Gauntry, the ruler of Gaunt; Tobin Gauntry, her son, who does not want a war; Captain Joss Marrick; Magister Aud Brennet, the first war mage; Lieutenant Roe, the Marshal's helpful aide |

Act II starts in the south. Marshal Ide Gauntry has an army of three
thousand soldiers, a hall with an empty pole for the banner of Ostrey,
and a plan. She wants the Dawnwatch as living banners at the head of her
army. With the Sleepers beside her, Gaunt is the true heir of Ostrey, and
nobody can say no to it.

The party goes to Stannard to see what Gaunt is. It finds a state of
soldiers that is clean, ordered, proud, and ready for a war that it says
it does not want. It finds war mages who teach a "Dawnwatch spell" that
the Spark cast on the first day, three hundred years ago, and who cast it
wrong. And it finds the Marshal's son, Tobin, who is the only person in
Stannard who is afraid of what comes next.

At the end of the chapter, the party has ranks: with Gaunt, with the
Church, or with both. Every power in the land now counts how much it
trusts the Dawnwatch.

This chapter has two jobs:

1. **Start Act II.** The powers of the land compete for the Dawnwatch.
   Gaunt makes its offer. The Church watches. The Hush plays both sides.
2. **Teach Factions and renown.** The party learns that each power keeps
   a score, that the score has ranks, and that a rank with one power can
   make an enemy of another.

## Running This Chapter

### What happens

1. **The road south (Scene 1).** Three days on the good road of Gaunt.
   At the border, a press gang of Gaunt takes the young men of a village.
   This is the first fight, or the first hard talk.
2. **Stannard (Scene 2).** The town of soldiers. The Gate of the Five,
   with statues of the Five in Gaunt armor. Captain Marrick and Lieutenant
   Roe meet the party.
3. **The Marshal's Hall (Scene 3).** Marshal Ide Gauntry makes her offer.
   Teach Factions and renown here.
4. **Tobin (Scene 4).** The Marshal's son shows the party what the offer
   really means: a war with the Church, this spring.
5. **The College of War (Scene 5).** The war mages show their "Dawnwatch
   spell". It goes wrong. This is the thread of the Spark, and the second
   fight.
6. **The Proving (Scene 6).** The Marshal tests the party on the drill
   field, in front of her army.
7. **The night of the knives (Scene 7).** The Hush try to kill Tobin, and
   to make it look like the Church did it. This is the last fight.
8. **The banner (Scene 8).** The party chooses what to be in Gaunt.

### Gaunt

Use these facts in every scene. The gazetteer has more.

- **Gaunt is a state of soldiers.** The Gauntry family held the south for
  the king of Ostrey. When Ostrey fell, the family kept its army and
  became a state. Every adult has a weapon. Every family has a soldier.
- **Gaunt says that it is the true heir of Ostrey.** It is not, but it is
  the nearest thing. It keeps the old roads, the old ranks, and the old
  drill. Its soldiers march as the soldiers of Ostrey marched, and the
  party will see that.
- **Gaunt has about three thousand soldiers and twenty war mages.** The
  Church has a guard of about four hundred. Gaunt could take Saint Ivo's
  in a week. Everyone knows it. Nobody says it.
- **Gaunt is not evil.** Its roads are safe. Its people eat well. Its
  laws are hard and fair. The Marshal believes that the land needs one
  strong hand, and that hers is the strongest. In a land where the days
  are getting shorter, many people agree with her.
- **Gaunt wants the pieces of the Dawnfire as weapons.** It does not know
  what they do. It knows that the Church has one, and that the Hush
  wanted it. That is enough.

### Lieutenant Roe

The Marshal gives the party an aide in Stannard: **Lieutenant Roe**, a
young officer with a friendly face, a quick smile, and a talent for
finding things. He is very helpful. He is also a secret agent of the
Hush. Nothing in this chapter reveals it, and nothing should. Chapter 9
unmasks him.

> **GM tip:** Play Roe as the most likable person in Stannard. He brings
> the party food, finds them good rooms, warns them about the Marshal's
> moods, and laughs at Wick's jokes. In Scene 7, he "finds" the false
> clue that blames the Church. Do not draw attention to it. When the
> players learn the truth in chapter 9, they should remember all the times
> he helped, and feel cold.

### The two factions

This chapter uses two factions from the Factions and Renown chapter of the
Game Master's Compendium: **Gaunt** and **the Church of the Five
Sleepers**. Their full entries are in Scene 3. Each character has a
renown score with each faction, from 0 to 50.

### Renown from Act I

The party did a lot in Act I, and both factions noticed. Before this
chapter starts, give each character renown for the deeds of chapters 3 and
4. Use the table. A character who was not part of a deed does not get its
renown.

| Deed in Act I | Faction | Renown |
|---|---|---|
| Stood at the Waking Service when the Ember flared | The Church | +1 |
| Saved Garret Pell from the Hush | The Church | +1 |
| Stopped Dace from stealing bones under the cathedral | The Church | +1 |
| Brought the Ember of Saint Ivo home from Kell | The Church | +5 |
| Gave the Ember back to the Abbess | The Church | +2 |
| Kept the Ember against the wishes of the Abbess | The Church | -2 |
| Gave the sealed letter of the Marshal to Captain Marrick or to the Abbess (chapter 2) | Gaunt | +1 |
| Answered Captain Marrick politely at the Saints' House | Gaunt | +1 |

Most characters start this chapter with about 8 to 10 renown with the
Church, and 0 to 2 with Gaunt. A character with 3 or more is a Friend of
the faction. With 10 or more, a Member. Scene 3 explains the ranks.

> **GM tip:** If you start Book One at chapter 5, give each character 5
> renown with the Church and 1 with Gaunt.

## Scene 1: The Road South (1 hour)

**Goal:** Show the land of Gaunt before the party sees Stannard. The first
fight, or the first hard choice.

### Why the party goes

At the start of the chapter, a rider of Gaunt brings a letter to the
Saints' House in Saint Ivo's. Give the players the handout.

{{handout: 05-the-marshals-letter}}

The party can go or not. If it does not want to go, let other people push:

- **The Abbess** asks them to go. "Go and see what she wants. Tell me what
  you see. If she means to march on us, I would like to know before she
  does it." This is a small service for the Church: +1 renown with the
  Church when the party reports back.
- **Brother Cade** wants to go too, but the Abbess will not let him. "Watch
  their war mages," he says. "Every report I have says they are bad at
  magic and good at fire."
- **Wick** is curious. "Gauntry? The March of Gauntry? I knew a Gauntry!
  Big man. Big beard. Shouted at horses. I wonder if they still shout at
  horses."

### The journey

Stannard is three days south of Saint Ivo's, on a good road that Gaunt
built. Use the Journeys rules, but quickly: the road is safe and easy.

| Day | Where | Navigation DC | Foraging DC | Event |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The pilgrim road south, then open farms | 5 | 8 | The toll gate |
| 2 | The border, and the village of Wendle | 5 | 8 | The press gang |
| 3 | The good road of Gaunt | 5 | 8 | Stannard |

Use the Roads and Farmland biome of the Journeys chapter for the weather
and the encounters.

> **Teach: the systems stay on.** "We use the Journeys rules again, but
> this road is easy. I roll for all three days now, and we only stop where
> something happens."

**The toll gate.** On the first day, the party crosses into Gaunt at a
stone gate across the road, with a tower and a bored sergeant. The toll
is 1 SP a head, and a pass to enter Gaunt, which costs 1 SP and lasts a
week. With the letter of the Marshal, the toll is free, the sergeant
stands up very straight, and he sends a rider ahead to Stannard. "They
are coming," the rider says to everyone he passes. By the end of the day,
every village on the road knows.

### The press gang at Wendle

On the second day, the road passes through Wendle, a village of farms on
the border. Wendle is in Gaunt by a mile. Its people pray at Saint Ivo's.

> The village is quiet when you ride in. Too quiet. On the green, a line
> of young men and boys stands with their hands tied, with soldiers of
> Gaunt on each side. A woman screams at a sergeant on a horse. The
> sergeant does not look at her.
>
> "By order of the Marshal," the sergeant calls out, "for the spring
> muster. Every family of the March gives one son. Wendle has given none
> in three years. Today, Wendle pays."

This is a press gang. The law of Gaunt says that each family gives one
soldier. Wendle hid its sons for three years, because they think of
themselves as the Church's people. The sergeant has the law on his side,
and twelve boys in a line. The youngest is thirteen.

The leader of the press gang is a hard, honest veteran of Gaunt: a
**Shield-Wall Captain** called Sergeant Hask. He has two **Crossbow
Sergeants** and four **Line Pikemen**. He does not like this job. He does
it anyway.

### What the party can do

This is a choice between the law of Gaunt and the people of the Church.
It is the first lesson of the chapter: every choice is seen by somebody.

| What the party does | Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shows the letter of the Marshal and asks Hask to wait | Charisma (Persuasion) | 15 | Hask waits one day for orders from Stannard. In Scene 3, the party can ask the Marshal to let the boys go. |
| Argues the law: the youngest boys are under the age of the muster | Intelligence (History) or Wisdom (Insight) | 13 | The law of Gaunt says sixteen. Hask did not check. He lets the five youngest go, and he is ashamed. |
| Offers to pay the fine of the village | 50 GP for each boy | None | The law allows a fine. Hask takes it, writes a receipt, and lets the boys go. 600 GP for all twelve. |
| Says: "We are the Five. Let them go." | Charisma (Intimidation) | 18 | Hask believes them, and he is afraid. He lets the boys go, and reports it. The Marshal hears of it in Scene 3. |
| Frees the boys by force | None | None | The fight below. |

**Renown.** Tell the players when renown changes, and why.

- Freeing the boys by any means: +1 renown with the Church for each
  character there (the village tells the Church).
- Freeing them by force: also -2 renown with Gaunt. Hask reports it.
- Freeing them by law or by money: no change with Gaunt. Hask respects it.
- Doing nothing: no change. Wendle remembers. In chapter 9, these boys are
  soldiers of Gaunt at Ashby Field.

> **Teach: renown.** "Each power in the land keeps a score of how much it
> trusts you. It is called renown. You just freed the boys, so the Church
> likes you more. You fought soldiers of Gaunt to do it, so Gaunt likes
> you less. I will always tell you when it changes, and why."

not murderers: they do not attack a creature at 0 Hit Points, and they
surrender when Hask falls or when half of them are down.

## Scene 2: Stannard (30 minutes)

**Goal:** Show the town of soldiers. Meet the people who will look after
the party.

> On the third day, the road climbs out of the farms, and you see
> Stannard. A great rock stands over the south road, and on the rock is a
> fortress that you know: the old seat of the Gauntry family, where the
> March held the border for the king. New walls ring it now, three of
> them, each lower than the last, and inside them a whole town climbs the
> slope in neat streets.
>
> Below the town, on a wide field, an army drills. Thousands of soldiers
> in red coats, in blocks, all in step. A drum beats, and they turn, all
> together, like one animal. The sound of their feet comes to you a moment
> late.
>
> The Shield of the Dawnwatch knows that drill. It is the drill of Ostrey.
> Nobody has used it in two hundred years. Somebody kept it.

### The Gate of the Five

The main gate of Stannard has five statues on top of it, as tall as two
men: the Five Sleepers, in the armor of Gaunt, with the iron gauntlet on
their shields. Under them, words cut in the stone: THE FIVE WERE OF THE
MARCH.

They were not. None of the Dawnwatch came from the March of Gauntry. A
character who makes a DC 10 Intelligence (History) check knows it. Wick
says it out loud: "That is a lie. That is a lie carved in stone. Who
carves a lie in stone? It takes so long!"

> **GM tip:** This is the lesson of Gaunt in one picture. Gaunt honors the
> Dawnwatch, and it uses them. The Church did the same with halos. Gaunt
> does it with shields. Let the players see that each power makes the
> Dawnwatch into what it needs.

### The welcome

At the gate, Captain Joss Marrick waits for the party with a guard of
honor, twenty soldiers in perfect lines. He salutes. The soldiers salute.
The crowd behind them cheers, but not like the pilgrims in Saint Ivo's.
These people cheer like soldiers cheer a general.

Captain Marrick takes the party up through the town to the Marshal's Hall.
On the way, he introduces their aide.

> A young officer jogs up beside you with a big smile and a stack of
> papers. "Lieutenant Roe," he says, and bows to each of you, and to Wick,
> very seriously. "I am to look after you while you are in Stannard. Rooms,
> food, passes, anything. If you need something, ask me. If you need
> something you should not have, also ask me." He winks. "Only not in
> front of the captain."

### Stannard

The party has a day before the Marshal sees them. Let them walk the town.
Choose two or three of these.

- **The market** closes at sunset by law. At the last bell, every stall
  shuts at once, and the square is empty in a minute.
- **Every street has a number.** The party lodges at "Third Wall, Street of
  the Lancers, Number Nine". Lieutenant Roe found it. It has the best
  baths in Stannard.
- **The children drill.** On a small square, twenty children with wooden
  spears march in step. A girl of ten shouts the orders.
- **The forges** never stop. They make spearheads, horseshoes, and helmets,
  day and night. A smith shows the party a helmet: it is the shape of a
  helmet of Ostrey, exactly. "We kept the patterns," he says proudly.
- **The shrine of the Church** in Stannard is small and quiet. Its priest,
  an old woman, is nervous. She asks if it is true that the Sleepers will
  march with the army. "Against whom?" she asks.

> **GM tip:** Show that Gaunt works. The streets are clean, the people are
> fed, and the children are safe. In a land with short summers, that is a
> lot. A player who likes order may like Gaunt very much. That is fine.
> The chapter does not tell the players that Gaunt is wrong. It shows them
> what it costs.

## Scene 3: The Marshal's Hall (45 minutes)

**Goal:** Meet the Marshal. Hear her offer. Teach Factions and renown.

> The great hall of the fortress is long and high, and cold. Banners hang
> from the beams, a hundred of them, faded and torn and stained: the
> banners of every war of Gaunt. At the far end, one pole stands empty,
> taller than the others, with a gold sun on top.
>
> Under the empty pole, a woman stands with her hands behind her back. She
> is tall, sixty, with grey braids and a soldier's back, in a plain red
> coat with no gold on it at all. She looks at each of you in turn, the way
> a sergeant looks at new recruits.
>
> "Light to you," says Marshal Ide Gauntry.

**Marshal Ide Gauntry** is the ruler of Gaunt. She is direct, sharp,
proud, and honest in her way. She has ruled for twenty years and never
lost a battle. Play her as the opposite of the Abbess: she says exactly
what she thinks, at once.

{{statblock: Marshal Ide Gauntry}}

### What the Marshal knows and wants

- **She knows that the party is real.** Captain Marrick saw the Ember
  flare. She believes her captain.
- **She wants them as banners.** "Stand with Gaunt at the spring muster,
  under the banner of Ostrey, and every soldier in the land will know who
  the heir of Ostrey is."
- **She wants the pieces of the Dawnfire.** "The Hush wanted the Ember. If
  the Hush want it, it is a weapon. A weapon should be with soldiers, not
  priests." If the party has the Ember, she asks for it, plainly. If the
  party says no, she accepts it, for now.
- **She does not want a war with the Church.** She says so, and she means
  it. She also says: "The Church cannot protect the Ember, or the pilgrims,
  or itself. Somebody must. If the Abbess asks for my help, she will have
  it."
- **She offers a lot.** Titles: "Knights of the March". Land: a manor each
  in Gaunt. Soldiers: a company of fifty to command. Money: 500 GP each,
  now, as a gift.

> **GM tip:** The Marshal is not lying. She believes that Gaunt is the
> best hope of the land. She is ready to take Saint Ivo's "to protect it".
> She has not said so, even to herself. Tobin shows the party in Scene 4.

### The Proving

The Marshal does not give titles for stories. "I know what the Church says
about you. Tomorrow, my army will see for itself." She asks the party to
take part in the Proving, on the drill field, in front of the army. That
is Scene 6.

### Teach: Factions and renown

This is the moment to teach the system. The Marshal just made an offer
that has numbers in it.

> **Teach: Factions and renown.** "The big powers of the land are
> factions: Gaunt, the Church, later others. Each of you has a renown
> score with each faction, from 0 to 50. Deeds for a faction raise it.
> Deeds against it lower it. At 3, 10, 20, 35, and 50, you reach a new
> rank, and the faction gives you more: help, gear, soldiers, a seat at
> its table. From the third rank on, the faction asks for a special task
> first."

> **Teach: rivals.** "Gaunt and the Church are rivals. A faction watches
> its rival closely. When you reach the third rank with one of them, the
> other treats you as an enemy. You can be friends with both for a long
> time. You cannot be a leader in both."

Give each player a small card, or a corner of the character sheet, for
renown. Tell each player the score that they have from Act I (see "Renown
from Act I", above). The players can see their scores at all times. Renown
is never a secret.

> **GM tip:** Use the table rule from the Compendium if your players want
> it: Party Renown, one score for the whole party with each faction. It is
> simpler. This book gives renown to each character, but it works the same
> with one score for the party.

**The promotion tasks of the Church.** For the third rank, the Abbess asks

day, and bless the pilgrims. For the fourth, to bring back a holy thing
that was lost. For the fifth, to speak for the Church at the council of a
rival power.

> **GM tip:** The Sleepers are the saints of the Church. That makes
> renown with the Church strange, and funny. A living saint is a Friend of
> the Church at 3 renown. Lean into it. "You are the Sleepers. You are also,
> technically, at the rank of Friend."

### The boys of Wendle

If the party asked Sergeant Hask to wait (Scene 1), it can ask the Marshal
to free the boys of Wendle. She thinks about it.

> "The law is the law," she says. "But the law says sixteen, and my
> sergeant took boys of thirteen. That is his shame and mine." She writes
> an order. "The young ones go home. The others serve, as the law says.
> And you owe me a favor, Sleepers. I always collect."

The party can argue for all twelve. A character who makes a DC 18 Charisma
(Persuasion) check wins all of them, and the Marshal laughs for the first
time. "You argue like my son."

## Scene 4: Tobin (30 minutes)

**Goal:** Meet the Marshal's son. Show the war that is coming.

Tobin Gauntry finds the party on the first evening, in the baths, in the
stables, or on the wall. He comes alone, without guards.

> A young man of twenty climbs up beside you. He wears the red coat of
> Gaunt, but it does not fit him well, and his armor is very new. He has
> his mother's grey eyes and none of her certainty.
>
> "Tobin," he says. "Her son. You met her. I am sorry." He looks out over
> the drill field, where the torches of the night watch march in lines.
> "Can I ask you something? Did the old kingdom have a lot of wars?"

**Tobin Gauntry** is the Marshal's son and heir. He is kind, clever,
nervous, and very brave in a quiet way. He has read every book in the
College of War. He does not want a war. He has never been in one.

### What Tobin knows

Tobin tells the party one thing at a time, as he learns to trust them. Use
the table.

| What the party does | Check | DC | What Tobin says |
|---|---|---|---|
| Answers his question about the wars honestly | None | None | He relaxes. He asks what a real battle is like. A character who tells him the truth has a friend for life. |
| Asks him what his mother plans | Charisma (Persuasion) | 12 | The spring muster is not a muster. It is a march. The army goes north to Saint Ivo's in the spring, "to protect the Church from the Hush". |
| Asks him if that is true | Wisdom (Insight) on Tobin | 10 | Tobin does not believe it. He thinks that his mother believes it. |
| Asks to see the plans | Charisma (Persuasion) | 15 | Tobin takes the party to the map room of the hall at night. On the big table, small wooden blocks show the army on the road north, and a red block on Saint Ivo's. The blocks are dated: the first week of spring. |
| Asks what he wants | None | None | "I want to find another way. I do not know one. I thought perhaps you might." |

> **GM tip:** Tobin is the best friend the party can have in Gaunt. In
> chapter 9, he begs the party to stop the war. Make him real now: let him
> ask questions about Ostrey, let him be bad at riding, let him laugh at
> Wick's songs. If the players like Tobin, chapter 9 has a heart.

### What the party can do with it

- **Tell the Abbess.** A letter to Saint Ivo's, by a fast rider. +1
  renown with the Church. If Gaunt learns of the letter, -2 renown with
  Gaunt. Lieutenant Roe offers to send it for them. He does. He also reads
  it first. Do not tell the players.
- **Talk to the Marshal.** She does not deny it. "The Church cannot
  protect itself. The Hush walked into its holiest place and walked out
  with its holiest thing. In the spring, I will protect it, whether it
  wants protection or not." A character can make her doubt (DC 20 Charisma
  (Persuasion)). On a success, she promises to wait until the party has
  dealt with the Hush. She keeps the promise until chapter 9.
- **Keep it secret, for now.** Tobin asks them to. "Give me time. I will
  talk to her. She listens to me, sometimes."
