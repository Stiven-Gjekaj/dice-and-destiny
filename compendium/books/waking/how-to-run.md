# How to Run This Book

This book is a campaign for Dice & Destiny. It takes a group of characters
from level 1 to level 10, in ten chapters. It starts with the tutorial
adventure, The Last Watch of the Sleepers, and continues the story from the
moment the Dawn Door opens.

This section is for the GM. Read it before you start. It tells you the full
story, with all of its secrets, so that you can plan ahead and drop hints
at the right time.

## The Story in Short

Three hundred years ago, the kingdom of Ostrey fought a long war against
Malgrath, the Unlit King. At the end of the war, the kingdom put the last
five knights of the Dawnwatch to sleep in crystal under Dawnwatch Keep.
They were to wake on the day that he came back.

That day is now. The characters are the Dawnwatch. They wake, they climb
out of the keep with Wick, a talking brass lantern, and they find a world
that forgot them. Ostrey fell two hundred years ago. A church prays to
them as saints. A state of soldiers wants them as banners. A city of
merchants wants their secrets. And a cult in grey wants the Unlit King
to wake.

The players think that they know the history. The player's guide tells
it: a demon king came out of the dark, and Ostrey pushed him back. This is
a lie. Keep the lie safe until chapter 10.

### The truth (for the GM only)

- Malgrath was **Sir Garrath**, the first knight of the Dawnwatch. About
  forty years before the sleep, he saw that the sun grew weaker each
  winter. He tried to take the fire of the sun into himself to save it,
  and it changed him. He became the Unlit King.
- Ostrey could not kill him. Steward **Haskel Orme** of Dawnwatch Keep put
  him to sleep in crystal under Kingsholm. Then Orme put the last five
  Dawnwatch to sleep under the keep, with the same spell, to wait. Orme
  wrote the official history: Ostrey "pushed the king back into the
  dark". People called Garrath "Mal Garrath", the bad Garrath. Over three
  hundred years, the name wore down to "Malgrath".
- The Dawnfire kept Garrath asleep. Ostrey split it into pieces and kept
  them in shrines. The Hush put out the piece in the chapel of the keep,
  and the crystal cracked. Wick is the last true piece of the Dawnfire.
  Nobody knows this in Book One.
- The sun really is dying. Garrath thinks that a sun that goes out and
  burns again can last. Book Two tells this. Book One only hints at it.

### The theme and the tone

The theme is **heroes who made a promise to a world that forgot them.**
Each chapter asks the players the same question in a new form: you swore
to protect these people, and they do not know you, they do not need you,
and some of them want to use you. Do you keep the promise?

The campaign starts light and spooky, as the tutorial does. Wick jokes,
the coins are three hundred years old, and a troupe of actors plays the
characters badly. It becomes darker as the days become shorter. It ends
with hope. A good session of this book has one laugh, one strange old
thing, and one choice.

## The Chapters

Each chapter is one part of the story and one region of the land. The
characters go up one level at the end of each chapter.

| Chapter | Name | Levels | Place | System that starts | Sessions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Last Watch | 1 to 2 | Dawnwatch Keep | Bonds (optional) | 1 |
| 2 | Brannock's Ford | 2 to 3 | A village and the road south | Journeys | 2 |
| 3 | Saint Ivo's | 3 to 4 | The town of the Church | None (a social chapter) | 2 to 3 |
| 4 | The Hush | 4 to 5 | The old shrine at Kell | Marks | 2 to 3 |
| 5 | Gaunt | 5 to 6 | Stannard and the land of Gaunt | Factions and Renown | 3 |
| 6 | Corrantey | 6 to 7 | The city of merchants | Crafting and Gathering | 3 |
| 7 | The Keep Again | 7 to 8 | Dawnwatch Keep | Strongholds | 3 |
| 8 | Fennick Under the Water | 8 to 9 | A drowned town in the Low Fen | Techniques | 3 to 4 |
| 9 | Three Banners | 9 to 10 | Ashby Field | Injuries and Scars | 3 |
| 10 | The Empty Crystal | 10 | Kingsholm, the old capital | None (the end) | 3 to 4 |

A session in this table is about four hours. The full book is about 25 to
30 sessions. Most groups need a year of play, one session each week or two.

### The two acts

- **Act I: Waking (chapters 1 to 4).** The characters wake, see the new
  land, meet the Church, and learn that the Hush exists. The act ends at
  Kell, when the party learns that Malgrath can feel where the black stone
  is.
- **Act II: The Ashes of the Kingdom (chapters 5 to 10).** The powers of
  the land compete for the Dawnwatch. The party finds the pieces of the
  Dawnfire, takes back the keep, and stops a war. The act ends in
  Kingsholm with a victory and the twist.

### The order of the chapters

Chapters 1 to 4 go in order. In Act II, chapters 5 and 6 can go in either
order: the party can go to Gaunt first or to Corrantey first. Change the
order of the levels if you do. Chapter 7 needs the party to know about the
Dawnfire pieces. Chapters 8, 9, and 10 go in order.

If the players go somewhere that no chapter covers, use the gazetteer. It
has each place of the land, rumors, and short adventure seeds. A side trip
gives no level unless it is as big as a chapter.

## Milestone Levels

This book uses milestone levels. Do not count experience points. At the
end of each chapter, the text says: "The characters go to level N." The
characters go up a level at the next Long Rest.

- The encounters use the XP budgets of Building Encounters in the Game
  Master's Compendium, for the level at the start of the chapter.
- Each encounter has a **Scaling** box for 3, 4, 5, and 6 characters.
- If the party is much stronger or weaker than the encounters expect, move
  one encounter up or down one step of difficulty. Do not change the
  levels.
- At level 5, the characters become much stronger. Chapter 5 is written
  for that jump.

Milestone levels are in Levels, XP & Progression in the Game Master's
Compendium.

## One System at a Time

Dice & Destiny has eight optional systems. This book teaches seven of them,
one in each chapter, when the story needs it. The tutorial teaches the
core rules in the same way. Book Two adds the last one, Legend Paths.

| Chapter | System | Why it starts here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bonds | The characters rest together for the first time and talk. |
| 2 | Journeys | Three days on a broken road to Saint Ivo's. |
| 4 | Marks | The black stone marks the character who carries it. |
| 5 | Factions and Renown | Gaunt and the Church both want the party. |
| 6 | Crafting and Gathering | The Lampwrights make items from what the party brings. |
| 7 | Strongholds | The party takes back Dawnwatch Keep as its home. |
| 8 | Techniques | A ghost of Ostrey teaches the old fighting ways. |
| 9 | Injuries and Scars | The first true battle, on Ashby Field. |

Each chapter has a **Teach** box when its system starts. The box tells you
how to explain the system in one minute. The full rules are in the
Player's Compendium and the Game Master's Compendium, in the chapter of
each system.

### If you do not want a system

Each system is optional. If your group does not want it, skip the Teach
box and run the scene with the core rules:

- **Journeys:** say how many days the trip takes, and run one encounter on
  the road.
- **Marks:** the black stone still hums and still draws the Hush. It gives
  no Mark.
- **Factions and Renown:** keep a short note of who likes the party and who
  does not.
- **Crafting and Gathering:** the Lampwrights sell the items instead.
- **Strongholds:** the keep is a safe place to rest, with no rules for
  rooms or staff.
- **Techniques:** the ghost of Fennick gives each character one uncommon
  magic item from the Game Master's Compendium instead.
- **Injuries and Scars:** use the normal rules for 0 Hit Points.

The story does not change. Only the extra rules go away.

### Too many systems at once

A group that plays all seven systems has a lot to track by chapter 9. Give
each system one moment in each session, not more. If the players forget a
system, it is fine. Remind them when it matters, or let it go.

## Starting the Campaign

### With new players

Run the tutorial, The Last Watch of the Sleepers, as chapter 1. It takes
about two hours and teaches the core rules, one rule in each scene. Use
the five pregenerated characters. At the end, the characters go to level
2. Then continue with chapter 2.

New players can keep the pregenerated characters for the whole book. Each
one has a "Before" question and a thread in a later chapter. When a new
player knows the game better, around level 3 or 4, let the player change
the subclass or the feats at the next level, if the player wants to.

### With experienced players

Players who know the game can make their own characters. You have three
ways to start:

1. **Run the tutorial fast.** Skip the Teach boxes. Run it in about one
   hour as a prologue, then go on to chapter 2 in the same session.
2. **Run the tutorial with harder fights.** Use the High column of the
   Scaling table for both fights, and add one Giant Rat for each player in
   the armory. Keep the story.
3. **Start at chapter 2.** Read the summary below aloud. The characters
   start at level 2, at the Dawn Door, with the black stone and one Scroll
   of the Last Watch each.

> You slept for a very long time. You woke in crystal, under Dawnwatch
> Keep. A small brass lantern named Wick led you up through rats, iron
> teeth, and a pack of fire dogs, to a white door with a sun carved on it.
> You pushed it open. Outside, the keep is a ruin, and trees grow where
> roads were. In the north, a black cloud stood over the mountains, and a
> voice spoke in your head: "Sleepers. I also slept. And now we are both
> awake." In your pocket, a small black stone hums.

Before the first session, give the players the player's guide. With
option 3, also ask each player the "Before" question of the nearest
pregenerated character (see below), and write the answers down.

### The players' own characters

Each character is one of the last knights of the Dawnwatch, no matter
what class or species the player chooses. Help each player answer three
questions:

- **What was your task in the Dawnwatch?** The pregenerated characters are
  the captain, the healer, the mage, the scout, and the muscle. A new
  character can take one of those tasks or a new one: the herald, the
  standard bearer, the quartermaster.
- **Where in Ostrey are you from?** Use the player's guide. A character
  from Fennick, Kingsholm, the March, or Corrin and Tey gets more out of the
  chapter in that place.
- **What is your Before?** Choose the nearest "Before" question from the
  table below.

### More or fewer than five

The Church of the Five Sleepers knows that there were five. If your party
is not five, use it:

- **Fewer than five.** The other crystals are in the Hall of Sleepers,
  empty or broken. Where are the others? Nobody knows. In Saint Ivo's, the
  Church has a statue for each of the five. One of them has a face that a
  character knows. The question can stay open for all of Book One.
- **More than five.** The Church counts wrong. Its art shows five. The
  Abbess says that the sixth must be an impostor. Brother Cade agrees with
  her, loudly. This makes chapter 3 more fun, not less.

## The Before and After Threads

In the tutorial, each player answers a "Before" question about the last
night before the long sleep. At the Dawn Door, each player answers an
"After" question. These answers are the most important notes that you
keep. Write them down in the first session, and keep them with this book.

Each of five chapters has a **Thread** box for one of the answers. In that
chapter, the answer comes back, changed by three hundred years.

| Character | Before question (short) | Chapter | What comes back |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Shadow | One last thing stolen | 2 | The stolen thing is now an old treasure. A collector wants it. |
| The Light | A sign from a god | 3 | The sign is in a church window, made 200 years after the sleep. |
| The Spark | A spell on the first day | 5 | The war mages of Gaunt teach it as a "Dawnwatch spell", and cast it wrong. |
| The Storm | Why the character agreed to sleep | 8 | The reason is in Fennick, under the water. |
| The Shield | A promise to someone | 9 | A family that came from the person of the promise is in the war. |

### How to use a thread

- **Use the words of the player.** If the Shadow stole "a silver comb from
  a duchess", the collector has a silver comb, and the duchess is in a
  history book. Do not change the answer to fit the chapter. Change the
  chapter to fit the answer.
- **Tell the player before the session.** A short word is enough: "Your
  stolen thing comes back this week." The player can think about it. It is
  not a spoiler. It is a gift.
- **Give the scene time.** A thread scene is short, about ten minutes, but
  it should not happen in the middle of a fight.
- **End with a choice.** The player chooses what to do with the past: keep
  it, sell it, let it go, or fight for it.

### Threads for the players' own characters

For a character that a player made, use the box with the nearest
question. If two characters share a question, use the thread for one of
them and move the other to a later chapter. If a "Before" answer fits no
box, make a short scene of your own in the chapter whose place matches it
best. The gazetteer tells you what each place is like today.

### The After answers

The After answers tell you how each player feels about the new world. Use
them to choose which hooks to push. A player who said "it was worth it"
wants to protect the new land. A player who said "everything is gone"
wants to find what is left. Give each one a scene of that kind in Act I.

## The Threads of Book One

Four threads go through all ten chapters. Keep them in mind when you
improvise.

### The black stone

The party finds one black stone in the tutorial. It is a piece of the
crystal of Garrath. It hums near the Hush and when Malgrath speaks. In
chapter 4 it gets rules: the bearer gets a Mark that grows each time the
bearer uses the stone. The Hush wants it. Malgrath can feel where it is.
Chapter 10 tells what it is. Do not let the party lose the stone before
chapter 10.

### The pieces of the Dawnfire

Book One finds three pieces of the Dawnfire: the Ember of Saint Ivo
(chapters 3 and 4), the First Lamp (chapter 6), and the piece in Fennick
(chapter 8). The Hush wants to put each one out. Gaunt wants each one as a
weapon. The Church wants each one as a relic. The party must decide who
keeps them.

Wick reacts to each piece. His flame leans toward it, like a plant to the
sun. Wick does not know why, and he says so. Use this. Do not explain it.

### The steward

Haskel Orme made Wick, put the Dawnwatch to sleep, and wrote the lie. In
chapter 6, the Lampwrights see his mark on Wick. In chapter 7, the party
finds his closed study in the keep, and his first letters. In chapter 10,
his last letter tells the truth. The letters are handouts. Each one shows
a little more of the lie.

Until chapter 7, the steward is a kind memory: the voice in the Dawnwatch
Oath, the box with the potion, the scrolls in the pockets. Let the players
love him a little. It makes the letters hit harder.

### The voice in the north

Malgrath speaks only through the black stone in Book One. He speaks
rarely, three or four times in the whole book: in the tutorial, in chapter
4, and in chapter 10. He is never cruel and never loud. He speaks like a
tired older knight who knows the characters. Each time, he says something
that is true, and the characters cannot yet understand why it is true.

## Keeping the Secret

The twist works only if the players believe the lie. Some rules help:

- **Everybody in the land believes the lie.** The Church, Gaunt, the
  Lampwrights, and even the Hush tell the story of the demon king. The
  Hush worships him, but it does not know who he was.
- **Wick believes the lie.** Wick learned it from the steward himself.
- **Hint, but do not tell.** These hints are safe: the black stone feels
  like the crystal of the Hall of Sleepers; Malgrath says "we" and "sleepers";
  the goliaths of the Grey Teeth call the Unlit King "the one who ate the
  fire"; a statue in Kingsholm has its face cut off. The gazetteer marks
  each hint. A sharp player can guess. That is
  fine. A guess is not proof, and chapter 10 still lands.
- **Do not show the name Garrath before chapter 10.** If a player asks
  about the founders of the Dawnwatch, Wick does not know, and the old
  records are lost.
- **The player's guide and the handouts are safe to give out.** They tell
  the lie, and nothing about the world of today.

## Wick at the Table

Wick is with the party for the whole campaign. He is a friend, a light,
and a guide. He is also a danger to your game, because a guide who knows
things can take the choices away from the players.

- **Wick knows the old world, not the new one.** He is as lost as the
  characters are. Everything that changed in three hundred years is news
  to him, and he takes it badly.
- **Wick does not solve problems.** He has no hands. He can give light, a
  memory, a joke, or a bad idea.
- **Wick gives a rule in the world.** When a chapter starts a system, Wick
  can explain it: "The steward always said, a long road is three short
  roads and a sore foot."
- **Wick gets darker as the light goes.** In Act I, Wick is proud and
  funny. In Act II, he is often quiet, and he asks the characters if they
  are sure. After the letters in chapter 7, he is hurt and angry. Let him
  be. He comes back to himself, a little older.
- **Wick is never a weapon.** Enemies do not attack him, as in the
  tutorial. The Hush wants to put him out, but only as one more light. Do
  not let the Hush capture Wick in Book One.

## Safety Tools

This book has war, a cult, drowned towns, ghosts, fanatics, injuries, and
the loss of everybody that the characters knew. Most of it is spooky
adventure, not horror. Still, talk about it before you start.

### Session zero

Before chapter 2, spend fifteen minutes with the players. Session Zero in
the Game Master's Compendium tells you how. For this book, ask these
questions:

- **The tone.** "This campaign starts light and becomes darker, but it
  ends with hope. Is that good for everybody?"
- **Lines and veils.** A line is a thing that does not happen in the game.
  A veil is a thing that happens off the scene: the story says that it
  happened, but you do not describe it. Write the answers down.
- **The themes of this book.** Tell the players about the themes below,
  and ask if any of them is a problem.

### What is in this book

| Theme | Where | How strong |
|---|---|---|
| Loss and grief | All chapters, the threads | Strong. Everybody that the characters knew is dead. |
| Religion used for power | Chapters 3, 5, 9 | Medium. A church and a state use faith as a tool. |
| A cult | Chapters 4, 7, 10 | Medium. The Hush is quiet and sincere, not cruel. |
| Drowning and the dead | Chapter 8 | Medium. A drowned town, ghosts, deep water. |
| War and injuries | Chapter 9 | Strong. A battle, wounds that stay, soldiers who die. |
| Darkness and fear | All chapters | Light. The days become shorter. |
| Betrayal by a loved one | Chapters 7 and 10 | Medium. The steward lied to the characters. |

The book has no torture, no sexual violence, and no harm to children on
the scene. Do not add them.

### At the table

- **Pause.** As in the tutorial, any player can tap the table or say
  "pause" to stop a scene at any time. Nobody asks why. You go back,
  change the scene, or go on when everybody is ready.
- **Check in.** After a hard scene, such as a thread or a death, ask: "Is
  everybody good to continue?" Take a short break if anybody is not.
- **Fade to black.** Each scene can end before the hard part. Say "and the
  door closes", and go to the next morning.
- **Change the thread.** A Before answer can hurt more than a player
  expected. If a player does not want a thread scene, skip it, or let the
  player choose a different answer.

### Losing a character

From level 3, the characters are tough enough to face real danger. A
character can die in this book. If a character dies:

- The Church of the Five Sleepers will want the body as a relic. This can
  be a scene of its own.
- The player can bring in a new character. Book Two tells of a second
  group of sleepers that Ostrey hid in a different place. In Book One, use
  a crystal that the party finds in a ruin, or a person of today who joins
  the oath. Wick can give the oath to a new knight.
- The Dawnwatch Oath of the tutorial was a house rule for the first
  session. Do not use it again, unless your group wants a softer game.

## After Book One

Book One has a true end. In chapter 10, the party breaks the Hush and
defeats Mother Sallow. Then it finds the empty crystal under Kingsholm,
and learns the truth: Malgrath was the first Dawnwatch, and he is awake.
The last words of the book are his: "Now you know what we are."

A group can stop there. The characters saved the land from the cult, and
they know the truth. That is a full story.

A group that wants more goes on to Book Two, The Last Dawn, at level 11.
Book Two starts with the Long Night, the cloud from the north, and the last
optional system, Legend Paths. The Before and After answers come back
again in Book Two, so keep your notes.
