# Session 1: The Last Watch

This is the full adventure. Read it one time before you run it.

- [Pregenerated characters](../../pregens.md)
- [Statblocks](../../statblocks.md)
- [Player cheat sheet](../../cheat-sheet.md)

## How to use this file

- **Read-aloud text** is in quote blocks. Read it to the players, or say it
  in your own words.
- **Teach** boxes tell you to stop and explain a rule. Keep each explanation
  under one minute. Then let the players use the rule at once.
- **Wick** often gives a rule in the world. Wick is a talking lantern.
  When Wick explains a rule, the players learn it, and they stay in the story.
- Teach a rule only when the players need it. Do not explain all rules at the
  start.
- When a player asks "can I do X?", the answer is usually "yes, roll for it."

## Things to prepare

- One character sheet for each player (see the pregens).
- One cheat sheet for each player.
- Dice for each player. At least one d20 each.
- Paper and a pen for the map, or a battle map with tokens for the two fights.

---

## Scene 0: At the table (10 minutes)

**Goal:** The players know their character and the dice.

1. Give each player a pregen. Let them choose. If two players want the same
   one, roll a d20. The higher roll chooses.
2. Each player chooses a name and writes it on the sheet.
3. Show the dice. Say: "The d20 is the most important die. You roll it when
   the result is not certain."
4. Tell each player to read the box "Your best moves" on the sheet. Tell them
   to ignore everything else for now.
5. Ask each player to say the name of the character and one sentence about
   them. The sheet has a description that they can read aloud.

> **Teach: the one rule.** "You tell me what your character does. I tell you
> what happens. Sometimes I ask you to roll a die to see if it works."

The wizard player can cast *Mage Armor* now, for free. Tell the player to
write AC 14 on the sheet.

---

## Scene 1: The Hall of Sleepers (15 minutes)

**Goal:** Teach ability checks, the DC, advantage and disadvantage. Give the
first story moment, the "Before" flashback.

### The dream

Dim the lights if you can.

> You sleep. You sleep for a very, very long time. And while you sleep, you
> dream of the last night before the long sleep.

Go around the table. For each player, read the "Before question" from the
character sheet. Let the player answer in two or three sentences. Help with
small questions: "What did they look like?" "What did you say?"

> **Teach: Heroic Inspiration.** When a player gives a good answer, give
> that player Heroic Inspiration. Say: "That was great. You get Heroic
> Inspiration. One time, you can roll any die again. Keep it for a bad
> moment." The players can use a coin or a token as a marker.

Write down each answer. Scene 7 uses them again.

### The waking

> A crack. A sound like ice on a lake in spring. Cold air hits your face.
> You open your eyes.
>
> You lie in a coffin of blue crystal, in a round stone hall. Around you are
> four more crystal coffins. Lines of light go out from them across the
> floor, like the roots of a tree. The lights go out one by one.
>
> The crystal in front of your face has a crack in it. Your arms and legs feel
> like wood. You are very, very thirsty.

Say to each player: "Your coffin is cracked, but it is still closed. What do
you do to get out?"

Let each player describe a method. Then choose the ability that matches:

| What the player does | Check |
|----------------------|-------|
| Pushes or kicks the crystal | Strength (Athletics) |
| Finds the crack and twists out carefully | Dexterity (Acrobatics) |
| Uses magic or studies the crystal | Intelligence (Arcana) |
| Prays or stays calm and waits for the right moment | Wisdom |

The DC is 10.

> **Teach: the ability check.** "Roll the d20. Add the number next to
> Athletics on your sheet. Tell me the total. If the total is 10 or more, it
> works. The 10 is the Difficulty Class, or DC. You do not know the DC. I
> know it."

**On a success:** The player gets out in a cool way. Let the player describe
it.

**On a failure:** The player still gets out, but it goes badly. For example,
the player falls out on the floor face first, or gets stuck halfway. A
failure is not a stop. It is a funny problem.

> **Teach: advantage and disadvantage.** Use the second or third player for
> this. Choose one:
>
> - *Advantage:* A character who is already out helps a friend. Say: "Your
>   friend helps you, so you have advantage. Roll two d20s and use the
>   higher one."
> - *Disadvantage:* One coffin has a thick layer of ice. Say: "Your coffin
>   has more ice. You have disadvantage. Roll two d20s and use the lower
>   one."

**Solo play:** Wick helps the player from outside, so the player rolls with
advantage. Then Wick introduces itself (go to Scene 2).

### What is in the hall

When all characters are out:

> Your gear lies on stone shelves next to each coffin. It is dusty, but it is
> all there. Above the door, words are carved in the stone: "WE SLEEP SO THAT
> THE DAWN CAN WAKE."
>
> Then a small voice comes from a dark corner. "Oh! Oh, oh, oh. It worked. It
> actually worked."

## Scene 2: Wick (10 minutes)

**Goal:** Teach skills and social checks. Let the players talk to an NPC.

> In a niche in the wall sits a brass lantern. A small orange flame burns
> inside it. The flame has two black eyes and a mouth. The lantern looks at
> you and clears its throat, which is strange, because it has no throat.
>
> "Sleepers of the Dawnwatch! I am Wick, Keeper of the Light, Guardian of the
> Hall, and Official Waker. I waited for you for three hundred years, four
> months, and two days. I counted. There was not much else to do."

Play Wick as proud, dramatic, and lonely. See the Wick statblock for more
personality notes.

### What Wick knows

Let the players ask questions. Wick tells them these things, but slowly, with
a lot of drama:

- The Unlit King is back. Wick felt it this morning. "The Dawnfire in the
  chapel went out. That only happens if he wakes."
- The keep is old and partly broken. Small demons came in during the last
  years. "Ash mites. Like rats, but worse. They bite. They squeak. Rude."
- The way out goes down the Stair of Teeth, through the old armory, and
  through the Chapel of Dawn. The Dawn Door is at the end of the chapel. It
  opens to the surface.
- Something big now sleeps in the chapel. Wick does not want to talk about
  it.

### The secret

Wick hides two facts:

1. Wick is very scared of the thing in the chapel.
2. Wick does not know how long ago the last human visited the keep. "Some
   time ago" is the answer. The true answer is two hundred years.

> **Teach: skills.** When a player asks Wick a question and you think that
> Wick hides something, say: "You can make a Wisdom (Insight) check to see if
> Wick tells the truth. Insight is a skill. Look for Insight on your sheet.
> If you have a big number there, your character is good at it."

| Check | DC | Result |
|-------|----|--------|
| Wisdom (Insight) | 12 | Wick is scared. The flame gets small each time someone says "chapel." |
| Charisma (Persuasion) | 10 | Wick tells the truth: "It is a big dog. Made of ash. It breathes fire. I hate it." |
| Charisma (Intimidation) | 10 | Same, but Wick sulks for the rest of the scene. |
| Intelligence (History) | 10 | The character remembers Wick. The steward made Wick on the last night. The steward said, "Do not let them oversleep." |

> **Teach: roleplay comes first.** If a player is kind to Wick with good
> words, give a success with no roll. Say: "That was so good that you do not
> need to roll." This shows the players that good roleplay matters.

### The gifts

Before the characters leave, Wick says:

> "Wait! The steward left this for you." Wick nods at a small wooden box on
> the shelf. "I could not open it. I have no hands. It was a long three
> hundred years."

In the box is one **potion of healing** (red liquid, a glass bottle). A
character can drink it or give it to a creature within 5 feet as a bonus
action. That creature regains 2d4 + 2 HP.

Wick asks to come with the characters. One character carries Wick. Wick gives
light.

> **Teach: light and darkvision.** "Wick gives bright light for 20 feet. Some
> of you have darkvision on your sheet. You see in the dark, but only in grey.
> The others need Wick to see."
