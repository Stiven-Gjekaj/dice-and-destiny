# Session 1: The Last Watch

This is the full adventure. Read it one time before you run it.

- [Pregenerated characters](../../pregens.md)
- [Statblocks](../../statblocks.md)
- [Player cheat sheet](../../cheat-sheet.md)

## How to use this file

- **Read-aloud text** is in quote blocks. Read it to the players, or say it
  in your own words.
- **Teach** boxes tell you to stop and explain a rule. Keep each explanation
  under one minute. Then let the players use the rule at once.
- **Wick** often gives a rule in the world. Wick is a talking lantern.
  When Wick explains a rule, the players learn it, and they stay in the story.
- Teach a rule only when the players need it. Do not explain all rules at the
  start.
- When a player asks "can I do X?", the answer is usually "yes, roll for it."

## Things to prepare

- One character sheet for each player (see the pregens).
- One cheat sheet for each player.
- Dice for each player. At least one d20 each.
- Optional: paper and a pen for a map, or a battle map with tokens for the two
  fights. On a map, one square is 5 feet.

---

## Scene 0: At the table (10 minutes)

**Goal:** The players know their character and the dice.

1. Give each player a pregen. Let them choose. If two players want the same
   one, roll a d20. The higher roll chooses.
2. Each player chooses a name and writes it on the sheet.
3. Show the dice. Say: "The d20 is the most important die. You roll it when
   the result is not certain."
4. Tell each player to read the box "Your best moves" on the sheet. Tell them
   to ignore everything else for now.
5. Ask each player to say the name of the character and one sentence about
   them. The sheet has a description that they can read aloud.

> **Teach: the one rule.** "You tell me what your character does. I tell you
> what happens. Sometimes I ask you to roll a die to see if it works."

The wizard player can cast *Mage Armor* now, for free. Tell the player to
write AC 14 on the sheet.

## Scene 1: The Hall of Sleepers (15 minutes)

**Goal:** Teach ability checks, the DC, advantage and disadvantage. Give the
first story moment, the "Before" flashback.

### The dream

Dim the lights if you can.

> You sleep. You sleep for a very, very long time. And while you sleep, you
> dream of the last night before the long sleep.

Go around the table. For each player, read the "Before question" from the
character sheet. Let the player answer in two or three sentences. Help with
small questions: "What did they look like?" "What did you say?"

> **Teach: Heroic Inspiration.** When a player gives a good answer, give
> that player Heroic Inspiration. Say: "That was great. You get Heroic
> Inspiration. One time, you can roll any die again. Keep it for a bad
> moment." The players can use a coin or a token as a marker.

Write down each answer. Scene 7 uses them again.

### The waking

> A crack. A sound like ice on a lake in spring. Cold air hits your face.
> You open your eyes.
>
> You lie in a coffin of blue crystal, in a round stone hall. Around you are
> four more crystal coffins. Lines of light go out from them across the
> floor, like the roots of a tree. The lights go out one by one.
>
> The crystal in front of your face has a crack in it. Your arms and legs feel
> like wood. You are very, very thirsty.

Say to each player: "Your coffin is cracked, but it is still closed. What do
you do to get out?"

Let each player describe a method. Then choose the ability that matches:

| What the player does | Check |
|----------------------|-------|
| Pushes or kicks the crystal | Strength (Athletics) |
| Finds the crack and twists out carefully | Dexterity (Acrobatics) |
| Uses magic or studies the crystal | Intelligence (Arcana) |
| Prays or stays calm and waits for the right moment | Wisdom |

The DC is 10.

> **Teach: the ability check.** "Roll the d20. Add the number next to
> Athletics on your sheet. Tell me the total. If the total is 10 or more, it
> works. The 10 is the Difficulty Class, or DC. You do not know the DC. I
> know it."

**On a success:** The player gets out in a cool way. Let the player describe
it.

**On a failure:** The player still gets out, but it goes badly. For example,
the player falls out on the floor face first, or gets stuck halfway. A
failure is not a stop. It is a funny problem.

> **Teach: advantage and disadvantage.** Use the second or third player for
> this. Choose one:
>
> - *Advantage:* A character who is already out helps a friend. Say: "Your
>   friend helps you, so you have advantage. Roll two d20s and use the
>   higher one."
> - *Disadvantage:* One coffin has a thick layer of ice. Say: "Your coffin
>   has more ice. You have disadvantage. Roll two d20s and use the lower
>   one."

**Solo play:** Wick helps the player from outside, so the player rolls with
advantage. Then Wick introduces itself (go to Scene 2).

### What is in the hall

When all characters are out:

> Your gear lies on stone shelves next to each coffin. It is dusty, but it is
> all there. Above the door, words are carved in the stone: "WE SLEEP SO THAT
> THE DAWN CAN WAKE."
>
> Then a small voice comes from a dark corner. "Oh! Oh, oh, oh. It worked. It
> actually worked."

## Scene 2: Wick (10 minutes)

**Goal:** Teach skills and social checks. Let the players talk to an NPC.

> In a niche in the wall sits a brass lantern. A small orange flame burns
> inside it. The flame has two black eyes and a mouth. The lantern looks at
> you and clears its throat, which is strange, because it has no throat.
>
> "Sleepers of the Dawnwatch! I am Wick, Keeper of the Light, Guardian of the
> Hall, and Official Waker. I waited for you for three hundred years, four
> months, and two days. I counted. There was not much else to do."

Play Wick as proud, dramatic, and lonely. See Wick in the statblocks for
more personality notes.

### What Wick knows

Let the players ask questions. Wick tells them these things, but slowly, with
a lot of drama:

- The Unlit King is back. Wick felt it this morning. "The Dawnfire in the
  chapel went out. That only happens if he wakes."
- The keep is old and partly broken. Animals came in during the last years.
  "Rats. Big ones. As big as dogs. They bite. They squeak. Rude."
- The way out goes down the Stair of Teeth, through the old armory, and
  through the Chapel of Dawn. The Dawn Door is at the end of the chapel. It
  opens to the surface.
- This morning, something came into the chapel from the outside. Wick does
  not want to talk about it.

### The secret

Wick hides two facts:

1. Wick is very scared of the thing in the chapel.
2. Wick does not know how long ago the last human visited the keep. "Some
   time ago" is the answer. The true answer is two hundred years.

> **Teach: skills.** When a player asks Wick a question and you think that
> Wick hides something, say: "You can make a Wisdom (Insight) check to see if
> Wick tells the truth. Insight is a skill. Look for Insight on your sheet.
> If you have a big number there, your character is good at it."

| Check | DC | Result |
|-------|----|--------|
| Wisdom (Insight) | 12 | Wick is scared. The flame gets small each time someone says "chapel". |
| Charisma (Persuasion) | 10 | Wick tells the truth: "Dogs. Little dogs made of fire and sulfur. One for each of you, I think. I hate them." |
| Charisma (Intimidation) | 10 | Same, but Wick sulks for the rest of the scene. |
| Intelligence (History) | 10 | The character remembers Wick. The steward made Wick on the last night. The steward said, "Do not let them oversleep." |

> **Teach: roleplay comes first.** If a player is kind to Wick with good
> words, give a success with no roll. Say: "That was so good that you do not
> need to roll." This shows the players that good roleplay matters.

### The gifts

Before the characters leave, Wick says:

> "Wait! The steward left this for you." Wick nods at a small wooden box on
> the shelf. "I could not open it. I have no hands. It was a long three
> hundred years."

In the box is one **Potion of Healing** (red liquid, a glass bottle). A
character can drink it, or give it to a creature within 5 feet, as a Bonus
Action. That creature regains 2d4 + 2 HP.

Wick asks to come with the characters. One character carries Wick. Wick gives
light.

> **Teach: light and darkvision.** "Wick gives light like a Hooded Lantern:
> Bright Light for 30 feet, and Dim Light for 30 feet more. Some
> of you have darkvision on your sheet. You see in the dark, but only in grey.
> The others need Wick to see."

## Scene 3: The Stair of Teeth (15 minutes)

**Goal:** Teach Perception, disadvantage in dim light, saving throws, damage,
and hit points.

> A long, narrow stair goes down into the dark. The steps are old, and some
> are broken. High above the middle of the stair, a wooden shelf sticks out
> of the wall. Something round sits on it, in the shadows. The air smells of
> dust, old grease, and rusty iron.
>
> Wick whispers: "The Stair of Teeth. The steward put iron teeth on it to
> stop thieves. I do not remember the safe way. Sorry. Three hundred years."

### The trap: Caltrop Spill

Use the **Caltrop Spill** from Traps & Hazards in the Game Master's
Compendium. For levels 1 to 4, the DC is 11 and the damage is 1d10.

- **The trap:** A thin tripwire goes across the stair, halfway down. Above it,
  a barrel on the shelf is tied to the wire. The barrel holds iron caltrops
  (the "teeth") and a bucket of grease.
- **The light:** The shelf and the wire are 30 feet or more from Wick, in Dim
  Light. Dim Light makes the area Lightly Obscured. A creature has
  Disadvantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks to see something there.
- **Find it:** A character who looks at the stair makes a Wisdom (Perception)
  check against DC 11, with Disadvantage. On a success, the character sees
  the tripwire or the barrel.
- **Passive Perception:** If no player asks to look, compare the passive
  Perception of each character with DC 11. Disadvantage takes 5 from a passive
  score. With the pregens, nobody notices the trap without a roll.

> **Teach: passive Perception.** "Your sheet has a number called passive
> Perception. I use it when you do not ask to look. It is what your character
> notices without trying. In dim light it is 5 lower, because you cannot see
> well."

> **Teach: disadvantage again.** When a player looks, say: "It is dark down
> there, so you have Disadvantage. Roll two d20s and use the lower one. Wick
> can go closer and give more light. Then you roll normally." Wick's Bright
> Light reaches 30 feet. If a character carries Wick closer, the Disadvantage
> goes away.

- **Avoid it:** When the characters know about the wire, they step over it.
  No roll. Tell the players: "Good thinking saves you a roll."
- **Disarm it:** A character with Thieves' Tools can cut the wire carefully.
  This is a Dexterity check with Thieves' Tools against DC 11. The Shadow is
  proficient with Thieves' Tools, so the Shadow adds +5. On a failure, the
  trap triggers.

### If the trap triggers

> A thin twang. Above you, a barrel tips over. Iron spikes and black grease
> pour down the steps like rain.

The caltrops cover a 10-foot square of the stair. Each character in that
square makes a Dexterity saving throw against DC 11.

> **Teach: the saving throw.** "This is a saving throw. It is not something
> that you try to do. It is something that happens to you, and you try to
> resist it. Roll a d20 and add your Dexterity save. It is on your sheet,
> under Dexterity."

- **Fail:** 1d10 Piercing damage. Also, the character's Speed goes down by 10
  feet until it regains at least 1 Hit Point.
- **Success:** Half the damage. Roll 1d10 and divide by 2, rounded down.
- **After the trap:** The square is Difficult Terrain. A creature that moves
  through it at more than half its Speed takes the damage again, with no
  save. To cross safely, move slowly, or sweep the caltrops away with a
  shield (1 minute).

> **Teach: damage and hit points.** "Roll a d10. That is the damage. Take it
> away from your HP. HP are hit points. They show how much more you can take.
> At 0, you fall down."

**Watch the Spark.** The Spark has 8 HP, and the trap can deal up to 10. If a
character drops to 0 HP here, teach death saving throws now (see Scene 6).
Then let a friend use the Potion of Healing from Scene 2, or the Light's
*Healing Word*.

### The Bell Gate (optional)

Cut this part if you are short on time. Then the gate at the bottom is open.

> At the bottom of the stair is an iron gate. It is closed. Next to it hang
> three small bells. The first bell has a black circle on it. The second has
> a star. The third has a sun.
>
> Above the gate is a painting, faded and old. It shows the sky over the
> keep. On the left side of the painting, the sky is black. In the middle,
> stars come out. On the right side, the sun comes up.

**Solution:** Ring the bells in the order of the painting: the black circle,
then the star, then the sun. This also matches the motto in Scene 1: "We
sleep so that the dawn can wake."

**Hints:**

- Intelligence (History), DC 10: The character remembers that the Dawnwatch
  marched at night and arrived at dawn.
- Intelligence (Investigation), DC 10: The rope of the black circle bell is
  the most worn.
- Wick: "I think it is the order of the sky? Or the order of my favorite
  colors? No, that is gold, then gold, then gold."

**Wrong order:** A wrong bell triggers a **Tripwire Bell Net** (Traps &
Hazards in the Game Master's Compendium; for levels 1 to 4, the DC is 11).
In the book, a wire triggers this trap. Here, the wrong bell triggers it.

> All three bells ring at once, very loud. A heavy net drops from the
> ceiling.

- The net covers a 10-foot square in front of the gate. Each character under
  it makes a Dexterity saving throw against DC 11. On a fail, the character
  has the Restrained condition.
- A Restrained character can take an action to make a Strength (Athletics)
  check against DC 11. On a success, it is free.
- A character can also attack the net. The net has AC 10 and 20 Hit Points.

> **Teach: conditions.** "Restrained is a condition. Conditions are short
> rules for a bad state. While you are Restrained, your Speed is 0. Attacks
> against you have Advantage, and your attacks have Disadvantage. You also
> have Disadvantage on Dexterity saving throws." The conditions are in the Rules
> Reference in the Player's Compendium.

**Right order:** The gate swings open with a long, sad sound. Wick sings a
short song of victory. It is bad.

## Scene 4: The Armory (25 minutes)

**Goal:** Teach combat: initiative, turns, movement, actions, bonus actions,
attack rolls, AC, damage, and HP.

> Beyond the gate is a long hall full of old weapon racks. Most racks are
> empty. Rusty swords and broken spears lie on the floor. In the middle of
> the hall is a stone well. At the far end, a big wooden door is half open.
>
> Wick's light falls on the floor. The floor moves. No: something on the
> floor moves. Grey shapes as big as dogs look up from a pile of old
> shields. They have long yellow teeth and pink tails. They squeak.
>
> "Rats," Wick whispers. "I told you. Rude."

Use the scaling table in the overview for the number of Giant Rats. See the
[statblocks](../../statblocks.md).

### The map

Draw a simple map: a hall 60 feet long and 30 feet wide. One square is 5
feet.

- The characters stand at the gate, at one end.
- The rats are in the middle, around the pile of shields, 30 feet away.
- The well is in the middle of the hall. It is 5 feet wide. A creature can
  hide behind it.
- The door to the chapel is at the far end.

### Step-by-step teaching

Teach the first round slowly. Do one step at a time. From round 2, let the
players play at normal speed.

> **Teach 1: initiative.** "This is a fight. Everyone rolls a d20 and adds
> the Initiative number on the sheet. I roll for the rats. The highest
> result goes first. Then we go down the list."

Write the order on paper where all players can see it.

> **Teach 2: your turn.** "On your turn, you can move and take one action.
> Look at the Speed on your sheet. 30 feet is about six steps. You can move before
> or after your action, or split the move."

> **Teach 3: the attack roll.** "To attack, roll a d20 and add the attack
> number for your weapon. If the total is equal to or more than the AC of the
> enemy, you hit. AC is Armor Class. It shows how hard the enemy is to hit.
> These rats have AC 13, but usually I do not tell you the AC."

Tell the AC of the rats one time, so that the players learn the idea. For
the pups in Scene 6, do not tell it.

> **Teach 4: damage.** "You hit. Roll the damage dice for your weapon and
> add the number. A rat has only 7 HP." Describe how the rat falls.

> **Teach 5: the bonus action.** When a player has a bonus action, tell
> them. For example:
>
> - The Storm: "Rage is a bonus action. You can rage and attack on the same
>   turn."
> - The Light: "Healing Word is a bonus action."
> - The Shield: "Second Wind is a bonus action."

> **Teach 6: the enemy turn.** On the first rat turn, say: "Now the rats
> attack. I roll for them. The rat rolls a 14, plus 5 is 19. Your AC is 14.
> 19 is more than 14, so it hits." Show the players that the same rule
> applies to the enemies.

> **Teach 7: other actions.** When a player does not want to attack, show
> the action list on the cheat sheet. Dash, Dodge, Help, and Hide are easy to
> teach here.

### Moments to look for

- **Critical hit:** If a player rolls a 20, make it big. "A natural 20!
  Roll the damage dice two times."
- **Pack Tactics:** When a rat attacks a character, and another rat is next
  to that character, the rat has Advantage. Tell the players why. This
  teaches them to keep enemies apart.
- **Opportunity attack:** If a character runs away from a rat, the rat
  bites. Say: "When you leave the reach of an enemy, it gets a free attack.
  This is an opportunity attack. The Disengage action stops it."
- **Weapon Mastery:** When the Shield hits with Sap, or the Shadow hits with
  Vex, remind the player of the effect.
- **Sneak Attack:** When an ally stands next to the target of the Shadow,
  remind that player to add 1d6.

**If the fight goes badly:** The last rat always flees (see its tactics). If
the players are in trouble, let two rats flee instead.

### After the fight

> The last rat squeals and runs into a hole in the wall. The hall is quiet
> again. Then Wick
> speaks, much too loud: "VICTORY! Ha! Take that, you rude little..." Wick
> stops. From behind the far door comes a sound. A yip. Then many yips, and
> a smell of sulfur. Then nothing.
>
> "That," says Wick in a very small voice, "was the dogs."

**Treasure in the armory:** In the pile of shields, the characters find 15
gold coins of the old kingdom and one shield in good condition. A character
who searches the racks (Wisdom (Perception), DC 10) finds 10 arrows and 2
javelins.

## Scene 5: A short rest (10 minutes)

**Goal:** Teach the Short Rest and Hit Point Dice. Give the players time to
talk to each other as their characters.

Wick tells the characters to rest before they go into the chapel.

> Wick hops on the edge of the well. "The steward always said: never fight a
> big dog when you are tired. Actually, the steward said: never fight a big
> dog. But you have to. So rest first."

> **Teach: the Short Rest.** "A Short Rest takes one hour. During the rest,
> you can spend Hit Point Dice to heal. Look at your sheet. Roll the die and
> add your Constitution modifier. You get that many HP back. You have only a
> few Hit Point Dice. You get them back on a Long Rest, after a full night of
> sleep."

Go around the table. Remind each player what comes back:

| Character | What comes back on a Short Rest |
|-----------|---------------------------------|
| The Shield | 1 use of Second Wind |
| The Light | Nothing at level 1. Spell slots need a Long Rest. |
| The Spark | 1 spell slot (Arcane Recovery) |
| The Shadow | Nothing special |
| The Storm | 1 use of Rage |

All characters can drink from the well. It is cold and clean.

### The campfire talk (optional)

If you have time, give the players one question to talk about as their
characters. Do not ask for a roll. Choose one:

- "Wick asks: what is the first thing that you want to eat when you get
  out?"
- "Who in this group did you trust the least before the sleep? Why?"
- "Each of you hears a sound from the chapel. It sounds like a voice that you
  know. Whose voice is it?"

> **Teach: the game is not only fights.** "In this game, you can talk as your
> character, also when nothing happens. This is often the best part."

**To save time:** Skip the questions. Say "An hour passes," let the players
roll Hit Point Dice, and go on.

## Scene 6: The Chapel of Dawn (25 minutes)

**Goal:** Teach spell slots, reactions, conditions of danger, dropping to 0
HP, and death saving throws.

> The chapel is tall and round. High above, a crack in the roof lets in one
> thin line of grey light. In the middle of the room is a stone bowl, as big
> as a cart wheel. It is cold and empty. On the far side, behind the bowl, is
> a big door of white stone with a sun carved on it: the Dawn Door.
>
> In front of the door lies a dog. It is as big as a pony, and it is made of
> grey ash. In its chest, a red coal glows and fades, glows and fades, like
> breath. It opens one eye.

Use the [Ash Hound statblock](../../statblocks.md#ash-hound). Use the scaling
table in the overview for its HP and for extra mites.

### The map

- A round room, 50 feet across.
- The characters enter from the armory door, on one side.
- The stone bowl (the Dawnfire) is in the middle. It is 10 feet wide. It
  gives cover.
- The hound lies in front of the Dawn Door, on the far side.

### Before the fight

Let the players plan. Some good ideas:

- The Shadow tries to sneak around. Dexterity (Stealth) check against the
  passive Perception of the hound (13). On a success, the Shadow gets one
  free attack with advantage at the start of the fight.
- A player who talks to the hound gets nothing. It only growls. It cannot be
  reasoned with.
- **The Dawnfire:** A character who studies the bowl (Intelligence
  (Religion) or Intelligence (Arcana), DC 12) learns that a spell, holy
  words, or fire can light it again. See "Light the Dawnfire" below.

### Teaching during the fight

> **Teach 1: spell slots.** Before the first spell with a slot, say: "Your
> cantrips are free. Your level 1 spells use a spell slot. You have 2 slots.
> When they are gone, they are gone until a Long Rest. Use them when it
> matters."

> **Teach 2: the saving throw in combat.** When the hound uses Ash Breath,
> say: "Each of you in the cone makes a Dexterity saving throw. If you fail,
> you take full damage. If you succeed, you take half." If the Spark casts
> *Burning Hands* or the Light casts *Sacred Flame*, show that the enemy
> makes saving throws, too.

> **Teach 3: reactions.** When the hound bites the Spark, say: "You can use
> a reaction now. Cast *Shield* and get +5 AC. Does the bite still hit?"
> When the hound bites the Storm, remind the player of Stone's Endurance.

> **Teach 4: Bloodied.** When the hound drops to half HP, describe the
> cracks and the red light. Say: "When I describe an enemy like this, it is
> badly hurt."

### Dropping to 0 HP

This is the most important lesson of the scene. Let one character drop to 0
HP. The best choice is a character with a friend near, who can heal.

When a character drops to 0 HP:

> The world goes grey. The sound of the fight is far away. You fall to the
> cold stone floor, and you hear Wick shout your name.

> **Teach: death saving throws.** "You are at 0 HP. You are unconscious, but
> you are not dead. At the start of each of your turns, you roll a d20. No
> modifiers. 10 or more is a success. Less than 10 is a failure. Three
> successes, and you are stable. Three failures, and your character dies. A
> 20 means you wake up with 1 HP."
>
> Then say to the other players: "Your friend is dying. Any healing wakes
> them up at once. You can also use a Medicine check or the healer's kit to
> stop the dying, or give them the potion."

Show the ways to help:

- *Healing Word* from the Light. This is the best way. It is a bonus action,
  so the Light can also attack.
- *Spare the Dying* from the Light. The character is stable, but stays at 0
  HP.
- The potion of healing from Scene 2. A bonus action to give it.
- A Wisdom (Medicine) check, DC 10, as an action. On a success, the
  character is stable.

> **The Dawnwatch Oath (secret safety rule).** No character dies in this
> tutorial. If a character fails the third death saving throw, read this:
>
> > The darkness closes around you. Then, far away, you hear an old voice:
> > the voice of the steward, from three hundred years ago. "You swore to
> > wake the dawn. You do not get to sleep yet." Warm light floods your
> > chest.
>
> The character wakes with 1 HP. Say: "In a real game, your character dies
> here. Today the Dawnwatch Oath saves you, one time." This teaches the
> danger, and nobody loses a character.

### Light the Dawnfire

The Dawnfire is an optional bonus. A character can use an action to light it.
Any fire works: *Fire Bolt*, a spark from Wick, or a prayer from the Light.

When the Dawnfire burns:

> Gold fire roars up from the bowl. The line of grey light from the roof
> turns gold. The hound howls in pain.

- The hound has disadvantage on its attack rolls until the end of its next
  turn.
- Each character within 10 feet of the bowl regains 1d4 HP. This happens one
  time only.

### The end of the fight

> The red coal in the hound's chest cracks in two. The hound falls apart
> into a pile of ash, and the ash blows away up into the crack in the roof.
>
> On the floor, where the coal was, lies a small black stone. It is cold, and
> it hums. When you touch it, you hear a voice far away. It is deep and
> slow. It says one word: "Awake."

The black stone is a hook. It is a shard of the Unlit King's power. He knows
that the Dawnwatch is awake. Let the players keep it.

## Scene 7: The Dawn Door (10 minutes)

**Goal:** Give the "After" moment. Give rewards. Teach the level up.

> The white door is heavy. You push together. Slowly, it opens.
>
> Light. Real light. For a moment you see nothing. Then your eyes clear.
>
> You stand on a hill, at the top of a ruin. The keep was once a great
> fortress. Now it is a few broken walls with trees growing through them.
> Below the hill, where the capital city stood, there is a forest. Far away
> is a small village that you do not know. Smoke comes from its chimneys.
>
> Wick looks at the trees for a long time. "Ah," says Wick. "So it was
> longer than I thought."

### The After moment

Go around the table one more time. Use the answers from Scene 1. Ask each
player one question. Choose the one that matches the "Before" answer:

- "You made a promise to someone. That person is gone for three hundred
  years. What do you do with that promise now?"
- "You still have the thing that you stole. Who did it belong to, and is it
  worth something now?"
- "Your god sent you a sign before the sleep. Look at the sky now. Do you
  see the sign again?"
- "You showed off a spell on your first day. Nobody alive remembers it. How
  does that feel?"
- "You did not want to sleep. Now you are awake, and everything is gone. Was
  it worth it?"

> **Teach: the story belongs to the players.** "Your answers become the
> story. A GM uses what you say to build the next adventure."

Give Heroic Inspiration again for the best answer, if the player does not
have it.

### The cliffhanger

> Then the light changes. In the north, the sky goes dark, as if night comes
> in the middle of the day. A black cloud rolls over the far mountains. In
> the cloud, you see a shape. It is very tall, with a crown of black fire. It
> does not move. It looks at you.
>
> The black stone in your pocket hums. A voice speaks in your head, deep and
> slow: "Sleepers. I also slept. And now we are both awake."
>
> The cloud is gone. The sun shines again. Wick's flame is very small.
>
> "Well," says Wick. "That was the Unlit King. I think he knows."

### Rewards

- **The Dawnwatch Sigil:** Each character finds a silver brooch with a sun on
  it, in the pocket of their old uniform. It is a common magic item. While a
  character wears it, they always know which way is east. One time per Long
  Rest, when the character makes a death saving throw, the character can
  treat a roll of 9 or lower as a 10.
- **Old coins:** The 15 gold coins from the armory are three hundred years
  old. A collector in the village pays 50 gold pieces for them.
- **Wick:** Wick goes with the party. Wick is a friend, a light, and a guide.

> **Teach: experience and levels.** "When characters survive danger, they
> get experience points, or XP. With enough XP, they go up a level. You go to
> level 2 now."

### Level up

Each character goes to level 2. The "At level 2" section on each sheet tells
the player what changes. Teach it in this order:

1. **More HP.** Add the number on the sheet. This is the average of the Hit
   Point Die plus the Constitution modifier. A player can also roll the Hit
   Point Die instead, but the fixed number is safer.
2. **One more Hit Point Die.**
3. **New features.** Read them together.
4. **More spell slots** for the Light and the Spark.

If the session ends here, the level up is the reward. If you play more
sessions, the characters start the next adventure at level 2.

### End of the session

> The village is far away. It is a long walk down the hill. Behind you, the
> keep is quiet. In front of you is a world that you do not know, and a king
> in the north who knows your names.
>
> The Dawnwatch is awake.

Ask the players three short questions:

1. "What was the most fun moment?"
2. "Which rule was hard to understand?"
3. "Do you want to play again?"

## What next

If the players want more, here are hooks for a second session:

- **The village:** The people in the village speak a strange new form of the
  old language. They have a legend about "the Sleepers under the hill." Some
  think it is a children's story. One old woman knows that it is true.
- **The black stone:** It hums louder at night. It points north, like a
  compass.
- **The other keeps:** Wick remembers that the kingdom built more than one
  hall of sleepers. Are there other Dawnwatch groups? Are they awake?
- **The "Before" answers:** Use the answers from Scene 1. The promise, the
  stolen thing, and the sign from the god can each become a quest.
